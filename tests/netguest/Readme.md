# Network gateway guests

The netguest suite gives Rach2's wired card to an unmodified gateway guest through VFIO and VT-d, reaches the network through that guest over a tap link, and takes the card back. The guests are FreeBSD 15.1 with pf and Debian 13 with nftables, each prepared once from its project's own cloud image; the tap link runs with QEMU moving its frames and with `/dev/vhost-net` moving them in the kernel. Every case is judged by what the kernel and the guest count: bytes on the tap, frames vhost-net moved, the guest's count of denied packets, and the VFIO, KVM, IOMMU and interrupt-vector counts before and after. The suite has a QEMU form, which etude runs on a Linux host, and a native form for the Lenovo, run from the Mac.

## Running

`ARCH=x86_64 tests/run netguest` runs the six cases of `tests/cases/machine.netguest.json`; `RACH2_CASES=machine.netguest.debian` selects one. Each case boots the GNU root of the assignment suite ([assign](../assign/Readme.md)) under KVM on q35 with QEMU's VT-d unit, the patched QEMU that suite builds, four processors and 6 GiB, and gives away an e1000 at `0000:01:00.0`, behind the PCIe root port at `0000:00:06.0`, on the user network `10.0.3.0/24`; Rach2 keeps a second e1000 on `10.0.2.0/24`. The guest runs under Rach2's KVM, a third level of virtualization on the testing host, so its times there mean nothing; nettput prints each test's seconds for the record, and no check reads them. The case's peer is `nettput -s -b 127.0.0.1` on the host at the case's port, which the machine and its guest reach at `10.0.3.2`.

On the Lenovo, from the Mac, with the machine taken and booted as the [machine skill](../../.agents/skills/machine/SKILL.md) says, a kernel with the tap device, vhost-net and VFIO on the GNU root:

```sh
make ARCH=x86_64 build/tests/cases/netguest/nettput build/tests/cases/netguest/nettput-host
sh tests/netguest/lenovo.sh -s STAGE stage
sh tests/netguest/lenovo.sh run
sh tests/netguest/lenovo.sh clean
```

STAGE holds `nettput` and the two gateway images; the images are made on Linux (Building, below), so STAGE is a copy of the testing host's `build/tests/cases/netguest`. `run` takes the machine's address and wired interface from `tools/flow/machines` (`lenovo network`, `lenovo interface`), so that this suite writes no address, station address or serial of the machine itself; `RACH2_MACHINE_MAC`, when set, is checked against the interface before anything runs. Start it from a shell with macOS's Local Network permission, the release's `tmux -L rach2-lan`, since the Mac's nettput is charged to the program that started it. The script's opening comment has every option.

## Results

`run.sh` prints netguest.sh's lines, among them `netguest: counts: STEP NAME=VALUE...` at the start, once the card is given, once the guest is ready, after the transfers and once the card is back, traffic.sh's `traffic: report: ...`, the guest's report each verdict reads, then `netguest: case: CASE: pass` or `netguest: case: CASE: fail: REASON` for each case and last, once the card, its interface and the other interfaces are put back, `netguest: result: pass: N cases` or `netguest: result: fail: F of N cases failed`, with what was not put back after a comma. Each case's directory under the run's scratch keeps the guest's console and QEMU's messages; the scratch is removed after a pass and named after a failure as `netguest: evidence: DIR`. On the Lenovo the run's directory is fetched into `build/runs/netguest-lenovo-TIME/run`, with the whole output in its `log` and the exit status in its `status`, both of which `lenovo.sh` judges.

## Interfaces

* `run.sh [-D] [-B] [-W] [-c BDF | -n IFACE] [-a ADDR/PREFIX] [-d SECONDS] [-o OUT] [-A kvm|tcg] PEER PORT CASE...` runs the cases as root on Rach2. The cases are `cycle`, `debian`, `freebsd`, `debian-vhost`, `freebsd-vhost` and `kill`, as Tests below says. `-D` detaches the run, once it has started, with its output in `OUT/log`, its process ID in `OUT/pid` and its exit status in `OUT/status`; `-W` takes every IPv4 address and default route off every other interface for the run and puts them back, read back with the first address the primary again, at its end, first putting back what an earlier run recorded in `OUT/others` and could not; `-B` reboots a machine whose network does not answer by the card once the card and the other interfaces are back, within `-d`'s seconds when it gives any; and `-d` gives netguest.sh its deadline for each boot and bounds cycle's probes the same way. `-D` keeps OUT, which its start line names, since `OUT/status` is the run's result. At its end the card's interface has the addresses and default routes it had at the start, whatever a case left, and every process the run started is gone.
* `netguest.sh -c BDF -i IMAGE [-V] [-F 0|1] [-p PEER] [-P PORT] [-k|-q] [-d SECONDS] [-- COMMAND...]` does one boot: the interface's address off, the card from its driver to vfio-pci, QEMU with the card through VFIO and a `virtio-net-pci` on `tap0`, with `vhost=on` under `-V`, the guest's ready line, which must name the filter asked for, PEER's first answer through it, COMMAND, the guest's end by its power button, by SIGTERM under `-q` or by SIGKILL under `-k`, and the card back with its addresses, the first the primary, and its default routes, metrics and all. It fails a boot whose QEMU ended before it was asked to or ended otherwise than asked, after which a VFIO or KVM count or the free vectors differ from the start, or in which an IOMMU fault was counted. Each attempt at PEER is a connection bound to the interface it means. A power button's end must show the guest's own power-off on its console, and a vhost boot must end one vhost-net attachment, tap0's. A signal or the deadline stops every process the boot started, QEMU and everything under the command among them, before the card is taken back.
* `traffic.sh transfers|deny|kill` is the COMMAND of the cases' boots.
* `nettput` is one TCP tool for Rach2, Linux and macOS whose tests end at a count, each of its connections bound to an interface with `-I` where the system can: `send` and `recv` of a number of bytes, `rr` exchanges and `crr` connections, each answer checked against what was sent and each stream's end where the test puts it; `wait`, the attempts the path takes to answer, each a connect that ends when the server answers or refuses or the kernel gives the handshake up; and `drop`, connects at once that must each end with `ETIMEDOUT`, unanswered and unrefused after the kernel's own retransmissions of the SYN. Its server reaps each client's process as it ends. Its server, `nettput -s [-b ADDR]`, serves each client in a process of its own, which ends with the server.
* The guest takes its parameters from the first sector of its second virtio disk, `rules=N opt=none|basic state=1|0 fwd=PORT filter=1|0`, and writes `NETGUEST READY ...` or `NETGUEST FAILED reason` on its serial console, then `NETGUEST STATS outer_in=N outer_out=N deny9=N states=N` every two seconds, as each system's `netguest.sh` says: deny9 is the count of the rule on TCP to port 9, which drops it with the filter and only counts it without, and `unknown` when it cannot be read. Rach2 has `10.77.0.2/24` on the tap and the guest `10.77.0.1`; the guest asks DHCP for the card's address, translates `10.77.0.0/24` out of it and forwards TCP port 22 of it to Rach2's.

The counts are the kernel's `/proc/stat` records ([vfio](../../rach2/sys/vfio/Readme.md#interfaces), [KVM](../../rach2/sys/kvm/Readme.md), and vhost-net's `vhost_net_detaches`, `vhost_net_detached_to_guest` and `vhost_net_detached_from_guest`, [proc(5)](../../man/man5/proc.5) and [vhost-net(4)](../../man/man4/vhost-net.4)) and tap0's line of `/proc/net/dev`.

## Sources and license

The gateway images are FreeBSD 15.1-RELEASE's BASIC-CLOUDINIT VM image, under FreeBSD's BSD licenses, and the Debian 13 generic cloud image with nftables from Debian's archive, each package under its own free license; neither is changed beyond the boot script and settings that its `setup.sh` writes. They are fetched by the digests their pins give and are never committed. Everything in this directory is Rach2's.

## Design

The card is the machine's only link on the Lenovo, so the way back is in every exit path: netguest.sh's trap gives the card back to its driver and puts the interface's address and default route back, each step read back and tried three times, at the end, after a failure, at a signal and at its deadline; run.sh's trap binds the driver again whatever stopped the run. FreeBSD's `em(4)` needs the 82540's I/O BAR, which Rach2's VFIO shows. The virtio card offers no mergeable receive buffers, since with them FreeBSD 15.1's `vtnet` takes a 4 KiB cluster for every receive buffer and ran out of them on the Lenovo. The inner link has no checksum or segmentation offload for either guest.

## Building

`make ARCH=x86_64 build/tests/cases/netguest/netguest.ext2` builds the payload: `run.sh`, `netguest.sh`, `traffic.sh`, the static `nettput` and the two images with their records, on an ext2 disk the cases mount read-only. `prepare.sh freebsd|debian OUT` makes an image on a Linux host with QEMU, qemu-img, xorriso, curl, xz and flock, and sha512sum for Debian, under KVM where `/dev/kvm` opens. FreeBSD's is the BASIC-CLOUDINIT image that `freebsd/image` pins by URL and SHA-256; Debian's is the cloud image of `tests/graphics/debian-ref/image`, with nftables from the snapshot that file names. One boot with a cidata seed runs the system's `setup.sh`, which installs the boot script, `netguest.sh` of the system's directory, for every later boot and powers the guest off; the result is converted to a compressed qcow2 with no backing file and kept in `build/cache/netguest` with the digest of its inputs, prepare.sh and debian-ref.sh among them, so that it is remade only when the recipe or the pins change. One preparation runs on the cache at a time, a download enters it only once its digest checks, and a record takes its name only after its image has taken its own, so that a record older than its image, or none, makes Make publish the pair again.

## Tests

* `cycle`: iproute2 makes, addresses and removes `tap9`, which must not be there before; the driver lets the card go and takes it back three times, the interface getting its addresses and default routes back and the peer answering by it after each; the free interrupt vectors are as before.
* `debian`, `freebsd`: the guest with its filter; nettput sends and receives 8388608 bytes and makes 1000 exchanges and 200 connections through it, which tap0's byte counts must show each way; ten attempts at once at the peer's port 9 must each go unanswered and unrefused until Rach2's TCP gives the handshake up after its retransmissions, and the guest's deny9 count must grow by every SYN they sent: each attempt's first and Rach2's seven retransmissions of it, 80, or fewer when `/proc/net/snmp`'s count of connections opened and segments sent again, which leaves out a retransmission the driver refused, grew by less, so that the guest dropped every one.
* `debian-vhost`, `freebsd-vhost`: the same transfers through the guest with translation alone and the tap on vhost-net. vhost-net's `vhost_net_detached_` counts must grow from the boot's start to its end by at least 5541 frames each way, the fewest frames of at most 1514 bytes that carry 8388608 bytes, and `vhost_net_detaches` by at least one, since FreeBSD attaches tap0 twice and moves no frame by the first; tap0's own byte counts of at least 8388608 each way put those frames on tap0, the suite's machine running no other vhost-net guest. QEMU's stderr must have no line on vhost, which QEMU writes when it falls back to moving the frames itself, and the guest's first report after the transfers must count no packet to port 9.
* `kill`: QEMU is killed under the Debian guest once tap0 has carried a mebibyte of a send through it, with the send still running, and must die of SIGKILL.

Every boot also requires the card back with its driver, the peer answering on it, and every VFIO, KVM, IOMMU and vector count as at its start, each count present. No elapsed time decides a result, and no wait in the suite or its guests has a bound of its own: a connect ends at the kernel's count of SYN retransmissions, and a case that cannot go on waits for its caller's bound, etude's on Linux and `-d` on the Lenovo.
