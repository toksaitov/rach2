# The KVM suite

The KVM suite runs Rach2's KVM the way a person uses it: Debian's QEMU on the x86-64 GNU root boots guests with `-accel kvm`, and kvm-unit-tests and Linux's KVM selftests, which the [KVM layer](../../vendor/gnu/kvm/Readme.md) stages under `/opt`, run under it. This directory has the runner that boots a guest to its token, the runner of the two test corpora with their lists, and the recipe of the Debian guest. `tests/run kvm` runs them in QEMU as the cases of [`machine.kvm.json`](../cases/machine.kvm.json), on the processor profiles `max-svm` and `host-svm` that give Rach2 AMD's virtualization, booted with `rach2.tscreliable`, since neither profile reports the invariant TSC that QEMU's TSC is and the kvmclock needs its rate ([timer](../../rach2/sys/arch/x86/Readme.md)); on the Lenovo, a VMX machine, the same runners run by hand on its installed root.

## Running

`tests/run kvm` runs these cases, each on an x86-64 Rach2 that boots `gnu-test.disk` with this directory's files on a data disk, `kvm.ext2`:

* `entry`: the small real-mode guest in `svm-entry.c` reads its memory slot, executes CPUID, reads CR0 and EFER, exits through port I/O and HLT, and reads a replacement frame after MAP_FIXED. It starts with XCR0 at its reset value, which also checks that the backend restores the full XSAVE image before applying the guest's XCR0.
* `slots`: `svm-entry slots` repeats the read after fork, mprotect, discard and munmap with a replacement page. A guest then counts in another page while the caller forks, protects and replaces the data page; its first counter increment proves it was running before the changes, and it exits only after reading the replacement value. The same case reads VGA memory through a `/dev/mem` slot and the end of a 4 MiB GEM object through another slot, on std-vga.
* `leak`: `leak.sh` kills QEMU after the Debian guest prints its kernel version. The five live KVM counts and `fs.file-nr` must return to their initial values, QEMU must have no pinned pages before the kill, and its address space must disappear after it is reaped.
* `boot-debian-kvm` and `boot-rach2-kvm`: Debian and the small i686 Rach2 guest boot under Rach2's SVM on `max-svm`, on Linux and macOS. These boots use the full in-kernel interrupt controller. The Rach2 guest boots with `rach2.tscreliable`, so that its clock and its waits read the TSC and not the HPET, each read of which is an exit to the guest's QEMU. The `-nested` variants run on `host-svm`, on Linux alone.
* `boot-debian-tcg`: `kvm-boot.sh -a tcg` boots the Debian guest in Rach2's QEMU under TCG to its token.
* `unit-tests` and `selftests`: `corpus.sh` runs the corpus under Rach2's KVM, on the `max-svm` profile, on Linux and on macOS.
* `unit-tests-nested` and `selftests-nested`: the same on the `host-svm` profile, on Linux alone.

The accelerator outside is the run's, as for every case: on the testing PC KVM, so that Rach2 runs its guests under nested KVM, and on the Mac TCG, which emulates SVM; `RACH2_QEMU_ACCEL=tcg` runs the `max-svm` cases under TCG on the testing PC too. `host-svm` starts only under KVM. Under TCG the outer machine has one processor, whatever the case asks ([etude](../../tools/etude/Readme.md)), for two faults of QEMU's that Results names.

`svm-entry race SECONDS CPU`, which no case runs, checks an outer TCG for the first of them: a guest on processor CPU reads a byte through nested paging and writes it to a port, while a child on another processor runs FXRSTOR without end, for SECONDS. It prints `svm-entry: race: pass: N exits in S s on processor CPU`, or the first exit that is not the port with the byte.

`tests/run bed-shm` runs the two cases of [`machine.bed-shm.json`](../cases/machine.bed-shm.json), `virgl` and `nocopy`, on the testing PC alone, under `host-svm`: an x86-64 Rach2 boots `gnu-test.disk` with `bed-shm.sh`, `vgshmprobe` and `nocopy.c` on a data disk, `bed-shm.ext2`, and the test bed's VM, which `bed/vm/image/vm.sh rach2` makes ([bed/vm](../../bed/vm/Readme.md)), as its third disk, `vm.disk`.

On a Rach2 with the GNU root, the runners run alone, as root:

    sh kvm-boot.sh -k guests/debian/vmlinuz -i guests/debian/initrd
    sh kvm-boot.sh -I split -k guests/debian/vmlinuz -i guests/debian/initrd
    sh kvm-boot.sh -a tcg -d /tmp/i686.img -t 'RACH2 OK'
    sh corpus.sh unit unit-tests.list
    sh corpus.sh selftests selftests.list
    sh bed-shm.sh -d /dev/disk2 -p ./vgshmprobe
    sh bed-shm.sh -d /dev/disk2 -p ./vgshmprobe -k ./nocopy.c

[`bed-shm.sh`](bed-shm.sh) runs Rach2's virtio-gpu over the region it shares with the VM. ivshmemd attaches a region of 32 MiB to `/dev/vgpu-shm` and two more instances must be refused as busy (`attach`). QEMU boots the VM's disk under `-accel kvm` with the region on an `ivshmem-doorbell` of two vectors; once virtio-gpu has bound over the region, `vgshmprobe draw` draws the recorded display frame with virgl in the VM, reads it back and needs llvmpipe's CRC, 6e8e217c (`draw`). Across the draw both sides must have taken the doorbells, `/proc/stat`'s `kvm_ioeventfd_signals` must have grown by at least the doorbells the VM rang, and `kvm_irqfd_raises` by at least those Rach2 rang, so that no doorbell went through QEMU (`doorbell`). Then QEMU is stopped, `vgshmprobe pending 4` submits four fenced commands, and QEMU is killed: every fence must fail, the buffer's `VIRTGPU_WAIT` must return, with 0 as on Linux once the fences have ended, a wait after the death must fail, and ivshmemd must report the detach for QEMU's closed connection (`death`). Each check prints `bed-shm: NAME: pass` or `fail` with its reason, and the last line is `bed-shm: result: pass` or `fail`; the VM's console, ivshmemd's output and the counts stay in the directory `-o` names, `/tmp/bed-shm` by default. The wait for the bind gives up when QEMU exits, or when for 600 s the VM's console stays silent and QEMU uses no processor time. The VM's disk takes no ioeventfd, and QEMU raises its interrupts without an irqfd, so that both counts are the doorbells'.

With `-k`, the case `nocopy`, the draw, the doorbells and the death with fences pending give way to Venus's buffers mapped from the VM's own pages ([the transport's page lists](../../rach2/sys/virtio/Readme.md)). [`nocopy.c`](nocopy.c) is built with the GNU root's GCC (`build`) and runs on Venus over the VM's lavapipe: sixteen rounds in which the CPU writes a pattern into one host-visible buffer, the GPU copies it to another and the CPU reads it back, and the GPU fills a value that the CPU reads, each ordered by a fence. The VM's backend must have pinned the buffers and answered their page lists; while both buffers are mapped, KVM's map count must exceed its initial value and its mapped-page count must have grown by at least the two buffers' pages. The rounds must read what was written, and once the program has ended `kvm_gpa_maps` and `kvm_gpa_map_pages` must be back to their counts before and ivshmemd must report no live map (`pages`, `coherent`, `release`). `vgshmprobe refuse` makes a GL buffer of virgl's, which Debian's virglrenderer cannot export, and a blob that is not mappable: the first map must be refused, with the backend's refusal on the VM's console, and the second must give no mapping (`refuse`). The timings of the mapped buffers, of copies through transfers and of blobs in the region go to `measure.txt`, with QEMU's version and the kernel's first (`measure`); each transfer must finish with a successful wait before its time is printed. Last, `nocopy hold` keeps a mapping touched while QEMU is killed: its next touch must raise SIGBUS, and both counts must be back to before, having grown while the mapping was held (`revoke`, `leak`).

[`kvm-boot.sh`](kvm-boot.sh) boots a kernel and initramfs, or a raw disk under SeaBIOS, with `-accel kvm,kernel-irqchip=on`, or `-accel tcg`, and the console on a serial port it reads once a second. It passes on the token, `KVMGUEST OK` by default, and fails when QEMU ends without it or the console stays silent for 600 s, a bound on silence that `-w` changes, not on the boot's length. `-I split` keeps the PIC, IOAPIC and PIT in QEMU; `-I off` also keeps the local APIC there. Its header lists the options. [`corpus.sh`](corpus.sh) runs each test that a list runs, kvm-unit-tests' through their own `run_tests.sh` with no timeout, and judges each result against the list; its last line is `kvm-corpus: result: pass` or `fail` with the names of the tests that gave another result.

## Design

The lists, [`unit-tests.list`](unit-tests.list) and [`selftests.list`](selftests.list), name every test of the two corpora, one a line: its name, what Rach2's KVM must give (`pass`; `skip`, the test's own skip for what the processor or QEMU lacks; `fail`, where Rach2 is to fail as Linux does; or `excluded`, not run), Linux's result on the testing PC, and the reason. A test whose result is the processor's names it for each, as `FLAG?A:B`: A where the `flags` of Rach2's `/proc/cpuinfo` have FLAG, and B where they do not, each of A and B a result or the same form again, which `corpus.sh` settles before it runs the list. Four FLAGs are not the processor's: `kvm_cap_vm_tsc_control`, where `/dev/kvm` answers KVM_CAP_VM_TSC_CONTROL above 0, which `corpus.sh` asks through perl; `kvm_lbr`, where the PERF_CAPABILITIES that `/dev/kvm` offers as a feature MSR has an LBR format, which `corpus.sh` reads through perl with KVM_GET_MSRS; `kvm_pmu`, where `/sys/module/kvm/parameters/enable_pmu` reads `Y`, as Rach2's KVM answers when the host's counters are usable; and `intel`, where `/proc/cpuinfo`'s `vendor_id` is GenuineIntel. Four rows of that kind are named here and the six PMU rows below: kvm-unit-tests' `smap` passes with `smap` and skips without it, as on the Lenovo's i3-4030U, and so does the selftest `xss_msr_test` with `xsaves`, which that processor also lacks; `tsc_scaling_sync` passes where KVM_CAP_VM_TSC_CONTROL is above 0, which Rach2's KVM answers with SVM's TSC ratio or VMX's TSC scaling, as the test itself asks, and skips where it is 0, as under TCG and on the Lenovo, where it is 0 on Linux too; and `monitor_mwait_test` skips without `monitor`, as on the testing PC under Hyper-V, and with it is expected to fail, since the 6.12.115 program turns a quirk back on with KVM_CAP_DISABLE_QUIRKS2, which Linux 6.12.115 no longer does: by the two sources its third case then takes a #UD it does not expect, a result read from them and not yet seen on Linux. Linux's results were taken by the same tests under WSL's Linux 6.18.58 with its KVM on the Ryzen 9 7950X3D, kvm-unit-tests with `kernel-irqchip=split`, and are kept in [`ref/`](ref). A test Linux passes must pass; a test Linux skips for a lack of the processor or of the testing PC's kernel, such as SEV, AMX, PKS, CET, TSX, Xen's hypercalls or the forced emulation prefix, must skip; a test Linux fails there must still pass, since its failure is the outer layers' or a selftest older than the kernel, and the row names Linux's failure, except `hyperv_connections`, which fails one of its seven tests under Linux and is expected to fail as it does there. The excluded tests are nested virtualization (every SVM, VMX and EPT test, and the selftests `hyperv_svm_test` and `triple_fault_event_test`, which needs SVM or VMX in its guest), which the owner excluded on 2026-11-10; guest_memfd, SMM and asynchronous page faults (the selftests `guest_memfd_test` and `smm_test` and kvm-unit-tests' `asyncpf`), which the owner left for a later milestone on 2026-11-10; and kvm-unit-tests' three tests built only for i386.

The PMU rows follow the host's counters. With them, the selftest `pmu_event_filter_test` must pass, and the selftests `pmu_counters_test` and `vmx_pmu_caps_test` must pass on Intel and skip on AMD, as on Linux, whose tests require an Intel host. kvm-unit-tests' `pmu` must pass on a machine of its own and fail under a hypervisor, the testing PC's Hyper-V and WSL's KVM, which count their own work on each exit while a guest's counter runs: there Linux 6.12.115 in a guest of WSL's KVM, as deep as Rach2, fails 21 or 22 of its 77 subtests, all its instruction counts 2 to 3% high, the six counters together and every overflow, and WSL's Linux itself fails 7 to 10 overflows, by turns. Without the counters, as under TCG, every PMU row skips, `pmu` because its configuration requires `enable_pmu` to read `Y`. `pmu_lbr` must pass where KVM offers an LBR format, as Linux's Debian passes it on the Lenovo's Haswell with 16 records, and skip elsewhere, as on Linux on the testing PC. `pmu_pebs` skips, as on Linux on both machines. The hard-lockup detector holds a counter, Intel's fixed counter 1 or general counter 0, which the PMU tests count on having: `pmu` and `pmu_lbr` require `kernel.nmi_watchdog` to read 0, and `pmu_counters_test` uses every counter, so `corpus.sh` sets it to 0 for its run and puts it back on every exit.

The Debian guest, which [`guests/mkdebian.sh`](guests/mkdebian.sh) makes, is trixie's cloud kernel, 6.12.107, and an initramfs of Debian's static BusyBox 1.37.0 whose [`/init`](guests/debian-init) prints the kernel's version, the processor count, the first processor's flags and the clock source its kernel chose, `kvm-clock` under KVM, then `KVMGUEST OK`, and powers off. Both packages are pinned in [`guests/debian.txt`](guests/debian.txt) by the SHA-256 that the Debian layer's signed snapshot index gives. The guest boots in seconds even under TCG inside Rach2 and needs no disk, so a failure to reach its token is the hypervisor's. Make writes it to `build/kvm/guests/debian`, and puts it with the runners and the lists into `build/tests/cases/kvm/kvm.ext2`, which every case mounts read-only and copies to `/tmp/kvm`.

The i686 Rach2 guest is the ordinary kernel with a 1 MiB KRFFS root. Its shipped `init-shell` starts the first user program, `guests/rach2-init.c`, as `/bin/sh`; that program prints `KVMRACH2 OK` and powers off. It needs no disk, network or input from the controller.

## Adding tests

A check of the shared region goes in `bed-shm.sh` as a line `bed-shm: NAME: pass` and an assertion NAME in `machine.bed-shm.json`, which [`bed_shm_scenario.c`](../cases/machine/bed_shm_scenario.c) reads by its list. A guest of its own goes under `guests/` with its recipe, the token its console prints and a case in `machine.kvm.json` whose last part of the identity [`kvm_scenario.c`](../cases/machine/kvm_scenario.c) reads; its files join `KVM_FILES` or the data disk's rule in `tests/system/suites.mk`. A new revision of a corpus changes its list: each new test gets its row from Linux's result on the testing PC.

## Limits

* The full controller boots the Debian x86-64 and i686 KRFFS guests under SVM on `max-svm`, and the i686 guest under nested KVM in 105 to 229 s, its 1000 Hz tick taking most of its time there. The full controller's nested-KVM boot of Debian, its VMX boots and the corpus still await qualification. The earlier split-controller boots and the corpus's rows that differed on the Lenovo are named in [Results](#results).
* The `max-svm` profile under TCG lacks NRIP save, TSC scaling and the pause filter, and has the seven faults of Results, the fifth of which, the timer interrupt left pending, can leave a guest of Rach2's KVM that spins with its IF clear waiting on an interrupt QEMU does not deliver; the `host-svm` profile runs only on the testing PC.

## Tests

The suite is its own test. `kvm-boot.sh` was tried on the testing PC with the Debian guest under KVM and TCG, with a token the guest never prints, with an option QEMU refuses and with a guest that never starts, and `corpus.sh` with short lists against the outer QEMU, where it reported a listed pass that failed and left no scratch.
