# The KVM suite

The KVM suite runs Rach2's KVM the way a person uses it: Debian's QEMU on the x86-64 GNU root boots guests with `-accel kvm`, and kvm-unit-tests and Linux's KVM selftests, which the [KVM layer](../../vendor/gnu/kvm/Readme.md) stages under `/opt`, run under it. This directory has the runner that boots a guest to its token, the runner of the two test corpora with their lists, and the recipe of the Debian guest. `tests/run kvm` runs them in QEMU as the cases of [`machine.kvm.json`](../cases/machine.kvm.json), on the processor profiles `max-svm` and `host-svm` that give Rach2 AMD's virtualization, booted with `rach2.tscreliable`, since neither profile reports the invariant TSC that QEMU's TSC is and the kvmclock needs its rate ([timer](../../rach2/sys/arch/x86/Readme.md)); on the Lenovo, a VMX machine, the same runners run by hand on its installed root.

## Running

`tests/run kvm` runs these cases, each on an x86-64 Rach2 that boots `gnu-test.disk` with this directory's files on a data disk, `kvm.ext2`:

* `entry`: the small real-mode guest in `svm-entry.c` reads its memory slot, executes CPUID, reads CR0 and EFER, exits through port I/O and HLT, and reads a replacement frame after MAP_FIXED. It starts with XCR0 at its reset value, which also checks that the backend restores the full XSAVE image before applying the guest's XCR0.
* `slots`: `svm-entry slots` repeats the read after fork, mprotect, discard and munmap with a replacement page. A guest then counts in another page while the caller forks, protects and replaces the data page; its first counter increment proves it was running before the changes, and it exits only after reading the replacement value. The same case reads VGA memory through a `/dev/mem` slot and the end of a 4 MiB GEM object through another slot, on std-vga.
* `leak`: `leak.sh` kills QEMU after the Debian guest prints its kernel version. The five live KVM counts and `fs.file-nr` must return to their initial values, QEMU must have no pinned pages before the kill, and its address space must disappear after it is reaped.
* `boot-debian-kvm` and `boot-rach2-kvm`: Debian and the small i686 Rach2 guest boot under Rach2's SVM on `max-svm`, on Linux and macOS. These boots use the full in-kernel interrupt controller. The `-nested` variants run on `host-svm`, on Linux alone.
* `boot-debian-tcg`: `kvm-boot.sh -a tcg` boots the Debian guest in Rach2's QEMU under TCG to its token.
* `unit-tests` and `selftests`: `corpus.sh` runs the corpus under Rach2's KVM, on the `max-svm` profile, on Linux and on macOS.
* `unit-tests-nested` and `selftests-nested`: the same on the `host-svm` profile, on Linux alone.

The accelerator outside is the run's, as for every case: on the testing PC KVM, so that Rach2 runs its guests under nested KVM, and on the Mac TCG, which emulates SVM; `RACH2_QEMU_ACCEL=tcg` runs the `max-svm` cases under TCG on the testing PC too. `host-svm` starts only under KVM.

`tests/run bed-shm` runs the one case of [`machine.bed-shm.json`](../cases/machine.bed-shm.json), `virgl`, on the testing PC alone, under `host-svm`: an x86-64 Rach2 boots `gnu-test.disk` with `bed-shm.sh` and `vgshmprobe` on a data disk, `bed-shm.ext2`, and the test bed's VM, which `bed/vm/image/vm.sh rach2` makes ([bed/vm](../../bed/vm/Readme.md)), as its third disk, `vm.disk`.

On a Rach2 with the GNU root, the runners run alone, as root:

    sh kvm-boot.sh -k guests/debian/vmlinuz -i guests/debian/initrd
    sh kvm-boot.sh -I split -k guests/debian/vmlinuz -i guests/debian/initrd
    sh kvm-boot.sh -a tcg -d /tmp/i686.img -t 'RACH2 OK'
    sh corpus.sh unit unit-tests.list
    sh corpus.sh selftests selftests.list
    sh bed-shm.sh -d /dev/disk2 -p ./vgshmprobe

[`bed-shm.sh`](bed-shm.sh) runs Rach2's virtio-gpu over the region it shares with the VM. ivshmemd attaches a region of 32 MiB to `/dev/vgpu-shm` and two more instances must be refused as busy (`attach`). QEMU boots the VM's disk under `-accel kvm` with the region on an `ivshmem-doorbell` of two vectors; once virtio-gpu has bound over the region, `vgshmprobe draw` draws the recorded display frame with virgl in the VM, reads it back and needs llvmpipe's CRC, 6e8e217c (`draw`). Across the draw both sides must have taken the doorbells, `/proc/stat`'s `kvm_ioeventfd_signals` must have grown by at least the doorbells the VM rang, and `kvm_irqfd_raises` by at least those Rach2 rang, so that no doorbell went through QEMU (`doorbell`). Then QEMU is stopped, `vgshmprobe pending 4` submits four fenced commands, and QEMU is killed: every fence must fail, the buffer's `VIRTGPU_WAIT` must return, with 0 as on Linux once the fences have ended, a wait after the death must fail, and ivshmemd must report the detach for QEMU's closed connection (`death`). Each check prints `bed-shm: NAME: pass` or `fail` with its reason, and the last line is `bed-shm: result: pass` or `fail`; the VM's console, ivshmemd's output and the counts stay in the directory `-o` names, `/tmp/bed-shm` by default. The wait for the bind gives up when QEMU exits, or when for 600 s the VM's console stays silent and QEMU uses no processor time. The VM's disk takes no ioeventfd, and QEMU raises its interrupts without an irqfd, so that both counts are the doorbells'.

[`kvm-boot.sh`](kvm-boot.sh) boots a kernel and initramfs, or a raw disk under SeaBIOS, with `-accel kvm,kernel-irqchip=on`, or `-accel tcg`, and the console on a serial port it reads once a second. It passes on the token, `KVMGUEST OK` by default, and fails when QEMU ends without it or the console stays silent for 600 s, a bound on silence that `-w` changes, not on the boot's length. `-I split` keeps the PIC, IOAPIC and PIT in QEMU; `-I off` also keeps the local APIC there. Its header lists the options. [`corpus.sh`](corpus.sh) runs each test that a list runs, kvm-unit-tests' through their own `run_tests.sh` with no timeout, and judges each result against the list; its last line is `kvm-corpus: result: pass` or `fail` with the names of the tests that gave another result.

## Results

The processor profiles were qualified on the testing PC, Linux 6.18.58 (WSL's) with QEMU 10.2.1, by kvm-unit-tests' own SVM tests, `svm.flat` and `svm_npt.flat` at the KVM layer's revision, each test run alone by the outer QEMU, under TCG on `max-svm` and under KVM on `host-svm`. Their statuses are in [`ref/`](ref): 0 a pass, 77 a skip, 3 a failure, 127 a crash and 2 QEMU stopped without the test's exit. [`qualify.sh`](qualify.sh) runs the qualification again on any host, `sh tests/kvm/qualify.sh tcg max-svm`, the tests one at a time as [`svm-tests.list`](svm-tests.list) names them, and writes the same statuses.

| Tests | `max-svm` under TCG | `host-svm` under KVM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `svm.flat`, 63 tests | 39 pass, 13 skip, 7 fail, 4 crash | 41 pass, 10 skip, 9 fail, 3 crash |
| `svm_npt.flat`, 11 tests | 7 pass, 3 skip, 1 fails | 8 pass, 3 skip |

Under TCG, VMRUN and VMEXIT, the intercepts of I/O, MSRs, selective CR0 writes, interrupts and NMIs, event injection with its pending events, the VMCB's guest state checks, nested paging with its NX, present, user and write faults and its MMIO, and VGIF pass. Four results are QEMU's TCG, which nested KVM passes, and are recorded among the faults of other projects that [dev-log/upstream.md](../../dev-log/upstream.md) indexes: the CR3 read intercept and LMSW's selective CR0 intercept give no exit, an exception injected after a VMRUN that failed on an invalid injection ends in #UD in the host, and nested paging raises no #NPF for the reserved bits of its entries. TCG gives no NRIP save, TSC scaling or pause filter, whose tests skip. Rach2's SVM backend is therefore proven under TCG on the paths that pass there and under nested KVM on the rest. On the Mac, with Homebrew's QEMU 11.1.3 on the M4, every status is the testing PC's ([`ref/svm-max-svm-tcg-mac.status`](ref/svm-max-svm-tcg-mac.status)) but `reg_corruption`'s: it passes in 1 s in about four runs of five and in the fifth never ends, its timer interrupt left pending while the nested guest runs, a fifth fault of QEMU's TCG that the testing PC's QEMU shows as interrupts taken at about 18 a second, so that the test takes some 9 minutes there. Under nested KVM, the LBR virtualization tests fail as under TCG, and `svm_pae_test`, `svm_pse_test`, `svm_guest_state_test`, `svm_tsc_scale_test` and `pause_filter_test` fail where TCG passes or skips them; `virq_inject`, `svm_exception_test` and `svm_unsupported_instruction_intercept_test` crash under both. These are tests of a hypervisor's nested SVM, which Rach2's KVM does not give its guests, so neither corpus runs them.

The Debian guest reports the profile's features: under `host-svm` and KVM, `svm npt nrip_save tsc_scale vmcb_clean flushbyasid pausefilter pfthreshold`, with none of the three the profile declares absent; under `max-svm` and TCG, `svm npt vgif`. It boots to its token in 1 s under KVM and 3 s under TCG on the testing PC, and in 9 s under TCG in the GNU root's QEMU on an x86-64 Rach2 that runs under KVM there, as `boot-debian-tcg` boots it.

The SVM backend boots Debian 6.12.107 to `KVMGUEST OK` under TCG on WSL and the Mac, and under nested KVM on WSL. The entry probe, active memory-slot changes, device mapping, 4 MiB GEM mapping and vCPU termination checks pass under TCG on the Mac. The QEMU termination check passes under TCG on WSL and returns all five KVM counts and the open-file count. These were individual author runs, not a run of the suite or either corpus. On 2026-11-10 the full in-kernel controller booted Debian 6.12.107 to `KVMGUEST OK` in 11 s and the i686 KRFFS guest to `KVMRACH2 OK` in 23 s on the Mac, with an outer QEMU under TCG on `max-svm` and QEMU 10.0.13 in Rach2. Each inner guest had one processor and 256 MiB of memory. The corpus still waits for the reviewed checkpoint and the Lenovo trial's last run; these boots do not establish its results.

The VMX backend, on the Lenovo by trial boot on 2026-11-10, boots Debian 6.12.107 to `KVMGUEST OK` in 2 s and Rach2's i686 GRUB disk to `boot: starting /sbin/init` at 3.262 s of the guest's clock. Both corpora ran there: of kvm-unit-tests 43 pass, 7 skip and 1 fails as the list expects and 7 of 58 differ (`asyncpf`, `debug`, `emulator`, `hyperv_clock`, `kvmclock_test`, `smap`, `syscall`); of the selftests 15 are as the list expects and 51 of 66 differ, 31 of them because the selftests' library calls KVM_CREATE_IRQCHIP.

For checkpoint planning, allow about 15 minutes for the 22 serial QEMU cases. The existing Debian boots took 18 s under TCG on WSL and 9 s under TCG on the Mac, as the runner reported; with outer exit tracing, WSL took 20 s. The small Mac entry, slot and termination payload completed before the outer kernel's 0.519 s shutdown timestamp; that is guest time, not elapsed host time. The estimate allows roughly 30 s per case plus 4 minutes for setup and the nested-KVM check. The corpus has not run under Rach2's SVM, so its share and the nested-KVM elapsed time are allowances, not measurements; revise the estimate from the reviewed checkpoint's own output. The Lenovo trial is separate. These figures came from the existing runs; no timing run was added.

`bed-shm.sh` passed twice on the testing PC, run by hand at the console of the x86-64 GNU root under `host-svm`, so that Rach2 ran under WSL's KVM and the VM under Rach2's: three hypervisors deep, the VM bound virtio-gpu 466 s and 522 s after the attach, and the draw and the death took 21 s and 26 s. The first RESOURCE_CREATE_3D after the bind was answered in 6.6 s, as a kernel that logged the answer's time measured, and the readback's `VIRTGPU_WAIT` said busy once, after its 15 s. Across the draw of the second run Rach2 rang 9 doorbells and KVM raised 9 irqfds, and the VM's one doorbell came as 1 ioeventfd signal; with the VM's disk on ioeventfds, in the first run, the signals were 127. These were author runs, not a run of the suite.

## Design

The lists, [`unit-tests.list`](unit-tests.list) and [`selftests.list`](selftests.list), name every test of the two corpora, one a line: its name, what Rach2's KVM must give (`pass`; `skip`, the test's own skip for what the processor or QEMU lacks; `fail`, where Rach2 is to fail as Linux does; or `excluded`, not run), Linux's result on the testing PC, and the reason. A test whose result is the processor's names it for each, as `FLAG?A:B`: A where the `flags` of Rach2's `/proc/cpuinfo` have FLAG and B where they do not, which `corpus.sh` settles before it runs the list. Three rows do: kvm-unit-tests' `smap` passes with `smap` and skips without it, as on the Lenovo's i3-4030U; `tsc_scaling_sync` passes with `tsc_scale`, SVM's TSC ratio, and skips without it, as under TCG and on the Lenovo, where KVM_CAP_VM_TSC_CONTROL is 0 on Linux too; and `monitor_mwait_test` skips without `monitor`, as on the testing PC under Hyper-V, and with it is expected to fail, since the 6.12.115 program turns a quirk back on with KVM_CAP_DISABLE_QUIRKS2, which Linux 6.12.115 no longer does: by the two sources its third case then takes a #UD it does not expect, a result read from them and not yet seen on Linux. Linux's results were taken by the same tests under WSL's Linux 6.18.58 with its KVM on the Ryzen 9 7950X3D, kvm-unit-tests with `kernel-irqchip=split`, and are kept in [`ref/`](ref). A test Linux passes must pass; a test Linux skips for a lack of the processor or of the testing PC's kernel, such as SEV, AMX, PKS, CET, TSX, Xen's hypercalls or the forced emulation prefix, must skip; a test Linux fails there must still pass, since its failure is the outer layers' or a selftest older than the kernel, and the row names Linux's failure, except `hyperv_connections`, which fails one of its seven tests under Linux and is expected to fail as it does there. The excluded tests are nested virtualization (every SVM, VMX and EPT test, and the selftests `hyperv_svm_test` and `triple_fault_event_test`, which needs SVM or VMX in its guest), which the owner excluded on 2026-11-10; the PMU, which Rach2's KVM does not yet give; and kvm-unit-tests' three tests built only for i386.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `unit-tests.list` | 49 | 7 | 1 | 29 | 1 |
| `selftests.list` | 49 | 14 | 0 | 20 | 2 |

The Debian guest, which [`guests/mkdebian.sh`](guests/mkdebian.sh) makes, is trixie's cloud kernel, 6.12.107, and an initramfs of Debian's static BusyBox 1.37.0 whose [`/init`](guests/debian-init) prints the kernel's version, the processor count, the first processor's flags and the clock source its kernel chose, `kvm-clock` under KVM, then `KVMGUEST OK`, and powers off. Both packages are pinned in [`guests/debian.txt`](guests/debian.txt) by the SHA-256 that the Debian layer's signed snapshot index gives. The guest boots in seconds even under TCG inside Rach2 and needs no disk, so a failure to reach its token is the hypervisor's. Make writes it to `build/kvm/guests/debian`, and puts it with the runners and the lists into `build/tests/cases/kvm/kvm.ext2`, which every case mounts read-only and copies to `/tmp/kvm`.

The i686 Rach2 guest is the ordinary kernel with a 1 MiB KRFFS root. Its shipped `init-shell` starts the first user program, `guests/rach2-init.c`, as `/bin/sh`; that program prints `KVMRACH2 OK` and powers off. It needs no disk, network or input from the controller.

## Adding tests

A check of the shared region goes in `bed-shm.sh` as a line `bed-shm: NAME: pass` and an assertion NAME in `machine.bed-shm.json`, which [`bed_shm_scenario.c`](../cases/machine/bed_shm_scenario.c) reads by its list. A guest of its own goes under `guests/` with its recipe, the token its console prints and a case in `machine.kvm.json` whose last part of the identity [`kvm_scenario.c`](../cases/machine/kvm_scenario.c) reads; its files join `KVM_FILES` or the data disk's rule in `tests/system/suites.mk`. A new revision of a corpus changes its list: each new test gets its row from Linux's result on the testing PC.

## Limits

* The full controller boots the Debian x86-64 and i686 KRFFS guests under SVM on `max-svm`. The full controller's nested-KVM and VMX boots and the corpus still await qualification. The earlier split-controller boots and the corpus's rows that differed on the Lenovo are named in [Results](#results).
* A row that depends on TSC scaling reads `tsc_scale`, which is AMD's flag: Rach2's `/proc/cpuinfo` has no `vmx flags` line, so on an Intel processor whose VMX scales the TSC `tsc_scaling_sync` would pass where its row expects a skip. The Lenovo's Haswell does not scale it.
* The `max-svm` profile under TCG lacks NRIP save, TSC scaling and the pause filter, and has the five faults of Results, the last of which can leave a guest of Rach2's KVM that spins with its IF clear waiting on an interrupt QEMU does not deliver; the `host-svm` profile runs only on the testing PC.

## Tests

The suite is its own test. `kvm-boot.sh` was tried on the testing PC with the Debian guest under KVM and TCG, with a token the guest never prints, with an option QEMU refuses and with a guest that never starts, and `corpus.sh` with short lists against the outer QEMU, where it reported a listed pass that failed and left no scratch.
