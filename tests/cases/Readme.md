# Etude's cases

This directory has the case manifests of the suites that etude runs as cases, one family to a file, `FAMILY.json`, the programs of their host cases under `host/`, and the scenarios of their machine cases under `machine/`. A manifest says what each case runs, what it asserts and how it ends, in the contract that [etude's Readme](../../tools/etude/Readme.md#cases) gives. The suite of a family is the last part of its identity, split at dots: `host.style.json`, the family `host.style`, is `tests/run style`, the source checks of the tree, whose program is [`host/style.sh`](host/style.sh). `host.codexapi.json` and `host.museapi.json` are `tests/run codexapi` and `tests/run museapi`, the fake API server proved against Codex and Muse Code, whose program is [`host/agentapi.sh`](host/agentapi.sh). `host.imagerace.json` is `tests/run imagerace`, how `krffs/mkimage.sh` publishes an image when two builds of it run at once or one is stopped, and the prerequisites that keep an image current, whose program is [`host/imagerace.sh`](host/imagerace.sh). `host.krffscheck.json` is `tests/run krffscheck`, the offline checker `fsck.krffs` on KRFFS images broken on purpose, whose program is [`host/krffscheck.sh`](host/krffscheck.sh). `guest.disk.json`, `guest.disk-ahci.json` and `guest.disk-q35.json` are `tests/run disk`, `disk-ahci` and `disk-q35`, whose program, [`blockprobe`](../blockprobe.c), writes files through the page cache of a KRFFS root that `rach2.fsdisk` mounts from the disk, and reads them back: on IDE, on an AHCI controller of its own, and on the AHCI controller of the q35 machine at 0:31.2. Each boots a private copy of the disk that [`etude-disk.manifest`](../../krffs/manifests/etude-disk.manifest) stages. `guest.pools.json` is `tests/run pools`, the kernel's pools of pipes, ptys, sockets and processes filled at once, and made and closed a thousand times each without a leak of the heap, whose program is the guest probe [`poolprobe`](../poolprobe.c). `guest.authority.json` is `tests/run authority`, which kernel may write the whole disk: two host cases read each kernel's authority off the built artifact with [`host/authority.sh`](host/authority.sh), and two guest cases boot each kernel with `tests/bootprobe` on a disk over AHCI. `guest.textbusy.json` is `tests/run textbusy`, on x86-64 alone, whose guest cases run `tests/musl/textbusymusl` and `tests/musl/bindholdmusl`. `guest.fpu.json` is `tests/run fpu`, a process's floating-point state by each path the kernel saves it with, whose programs are the probes `fpuprobe` and `xsaveprobe`, and `guest.noentropy.json` is `tests/run noentropy`, the refusals of a machine without trusted entropy, whose programs are `rngprobe` and the musl programs `saltnoent`, `passwdmusl` and `keygennoent`; their sources are in [`tests/`](../Readme.md). `guest.ping.json`, `guest.ping-e1000.json`, `guest.packet.json` and `guest.packet-e1000.json` are `tests/run ping`, `ping-e1000`, `packet` and `packet-e1000`, the kernel's ICMP echo and the packet socket that a DHCP client reads its replies through, on the RTL8139 and on the e1000, whose programs are the guest probes [`pingtest`](../pingtest.c) and [`packetprobe`](../packetprobe.c). `machine.crash.json`, `machine.reset.json` and `machine.shutdown.json` are `tests/run crash`, `reset` and `shutdown`: the dirty-mount flag of a KRFFS disk after a crash and after an orderly stop, each of the kernel's reset mechanisms with the ones before it left out, and userland stopping the machine. Their scenarios are [`machine/crash_scenario.c`](machine/crash_scenario.c), [`machine/reset_scenario.c`](machine/reset_scenario.c) and [`machine/shutdown_scenario.c`](machine/shutdown_scenario.c), over [`machine/lifecycle.c`](machine/lifecycle.c), which they share. `guest.subset.json` is `tests/run subset`, the kernel booted with each optional device probe left out by its option, with all five, and with none, whose program is the guest probe [`bootlogprobe`](../bootlogprobe.c). `machine.raw.json` and `machine.raw-e1000.json` are `tests/run raw` and `raw-e1000`, the raw ICMP socket under `ping` and `traceroute`, whose scenario is [`machine/raw_scenario.c`](machine/raw_scenario.c). The other suites are still the `run-` targets of [`system/suites.mk`](../system/suites.mk).

## Running

`tests/run NAME` runs a family's cases as it runs any suite, and prints a line for each case as it starts and the suite's result at the end. Before any suite runs, `casecheck families` checks every manifest here against its schema and its rules, and a manifest at fault refuses the run, whatever suites it names. A host case's program can also be run alone, from the root of a tree that `make` has built:

    sh tests/cases/host/style.sh conventions
    sh tests/cases/host/agentapi.sh codex exchange
    ARCH=x86_64 sh tests/cases/host/imagerace.sh prerequisites
    sh tests/cases/host/authority.sh recovery
    sh tests/cases/host/krffscheck.sh prefixes

The Muse cases also need Muse Code, which `make build/downloads/muse/muse` fetches.

A machine case's scenario runs alone with a scenario invocation, as [Machine scenarios](../../tools/etude/Readme.md#machine-scenarios) gives it, whose `case_id` names the case; a scenario of several cases reads it to choose its work:

    build/tests/cases/machine/raw_scenario invocation.json

## Interfaces

A host case's program is the node its `prepare` names, which the preparation builds under `build/`, or under the build of the architecture when the step is `per_architecture`; the rule in the root `Makefile` copies `host/NAME.sh` to `build/tests/cases/host/NAME`. The set driver runs it with the workload's arguments in a workspace of the item's own under the evidence directory, `build/tests/failed`, which the driver keeps after a failure and removes otherwise. It gives `ETUDE_TREE` the root of the tree, `ETUDE_CASE` the case's identity, `ARCH` the item's architecture when the case has one, `ETUDE_SEED` when the workload gives a seed, and `TMPDIR` the workspace when the case has the `scratch` fixture. A program that reads the tree changes to `ETUDE_TREE` first, as `host/style.sh` does. A guest case's program runs on Rach2 instead, under the machine its QEMU profile gives, as [etude's Readme](../../tools/etude/Readme.md#the-set-driver) says: a guest program of [`tests/`](../Readme.md) that the test image stages at its root, which the preparation names by its file in the architecture's build, such as `tests/poolprobe`, through a rule of the root `Makefile` that runs the `tests` sub-make. The machine boots the test image, whose debug shell starts `etude-guest`, so the program runs as an ordinary child of it: in a workspace of its own with no terminal, standard input from `/dev/null` and its output to pipes, not as the shell's transient init on the console. The program writes its report records on its standard output, as [Report records](../../tools/etude/Readme.md#report-records) gives them, with any lines for a person between them, and `casecheck attempt` judges the item by the manifest, the records and how the program ended. A case passes only when every assertion its branches select passed, the program ended as `termination` says and nothing in the report contradicts the manifest; a check whose tool is absent or at a version that does not judge reports a branch, never a printed skip.

A guest program reports through [`tests/probe_report.h`](../probe_report.h), and takes its case's identity from `ETUDE_CASE`. The authority cases have the role `boot`: each boots the test image with `rach2.bootprobe=auth-ordinary` or `auth-recovery` on the kernel's command line, which selects `bootprobe`'s mode, and a private copy of `images/auth-test.disk`, 4 GiB of zeros with an MBR signature that `mkfixture auth` makes; the kernel publishes `/dev/grubcore` only on a disk with that signature. `bindholdmusl` writes its report only once its rows have ended, since each row counts the free blocks of the boot root, on which `etude-guest` keeps the program's output. The textbusy cases have no `scratch` fixture, so that each program makes its directory under `/tmp`: their mount points would be longer under the workspace than the 63 bytes the kernel takes for one (`VFS_COVERED_PATH_MAX` in `rach2/sys/fs/vfs.h`).

The API cases declare `host_api` `linux`, since `agentapi.sh` runs itself again in a user, network and PID namespace of its own, which `unshare` and `ip` make; on a host that is not Linux, etude places such a case in its tooling machine ([Cases](../../tools/etude/Readme.md#cases)). The program is the namespace's first process, so the agent, the server and whatever they started end when it does, and it reads the namespace's `/proc` to report any that outlived the agent. The Muse cases also prepare `build/downloads/muse/muse`, which the root `Makefile` links to the Muse Code that [`fetch_muse.sh`](../system/fakeapi/fetch_muse.sh) downloads and checks against Meta's digest.

Each guest case of `fpu` and `noentropy` boots the test image under its own processor profile and boot line, so that the kernel chooses its path once: `max`, which declares XSAVE, AVX, RDRAND and RDSEED; `max` with `rach2.noxsave` or, on i686, `rach2.nofxsr`; and `qemu64`, which declares none of them. A probe reports the branch it took, which `casecheck attempt` judges against the profile where a group is decided by features, and a case whose boot line or profile turns XSAVE or FXSR off also requires, as an assertion of the executor, the kernel's line `fpu: fxsr: ..., sse: ..., xsave: ...` that says so on the console. The musl programs are not at the test image's root, so the set driver sends their bytes to `etude-guest`. `passwdmusl` exits with status 1 on its fail-closed side, which its case expects, and `keygennoent` runs the `dropbearmulti` that the test image stages at os-real's path.

The `imagerace` cases `concurrent`, `interrupted` and `restage` build their images in a private tree under `TMPDIR`, running `krffs/mkimage.sh` from the root of the tree as the root `Makefile` does. The first two put shims of `dd`, `sed`, `grep`, `mv` and `touch` first on the build's path. A shim whose arguments name the image under test is a step of the build: it counts the step, stops the build with SIGTERM at the step the case chose, or holds the touch of the temporary image until the other build has made its own. `prerequisites` asks the tree's own make about the shared images and writes a marker below the build tree's `tests/system`, which it removes; `probe` runs [`imagerace_probe.sh`](../system/imagerace_probe.sh), which copies the makefiles into a directory of its own under `build/tests/system`.

A machine case's program is a scenario, a host program that `make build/tests/cases/machine/NAME` links with `libetude-case`, the same for both architectures. The set driver publishes the artifacts of the case's `prepare` to a store under `build/tests`, and runs the scenario with one argument, a [scenario invocation](../../tools/etude/Readme.md#machine-scenarios) that gives their digests, the emulator on the path, the bounds of the steps and the scratch directory. The scenario boots the machines its manifest declares on private copies of their disks, reports each assertion, and keeps each machine's console in the scratch directory. `casecheck attempt` reads the consoles for the assertion `etude.kernel`: a record the kernel printed at the critical level fails the case, unless the case ends in a panic or the record has the text that its machine's `critical` gives, as `machine.shutdown.init-death` gives for the halt after init's death.

The scenario of `raw` is also linked with [`machine/console_report.c`](machine/console_report.c). A scenario that needs the host to act while its probe runs, or to read what the probe cannot see, such as a capture of the card's frames, boots a machine without `etude-guest`, types `exec /PROBE` at the kernel's debug shell as the old suites did, and gives `console_report` the console from that point: the probe's records on the console become the scenario's own, so its manifest lists the probe's assertions beside its own, all with the source `scenario`.

## Adding tests

A new case goes into its family's manifest with a new `case_id`, and a new assertion with a new `assertion_id`; changing what an identity means takes a new one. The program reports each assertion the manifest lists for the branches it chooses, and nothing else. A new family takes a row in `tests/registry` whose name is the last part of its identity, a sentence `` `tests/run NAME`: `` in `system/suites.mk` with no target under it, and the suite's line in `system/suites.md`, which `tests/run inventory` prints. `tests/run style` refuses a row that has both a target and a family, and a manifest that is at fault.

## Tests

`tests/run style` checks the manifests with `casecheck` and the programs with ShellCheck, and runs [`style_cases_test.sh`](../system/style_cases_test.sh), which requires a planted source fault and a planted manifest fault each to fail its case. [`verify_temp_test.sh`](../system/verify_temp_test.sh) requires `style.sh` to keep its logs in a private directory of each run. The catalogues `codexapi.json` and `museapi.json` of `tests/proofs/` prove the API cases with faults in the fake API server and in `agentapi.sh`, `imagerace-publish.json` the `concurrent` and `interrupted` cases with faults in `krffs/mkimage.sh`, `imagerace.json` the `probe` case with a fault in a copy of the root Makefile, `disk.json` the disk cases with faults in `blockprobe` and in the AHCI driver, `authority.json` the authority cases with faults in the kernel, `krffscheck.json` the krffscheck cases with faults in `fsck.krffs`, `subset.json` the subset cases with faults in the kernel's probes and in `bootlogprobe`, and `textbusy.json` and `bindhold.json` the textbusy cases with faults in the kernel, as [Fault proofs](../system/Readme.md#fault-proofs) lists them.

[`tests/proofs/lifecycle.json`](../proofs/lifecycle.json) proves the machine cases on i686 with three faults in the kernel's power code: the keyboard controller's reset never sent, a missing event; a halt that powers the machine off, an unexpected exit and a lost transport; and a reset after a halt, a wrong end.
