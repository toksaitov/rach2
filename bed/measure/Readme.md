# The test bed's measurements

`bed/measure/` is the harness that measures the same scenes on each of the test bed's rungs, native on Rach2, native on Linux, forward through QEMU's virtio-gpu and reversed through the test bed's VM by each protocol, and prints one line of numbers for each run (milestone 83, [its plan](../../dev-log/83-testbed/plan.md)). [`measure.sh`](measure.sh) runs one scene and reads the machine's counts around it; [`latency.c`](latency.c) times a round trip through the GL driver; [`webgl.html`](webgl.html) is the WebGL scene and [`piglit.txt`](piglit.txt) piglit's. The scenes run through the graphics runners of [tests/graphics](../../tests/graphics/Readme.md), whose `common.sh`, `piglit.sh`, `games.sh`, `server.py` and `events.py` it uses, so that the same programs, settings and environment serve both.

## Use

    sh bed/measure/measure.sh [-r RUNG] [-o OUT] [-t SECONDS] [-u USER] [-d] [-p] [-c]
                              [-L LATENCY] [-v RELAYLOG] [-R REPLAYS] glmark2|piglit|webgl|game

The runners are `tests/graphics/runners` of the tree, or this directory when `common.sh` was staged beside the harness, or the directory `MEASURE_RUNNERS` names. The scenes:

* `glmark2`: glmark2-wayland's `build`, `texture`, `shading` with Phong shading and `bump` with normal maps, on screen at 1280x800, or with `-d` fullscreen on the caller's output, as Linux's references on the Lenovo ran at 1366x768, each for SECONDS (10).
* `piglit`: the tests of `piglit.txt`, ten tests of piglit's quick profile that draw, upload or read back and pass on virgl in the testing PC's reference, one at a time on the device EGL's surfaceless platform finds (`piglit.sh -g -l`), once, or with `-t` again and again until SECONDS have passed, which makes it a load as long as the others.
* `webgl`: `webgl.html` in Chromium, fullscreen at 1280x800, for SECONDS times 60 frames (600): a grid of 32 by 32 lit cubes turning in one instanced draw of 36,864 vertices, one cube's 36 a thousand and twenty-four times, over a full-window quad whose fragment shader runs 24 rounds of a hash, the same work in every frame. Chromium starts with `--disable-gpu-vsync --disable-frame-rate-limit`, so that the frames come as fast as the path draws them, as glmark2's at swap interval 0, and not at the display's refresh.
* `game`: SuperTuxKart's replay as `games.sh -r REPLAYS` plays it, the reference route of the Lenovo's Linux runs, with SECONDS of the race's own time when `-t` gives them. REPLAYS is the games layer's `/usr/share/games/rach2/replays` by default, `vendor/gnu/games/replays` of the tree, which a machine without the games layer, as Debian, is given.

`glmark2` and `webgl` draw under a headless Weston of the harness's own with its GL renderer, or Pixman with `-p`, which `webgl` always has, since Chromium's GPU process loses every context under Weston's GL as an ordinary user ([Browser runners](../../tests/graphics/Readme.md#browser-runners)), or with `-d` under the caller's Wayland display, as `WAYLAND_DISPLAY` and `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR` name it; the game draws on the same display. `RUNG` names the path in the line: `native-rach2`, `native-linux`, `forward-virgl`, `forward-venus`, `vtest`, `shm-virgl`, `shm-venus` or `nctx` by the test bed's convention. With `-u USER` the harness reads the counts as its caller, root where `ivshmemd -i` and KVM's debugfs need it, and runs the scene as USER, which Chromium needs, from a copy of itself, its runners and REPLAYS in `OUT/harness`, since USER may not read the caller's directories, as `gfx` cannot read root's home on Debian; OUT must be a path USER can reach. With `-c` the harness prints `measure: counts: before` and `measure: counts: after` as it reads the counts, for a caller that reads its own counts around the scene, as `bed/forward/forward.sh` does on the host of its guest. `-v RELAYLOG` names the standard error of `vtestrelay -v` for the `vtest` rung. `-L` names a built `latency`; without it the harness compiles `latency.c` with `cc`, and leaves the latency out where there is none.

## Results

The last line, kept in `OUT/line`:

    measure: result: SCENE RUNG frames N frame_ms MEAN p50 P50 p99 P99 cpu_ms_per_frame C latency_ms L copied_bytes B exits E round_trips T seconds S passed P

* `frames`, `frame_ms`, `p50`, `p99`: the frames and their mean, median and 99th percentile in milliseconds. A frame's time is the Gallium HUD's for glmark2 and the game, the time between the page's `requestAnimationFrame` callbacks for webgl, and a passing test's time from piglit's results for piglit, whose test counts as one frame.
* `cpu_ms_per_frame`: the busy time of every processor of the machine, from `/proc/stat`'s first line, over the frames, so that the compositor, the browser's processes and a guest that is QEMU's process on this machine all count. The busy time spans the whole scene, so the game's is over every frame the HUD wrote, its loading screen's among them, and not over its window's alone. A forward rung's harness runs inside the guest and counts the guest's processors; `forward.sh` puts Rach2's own counts in its place ([bed/forward](../forward/Readme.md)).
* `latency_ms`: `latency`'s median of 200 round trips, before the scene: a clear of a 64 by 64 framebuffer and the read of one pixel, which returns only once the whole path has run the clear.
* `copied_bytes`: what the rung copied between a resource and its backing. On Rach2, `/proc/stat`'s `virtio_gpu_copied_bytes`, which counts each answered transfer, 2D or 3D, by ioctl or encoded in a virgl submission, at its format's size ([proc(5)](../../man/man5/proc.5)); for the `vtest` rung, the relay's bytes both ways. A native rung copies nothing this way and reads 0 where the counter exists.
* `exits`: KVM's exits on the machine, `/proc/stat`'s `kvm_exits` on Rach2 or the sum in `/sys/kernel/debug/kvm/exits` on Linux.
* `round_trips`: the doorbells Rach2's driver rang on the VM's queue vector, from `ivshmemd -i`, while a region is attached, each a batch of commands the VM answered; for the `vtest` rung, the commands the relay carried.
* `seconds`: the scene's time on the wall.
* `passed`: for piglit, the tests that passed of the tests run, `P/T`; the frames are the passing tests', so that a test that skips or crashes on a rung adds no frame. `-` for the other scenes.

A count the machine does not keep is `-`. `OUT/frametimes` has every frame's milliseconds, `OUT/counts` the readings before and after, `OUT/scene/record` the runners' record of the machine, its Mesa, its IOMMU and its load, and the scene's own output beside them. Each scene compiles its shaders into a new empty Mesa shader cache, `OUT/shader-cache`, and the forward rungs' QEMU, where the host's compiles happen, into one of its own under the forward runner's OUT, so that no rung starts warm from an earlier run. A run first removes what an earlier run wrote in OUT under the names the harness writes, and nothing else. The scene runs in a process group of its own, which the harness ends, TERM and then KILL after 15 s, longer than the scene's half takes to end its browser, 5 s, and send its server and Weston TERM, on every exit and on SIGTERM, SIGINT or SIGHUP, so that TERM to the outer half's PID stops the scene's programs with it. The stop does not wait for the scene's half, which a tracer would keep unreaped, and leaves it to the shell's reaper. With `-u` under util-linux's su, Debian's, whose `-c` starts the shell in a session of its own and which on TERM kills that shell 2 s later, the harness finds su's child in `/proc` and sends TERM to the child's group alone, and su's group gets only the KILL. su's command line runs `OUT/harness/start`, which execs the scene's half. A `pkill` pattern must escape the dot, as in `pkill -TERM -f 'measure\.sh'`: an unescaped dot also matches `measure/shm` in su's arguments. Use the outer half's PID when OUT itself contains `measure.sh`, and restrict any `pkill` pattern to the run's full script path so it cannot stop another run.

With `-u` as root, OUT stays root's: the harness takes it back at its start, root's and mode 700, and while USER may not write it removes what an earlier run wrote under the harness's names, USER's among them, with `rm -r`, which unlinks a link and never follows it; what else USER left in OUT it neither removes nor changes. It creates its own files, `counts`, `line`, `scene.log`, the latency's and the directory `taken`, and gives USER only `harness`, which it made, before USER may write OUT. USER then writes the scene's files through its group, and the sticky bit keeps it from removing or renaming root's, so that root never writes, runs, reads or signals through a name USER chose. `frametimes`, `cpuframes` and `passes` are checked for plain files and read once, by USER under su, into root's copies in `OUT/taken`, which USER may not enter, and the line is made from those copies: root opening a name USER could swap for a link, to `/dev/watchdog` say, would open its target with root's rights. Each reader runs in the background, waited for by the shell's `wait`, which a trapped signal interrupts, and is ended by the exit trap with TERM and CONT and then, once it has gone or 5 s have passed, KILL to it and to su's child's group. The trap does not wait for the reader, which a tracer's zombie or a leader with live threads would keep unreaped, and leaves it to the shell's reaper, so that a reader USER stopped with SIGSTOP or traces cannot hold off the stop. A FIFO substituted after the check may block the reader, but cannot hold off TERM to the harness. Paths go to the reader and scene launcher as arguments, so a quote in OUT is literal. The status is 0 when the scene ran to its end and gave frames, 1 otherwise; the harness sets no time limit of its own.

## Design

The line is a run's, never a verdict: the plan's tables set each rung's figures beside the others' and beside Linux's with its IOMMU configuration named. Each count is read before and after the scene, and the line gives the difference. The latency runs before the scene, since its round trips would load the scene's path. The scenes come from the graphics runners, so that a figure here and a figure of the graphics suites on the same machine measure the same thing.
