# The native inventory of the guest programs

This part of [the native inventory](native.md) covers the programs that run on Rach2: the probes that `PROGS` in `tests/Makefile` builds, the programs in `tests/musl/`, and the checks the probe runner `tests/system/runner/runtests.c` makes beyond a program's exit status. The units, their order and their suites are those of [the inventory of the guest programs](inventory-guest.md); the native machine, the start every native case has, the fixtures, the fields and the plan forms are those of [the native inventory](native.md). A file and line is in the source the entry describes unless it names another.

## The probes

* `ahciprobe` (queue): Role: ordinary, the transient init of one boot per mode, `read`, `fault` and `write`. Privileges: root, for raw opens and mount and umount2. Paths: `/dev/disk0p1`, `/dev/disk1p1` and `/dev/disk0p2` read raw; `/blockprobe` executed (`fault`); `/late` mounted and removed after unmount (`write`). Devices: unthrottled AHCI disks on ports 0 and 1, with private FAT32 or ext2 partitions. Global: the `ahci` line of `/proc/interrupts`. Children: two readers or `blockprobe`; failure kills and reaps readers still running. Recovery: the suite creates disk images of its own, and nothing outlives the boot. Native: machine: layout: the two-disk read and ext2 write need disposable disks; boot: `fault` needs a KRFFS root through its page-cache window on AHCI port 0 (`rach2.root=disk`) and a raw second partition. T37 moves deadline, quarantine and independent-completion assertions to controlled host models, which need no native disk.
* `auxvprobe` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): Role: ordinary. Privileges: any. Paths: none. Devices: none. Global: none. Children: none. Recovery: nothing to remove. Native: native: workspace; the case's environment of `PATH` and `HOME` meets its check of at least one envp entry (auxvprobe.c:245), and its check that `AT_RANDOM` lies in the stack pointer's page (auxvprobe.c:298, absent from the T01 entry) holds while the arguments and environment stay that small.
* `bigfileprobe` (large): Role: ordinary, the transient init of one boot per run. Privileges: root, for mount and umount2. Paths: makes `/big` and mounts NODE there (bigfileprobe.c:565); makes `/big/big.bin`, `/big/sparse.bin` and `/big/limit.bin`, and removes the last two. Devices: NODE, an ext2, exFAT or FAT32 partition of a 6 GiB AHCI disk of the suite's making. Global: the `disk-commands` line of `/proc/disk` around the truncate (bigfileprobe.c:401 to 415), a mount; the suite reads the kernel's refusals by name and runs the host's `e2fsck` and `fsck.exfat` on the image after the boots. Children: none. Recovery: a failed check exits with the volume mounted on `/big`; the disk image is the suite's, removed at its end. Native: native after T44: the mount point taken as an argument, `write` and its `verify` run on the same volume with an unmount between them, and the truncate's bound counted in the `writes` of the volume's own loop device in `/proc/loop` in place of the whole machine's `disk-commands`, which the services move; `volume ext2`, `volume exfat` or `volume vfat` given to the probe as its loop node, mountns, `log` for the refusals by name, and the image returned to the controller for the host's checkers.
* `blockprobe` (disk, disk-ahci, disk-q35, bus, queue): Role: ordinary, the transient init of a boot whose root is KRFFS on the boot disk through the page cache's window, or the child of `ahciprobe fault`. Privileges: any that can write `/`. Paths: makes `/bp00` to `/bp23`, 256 KiB each, and removes each after it compares equal (blockprobe.c:59, 156). Devices: none it opens; the root's disk is IDE, AHCI or the q35 controller's AHCI function 0:31.2, as the suite attaches it. Global: the page cache that the root's window uses, 8 MiB on these machines. Children: none. Recovery: a failure leaves the files on the root; each suite rebuilds its disk image before the boot. Native: machine: boot: the window it tests exists only for a root mounted by `rach2.fsdisk` or `rach2.root=disk`, and a KRFFS `volume` mounted by mount(2) goes through the buffer cache of `krffsmount.c` instead; card: `disk` and `bus` run it over the IDE controller.
* `bootprobe` (bootpart, fdlock, authority): Role: ordinary, the transient init of seven boots told apart by `rach2.bootprobe=` on the kernel's command line (bootprobe.c:682 to 896). Privileges: root, and `setuid(1000)` for the unprivileged opens of `ramroot` and `fixture` (bootprobe.c:784, 984). Paths: `/proc/cmdline`, `/proc`, `/proc/self/fd/N`, `/proc/PID/stat`. Devices: `/dev/bootpart`, `/dev/grubcore`, `/dev/disk0` and `/dev/disk0p1`, read and written raw: sector 7 of the fixture's partition, 200 sectors of the loader's gap, sectors at 3 GiB of `/dev/disk0`; a 260 GB AHCI drive with markers at 5 and 258 GiB for `wide`. Global: the command line; the `raw-driver-calls`, `raw-batch-sectors`, `disk-commands` and `disk-flushes` lines of `/proc/disk`, required to move by exact counts (bootprobe.c:308, 315, 338); the one-writer claim on the boot disk. Children: one forked reader or writer per race of `shared`, reaped on every path (bootprobe.c:524, 615). Recovery: writes land on the suite's disk images, rebuilt per run; nothing outlives the boot. Native: split in T44: the checks of `/proc/cmdline` that hold on any boot (its trailing newline, its `/proc` entry, mode 0100444 and inode 0x101 by `stat` and `fstat`, the descriptor's link) native, with the mode taken as an argument in place of the command line; the modes machine, boot: they are chosen by `rach2.bootprobe=`, require `rach2.fsdisk` or its absence, and `shared` needs `rach2.blocklag=10`; disk: `fixture`, `ramroot`, `auth-*` and `shared` write `/dev/bootpart`, `/dev/grubcore` or `/dev/disk0`, or check a refusal that would write it; layout: they need the fixture's partition of known bytes, a 260 GB drive with markers or a sector at 3 GiB known to be zero, and count a quiet disk's commands exactly.
* `busprobe` (bus): Role: ordinary, the transient init of two boots, `four` and `big`. Privileges: root, for mount and umount2. Paths: makes `/mnt0` to `/mnt3` and mounts there, and removes the directories after the unmounts. Devices: `/dev/disk0p1` to `/dev/disk3p1`, FAT32 partitions of four IDE drives of 16 to 28 MiB, or of a sparse 130 GiB IDE drive with its partition past sector 2^28. Global: the `disk N ata N SECTORS` and `part` lines of `/proc/disk`, mounts; the suite reads the `ata:` records and the written files from the images. Children: none. Recovery: a failed check exits with a partition mounted; the images are the suite's. Native: machine: card: every assertion is of the IDE driver: the four positions of its two channels and its 48-bit commands past 2^28 sectors, and the Lenovo's one disk is on AHCI.
* `calibprobe` (helper of `tests/system/calibrate.sh`, which no suite runs): Role: ordinary, the transient init of WIDTH boots at once under `rach2.diskdiag`. Privileges: root, to read `/dev/disk0`. Paths: none. Devices: `/dev/disk0`, 32 MiB read raw from a 64 MiB IDE disk of zeros. Global: none it reads; the host times it. Children: none. Recovery: nothing in the guest; the script's images are in its directory. Native: removed in T89.
* `conloadprobe` (conload): Role: ordinary, the transient init at the debug shell, whose standard input is the serial console. Privileges: root, for the mount. Paths: makes `/lo` and mounts `/dev/disk0p1` there as vfat, writes `/lo/conload.bin` again and again; executes `/conloadprobe D` (conloadprobe.c:584). Devices: the console's serial port, and a 16 MiB IDE disk with a FAT32 partition. Global: the `irq-bytes` line of `/proc/console`, required to rise in stage A (conloadprobe.c:521); the console's foreground group, set by `TIOCSPGRP` to a child and back. Children: five spinners and a writer, killed and reaped by `stop_load` on every failure path; the foreground child of stage C is killed and reaped when `TIOCSPGRP` or its wait fails (`end_child`). Recovery: the volume stays mounted after a failure; the disk image is the suite's. Native: machine: input: every stage reads lines the harness types at the console, an interrupt character among them; QEMU: `irq-bytes` counts the bytes the serial port's interrupt moved, and the Lenovo has no serial port.
* `cowprobe` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): Role: ordinary. Privileges: any. Paths: none. Devices: none. Global: the frame allocator, which its check of 64 forks without exhaustion reads only through a failed fork. Children: 65 forked children, each reaped before the next; a failed check exits with none outstanding. Recovery: nothing to remove. Native: native: workspace; the leak check cannot fail for a leak of a few frames a cycle on 128 MiB of QEMU's default memory, nor on 16 GiB (cowprobe.c:119 to 128).
* `cowregrant` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): Role: ordinary. Privileges: any. Paths: none. Devices: none. Global: the frame allocator, read only through a failed fork. Children: 200 forked children, each reaped before the next. Recovery: nothing to remove. Native: native: workspace; as with `cowprobe`, 200 cycles of a frame leaked per cycle do not exhaust either machine's frames, so the leak half of its claim cannot fail (cowregrant.c:99 to 102).
* `credprobe` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): Role: ordinary. Privileges: root at the start (credprobe.c:182); a child drops to the fixed ids 1000, 1001 and 1002 and gids 1000, 2000 and 3000 (credprobe.c:72 to 158), and the parent stays root. Paths: none. Devices: none. Global: none. Children: one child and its grandchild, each reaped by its parent; a failed step in the child exits it, and the parent reaps it and fails. Recovery: nothing to remove. Native: native after T44: the uids and gids taken as arguments in place of the fixed ones, since 1000 is the machine's `test`; workspace, ids.
* `devprobe` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2; ext2 and selfbuild-ext2 through `probe_roots.sh`; by hand through `manual_probes.sh probes`): Role: ordinary. Privileges: any that may open `/fb` for writing; root in every run. Paths: `/dev`, `/dev/null/x`, `/dev/nosuch/x`, `/proc/self/stat`, `/proc/1999999999/stat/x`, `/proc/self/fd/0`, `/sys/bus/pci/devices/0000:00:00.0/uevent/x` and `0000:00:1e.7/uevent/x`, `/dev/pts/N/x`. Devices: the synthetic devices, `/dev/ptmx` and a pair of its own, and `/fb`, which it switches to text mode and to mode 13h and back (devprobe.c:251 to 311). Global: the framebuffer's owner and mode, the console; the PCI functions present: 00:00.0 must exist and 00:1e.7 must not. Children: one forked child for the framebuffer's rows, reaped on every path past the fork (devprobe.c:306). Recovery: a kill between its mode switches leaves the display in graphics mode until a new owner sets text mode; nothing on disk. Native: native after T44: the console row reads the link of a descriptor it opens on `/dev/console` in place of descriptor 0, which a native case does not have on the console, and the absent PCI function is one the case finds absent on the machine and declares (T41); workspace, console, alone.
* `dgramprobe` (dgram, dgram-e1000): Role: ordinary. Privileges: root; a child drops to the fixed uid 1000 to be refused port 68 (dgramprobe.c:586). Paths: none. Devices: none; the interface is the card the suite attaches, rtl8139 or e1000. Global: the machine's address, fixed at QEMU's 10.0.2.15 with its subnet broadcast 10.0.2.255 (dgramprobe.c:60 to 62); UDP ports 7500 to 7503, 7510 and 7600 to 7607, fixed; the interface's name `eth0` for `SO_BINDTODEVICE`; one broadcast datagram to port 7500 put on the wire, which the suite finds in QEMU's capture; a datagram from outside, through QEMU's forward to port 7502, after `DGRAMPROBE OUTSIDE`. Children: one forked child for the low port, reaped on every path past the fork. Recovery: sockets close at exit; nothing to remove. Native: split in T44: the probe's rows native after the address, the subnet broadcast, the interface's name, the ports, the low port and the uid are taken as arguments, with workspace, ports with one port below 1024 that no service binds, ids, and peer for the outsider's datagram; the suite's check of the broadcast in the capture machine, network, since it reads a capture of the wire. The outsider row's 600 ms window passes without a datagram, as T01 says, until T33 replaces it.
* `dirgrow` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): Role: ordinary. Privileges: any that can write `/`. Paths: makes `/dg`, `/dg/f` and `/dg/g` and removes them before exit; needs `/f` absent. Devices: none. Global: none; its subject is the KRFFS root of the test image, whose relocation of a grown file (`krffs_resize_at`) the format code shares with a mounted KRFFS volume. Children: none. Recovery: the probe removes its two files and directory before exit. Native: native after T44: the three names made relative to the working directory, the check of `/f` made a check of `f` at the volume's root, and the files removed at the end; volume krffs as the working directory, mountns.
* `dirprobe` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2; ext2 and selfbuild-ext2 through `probe_roots.sh`; by hand through `manual_probes.sh probes`): Role: ordinary. Privileges: any that can write `/`. Paths: makes and removes `/d`, `/d/f`, `/e`, `/e/a`, `/e/b`, `/ids`, `/ids/bC`, `/g`, `/h`, `/m`, `/work` and `/work/rel.txt`; needs `/nope` absent; reads the type of `/` from `/proc/mounts` (dirprobe.c:106), requires `getcwd` to give `/` after `chdir ..` from `/work`. Devices: none. Global: none. Children: none. Recovery: a failed row can leave any of its names at the root, since the later rows go on; the root is the boot's image. Native: native after T44: the type of `/` read by `statfs` of `/` in place of `/proc/mounts`, whose line for `/` inside a chroot is the machine's ext2 root; root krffs and root ext2-dir, as two cases, the private root giving it its fixed names, `/nope` absent and `getcwd` of `/`.
* `dirtest` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): Role: ordinary. Privileges: any. Paths: `chdir` to `/`, `.` and `..`, and `getcwd` of `/`; needs `/nonexistent` absent and `/hello.txt` a regular file, which the test image stages (krffs/manifests/test.manifest:125). Devices: none. Global: none. Children: none. Recovery: nothing to remove. Native: native: root ext2-dir with `/hello.txt` a file; without it the row on a plain file passes vacuously, since it takes any failure of `chdir`, ENOENT included, where the claim is ENOTDIR (dirtest.c:150).
* `dirtyprobe` (crash): Role: ordinary, the transient init of a boot whose root is a disk-backed KRFFS through the page cache's window, mounted dirty. Privileges: root, for `chown` of `/` to group 1 and back (dirtyprobe.c:168 to 171). Paths: `/`, by `chown`, `chmod`, `open`, `creat` and `write`, each followed by `sync`. Devices: none it opens; the root's IDE disk. Global: the root node's mode, whose set-user-ID bit is the dirty-mount flag; the suite kills QEMU after `DIRTYPROBE OK` and runs `fsck.krffs` on the image. Children: none. Recovery: the root's group is put back by the probe's fourth call, and a failure before it leaves group 1 on the image's root, which is the suite's scratch copy. Native: split in T44: the rule on a mounted KRFFS volume, which sets the same flag at its mount (krffsmount.c:1211), native after the suite's check becomes `fsck.krffs` on a copy of the volume's image taken after the probe's syncs and before the unmount clears the flag; root krffs, mountns; the boot root through the window, killed without a halt, machine, boot, since it needs `rach2.fsdisk` and an end without the clean unmount.
* `diskfull` (diskfull): Role: ordinary, the transient init of a boot of its own, since it fills the test image's root. Privileges: any that can write `/`. Paths: makes `/diskfull.tmp` and fills `/` with it in 4 KiB writes, at most 8192 of them (diskfull.c:31 to 35; T01's "32 KiB blocks" is wrong), and removes it on every path but a failed unlink. Devices: none. Global: the free space of the root's file system. Children: none. Recovery: a failed unlink leaves the root full; the RAM image goes with the boot. Native: native: root krffs on a volume with less than 32 MiB free, so that the writes reach the refusal within their 8192 tries and fill the private root alone.
* `disksprobe` (disks): Role: ordinary, the transient init of a boot under `rach2.diskdiag`. Privileges: root, for mount and umount2. Paths: makes `/mnt0` and `/mnt1`, mounts there, writes `/mnt0/zero.txt` and `/mnt1/one.txt`, and removes the directories after unmounting. Devices: `/dev/disk0`, `/dev/disk0p1`, `/dev/disk1` and `/dev/disk1p1` by stat, and `/dev/disk1p2` and `/dev/disk2` required absent; a 64 MiB KRFFS disk on AHCI port 0 and a 20 MiB disk of 40960 sectors with one FAT32 partition on port 1. Global: the `disk N ahci PORT SECTORS` lines of `/proc/disk`, mounts; the suite reads `one.txt` from the image with mtools. Children: none. Recovery: a failed check exits with a partition mounted; the images are the suite's. Native: machine: layout: every check is of two disks on one controller in an arrangement of the suite's, a second disk of 40960 sectors on port 1 and no third, where the Lenovo has one disk on port 0.
* `dnstest` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2; ext2 and selfbuild-ext2 through `probe_roots.sh`; by hand through `manual_probes.sh probes`): Role: ordinary. Privileges: any. Paths: reads `/etc/hosts`, or `/hosts` when it is absent (utils/resolv.c:30), and requires `gateway`, `dns` and `rach2` there at QEMU's 10.0.2.2, 10.0.2.3 and 10.0.2.15 (dnstest.c:131 to 133); `manual_probes.sh` rewrites the machine's `/etc/hosts` for it and puts it back. Devices: none. Global: none without `--live`, which no suite passes. Children: none. Recovery: nothing to remove. Native: native: root ext2-dir with the test image's hosts file (krffs/manifests/test.manifest:127) as its `/etc/hosts`, so that the machine's own is neither read nor written.
* `dup2test` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): Role: ordinary. Privileges: any. Paths: makes and removes `dup2test.tmp` in the working directory. Devices: none. Global: none. Children: none. Recovery: a kill leaves `dup2test.tmp`. Native: native: workspace; descriptor 1 is put back from descriptor 2, so after the redirection its output goes to the case's error pipe, and the 1024-descriptor limit of the native case is the table's own, which the `dup2(1, 2000)` row needs.
* `errnoprobe` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): Role: ordinary. Privileges: any. Paths: needs `/no_such_file_errnoprobe` absent (errnoprobe.c:123). Devices: none. Global: none. Children: none. Recovery: nothing to remove. Native: native: workspace; the machine's root has no such name, and descriptor 999 is under the case's limit of 1024 and not open.
* `exitprobe` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2; `exit_mutate.sh` runs single rows): Role: ordinary. Privileges: any that can write `/`. Paths: makes `/exit-cwd` and `/exit-bad` and removes both before the supervisor exits; the victim makes and unlinks `/exit-file`; reads `/proc/PID/stat` of the reaper and of the reaped victim. Devices: none. Global: none. Children: per row a reaper and its victim; the reaper reaps the victim, and the probe reaps the reaper; on a row that fails by the bound or by its report, it kills the reaper and reaps it within the row's bound (`end_reaper`), and a victim still polling `/proc` exits once its parent is gone. Recovery: the supervisor removes `/exit-cwd` and `/exit-bad` before exit. Native: native after T44: the three paths made relative to the working directory and removed at the end, and a failed row's reaper killed and reaped before the probe exits, since natively its group outlives a case's end only until `etude-guest` ends the case's processes; workspace.
* `ext2probe` (ext2; `ext2_batch_mutate.sh` runs `cost-append`): Role: ordinary, the transient init of one boot per mode. Privileges: root, for mount and umount2. Paths: makes `/e2` and mounts `/dev/disk0p1` there, read-only for `links` and `MS_SYNCHRONOUS` for `cost`; `links` requires `symlink` of `/newlink` on the KRFFS root `EPERM` and `link` to `/another` across mounts `EXDEV` (ext2probe.c:280 to 285), and opens `/proc/self` and `/proc/self/fd/1`; `cost` makes `/e2/cost-rewrite` and `/e2/cost-append` on the fixture. Devices: `/dev/disk0p1`, the IDE fixture disk of `mkext2fixture.sh`. Global: the `disk-commands` and `disk-flushes` lines of `/proc/disk`, required to move by exactly four and two for the rewrites and by none before `fsync` (ext2probe.c:523 to 530); a mount. Children: none. Recovery: a failed check exits with the fixture mounted; the fixture disk is the suite's. Native: native after T44: the node taken as an argument, and the counts of `cost` and `cost-append` read from the `writes` and `syncs` of the volume's own loop device in `/proc/loop` (rach2/sys/block/loop.c:362), which no other process moves, in place of the whole machine's `/proc/disk`; root krffs for the root's `EPERM`, a `volume ext2` of the fixture attached and not mounted, mountns.
* `fatwriteprobe` (fatwrite with `vfat`; exfat with `exfat`): Role: ordinary, the transient init of one boot per mode, `write`, `readback`, `crash` and `repair`, in that order on one disk image. Privileges: root, for mount and umount2. Paths: makes `/fw` and mounts `/dev/disk0p2` there (fatwriteprobe.c:120 to 121); writes `pattern.bin`, `Long Name File.txt`, `Sub Dir/inner.txt`, `renamed.txt`, `synced.txt` and the removed `tmp.txt` and `gone.txt` on the volume, and `f000` to `f199` in `crash`. Devices: `/dev/disk0p2`, a FAT32 or exFAT partition of the suite's IDE disk. Global: a mount; the suite reads the volume with mtools, `fsck.fat` and its fixture's walker, and the kernel's `vfat: check:` records. Children: none. Recovery: `crash` never ends by itself; the suite kills the machine mid-sequence, which leaves the volume marked dirty for `repair`. Native: split in T44: `write` and `readback` native after the node and the mount point are taken as arguments and the read back follows an unmount and a new mount in place of a reboot, with `volume vfat` or `volume exfat` attached and not mounted, mountns, `log` for the records, and the image returned to the controller for mtools and the checkers; `crash` and `repair` machine, boot, since the volume must be left dirty by the end of the kernel under its writes, which a native case cannot make.
* `fdfull` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): Role: ordinary. Privileges: any. Paths: opens `/hello`, the `hello` probe the test image stages at its root, up to 1040 times (fdfull.c:102). Devices: none. Global: none; the table it fills is its own, and the limit it requires is 1024 with three descriptors open at the start (fdfull.c:22 to 23, 118). Children: none. Recovery: nothing to remove. Native: native: root ext2-dir with `/hello` staged, since the machine's root has none; the case's three descriptors and its limit of 1024 are the ones the count assumes. The T01 entry's "every descriptor must close" is not checked: `close_all` ignores each close's result, and only the close of `fds[0]` is judged (fdfull.c:82 to 90, 126).
* `fdstale` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2; fdlock on a disk-backed KRFFS root): Role: ordinary. Privileges: any that can write `/`. Paths: makes and removes about twenty names `/fdstale.*` at the root (fdstale.c:147 to 166); reads the type of `/` from `/proc/mounts` and takes KRFFS when it cannot (fdstale.c:566). Devices: none. Global: the free space of the root's file system, which the three leak loops of 256 rounds of 64 KB judge only when the root holds less than 16 MB (fdstale.c:186, 835 to 910). Children: four children over the pipes `up` and `down`, each reaped on the passing path; each child closes the write end of `down` and the read end of `up` after its fork (`spawn`), so a failure in the parent between a fork and its reap exits and ends the child's read of `down` with end of file, and the child fails and exits. Recovery: a failed case leaves its `/fdstale.*` names and a blocked child; the boot removes both. Native: native after T44: the type of `/` read by `statfs` of `/` in place of `/proc/mounts`; root krffs on a volume of at most 10 MB, so that a leak of 16 MB exhausts it as it exhausts the test image.
* `filetest` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): Role: ordinary. Privileges: any. Paths: opens `hello.txt` relative to the working directory, which is `/` on the test image (filetest.c:72). Devices: none. Global: none. Children: none. Recovery: nothing to remove. Native: native: workspace, stage `hello.txt`; the content is printed and not compared, as T01 says, so the read is judged only by its status.
* `forkleak` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): Role: ordinary. Privileges: any. Paths: none. Devices: none. Global: `sysinfo`'s `freeram`, the whole machine's free memory, required not to fall by more than 64 KiB across 120 cycles (forkleak.c:22 to 23, 141). Children: 128 forked children, each reaped before the next. Recovery: nothing to remove. Native: split in T44: the 128 forks and reaps native, workspace; the bound on free memory machine, count, since `freeram` is the whole machine's and the services move it by more than the 64 KiB allowed.
* `forkro` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): Role: ordinary. Privileges: any. Paths: none. Devices: none. Global: none; the child's death by `SIGSEGV` writes the kernel's fault record. Children: one forked child, killed by its own write and reaped. Recovery: a core file, where the case's limits allow one, lands in the working directory. Native: native: workspace.
* `forktest` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2; exitline types `exec /forktest` at the debug shell): Role: ordinary in the default set; in `exitline` the transient init whose exit the debug shell reports. Privileges: any. Paths: none. Devices: none. Global: none. Children: one forked child, reaped. Recovery: nothing to remove. Native: split in T44: the default set's run native, workspace; the `exitline` run machine, image, since it reads the line the test image's debug shell prints when its transient init exits. The T01 entry's "`waitpid` returns the child's pid" is not checked: only a negative result fails (forktest.c:126).
* `fpuprobe` (fpu): Role: ordinary, the transient init of three boots on x86-64 and four on i686. Privileges: any. Paths: a child executes `/fpuprobe reset` or `/fpuprobe reset-x87` by its fixed path (fpuprobe.c:473). Devices: none. Global: the processor's floating-point save path, `xsave`, `fxsave` or `fnsave`, which the suite reads from the kernel's boot record `fpu: fxsr: ..., sse: ..., xsave: ...`. ## The default suite's own checks

* `runtests: exit marker` (default, selfbuild and selfbuild-ext2 through `tests/system/runner/runtests.c`, `score_window`): Role: a check the host runner makes on the serial transcript of the transient init's exit record. Privileges: none in the guest. Paths: none. Devices: the serial console. Global: the kernel's log on the console. Children: none. Recovery: nothing. Native: native: no fixture; in its place `etude-guest` takes the case's exit status by `waitpid` of its own child, the result native.md gives every case, so that no text the case prints can stand for its result or end it early.
* `runtests: kill marker` (default, selfbuild and selfbuild-ext2, `score_window`): Role: a check the host runner makes on the serial transcript of the kernel's signal-death record. Privileges: none in the guest. Paths: none. Devices: the serial console. Global: the kernel's log on the console. Children: none. Recovery: nothing. Native: native: no fixture; in its place a wait status that reports a death by a signal fails the case with that signal, read by `etude-guest` from `waitpid`, not from the kernel's records.
* `runtests: timing` (default, selfbuild and selfbuild-ext2, `score_kernel_timing`): Role: a check the host runner makes of the kernel's `timing:` record at boot. Privileges: none in the guest. Paths: none. Devices: the serial console. Global: the kernel's boot log. Children: none. Recovery: nothing. Native: machine: boot: the verdict is the record the kernel writes once while it boots, and a native run starts on a Lenovo booted long before, whose log ring need not still have it.
* `runtests: clock` (default, selfbuild and selfbuild-ext2): Role: a check the host runner makes of musl/clockmusl's `CLOCKMUSL EPOCH` line against the host's clock. Privileges: none in the guest. Paths: none. Devices: the serial console. Global: the guest's real-time clock. Children: none. Recovery: nothing. Native: native after T44: the controller compares the epoch the case reports with its own clock under T39's bound for the transport's delay, in place of the arrival of the marker; on the Lenovo the clock compared is the one `ntpd` keeps, so a CMOS clock kept in local time shows only until `ntpd` steps it, and musl/clockmusl's own split keeps the CMOS reading in QEMU.

Native: native: no fixture; the Lenovo runs the x86-64 kernel, so `etude-guest` reports tlsprobe's case as one that does not apply, T06's result, and counts it neither run nor passed.
