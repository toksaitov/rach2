# Inventory of the guest programs

This part of [the inventory of assertions](inventory.md) covers the programs that run on Rach2: the freestanding probes that `PROGS` in `tests/Makefile` builds, the programs in `tests/musl/`, and the checks the probe runner `tests/system/runner/runtests.c` makes beyond a program's exit status. What runs a program is a `tests/run` suite, or "helper" for a program that `tests/probe-helpers` lists and no suite runs as a probe. "Global state" marks a program that reads or changes state of the whole machine beside its own behavior, which the native plan of T04 and the adaptation of T44 must account for.

## The probes

* `ahciprobe` (queue): Three modes ending `AHCIPROBE OK` or `AHCIPROBE FAIL: reason`. `read`: children read 1 MiB and 256 KiB from two real AHCI partitions; both must succeed and the controller's vector must count completions. `fault`: `blockprobe` runs on the KRFFS root beside raw reads from its second partition. `write`: an ext2 batch is written, synced, read back byte for byte, and followed by another write and sync. Timing: completion bounds for `read` and `write`; `fault` still requires at least 64 raw reads beside the child (T38). T37 moves the former slow/fast ordering, deadline boundaries, quarantine release and full-pool ordering to `request_hosttest.c` and `ahci_hosttest.c`, using held completions, explicit register state and logical ticks. The device nodes are prepared before the probe runs. Fate: kept, T75.
* `auxvprobe` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): The initial process stack and entry state: the stack pointer 16-byte aligned; every general register but the stack pointer 0 at entry; argc at least 1 and `argv[argc]` NULL; at least one envp entry and its NULL; the auxv ends with `AT_NULL`; `AT_PAGESZ` 4096, `AT_PHNUM` and `AT_PHDR` nonzero, `AT_PHENT` 32 on i686 and 56 on x86-64, `AT_CLKTCK` 100, `AT_SECURE` present and 0, `AT_ENTRY` nonzero, `AT_MINSIGSTKSZ` above 512, `AT_RANDOM` present, in the stack pointer's page, and its 16 bytes not all zero. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `bigfileprobe` (large): Files past 4 GiB on the volume the suite mounts at `/big`. `write MIB`: a file of MIB MiB of numbered 4 KiB blocks; `fsync`; size by `fstat`; the last block, the blocks at and before the 4 GiB line and one near the end read back; a second descriptor and a `dup` see separate and shared offsets at the line; `ftruncate64` to three quarters issues at most 8 disk commands per 4 MiB freed plus 64 (read from the global `/proc/disk` counter); the size after it; an appended mebibyte and the block before the truncate point read back; `fsync`. `verify MIB`: after a remount every block's number matches the size `write` left. `sparse`: a mebibyte astride the 4 GiB line reads back, a block in the hole reads zeros, a truncate past the line. `limit OFFSET`: a one-byte write at OFFSET fails with `EFBIG`. Ends `BIGFILE OK (mode)`. The write rate is printed and not judged. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T76.
* `blockprobe` (disk, disk-ahci, disk-q35; bus as its file check on each IDE layout; queue's `fault` mode runs it as a child of `ahciprobe`): On a KRFFS root on disk through the page cache (`rach2.fsdisk`), writes NFILES files of a pattern of index and offset, `fsync` each, reads every file back and compares every byte; open, write, fsync, read and mismatch each fail with `BLOCKPROBE FAIL`. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T74.
* `bootprobe` (bootpart: `fixture`, absent, `ramroot` and `wide` boots; fdlock: `shared`; authority: `auth-ordinary`, `auth-recovery`): Selected by `rach2.bootprobe=` on the command line. Every boot: `/proc/cmdline` content (`rach2.fsdisk` present, or absent on a RAM root), its trailing newline, its entry in `getdents` of `/proc`, `stat64` and `fstat64` mode 0100444 and inode 0x101, and `readlink` of its descriptor. `fixture`: `/dev/bootpart` a 0600 character device by `stat64` and `fstat64`; sector 5 has the fixture pattern; `SEEK_END` is the partition size; a read at and past the end returns 0; a write past the end `ENOSPC`; an unaligned count or offset `EINVAL`; sector 7 written and read back; uid 1000 open read-only and read-write `EACCES`. Absent: `stat64` and `open` of `/dev/bootpart` `ENOENT`. `ramroot`: `/dev/bootpart` and `/dev/grubcore` exist as 0600 devices; grubcore spans LBA 1 to 2047; first sector reads; read at the end 0; writes at and across the end `ENOSPC`; unaligned seek allowed, unaligned read, write and count `EINVAL`; after dropping to an unprivileged uid the descriptor still seeks and reads but write is `EPERM`, and a new open `EACCES`. `wide` (AHCI, 260 GB sparse drive): `_llseek` to the end gives the whole size; sectors at 5 GiB and 258 GiB have their markers; `SEEK_CUR` crosses 4 GiB both ways; a seek before the start `EINVAL`; a `dup` shares the wide offset; 32-bit `lseek` past 4 GiB `EOVERFLOW` and still works below. `auth-*` (`auth_case`, `window_batch_case`): a second writer of `/dev/disk0` `EBUSY` however opened, a writer of the gap `EBUSY` or `ENOENT`, a reader allowed; an ordinary kernel's write past 2 GiB `EPERM` and the sector still zero; a recovery kernel writes and reads it back byte for byte; a 200-sector read and write of the whole-disk window are one driver call (`raw-driver-calls`), `raw-batch-sectors` 2048, one cache flush (`disk-flushes`); a 2100-sector write and read are two driver calls, four disk commands, two flushes, and the bytes cross the batch boundary; a 200-sector read and write of `/dev/grubcore` are one driver call each. `shared` (`rach2.blocklag=10`): a `readv` of two sectors on an inherited description blocks on its second vector while the parent reads the third sector, and the offset ends at three sectors; concurrent raw readers, writers, `lseek` and `_llseek` on a shared description wait for the transfer's offset; raw writers use separate sectors. Reads the global `/proc/disk` counters throughout. Timing: `raw_child_sleeping` polls `/proc/PID/stat` up to 100000 times for state D, and `raw_readv_case` polls `raw-driver-calls` up to 100000 times for the second call; both are fixed spin counts against the 10-tick lag. Fate: kept, T78 (bootpart), T70 (authority, fdlock); replaced in T38: the 100000-read spins by a wait that ends on the witnessed state D or counter value with a completion bound only.
* `busprobe` (bus): `four`: `/proc/disk` lists `ata` 0 to 3 at 32768, 40960, 49152 and 57344 sectors; each FAT32 partition mounts and a file written reads back. `big`: the 130 GiB disk listed at 272629760 sectors, its partition at sector 268437504 (past 2^28) listed, mounted, written and read back through 48-bit commands. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T78.
* `calibprobe` (helper: fixed work for `tests/system/calibrate.sh`, which no suite runs): `compute` runs a xorshift generator 100000000 rounds and prints the value; `storage` reads 32 MiB of `/dev/disk0` in 32 KiB reads. Its only assertions are that `/dev/disk0` opens and no read fails before 32 MiB; it tests no timer calibration. The script times each part by the host's clock to measure the emulator's rate. Timing: none in the probe; the script's measurement is host wall time by design. Fate: removed in T89: its one user, `tests/system/calibrate.sh`, which no suite runs, is removed there, and the timing history of T07 and T14 measures each host's rate in its place.
* `conloadprobe` (conload): The console's receive path under load (five spinning children and one writing and syncing a 256 KiB file on the mounted volume). Four stages of numbered lines with Adler-32 sums, each line required in order with a correct sum: A, 200 lines read through `poll`, and the global `/proc/console` counter `irq-bytes` must rise (the port's interrupt moved at least one byte); B, 60 lines typed while the probe does not read; C, 200 lines typed while a child in its own group owns the terminal (`TIOCSPGRP`), then the interrupt character must kill the child by `SIGINT`, then 60 lines read after `TIOCSPGRP` back to the parent; D, 40 lines typed before the probe executes itself, read by the new image. Also fails on end of file, a failed mount, a failed fork, a load process that ended on its own, and an exec that did not happen. Writer progress and stage times are printed, not judged. Timing: `read_line` fails when `poll` sees no byte for `READ_LIMIT_MS` (5000 ms) per byte; stage B relies on `spin_ms(2000)` lasting while the harness types the 60 lines; stage D relies on `sleep_ms(1000)` before the exec lasting while the harness types the 40 lines. Fate: kept, T71; replaced in T38: the type-ahead stages B and D wait for a witnessed count of bytes queued in the console ring (a `/proc/console` counter) before reading or executing, in place of the 2 s spin and 1 s sleep, leaving the 5 s per-byte poll as a completion bound only.
* `cowprobe` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): Copy-on-write fork: a value written before `fork` is seen by the child; the child's write leaves the parent's copy unchanged; the parent's own write then sticks; 64 fork and reap cycles neither fail `fork` with frames exhausted nor fail `waitpid`. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `cowregrant` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): The sole-owner re-grant arm of `cow_fault` over many cycles: the child writes first and exits cleanly, the parent's bytes are unchanged by the child's write, and the parent's write after the reap sticks; a `fork` that fails with frames exhausted fails the run (a leaked or double-freed re-granted frame). Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `credprobe` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): The credential model in a child of the root probe: `setgroups` and `getgroups` round trip, the count query, a short list `EINVAL`, an oversize `setgroups` `EINVAL`; an effective-id excursion out and back keeps the saved id, and privilege follows the effective id; `setresgid` with three distinct ids and the gid drop; `setresuid` parking; `setreuid` of the real id from the saved id `EPERM`; `setresuid` saved from real; `setreuid` real from effective; the triple after the unprivileged walk; after the drop `setuid(0)`, `setreuid(0)`, `setresuid(0)` and `setgroups` all refused; `setuid` to its own real id allowed; a grandchild inherits the dropped ids; the boot identity is root. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `devprobe` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2; ext2 and selfbuild-ext2 on an ext2 root through `probe_roots.sh`; by hand on an installed machine through `manual_probes.sh probes`): The synthetic devices: `/dev/zero` reads 64 zero bytes; `/dev/null` reads EOF and takes a 10-byte write; `/dev/full` write `ENOSPC`; `/dev/urandom` differs between reads; `BLKRRPART` on `/dev/null` `ENOTTY`, and its `fstat` inode equals the `/dev` listing's. Paths past a file: `/dev/null/x`, `/proc/self/stat/x`, a PCI device's `uevent/x` and a live pty slave's path `ENOTDIR`; `/dev/nosuch/x`, `/proc/1999999999/stat/x`, an absent PCI device's path and a freed pty slave's path `ENOENT`. `/proc/self/stat` has the pid and `(devprobe)`; `/proc/self/fd/0` reads `/dev/console`. Framebuffer ownership (`/fb`): text mode and 13h re-entry by the owner; a second opener `EBUSY` and its mode ioctl `EBUSY` while owned; the second opener acquires after release; an inherited descriptor of the old owner gets `EBUSY` for `FB_MODE_TEXT`, `FB_MODE_13H` and `FB_SETPALETTE`; a third open `EBUSY` while the second owns it. Uses the VGA framebuffer, a global device. Timing: none (children synchronize through pipes). Fate: kept, T68.
* `dgramprobe` (dgram, dgram-e1000): A UDP socket as a DHCP client uses it, against the machine's own address: a write with no peer `EDESTADDRREQ`; connected, it writes to its peer and receives the peer's reply, has a source port, and does not receive a third local socket's datagram or the suite's datagram from outside after `DGRAMPROBE OUTSIDE`, yet still receives its peer; `connect` on the console `ENOTSOCK`; `AF_UNSPEC` disconnects a datagram socket (a later write refused) and is `EAFNOSUPPORT` on a stream socket; a packet socket cannot be connected and does not exhaust the UDP binding table; a second bind of a port needs `SO_REUSEADDR` on both, the reply goes to the connected one, the listener does not take it, and closing one leaves the other bound; uid 1000 cannot bind below 1024; a connect beside eight bound sockets answers 0 or `EAGAIN`; a broadcast (and the subnet broadcast) `EACCES` until `SO_BROADCAST`, then sent; `SO_BROADCAST` and `SO_REUSEADDR` read back; `SO_BINDTODEVICE` accepts the machine's interface by name and as a whole `struct ifreq`, refuses an unknown name, unbinds with an empty name, and reads back. The suite checks the broadcast in the capture. Timing: the absence of the third socket's datagram is polled 40 times with `nap(10)`, and the absence of the outsider's datagram 60 times with `nap(10)` after printing `DGRAMPROBE OUTSIDE` (`main`, lines 338 and 360); if the harness's datagram arrives after those 600 ms, the outsider row passes without being exercised. Fate: kept, T84; replaced in T33: the outsider row waits for a witnessed arrival of the outside datagram at the machine (a count of datagrams dropped as not from the peer, or a second socket bound to receive a copy) before it asserts the absence, in place of the 600 ms window.
* `dirgrow` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): KRFFS relocation on growth: `/dg/f` grown past its slack with `/dg/g` after it still resolves under `/dg`, keeps its first bytes, and does not appear as `/f`; mkdir, create, write and grow each fail the run. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `dirprobe` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2; ext2 and selfbuild-ext2 on an ext2 root through `probe_roots.sh`; by hand on an installed machine through `manual_probes.sh probes`): Directories on the root, whose type it reads from `/proc/mounts` and must be KRFFS or ext2: mkdir `/d`, a second mkdir fails, create, write and read back `/d/f`, create in a missing directory fails, rmdir of a non-empty directory fails, unlink and rmdir succeed and the file is gone; `getdents64` of `/e` lists `a` and `b`, and on ext2 also `.` and `..` (KRFFS lists neither); on KRFFS `mkdir /ids/cb` beside `/ids/bC` is `EINVAL`; `openat`, `fstat64` (regular, size 5), `_llseek` (offset 1, next byte `e`), `F_SETFD` and `F_GETFD` `FD_CLOEXEC`, `fstatat64` of a file and a directory, `O_APPEND` writes at the end, `F_DUPFD` honours its minimum, `O_CREAT` applies mode 0644; chdir into `/work`, a relative create lands there, `openat` on a directory descriptor reads it, `chdir("rel.txt/x")` `ENOTDIR`, `/work/..` is a directory, `chdir("..")` and `getcwd` give `/`; cleanup by unlink and rmdir is asserted. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `dirtest` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): `getcwd` at the root; `chdir` to `/`, `.` and `..` succeed and `getcwd` stays `/`; `chdir` to a missing name fails; `chdir` to a plain file fails. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `dirtyprobe` (crash): On a disk-backed KRFFS root booted dirty, each call near the root node's mode, each followed by `sync`, gives its listed answer: `chown("/")` with -1:-1, 0:0, -1:1 and -1:0 return 0; `chmod("/", 0755)` returns 0; `open("/", O_WRONLY|O_TRUNC)` and `creat("/")` `EISDIR`; `write` on a descriptor opened on `/` `EBADF`. The suite then kills QEMU and requires `fsck.krffs` to report the volume not cleanly unmounted (the dirty flag, which shares the set-user-ID bit, survived). Timing: none. Fate: kept, T83.
* `diskfull` (diskfull): Writes 4 KiB blocks to the mounted root until refused (at most 8192 tries, 32 MiB): the file system must refuse a write, the refusal must be `ENOSPC` (not `EIO`), some bytes must have been written first, the file must unlink, a new file must be created and take a block (the space came back). Timing: none. Fate: kept, T75.
* `disksprobe` (disks): Two disks on one AHCI controller: `/proc/disk` lists disk 0 on port 0 and disk 1 on port 1 with 40960 sectors, disk 0 the larger, and no third disk; the whole-disk and partition nodes of both exist and no node for an absent partition or disk; disk 0's KRFFS partition mounts at `/mnt0` and disk 1's FAT32 partition at `/mnt1`; a file written on each reads back; both unmount. The suite reads the FAT32 file from the host with mtools. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T78.
* `dnstest` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2; ext2 and selfbuild-ext2 on an ext2 root through `probe_roots.sh`; by hand on an installed machine through `manual_probes.sh probes`): The stub resolver of `utils/resolv.c`, linked with musl: `rach2_inet_aton` on four good literals and six bad ones (out of range, too few or many parts, trailing dot, letters, empty); `hosts_find` on a crafted buffer (name, alias, case-insensitive, localhost, a miss, a commented line not matched); `resolve_host` on a literal and on localhost, gateway, dns and rach2 from the image's hosts file; `dns_parse_a` on an A record, CNAME then A, id mismatch, NXDOMAIN, NODATA, truncated rdata, a malformed name and a short buffer; `dns_build_query` length 29, id, RD flag, qdcount, labels, qtype A, qclass IN, and refusal of an empty name, `a..b`, a leading dot and a small buffer. The `--live` block (one.one.one.one and dns.google resolve, nxdomain.invalid fails) runs only by hand and needs the internet. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `dup2test` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): `dup2(1, 1)` returns 1; a created file put on fd 1 with `dup2` takes a write through fd 1 and reads back equal; `dup2(99, 5)` fails; `dup2(1, 2000)` fails past the 1024-descriptor table; `dup2(2, 1)` restores fd 1 and returns 1. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `errnoprobe` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): The negative-errno return contract of raw calls: an unimplemented call number and number 909 return `ENOSYS`; `open` of a missing path `ENOENT`; `close(999)` and `read(999)` `EBADF`; `writev` with an iovec at -4096 `EFAULT`. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `exitprobe` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2; `tests/system/exit_mutate.sh` runs single rows): Three rows, each a victim that holds a pipe's write end, an unlinked file and a working directory, waits until `/proc/PID/stat` shows its parent in state S, and ends by `exit(37)`, by `SIGTERM`, or by `exit(38)` after `execve` of a truncated ELF returns `ENOEXEC`; the reaper checks the encoded wait status, end of file on the pipe, `ECHILD` on a second wait and the absence of `/proc/PID`; a supervisor fails the row as `reap or release` when no report comes. Timing: `wait_parent` polls `/proc` every 1 ms for the parent's state S (a witnessed state); the supervisor's `poll` in `run_row` fails a row after 5000 ms by default (a completion bound that names a lost zombie, settable by argument). Fate: kept, T68.
* `ext2probe` (ext2; `tests/system/ext2_batch_mutate.sh` runs `cost-append`): `links`, on the read-only fixture volume at `/e2`: relative and absolute links read their targets; a link to a directory is walked into, and `..` through it reaches the target's parent; a chain of forty links is followed and forty-one is `ELOOP`; `O_NOFOLLOW` on a link `ELOOP` and on a file opens; `O_CREAT|O_EXCL` on a dangling link `EEXIST`; `lstat` reports the link and `stat` the target (also through the old calls); `readlink` gives the target and `EINVAL` on a file; a dangling link `ENOENT` to `stat` and `open` but present to `lstat`; a hard link's count is two; a directory cannot be hard-linked (`EPERM`); hard and symbolic links on the read-only mount `EROFS`; a symbolic link on the KRFFS root `EPERM`; a hard link across mounts `EXDEV`; `O_CREAT` through a dangling link `EROFS`; `fstatat64` with and without `AT_SYMLINK_NOFOLLOW`, and `AT_SYMLINK_FOLLOW` `EINVAL` for `fstatat64` and `utimensat`; `/proc/self` with `O_NOFOLLOW` `ELOOP` and `/proc/self/fd/1` with `O_CREAT|O_EXCL` `EEXIST`. `cost`, mounted `MS_SYNCHRONOUS`: rewrites cost exactly four commands and two flushes through `fsync` and none before it; appends cost between one and ten commands and one to five flushes and no flush before `fsync`; both files reread with the right bytes and length. Reads the global `/proc/disk` counters. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T76.
* `fatwriteprobe` (fatwrite with `vfat`; exfat with `exfat`): `write` mounts `/dev/disk0p2` on `/fw` and creates a 64 KiB pattern file, a long-named file, a directory with a file, a renamed file, a removed file and an fsynced file, then unmounts cleanly. `readback` on the next boot, mounted read-only: the pattern file's size and bytes, the long name and its case-folded lookup, the FAT32 short alias (not on exFAT), the file in the directory, the renamed file, the old and removed names gone, the fsynced file, `umount2`. `crash` mounts, prints `FATWRITE WRITING` and rewrites 200 files of 1 KiB round and round until the suite kills the machine; any write error fails. `repair` mounts what the crash left (the mount runs the check) and requires at least one file of the sequence in the root. The host checks the volume with mtools and its checker. Timing: none in the probe; the point the kill lands is chosen by the suite after `FATWRITE WRITING`. Fate: kept, T76.
* `fdfull` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): Opens descriptors until refused (at most 1040): the table must refuse, with `EMFILE`; the count opened must be 1024 less the three open at the start; a descriptor must close and a new open must then succeed. The probe closes the rest without checking the result (`close_all`, tests/fdfull.c line 82). Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `fdstale` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2; fdlock on a disk-backed KRFFS root): A descriptor names its file from open to close. Control: a quiet descriptor reads its file then end of file. Unlink under a descriptor: `read` gives the original bytes, `lseek` and `_llseek` to `SEEK_END` and `fstat` the original size, `fchmod` succeeds, a fresh `open` `ENOENT`. Orphan truncate: `ftruncate` of an unlinked file shrinks it for an independent descriptor and keeps a duplicate's offset, a grow relocates it, both descriptors see the new size, the first byte survives and the growth reads zeros, the name stays `ENOENT`, the last holder keeps the size. Orphan scans: `getdents64` of the parent lists neither the name nor a nameless entry; `rmdir` of a directory whose only child is a held orphan succeeds. Failed renames: a directory renamed onto an open file's name `EPERM` on KRFFS and `ENOTDIR` on ext2, the name still readable through the descriptor and a fresh open; a file renamed onto an open directory `EISDIR` and the directory survives. Across two processes synchronized by pipes: a child's two descriptors still read their whole original files after the parent renames a replacement over one name and re-creates the other, and the parent then reads the replacements; a holder child's descriptor survives the parent's unlink; a child that `_exit`s without closing releases the orphan; a write by another process that relocates a file leaves the other table's descriptor on the moved file. Single process: one of a `dup` pair closed leaves the other reading; a 1024-byte write past capacity relocates the file and the reader opened before it follows. Leak checks: 256 rounds each of open, unlink, close of 64 KB; of an unheld removal; and of a write renamed onto a name; none may exhaust the root image. Directories: KRFFS refuses `rmdir` with `EBUSY` while a descriptor, an `O_PATH` descriptor or a child's working directory refers to it, the descriptor still lists it, and `rmdir` succeeds afterwards; on ext2 `rmdir` succeeds and `getdents64` through the descriptor `ENOENT`. Every wait checks `WIFEXITED`. The leak rows judge the free space of the whole root image, a shared state. Timing: none (pipes order the processes). Fate: kept, T68 (default), T70 (fdlock).
* `filetest` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): `open` of `hello.txt` relative to the working directory, `read` and `close` succeed; the content is printed and not compared. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `forkleak` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): After a warm-up, 120 fork, exit and wait cycles must not fail `fork` for memory, `waitpid` must succeed, and the system-wide free memory from `sysinfo` must not fall by more than 64 KiB. The verdict reads global kernel state (`sysinfo` freeram), which any other process on the machine also moves. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `forkro` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2): A read-only anonymous page faulted in reads zero, and a forked child's write to it kills the child by `SIGSEGV` (`WIFSIGNALED`, `WTERMSIG`); a child that survives fails. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `forktest` (default; selfbuild, selfbuild-ext2; exitline types `exec /forktest` at the debug shell to read the shell's exit line): `fork` succeeds, the child sees 0 and exits with a known status, `waitpid` does not fail, and `WIFEXITED` with that status. The pid `waitpid` returns is printed and not compared with the child's (tests/forktest.c line 126). Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68 (default), T71 (exitline).
* `fpuprobe` (fpu: boots with XSAVE under `-cpu max`, with `rach2.noxsave`, under `qemu64`, and on i686 alone with `rach2.nofxsr` as `fpuprobe x87`): A parent and a forked child each keep their own `st(0)`, `st(1)`, `xmm0` to `xmm7` (to `xmm15` on x86-64), a round-down control word and an MXCSR with flush-to-zero, and each compares them repeatedly while yielding; a signal handler starts with the initial state, a nested second signal runs, and the values survive the handler's clobbering; a child that sets the state and executes the probe with `reset` finds control word 0x37F, every register tagged empty and MXCSR 0x1F80; the ucontext is where Linux's rt frame puts it; a handler that sets a reserved MXCSR bit in its frame must have the frame refused and the child killed by `SIGSEGV`. The `x87` mode drops the XMM, MXCSR and damaged-frame rows. The suite also requires the kernel's `fpu: fxsr: ..., sse: ..., xsave: ...` boot line for each profile. Timing: `hold` compares for 500 ms of `gettimeofday` time and yields after each comparison, so each comparison follows a switch whenever the other process is runnable, whatever the speed; preemption inside the 20000-iteration spin is not required. Fate: kept, T69; replaced in T41: each boot reports the save path it exercised (xsave, fxsave or fnsave) as an assertion.

## The default suite's own checks

* `runtests: exit marker` (default, selfbuild and selfbuild-ext2 through `tests/system/runner/runtests.c`, `score_window`): A probe passes only when the first `sys_exit: init process N exited with status M` record at the start of a line in its window (after an optional `<L>[time] ` prefix, matched whole with its component and pid) has M equal to 0; a window with no such record scores `no exit reported`, a failure, so a probe that prints the marker's text itself ends its own window early and fails. The kernel's per-process `process: exited with status N` records do not count. The window starts where the probe's output starts and ends at the marker of its return to the shell. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68; replaced in T11: the marker on the shared serial console, by the guest transport's framed result for each case.
* `runtests: kill marker` (default, selfbuild and selfbuild-ext2, `score_window`): A `terminate: init process N killed by signal S` record at the start of any line of the window fails the probe with `killed by signal S`, whatever exit record follows it; `tests/system/suites/exitline.sh` requires the kernel to keep printing both records whole. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `runtests: timing` (default, selfbuild and selfbuild-ext2, `score_kernel_timing`): The kernel's boot record `timing: tick N Hz, ...` is the verdict by its level: the first `<6>[...] timing: ` at a line start passes, a `<3>` record (when no `<6>` one exists) fails, and no record fails with `the kernel said nothing about time`. The measured delay, wait and walk on the line are printed for a person and not judged; the level reflects the kernel's check of its period accounting on fixed readings. It counts as a failure but not as a probe. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `runtests: clock` (default, selfbuild and selfbuild-ext2): After musl/clockmusl returns, the host's `gettimeofday` at the arrival of its exit marker is compared with the first `CLOCKMUSL EPOCH n` in the whole transcript, which is not required to start a line; the row passes when they agree within 30 s, and fails when the probe printed no epoch or did not run. It catches a date wrong by hours, such as a CMOS clock kept in local time. It counts as a failure but not as a probe. Timing: the 30 s window includes serial and host latency between the guest's reading and the marker's arrival. Fate: kept, T68; replaced in T39: the comparison at the marker's arrival, by one that transport delay cannot turn into a false failure or pass.
* `runtests: i686 only` (default, selfbuild and selfbuild-ext2, `not_built_here`): When `RACH2_ARCH` names an architecture other than i686, each program of `I686_ONLY` (tlsprobe) is not run and is reported `i686 only, not run` with a pass, and counted among the probes that ran; with `RACH2_ARCH` unset the runner treats the boot as i686 and runs it. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68; replaced in T06: the pass given to a program that did not run, by a result that says the case does not apply and is not counted as a pass.
* `runtests: hang` (default, selfbuild and selfbuild-ext2): A probe whose return marker does not arrive within its step limit, before the run's deadline or before QEMU exits, scores `no shell return (hang?)`, a failure, and ends the boot; every probe after it is named on a `never ran` line and each counts as one failure. Timing: the step limit and the run's deadline decide when a slow probe becomes a hang. Fate: kept, T68; replaced in T15: the end of the whole boot at one hang, by a watchdog per case that ends the case alone.
* `runtests: boot` (default, selfbuild and selfbuild-ext2): The run waits for the kernel shell's `Type 'help'` within the boot limit (60 s, scaled) and exits 3 with `kernel shell never appeared` otherwise; an image with `/sbin/init`, which never reaches the shell, ends this way. Timing: the boot limit. Fate: kept, T68; replaced in T11: the wait for the shell's banner, by the guest transport's readiness.
* `runtests: bounds` (default, selfbuild and selfbuild-ext2): `--timeout` bounds the run (180 s by default, 300 in the `default` recipe), `--steptmo` each probe (20 s by default, 60 in the recipe) and `--boottmo` the boot (60 s), each multiplied by `RACH2_DEADLINE_FACTOR` (1 to 64) when `RACH2_DEADLINES=scaled`; a bound that runs out is scored as a hang. The exit status is 0 when every probe and both checks pass and 1 otherwise. Timing: the bounds are completion limits, not judged results. Fate: kept, T68; replaced in T15: fixed step and run limits, by per-case watchdogs that judge progress.
* `runtests: scan order` (default, selfbuild and selfbuild-ext2): A marker found before the window of the probe just started means the scan is out of step, and the runner exits 2 rather than score it; a usage error or a run that cannot start also exits 2. Timing: none. Fate: removed in T89: a check of the old runner's own scan.
* `runtests: probe order` (default, selfbuild and selfbuild-ext2, the `PROBES` table): The probes run in one boot in a fixed order on which some depend: musl/pwauthmusl, musl/passwdmusl and musl/addusermusl after the identity probes, since they rewrite and restore the credential databases; searchprobe and stickyprobe last, since they change staged modes and make files at the root. A probe that leaves state behind can make a later one fail or pass. Timing: none. Fate: kept, T68.
* `runtests: selftest` (host, which builds runtests.c under the sanitizers and runs `runtests --selftest`): On fixed text, nine window cases (a clean exit with and without the kernel's prefix passes; a forged prefix, a child's exit followed by the init's signal death, an orphan exiting after its parent, no status, a non-zero status, and forged marker text alone or followed by a kill each score as they must), four scan cases (a marker on its own line, a marker after a probe's partial line bounding the window and its scoring, a pid of more digits than a long holds) and four timing cases (a level-6 record passes, a level-3 record fails, a record in a probe's text does not count, no record fails); it exits non-zero if any case differs. Timing: none. Fate: removed in T89: a test of the old runner.
