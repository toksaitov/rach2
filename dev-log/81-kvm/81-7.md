# Exit costs and the KVM fuzzer (81-7)

Rach2's KVM counts its guests' exits by the processor's exit code, with the cycles each kind waits for the big lock and spends in the kernel, in the debug file `kvm/exit_stats`; `tests/kvm/exits.sh` reads those counts around one command, or Linux's from a histogram on the `kvm:kvm_exit` event. On the testing PC, under QEMU's TCG with one processor, the same boots of the Debian guest take 87,764 exits under Rach2's KVM and 57,704 under Linux's, and all of the difference but 3,915 exits is Linux's 2 MiB leaves for transparent huge pages, which Rach2's memory lacks: with them turned off, Linux takes 83,849. The fuzzer's KVM mode makes 20,000 calls on `/dev/kvm`, a VM and a vCPU as an ordinary user with no fault and no leak. The Lenovo's numbers, beside Linux's of 80-2, come from the step on the task's issue and go in the last section.

## The configuration on the testing PC

Every run here is on the testing PC under WSL, inside `qemu-system-x86_64` 10.2.1 (Ubuntu's 1:10.2.1+ds-1ubuntu3.3) with `-accel tcg -machine q35 -cpu max,rdrand=on,rdseed=on,xsave=on,avx=on,svm=on,npt=on -smp 1 -m 4096`, the `max-svm` profile with one processor, as the owner's order of 2026-11-10 on the task asks: no nested KVM. The first disk is the x86-64 GNU root, `gnu-test.disk`, and the second the kvm suite's data disk, `kvm.ext2`, both on the q35 machine's AHCI and both under QEMU's snapshot. The host of the guests is either:

* Rach2: the x86-64 kernel of the task branch at d519da597, booted with `rach2.root=ext2 rach2.tscreliable` and driven over its serial console as root.
* Linux: Debian's cloud kernel 6.12.107+deb13-cloud-amd64 of `tests/kvm/guests/debian.txt`, with an initramfs from `tests/kvm/guests/mklinuxhost.sh` that loads its AHCI and `kvm-amd` modules and runs the GNU root in a chroot.

Either host copies the data disk to `/tmp/kvm` and runs there, with the GNU root's own QEMU 10.2.1 under its KVM:

```
sh exits.sh sh kvm-boot.sh -a kvm -o boot1.log -k guests/debian/vmlinuz -i guests/debian/initrd
sh exits.sh sh kvm-boot.sh -a kvm -o boot2.log -k guests/debian/vmlinuz -i guests/debian/initrd
sh exits.sh sh kvm-boot.sh -a kvm -o boot3.log -k guests/rach2/kernel -i guests/rach2/ram.krffs -l rach2.tscreliable -t 'KVMRACH2 OK'
```

The Debian guest is Linux 6.12.107 with 256 MiB and one processor, booted to its token `KVMGUEST OK`; the third guest is Rach2's i686 kernel with its RAM disk, booted to `KVMRACH2 OK`. Each boot passed. The times below are the testing PC's clock under TCG and are not compared.

* npf: Linux's KVM maps QEMU's transparent huge pages as 2 MiB leaves, so one fault maps 512 pages of the guest; Rach2 maps user memory in 4 KiB pages and its tables take a 2 MiB leaf only where 512 pages are consecutive frames, as the Limits of `rach2/sys/kvm/Readme.md` say. With huge pages off, Linux takes 28,172 and 28,155 faults to Rach2's 27,793 and 27,785. Transparent huge pages in Rach2's memory are work the size of a milestone, which the owner decides.
* io and cpuid: the same within 2 percent; the control registers, wbinvd and xsetbv within two exits.
* The debug registers: Linux's `dr_interception` stops intercepting them at the guest's first access and loads them at each entry (`arch/x86/kvm/svm/svm.c` of 6.12), so only the first write, DR7's, exits; Rach2 exits on each of the six writes.
* cr0_sel_write and rsm: Linux sets the selective CR0 write intercept, 34 exits, and Rach2 does not; Rach2's guest made no RSM exit.
* interrupt and vintr: an interrupt exit is a host interrupt that arrived while the guest ran, so its count follows the host's timer and the boot's length on the host, which differ between the two kernels; they are recorded, not compared.
* msr: Rach2's guest made 2,230 and 2,068 MSR exits to Linux's 1,138 and 928 (1,139 and 1,172 without huge pages). Neither count is by MSR, so which MSRs make the difference is not known from these runs. Linux passes eight MSRs to the guest from its start (`direct_access_msrs` with `always`, SEV-ES's GHCB aside): STAR, LSTAR, CSTAR, SYSCALL_MASK, SYSENTER_CS and the three bases; Rach2 passes the bases. A Linux guest writes the other five once per processor at boot, so they make a few of the exits, not a thousand.

The third boot, Rach2's i686 guest, ran under Rach2's KVM and stopped the outer QEMU under Linux's: QEMU's TCG aborts with `qemu: fatal: invalid tss type` on an interrupt at CPL 3 of a 32-bit guest whose cached TR type is a busy TSS, which a VMCB from Linux's KVM can load. The dump at the abort:

## The big lock on the hottest exits (81-7.5)

Rach2's KVM drops the big lock only around the guest's run (`bkl_drop` and `bkl_take` in `run_enter`, `rach2/sys/kvm/vcpu.c`), and every exit takes it back before its handler runs. `exit_stats` gives, for each kind, the TSC cycles from the guest's exit to the lock taken back (wait) and from there to the next entry or to KVM_RUN's return (handle). Under TCG the TSC follows the host's clock, so these are shares of the emulated machine's time, not a processor's costs; the Lenovo's step measures those.

| Boot | Exits | Wait, cycles | Handle, cycles | Wait's share | Wait per exit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Debian 1 | 87,764 | 1,236,588,531 | 14,463,855,533 | 7.9% | 14,090 |
| Debian 2 | 87,721 | 1,283,423,674 | 15,770,169,664 | 7.5% | 14,631 |
| Rach2 i686 | 68,816 | 932,887,287 | 9,680,919,269 | 8.8% | 13,556 |

With one processor no other processor can hold the lock, and the wait per exit is within 12,700 and 17,400 cycles for every kind of more than 50 exits in the first boot: what it measures here is the cost of taking the lock back, the same for every exit, not contention. Contention needs two or more processors, which the Lenovo's step gives the Debian guest.

Which exits a lock-free path would serve, by the first Debian boot's counts:

* 50,748 exits, 58 percent, end KVM_RUN for QEMU: 50,637 port I/O, 110 MMIO and one interrupt. They return through the ioctl, which runs under the big lock, so a lock-free exit path does not spare them the lock.
* 37,016 exits, 42 percent, the kernel handles and enters the guest again. Of them, 27,683 are nested page faults, whose handler reads the caller's page tables and allocates table frames, so a lock-free path for them needs the address space's own locking first.
* 9,010 exits, 10 percent, touch only the vCPU's own state: 5,218 interrupts, which need nothing but the next entry; 2,230 MSR accesses, the local APIC's and the TSC deadline among them; 1,042 CPUIDs, read from the vCPU's table; and 520 interrupt windows. These are the exits a lock-free path serves first, and at the wait measured here they would save 9,010 times 14,000 cycles, 127 million of the boot's 15.7 billion, under 1 percent; the share that matters is the Lenovo's, with contention.

Taking the lock back costs under 9 percent of the time spent on exits in every boot here, and no kind of exit waits longer than another. The npf exits, the most numerous the kernel handles, would gain more from Linux's 2 MiB leaves, 26,000 fewer exits a boot, than from any lock-free path.
