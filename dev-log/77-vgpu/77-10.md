### The frame's path on the testing PC

`77-10/wsl-cube.sh` runs the step's three vkcube rounds in one guest on the testing PC, under KVM with Venus over lavapipe and QEMU's egl-headless display, and records QEMU's trace of each virtio GPU command, fence and notify with its time. `77-10/segments.py` cuts the trace into frames, from one `RESOURCE_FLUSH` to the next, and times each step: A from the flush to vkcube's first submission, B from vkcube's last submission to Weston's first, C Weston's two submissions, D from Weston's last to the last fence answer before the `SET_SCANOUT`, and E from that answer to the `SET_SCANOUT`. QEMU 10.0's `virtio-vga-gl` takes its notifies without an ioeventfd (`info qtree`), so each notify runs the queued commands in the notifying processor's exit, under QEMU's global lock: some 0.8 ms for a Venus submission and 2.4 to 3.0 ms for a flush, which includes egl-headless's redraw. QEMU answers a fenced command only when it next polls virgl's fences, at the guest's next notify or on a 10 ms timer, and an unfenced one at once.

Four differences from Linux 6.12's driver held back each frame, and each is fixed on this task's branch:

* `RESOURCE_FLUSH` went fenced, and the commit waited for its answer before it sent the flip's event, so each event waited for QEMU's next poll of virgl's fences. Linux's `virtio_gpu_resource_flush` fences and waits only for a dumb guest blob, which Rach2 never makes, and sends `SET_SCANOUT` and `SET_SCANOUT_BLOB` unfenced. Rach2 now sends all three unfenced and unwaited. This was the largest step: 70.21, 72.57 and 72.67 presents a second before, 86.48, 83.82 and 85.76 after. How much of the Lenovo's gap this wait was, the rerun measures.
* Planes took the implicit fence of their framebuffers' writes. Commit 17bac8010 dropped it on the claim that Linux's `virtio_gpu_plane_prepare_fb` takes none; the review of round 82 found the claim wrong, since that function calls `drm_gem_plane_helper_prepare_fb` (virtgpu_plane.c:281), and the fence is back (77-10.4). A Venus ring's fence signals when the host finishes that ring's work, not in queue order, so without it a flip could show a frame before its rendering ended.
* The notify was made under the big lock, which the processor held through QEMU's whole exit: `EXECBUFFER` held it 739 us a call and the atomic commit 584 us. A sender that may sleep now notifies with the lock let go, as Linux holds no lock there that the other processors need; the holds fell to 19.7 and 27.4 us. The rate moved little, since the processor still waits in QEMU.
* A commit notified once for `SET_SCANOUT` and again for the flush, 0.36 ms apart. Linux's plane update queues both and notifies once (`virtio_gpu_notify`); Rach2 now queues the copy and the scanout without a notify and the flush's notify carries them. Back to back on the same host, 86.31, 86.78 and 86.53 a second against 80.82, 79.16 and 81.10 without it.

One more fault, outside the driver: a relative timerfd counted from the tick after the one in progress, so it expired a tick later than a sleep of the same length, 1 to 2 ms after its time for a 1 ms timer, where Linux's hrtimer is late by its slack alone. Weston arms a timer of at least 1 ms before each repaint. ### The lost poll timeout

The poll's record fell off the list of timed sleepers, the list the timer's passes walk, while it stayed marked as on it. At `c9bb6f0ba` that list (`timers` in `rach2/sys/proc/process.c`) has no lock of its own. `proc_timer_update` changes its links with only the local processor's interrupts off, and its callers hold different locks: `schedule` (`sched_prepare_old`) and a wake (`sched_ready_locked`) hold `sched_lock` and often not the big lock, since poll, ppoll and futex run without it; a monotonic sleep (`sleep_time_come`, added on this branch), the tick's and the interrupt's passes, setitimer and `free_process` hold the big lock or nothing, and never `sched_lock`. So two processors can insert at the list's head at once: both read the same head, and the second store of `timers` drops the first record, whose `timer_linked` stays 1. `proc_timer_update` then finds the record's membership as it wants it and never links it again, and no pass sees it. The server thread's poll, on a listening socket that no client would connect to, blocked with its deadline in `wakeup_tick` and was dropped in this way; only a wake from the socket could end it, and none came. A record dropped so does more harm when a wake at last unlinks it: its `timer_prev` is NULL, so `timers` becomes its `timer_next`, and the records inserted ahead of it fall off too, each still marked linked. The GTK session's mix, Chromium's and Venus's threads in monotonic sleeps on four processors beside polls and futex waits, makes the two kinds of insertion frequent; before this branch armed sleeps between ticks, a sleep did not change the list outside `schedule`.

Linux links and unlinks a timer under its base's lock (`lock_timer_base`, kernel/time/timer.c:1034-1051) and an hrtimer under its clock base's. `599bf0ca3` puts the list and the passes' cursor under a spinlock of their own, `timer_list`, which comes after `sched_lock`, and the passes read each next record under it and let it go before they act on the record, since a wake takes `sched_lock`; the big lock, which every pass holds and `free_process` asserts, keeps a record visited from being freed. The cause was found by reading, so the forcing load was not built.

The milestone's head does not have the fault: 84-6a (`8ee71137a`) put every change of the list under `timer_list_lock` and has the tick's pass collect its records under it. It has neither `sleep_time_come` nor the interrupt's pass (`proc_wake_due`) of this branch. When this branch merges the milestone, `sleep_time_come` keeps calling `proc_timer_update`, which takes the lock there, and `proc_wake_due` walks the list in the form of the head's `proc_wake_sleepers`.
