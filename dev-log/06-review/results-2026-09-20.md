Nine findings came back. Seven are fixed and recorded as entries 167 and 168. Two are rejected, with the source that shows why:

* `sync` returning while a page dirtied during its own writeback is still dirty. The page's earlier bytes are written: `pc_writeback` maps the page read-only before it writes (`rach2/sys/mm/pagecache.c`), so a store made after the write began faults and marks the page dirty again. Chasing such pages would let one steady writer hold `sync` for its whole bound of thirty seconds.

Phase 4, the network stack:

* A reader asleep on a stream another process shut down never woke, and a shut-down stream polled as nothing: entry 133, fixed in `f5a12bef`.
* The plan's question whether a TCP receive ring can be freed or reused under a reader's copy has the answer no, from the code. The ring belongs to the control block (`tcp.h`), and control blocks are heap blocks, not slots of a pool. The interrupt side frees a block only when no socket holds it: a connection never accepted, or one its socket has left in TIME_WAIT, FIN_WAIT_2 or LAST_ACK. A reset of a connection a socket holds marks it closed and wakes the socket, and the free waits for the socket's close. The reader checks `sock->tcb` again after its sleep (`socket.c`), and its copy can neither fault nor sleep, since `user_buffer_ok` faults the destination in first (`syscall.c`) and the kernel switches only at a call's exit or a sleep; so nothing frees or reuses the ring under the copy.
