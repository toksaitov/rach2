# GPU handoff

Rach2's Intel driver now lets its GPU go to another driver and takes it back from a reset, through `/sys/bus/pci/drivers/i915/unbind` and `bind`, and `tests/assign/gpu.sh` is the script the Lenovo's step runs: the unbind, vfio-pci, QEMU with the q35 command line that Linux's run used, and the way back. The driver's path is proved on the host model of the HD 4400, and the script in QEMU on EDU under the driver's fake chip. These are author runs of the new test and cases on 2026-11-10, not the milestone's reviewed checkpoint. Nothing here ran on the Lenovo; that is 82-4b.

## The driver

`rach2/sys/drm/i915/i915_handoff.c` is the whole of it, and the driver's Readme has the design under "Release and bring-up". In short, the release turns the display off with one commit, stops the rings and resets the GPU, unplugs the DRM device, turns bus mastering off and gives up the kernel's claim on the function; the bring-up resets the function, waits out the panel's power cycle, and binds as at boot, with nothing to adopt.

Faults met on the way and fixed in the same commits:

* A record that a program still held after an unplug could reach the registers. The record is now cut off: its mappings are given back, its reads are all ones and its writes are dropped.
* Every bind mapped BAR 0's registers and the GTT and no path unmapped them, so each bind and unbind would have leaked 4 MiB of the kernel's device window. The record's end, a failed bind and the release unmap them.
* The boundary's check of a register's offset wrapped for a backend of size zero and let every access through.
* A bind that failed after its first step left the kernel's claim on the function, so nothing could bind it again before a reboot. The bring-up gives the claim up.
* The fake chip's `MCH_SSKPD` read zero, so the watermarks' check refused every commit on it, as Linux refuses one without a level 0 latency, and the release's commit with them. The fake now has the Lenovo's latencies.
* The console could be put back on the loader's framebuffer when a device left under a master, though the framebuffer had gone with the device. `drm_client_boot_screen_gone` tells it.

## The host test

`tests/host/i915_handoff_hosttest.c` stands the whole driver, bind and display, on the model, and drives it only through the two files of /sys. Its function's reset is the test's own. It keeps what the run of Linux on the Lenovo showed kept or what is a strap: the fuses, the detected display of DDI A, the raw clock and the PWM's granularity, from which Linux read 938 levels after the reset where the firmware's period gave 937. It clears the rest of the display engine's and the PCH's registers, DDI A's four-lane bit among them, the interrupts, the message, bus mastering, the power well, the link and the panel's power with the sequencer's count of the power cycle. The GTT's entries stay.

The test passes under ASan and UBSan on the Mac. Its rows were tried against eight faults put in the driver by hand, one at a time, and each was caught:

| Fault | Rows that failed |
| --- | --- |
| No wait of the panel's power cycle after the reset | the bring-up's first access, both times |
| The released record not cut off | the held record, in each of the three releases |
| No stand-in for the backlight | the backlight's write after the release |
| No stop and reset of the GPU | the order of the release, the rings, and the stuck GPU's refusal |
| The console not told of its screen | the console's calls at the release |
| Bus mastering left on | the released state, three times |
| The reset's error ignored | the failed reset and the bring-up after it |
| The claim kept after a failed bind | the failed bind and the bring-up after it |

## The script in QEMU

The four cases `machine.assign.gpu`, `gpu-uefi`, `gpu-grub` and `gpu-kill` boot 82-3's outer machine with `rach2.i915fake`, so that the driver has EDU at 0000:00:07.0 through its fake chip, and run `gpu.sh` on it. The nested guest gets the function at 00:02.0 with `-machine q35,kernel-irqchip=split -nodefaults -vga none -device vfio-pci,host=0000:00:07.0,bus=pcie.0,addr=02.0`, prints its ids, 1234:11e8, and reads EDU's identification register, 0x010000ED, through its BAR. All four passed in one run on the testing PC, at the tree of the commit that adds the cases, with the patched outer QEMU 10.2.1 of 82-3 and Debian's QEMU in the GNU root. Both levels ran under TCG there, so the seconds say nothing of the Lenovo.

| Case | Firmware and loader | Nested seconds | Case seconds | Host traffic rounds | Result |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `gpu` | SeaBIOS, the kernel loaded by QEMU | 195 | 264 | 582 | released, EDU read in the guest, bound again |
| `gpu-uefi` | OVMF, the kernel loaded by QEMU | 220 | 296 | 666 | the same |
| `gpu-grub` | OVMF, Debian's GRUB from the guest's disk | 244 | 322 | 758 | the same, with the menu's line seen |
| `gpu-kill` | SeaBIOS, QEMU killed while the guest held | 163 | 249 | 545 | the same after SIGKILL |

At the kill the guest held one group, one device, seven mappings, 65600 pinned pages and 256 pages of device memory; after each case every count of VFIO, KVM and the free vectors was as before it, no IOMMU fault was counted, and the driver was on the function again with `card0`. An earlier run of `gpu` alone passed in 167 s, 115 s of them in the nested guest; its command line still had `x-igd-opregion=on`, read from the fake's identity, which QEMU passes over for a device that is not an Intel display. The script now reads the ids once the driver has let the function go.

The first run failed at the unbind with `EINVAL`, which was the fake chip's zero latencies above.

## What 82-4b still has to find

* The reset. EDU has no reset, and QEMU says so, so the script's way back ran without one; `pci_reset_function` and the wait after it ran only in the host test.
* The chip after its reset under this driver. The model's reset is built from one run of Linux; the driver reads the raw clock as Linux does and has not read the chip.
* `x-igd-opregion=on` with a real OpRegion, and the guest's i915 on the panel.
* KVM. The nested guests ran under TCG; on the Lenovo the guest runs under Rach2's KVM with the split interrupt controller.
* OVMF under Rach2's KVM. The script gives OVMF as two flash drives, which under KVM need read-only memory slots; under TCG they need nothing of the kernel.

For the readiness note: every wait of the release and the bring-up has a bound. The display's commit waits for vblanks and the panel's sequencer with the driver's timeouts; the GPU's reset is tried four times, 2 ms each and 10 ms more apart; the function's reset is the kernel's, 100 ms; and the power cycle is one sleep of the VBT's delay, 510 ms at least. A refused release leaves the driver bound, and the script returns the function to the driver after any failure once it has left.
