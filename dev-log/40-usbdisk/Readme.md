# Milestone 40: the USB disk

A flash drive plugged into a root port is a disk. The USB core enumerates it, a mass-storage class driver drives it over Bulk-Only Transport, the block layer publishes it as `/dev/usbN` with its partitions read from an MBR or a GUID table, the FAT32 and exFAT drivers mount those partitions, BusyBox finds them by label, and the stick is safe to pull out at any moment: the read in flight answers `EIO`, the mount unmounts once its files are closed, and a stick plugged back in is a new device with nothing inherited. A machine then boots from a stick image through its firmware and GRUB and mounts that stick's own second partition. Nothing touched the Lenovo; the owner's stick in both table formats and the boot from the Rach2 stick are written into the metal acceptance and run in milestone 43.

## What was built

`sys/usb/xhci.c` learned bulk endpoints, endpoint numbers to 15 in each direction, eight slots, and a generation on every transfer that a completion gives back, so a transfer abandoned by its owner can be told from the one in flight. `sys/usb/core.c` enumerates a mass-storage interface, opens its two bulk endpoints, hands the device to the class driver, and clears a halt on its behalf inside the one turn the command ring gives.

`sys/usb/msc.c` is the class driver: a state machine over the three transfers of every command, stepped from the pump the timer and the controller's interrupt share, with one command in flight per stick. Bring-up asks the stick what it is before it is believed, unit by unit; every way a command can end has one answer from the error matrix, with recovery in the order the specification fixes; a check condition is asked about with `REQUEST SENSE` and the sense decides the request; a transfer that never answers is failed only after recovery has taken it back from the controller, because its page is the block layer's. The stick is a block device with `submit` and `flush`, an admission queue served in order, and a slot that stays taken after the stick is pulled until nothing holds its device. The two entries from a process run with interrupts masked, because the emulator completes a transfer inside the doorbell write.

`sys/block/gpt.c` reads a GUID table, the header checked before any field is used and the entry array read whole and checked against its checksum before an entry is looked at. `sys/block/mbr.c` gained `mbr_take`, the judgement of which entries a medium can publish, a pure function the kernel and the host's image checker share. `sys/block/usbdisk.c` is the stick as a disk: the lowest free number, the table read from the pump without sleeping through `block_request_begin` and `block_request_ended`, the nodes published as block devices so `blkid` finds them, removal with a teardown count that is computed rather than kept, and the reclaim of the number, the nodes and the class driver's slot when the count reaches zero. `sys/fs/bcache.c` deals with the buffers of a device that went, `sys/fs/vfs.c` unmounts such a device without writing and flushes a stick's own cache at an unmount, a sync and an fsync through `block_flush`, and `sys/fs/devfs.c` has the ten nodes, byte-granular reads for the probers that ask for a structure's size, and one transfer at a time through the staging buffer.

`tools/image/mkstick.sh` builds the stick image, the metal hybrid ISO followed by an exFAT volume with its `PartitionOffset` set, judged whole by `tools/image/stickcheck.c` with the kernel's own rule; `tools/image/mkgpt.c` writes GUID tables for the suite; `mkexfatfixture` builds a volume for a place on a larger medium; the harness plugs a medium and a device in through the monitor and boots the emulator from its firmware.

## What running it found

A stick's table was first read at the syscall entry, where a process can sleep; a stick plugged in while the debug shell was idle waited sixty seconds for its table, because no process entered the kernel. The reading moved into the pump as a state machine over requests that are begun and asked after.

With the controller's messages on, the first large read failed at once with a refused command wrapper, every time, and never on the timer's pump: the submit ran in process context with interrupts on, and the emulator completed the wrapper inside the doorbell write, so its interrupt arrived before the stage that sent it was recorded.

BusyBox's `blkid` found no volume: the FAT32 fixture had fewer than the 65525 clusters a FAT32 volume must have, so `blkid` judged it FAT16 and read the label from the wrong field, and the exFAT prober asked for 120 bytes, which the disk windows' whole-sector rule refused and which poisoned every prober after it. An unmount of a pulled stick failed for ever, because its three writes reached a device that was not there. And nothing in the kernel called a stick's `flush` until the mutation that removes it was laid out.

Throughput, a 16 MiB read through `/dev/usb0` under QEMU: about 50 MB/s on the controller's messages, and 133 KB/s on the timer's pump alone, three ten-millisecond ticks for each page-sized request.

## What the review found

The gate review of the disk half, before the image was built, was two Claude reviewers at Opus strength reading six files each; the round at the close was two Codex units, one over the reader-facing documents and one over the suite, the probe and the image tools, and this is the first milestone since 38 in which Codex returned its report. Every finding is fixed and the ones a test can see are each proved by a mutation.

The class driver's retry path never retried and never read the sense, so a read the stick failed with a medium error was reported to the block layer as success with whatever the failed command left in the page, and a refused flush was reported as a medium made whole. A transfer that never answered had its request completed at the deadline with the controller still owning its page. Tags restarted at one on each attach while completions were matched by the controller's device handle. After a timeout the queue was not dispatched until the next command ended, so a request submitted in that window overtook the ones waiting.

In the cache, a buffer marked for discard by a device that went, and then dropped by a failed fill or reused after a writeback, kept the mark; the next user of the buffer, on a sound device, had its dirty block thrown away at its release. The raw windows' staging buffer was one buffer for everyone, which was safe while every window was over a device that polls and is not once a stick's window sleeps inside its request with the sectors in it. The disk layer's last word on its pending work could be written over by an arrival or a departure during it, and a stick pulled during bring-up was never named and so never had its slot released; two such pulls took both slots for the life of the machine. The GUID header check bounded the array's first sector against the device and not its last. The probe's hold part used a negative errno as a descriptor on one path in 128, and its volumes part checked only a signature where it said it checked the table. Codex found the block Readme still describing the table as read in a syscall, the acceptance script putting a stick's label unquoted into a remote command, the image checker pairing one entry's type with another's range, and a hold row whose pull could land in a sleep rather than in a read.

## Verification

`tests/run host` runs four new host tests and rows in five others. `msc_hosttest` drives the class driver against a model of a Bulk-Only device that can be told to stall, fail, answer wrongly or vanish at any stage: bring-up in each of its forms, every row of the error matrix, the sense answers, the flush's three answers, the admission order, the late failure after recovery, the tag across attaches, and the slot kept until released. `usbdisk_hosttest` drives the disk layer against a medium in memory: each table format, each way an entry or a table can be wrong, the numbering, and the reclaim at once for a stick nobody held and after the last hold otherwise. `gpt_hosttest` checks the CRC32 against its published value, every header rule, the whole-array checksum and every entry rule. `bcache_hosttest` gained the device that goes, in the middle of a read and of a write. `mbr_hosttest`, `block_hosttest`, `xhci_hosttest` and `usb_hosttest` gained rows for what they own.

`tests/run usbdisk` is nine boots: the MBR stick, the GUID stick with its first used entry at index six, the stick with no table, a write read back after a remount and off the image by mtools, the stick pulled from under a mount with a file open and plugged back in, ten plugs and pulls of a stick nobody opened, BusyBox mounting by label, the throughput line, and the stick image booted by the firmware with no kernel given to the emulator, where the machine mounts its own second partition. `tests/run metal` rehearses the acceptance script's stick section against a stick.

The full set runs all 65 suites in one pass on the closing tree. `make test` is 7 minutes 6 seconds against milestone 39's 5 minutes 6, the two minutes being the stick suite run alone after the pool. One flake is on record: the mount suite's lag-sleep boot failed twice in the full pool on earlier trees of this milestone and never alone or in the final run; it keeps a log now.

Every check added here was proved by a mutation that makes it fail: ten for the class driver in phase 3, eight for the naming and removal in phase 4, five for the review's fixes in phase 5, three for the image in phase 6, each failing exactly its row. Two are recorded as not caught and cannot be: the staging buffer's exclusion needs two processes asleep in the kernel, and the emulator's completion inside the doorbell write is a race the model cannot make.
