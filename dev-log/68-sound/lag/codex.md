diagnosis: The strongest explanation is queued old audio plus oversized DMA periods. The code supports this mechanism, but the idle report cannot establish the live queue depth. I checked master `2dd8dd1b7`; no files were changed.

codec: `0x000A0560` sets rate bits 5, 6, 8 and 10: 44100, 48000, 96000 and 192000 Hz. Size bits 17 and 19 allow 16 and 24 bits. `formats=1` means PCM. Doom requests 11025 Hz, unsigned 8-bit mono. The driver selects the lowest supported rate, 44100 Hz, and converts to signed 16-bit stereo, format `0x4011`. Its resampling step is exactly `11025*65536/44100=16384`, so this conversion causes no rounding drift. Evidence: [Intel specification, 7.3.4.7](https://www.intel.com/content/dam/www/public/us/en/documents/product-specifications/high-definition-audio-specification.pdf), [hda.c:1918](rach2/sys/audio/hda.c:1918), [hda.c:2412](rach2/sys/audio/hda.c:2412).

1. Doom/HDA: The largest possible contribution is accumulated source audio. Doom generates samples for elapsed wall time, capped at `5040/11025=457.14 ms` per update, without checking queued duration. After an underrun, this can submit past audio after hardware has already played silence. The resulting offset persists when production and consumption balance. HDA holds up to `8191/11025=742.95 ms` of input. That is capacity, not proof of occupancy. One maximum catch-up burst plus DMA accounts for about 643 ms; a nearly full ring requires further accumulation, such as missed refills or clock error. Evidence: [i_pcsound.c:309](ports/doom/i_pcsound.c:309), [hda.c:117](rach2/sys/audio/hda.c:117), [hda.c:395](rach2/sys/audio/hda.c:395).

2. HDA: Each period lasts `16384/(44100*4)=92.88 ms`; both halves span 185.76 ms. Writes only append to the ring. Service waits for a period transition, then fills the other half. Even with no older ring data, a new sample ordinarily waits roughly 93-186 ms. With nearly 743 ms queued, the total approaches 836-929 ms, before input and computation. The comment's 85 ms assumes 48000 Hz, which this request does not select. Evidence: [hda.c:111](rach2/sys/audio/hda.c:111), [hda.c:484](rach2/sys/audio/hda.c:484), [hda.c:2749](rach2/sys/audio/hda.c:2749).

keyboard/engine: PS/2 input goes directly from IRQ to the input layer; Doom reads raw scan codes without blocking. The 35 Hz game clock normally adds up to 28.57 ms before both effects. The pistol calls `S_StartSound` before setting its flash; `S_UpdateSounds` calls `I_UpdateSound` before display. There is no separate one-second sound timer. Cold sound loading and mixing add computation time. Evidence: [ps2kbd.c:155](rach2/sys/arch/x86/ps2kbd.c:155), [doomgeneric_rach2.c:236](ports/doom/doomgeneric_rach2.c:236), [p_pspr.c:660](ports/doom/src/p_pspr.c:660), [d_main.c:410](ports/doom/src/d_main.c:410), [s_sound.c:513](ports/doom/src/s_sound.c:513).

clock/service: Doom uses millisecond-truncated `gettimeofday`, backed by sub-tick interpolation. Wall-clock adjustments and drift remain possible contributors. MSI and the 100 Hz tick call the same period-gated service; increasing tick frequency alone leaves buffering unchanged. Missing a complete DMA revolution can escape wrap detection and replay old samples. The 20 ms position check happens only at first stream startup. Evidence: [doomgeneric_rach2.c:524](ports/doom/doomgeneric_rach2.c:524), [clock.c:231](rach2/sys/clock.c:231), [kernel.c:300](rach2/kernel.c:300), [hda.c:512](rach2/sys/audio/hda.c:512).

SB16: Doom gets 128-frame periods, `128/11025=11.61 ms`, and a 1023-byte usable ring, 92.79 ms. Its corresponding maximum is about 116 ms, before QEMU's host buffering. Evidence: [sb16.c:503](rach2/sys/audio/sb16.c:503).

fix: Change both sides. Make HDA periods depend on duration and actual format. At 44100 Hz, use 4096-byte halves: 23.22 ms. Have Doom maintain about 90 ms total queued duration, discard expired catch-up audio, and use a monotonic clock. This targets about 90 ms from sound trigger, roughly 120 ms from key press. A period covers two 10 ms tick intervals plus 3.22 ms for service; `90-2*23.22-28.57=14.99 ms` remains for producer jitter. Verify those margins on the Lenovo. With 1000 Hz service, 1024-byte halves give 5.80 ms; a 45 ms queue target leaves similar room for one game tic. Arbitrarily long stalls still require recovery.

interface: Add `AUDIO_GET_DELAY`, backed by a driver operation, returning delay in requested-rate frames. Include resampler state, unplayed DMA and known FIFO latency, not merely ring occupancy. Return writable space separately. This follows [ALSA delay semantics](https://www.alsa-project.org/alsa-doc/alsa-lib/group___p_c_m.html). The current interface has neither operation: [audio.h:39](rach2/sys/audio/audio.h:39).

measurement: Before and after, collect:

* Running `/proc/audio`, ring occupancy, accepted bytes, underruns, DMA fills and MSI counts. Existing counters are in [hda.c:2241](rach2/sys/audio/hda.c:2241).
* In-memory timestamps for Ctrl receipt, sound activation, first shot sample submission and its mapped DMA frame. Extend LPIB across wraps; compare progress against HDA's 24 MHz wall clock.
* Maximum service and producer gaps, queue-delay percentiles, silence and replay counts. Compare Doom elapsed time against HDA time to detect drift.

measurement: Stock counters cannot identify an individual shot. Proposed tracing would measure key-to-link delay; measuring the remaining codec-to-analog delay requires synchronized electrical capture, counted in samples.
