# 84-176: the GPU's clock under WineD3D's GL round trip

84-163 found the GL loop's whole gap to Linux in the GPU's clock: Rach2's GPU stayed at 200 MHz, RPn, through its loops of 302.2 to 319.7 ms, where Linux's ran at a median 450 and 500 MHz in loops of 233.7 and 253.7 ms, and at 200 MHz took 360.6 ms. This task finds why Rach2's RPS does not raise the clock and makes it follow Linux 6.12.115's.

## The cause

Neither system raises the clock in this loop by RPS's up interrupt; Linux holds it at RPe through the GT's parks, and Rach2's GT, which parked only once a second, did not park in the loop.

Step 1 read both systems on the Lenovo on 2026-11-13, under `trip.sh -n 2 -t wined3d_cs` with the clock sampled every 20 ms. Linux, Debian 13.7 on p5, kprobes on RPS's work, interrupt handler, requests, boosts and the GT's and engines' parks:

| Loop | ms | act MHz median (range) | up / down / timeout | GT parks | waitboosts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| a, cold | 233.7 | 450 (200 to 500) | 0 / 6 / 0 | 6 | 0 |
| a, warm | 325.1 | 200 (200 to 250) | 0 / 1 / 0 | 4 | 0 |
| b, cold | 237.4 | 500 (200 to 900) | 0 / 6 / 0 | 7 | 0 |
| b, warm | 233.7 | 500 (250 to 850) | 0 / 7 / 0 | 9 | 0 |

The setup's waits boost Linux's GPU to 1000 MHz before each run's first loop; inside a loop there is no waitboost and no up event, and the down events step the request down. The GT parks every 12 to 30 ms and unparks 0.05 to 0.2 ms later, and each park, `intel_rps_park`, starts the next unpark a step lower but never below RPe, 500 MHz, so a loop that starts above RPn stays at 450 to 500 MHz. Once a down step reaches RPn with the GT awake, `intel_rps_park` returns early and nothing raises it: loop a-warm, 325.1 ms at 200 MHz, is that case. Linux read RPn 200, RP1 200, RPe 500 and RP0 1000 MHz.

Rach2, a trial kernel with this task's counters on 84-58's trial root:

| Loop | ms | act MHz median (max) | i915 interrupts |
|---|---|---|---|
| a, first | 302.6 | 200 (250) | 1,004 |
| a, second | 295.5 | 200 (650) | 1,005 |
| b, with i915_frequency_info every 3rd sample | 319.3 | 200 (200) | 1,004 |

From the boot to after run a and then run b: waitboosts 4 and 6; RPS's work ran 20 and 32 times, up 0, down 16 and 26, timeout 0; RPS parked 28 and 51 times, about one a second; retire passes that found the GT awake with every engine idle, 4,310 and 6,575, about one a trip. Rach2 read the same RPn, RP1, RPe and RP0; RPNSWREQ and CAGF both read 200 MHz. So the down events walked Rach2's request from the setup's boost to RPn, and with no park in the loop nothing lifted it.

The issue's other candidates, read against Linux:

* A waitboost on the loop's wait. WineD3D's command thread waits in SYNCOBJ_WAIT, which on Linux adds a callback to the request's fence and never calls the fence's `wait`; Linux's i915 has no `set_deadline`, so no boost reaches it (`drm_syncobj.c`, `i915_request.c`), and Linux counted none in the loops. Rach2's SYNCOBJ_WAIT does the same. Not the cause.
* The unpark's GEN6_RPNSWREQ without forcewake. Linux writes it with `intel_uncore_write_fw` through `set()` (`gt/intel_rps.c:70-73, 812`), with no forcewake and outside the GT FIFO's count, as Rach2 does. Not the cause. Linux writes GEN6_PMINTRMSK in `rps_enable_interrupts` and `rps_disable_interrupts` with `intel_uncore_write`, through the FIFO's count, where Rach2 wrote it directly; it now writes it through `i915_uncore_write`.
* The limits, the interrupt mask, and efficient_freq read as RPn: Rach2's limits and mask follow `rps_limits` and `rps_pm_mask`, and it read RPe 500 MHz as Linux did. Not the cause.

## How often Linux parks

Linux's engine parks when its wakeref's last reference goes, and the engine's last reference in this loop is the user context's. Each call pins the context, and its pin takes the engine's wakeref for the context's idle barrier (`i915_active_acquire_preallocate_barrier`, `i915_active.c:922`); the last request's retire unpins it, and the unpin puts that reference with `intel_engine_pm_put_delay(engine, 2)` (`i915_active.c:983`), which, as the last, queues the put two jiffies on. At Debian's HZ of 250 that is 4 to 8 ms. If the engine is busy when the put runs, it only drops its count, and the next idle unpin queues another. When it finds the engine idle, `__engine_park` switches the engine to its kernel context, a request whose signal is asked for, and returns busy (`gt/intel_engine_pm.c:151-235, 258-288`); the switch's retire puts the wakeref at once, and the engine, now in its kernel context, parks, and with it the GT. So under a client that calls every quarter millisecond, Linux tries to park about every 8 to 16 ms and parks when the switch retires before the next call: Linux's 6 to 9 GT parks in 233 ms loops fit that.

Rach2 had modelled Linux's retire and its listened signal, but not the delayed put or the switch: since 84-157 its GT parked only at the retire worker's once-a-second pass.

## The fix

The retire worker now parks the GT as Linux's engines park (`retire_park` and `gt_park_idle` in `rach2/sys/drm/i915/i915_request.c`). The worker's pass that first finds every engine idle sets the GT's `park_next` two ticks on, Rach2's tick being 4 ms as Debian's jiffy is; the worker wakes for it as for a heartbeat. The pass at that tick, with every engine still idle, puts a request on the GT's own context on each engine whose `serial`, its count of requests, has passed its `wakeref_serial`, the count its last switch left, and asks for that request's signal; the pass that retires the switches finds every engine in the GT's own context and parks the GT at once. A pass that finds an engine busy once the delay is over ends the delay, and the next idle pass sets another. A wait for the GT's idle still parks it at once. `i915_engine_info` counts the switches, `GT park switches`, beside the idle passes.

`i915_rps_boost_info` gives, past Linux's lines, RPS's work runs with its up, down and timeout events, its parks and unparks, and GEN6_RPNSWREQ against RPSTAT1's CAGF, so that one read every few samples gives the counters and both clocks.

The exec host test's new row `park_after_delay` runs this on the chip model: a call with a listener on its fence retires at its signal and leaves the GT awake with its park two ticks on; at that tick the pass switches each engine to the GT's own context; the pass that retires the switches parks the GT. Run alone with its test file on this task's head, the whole exec test passes, `park_per_call` still counting no park between eight waited calls. The context test's `row_park_unwatched` now expects the park two ticks after the worker's whole second and a switch; it was compiled, not run, as the milestone's rules have it.
