# 5259: virtio-gpu buffers backed by the bed VM's memory

Over the shared region, every buffer's backing came from the region's heap of 8189 pages, 32 MiB, so a program that keeps more than that alive between two submissions had its buffers refused. With `VGS_TF_BACKING` the device backs each 2D and 3D resource with memory of its own VM: `VGS_CMD_BACKING` follows the create, the backend makes a memfd of the resource's size, pins it through the module and attaches it to virglrenderer as one iovec, and Rach2 maps the pin's VM-physical pages through KVM, as it maps a blob's (`kvm_gpamap_new`), and takes the frames as the object's pages. A buffer is then bounded by the VM's memory, as a Linux guest's is by its RAM.

## Design

* The transport offers `VGS_TF_BACKING` only with `VGS_TF_PAGES`. A device that refuses a backing, past the module's 1 GiB pin or short of memory, answers with an error, and the driver attaches pages of the heap as before (`obj_back`). The wait's error answer comes back as `-EREMOTEIO` (`ask_error`), so that a refusal is told from the transport's own errors.
* A buffer made under the mode lock, as the console's are, takes the heap's pages: a reap that another thread began may hold the backing's answer while it sleeps on that lock (`vm_backing`).
* A VM-backed object lets go of its frames before its UNREF. The VM may give a page it unpinned to the next backing at once, and `paging_user_ram_frames` refuses a private frame with more than one reference. Before this, piglit's tests in QEMU had a backing refused with EFAULT every few seconds, each page present and writable (`kvm: gpamap: gfn ...: refused: entry ...` gives the entry and region now).
* The backend refuses a backing that would leave the VM less available memory than an eighth of its own (`mem_room`). Without it, a guest that asked for more than the VM had stalled the VM, and every answer with it, during the pin's reclaim: the program sat in `virtio_gpu_wait_answer` and ivshmemd's counts stood still. A resource's own storage is not judged so, since llvmpipe keeps the textures it frees in the C library's heap and the counts would refuse the next one; a guard there made every create after streaming-texture-leak fail, the death row's among them.
* Frames of a backing taken back while the device lives stop the device, every pending fence failing, before the unplug (`backing_lost`), as the transport's word that the device has gone does. The VM's end takes the backings back before that word: in vm-death's first run the log has `resource 7: the VM's pages behind its backing changed; reset` 8 ms before `vgpu-shm: detached: epoch 2, the device's VM died` and no `the device has gone`, since `vgpu_removed` finds the device unplugged and so never fails the fences; Weston, glmark2 and the probe then sat in state D in their close of the card, waiting for the mode lock (`drm_mode_file_release`), as the hung-task reports from 240 s on show. The holder was not reported; the display's shutdown in the unplug takes that lock and waits on commits, as `vgpu_removed`'s comment says. With the device stopped first, vm-death's run has the same two lines 8 ms apart and, 10 s after the death, no process of the old session left: only the headless Weston of the fallback runs.
* bed-shm's death rows now make the probe's render target before they stop the VM, since its backing is the VM's to give, and the probe stops itself until the script has stopped QEMU.

## Measurement

machine.bed-shm.virgl in QEMU on the testing PC, host-svm under KVM, Rach2 with 6 GiB, the bed VM with 2 GiB (`bed-shm.sh`'s default):

    bed-shm: readback: pass: fbo-readpixels pass, glsl-fs-loop pass, fbo-generatemipmap pass; 0 refused exports, 0 refused page lists, 0 refused backings, 0 refused heap pages, 20 maps of the VM's pages
    bed-shm: death: pass: error -19
    bed-shm: result: pass

piglit's streaming-texture-leak makes 5000 textures of 1024 by 1024 RGBA, 4 MiB each, uploads each through a staging buffer of 4 MiB and deletes it, with no flush in the loop. Mesa 25.0.7's virgl holds each staging buffer and each texture until its command buffer fills, so the VM holds both. Live backings, from ivshmemd's `map_pages` less those let go, sampled every 20 to 45 s:

| VM | Rach2 | Peak of live backings | End |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 GiB | 6 GiB | 220,633 pages, 862 MiB | the VM stalled, before `mem_room` |
| 4 GiB | 6 GiB | 463,559 pages, 1.77 GiB | the VM stalled, before `mem_room` |
| 12 GiB | 14 GiB | 1,272,284 pages, 4.85 GiB, then a flush | backings refused, the program went on |
| 14 GiB | 16 GiB | 1,165,683 pages, 4.45 GiB, then a flush | 3D creates refused at 3.23 GiB of backings, the program crashed in Mesa |

At 14 GiB the VM's own texture memory under llvmpipe filled what the backings left. Linux's reference passes the test in a guest of 8 GiB with the textures in the host GPU's memory. On the Lenovo the VM's textures are i915's, in the VM's memory too, so the VM would need some 10 GiB of the Lenovo's 16 GiB. The owner chose 8 GiB for native `bed/shm`. The test stays out of the bed's piglit scene and the readback row.

The evidence is in `build/runs/task-5259` of toksaitov-a's clone on the testing PC: `virgl-*.out` and `rest.out` for each run, `watch-vm*.txt` for each size's samples, and `vm.disk`, SHA-256 3ac5c86a15f3afa7e54ce32ab864feded60d081c8514ac4ede72c1cee3920b8d, the disk this tree builds.
