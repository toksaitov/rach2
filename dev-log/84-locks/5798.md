# 5798: wineboot's cold read of Proton, forced with the guest's disk throttled

Card 5787 found that GE-Proton's `wineboot.exe -u` stalls for ever when the first prefix after boot reads its disk very slowly, on Rach2 and on Linux alike ([5787.md](5787.md)). This card forced the stall at the head of milestone/76-display (f6b8c1e5a8, kernel unchanged since 19a1eb6354) by slowing only the guest's reads, from QEMU, through a set of rates, and asked whether a disk held to one read a second at the start and then given full speed, as a host disk that is saturated for a while is, stalls it too. Ten boots of that made the prefix, and so did the six of the main run. A disk still slow at about 492 MB of reads from boot, where the reads of the stalls stopped, stalled it every time: 18 of 18 boots at 2 MB/s, 500 KB/s and 50 iops. Each stall has Wine's "The explorer process failed to start" and every processor idle. The futex words were read in two of the 18, boots 2 and 3, and held the value their waiters expect, as the issue's comments record; what happened before those snapshots, a wake sent or lost, is unresolved. Card 5804 saw the same log line in 10 runs of 10 on plain Debian at 2 MB/s under the same throttle, and the same waiters in the run it captured whole ([5804.md](5804.md)), and on that symptom alone the stall at 2 MB/s is recorded as Proton's. At 50 iops Debian made the prefix in 10 runs of 10, and why the two systems part there is not known (Linux, below). No code changed.

## The runs

`drive.py` ([scripts/](5798/scripts/)) ran on the testing host on 2026-11-24, from 14:18 to 21:36 UTC+6, as one `etude admit -c` claim, `t5798-s-force-1795508285`, of 4 processors and 9 GiB (`cpu_millis 4000`, `memory_bytes 9663676416`), taken before its first QEMU and held until its last ended. Each boot is the games-wine machine: KVM, 4 processors, 8 GiB, virgl, an overlay of the clone's `gnu-test.disk` on virtio-blk as `d0` and a payload disk with the head's `run.py` and `one.py` as `d1`.

One fresh boot made each prefix. Rach2 has no way to drop its page cache, so only the first prefix after a boot reads Proton from the disk. Root logged in on the serial console, mounted the payload and started `one.py` as the desktop account, which started Weston as `run.py` does. With Weston up, the host set QMP's `block_set_io_throttle` on `d0` (reads only, every other limit 0) and root made the go file; `one.py` then made the prefix as `make_prefix` does. A slow step kept the disk slow until the prefix ended or 900 s passed, the one-IOPS step (`pause60` in `drive.py`) for 60 s from the go; then every limit went to 0. A stall was no end of the prefix within 120 s after the disk was back at full speed.

The steps ran round-robin, one boot of each in turn: 60 s at one read a second (`iops_rd` 1), 2 MB/s (`bps_rd` 2000000), 50 iops, 500 KB/s, 5 iops.

| step | boots | stalled | the prefix's end | reads where a stall stopped |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60 s at 1 iops | 6 | 0 | 68.3 to 78.2 s after the go; see The one-IOPS start | |
| 2 MB/s | 6 | 6 | none | 9,165 to 9,176 requests, 492 MB |
| 50 iops | 6 | 6 | none | 9,182 to 9,193 requests, 492 to 493 MB |
| 500 KB/s | 6 | 6 | none | 9,161 to 9,179 requests, 491 to 492 MB |
| 5 iops | 6 | 0 | 908.2 to 914.0 s | |

Reads are the system disk's, from QEMU's `query-blockstats`, counted from boot; a made prefix read 14,280 to 14,351 requests, 846 to 848 MB, after the go. Before the run, a smoke run of two boots made a cold prefix at full speed in 17.3 s from 14,343 requests, 847 MB, and one under the 60 s cap of one read a second in 76.8 s ([evidence/smoke-drive.log](5798/evidence/smoke-drive.log), whose `failed` is the smoke driver's misreading of the guest's `ONE END status 0 made True` line, fixed before the main run). Each boot's line is in [evidence/drive.log](5798/evidence/drive.log), and each stall's in [evidence/stalls.txt](5798/evidence/stalls.txt).

## The one-IOPS start

The main run's go was `touch /tmp/one-go` typed at the guest's root shell. Under the cap the shell first waited for `touch` itself to load, a read a second, so wineboot started 11 to 49 s into the 60 s and was held for only the rest of it: the guest's time from the go to the prefix's end against the host's shows by how much. The other steps barely felt this, since `touch` loads in well under a second at each of their rates.

A second run under its own claim, `t5798-s-pause-1795522121`, made the go with the shell's builtin (`: > /tmp/one-go`), the one change from `drive.py`, and ran ten boots with one read a second for the first 60 s ([evidence/pause-drive.log](5798/evidence/pause-drive.log)). All ten made the prefix in 68.2 to 68.5 s from the go, 8 s after the throttle came off; the guest's and the host's times agreed to 0.1 s. The cap is not a stop: the disk kept reading. The log has no count at the go, so the first 30 s are not measured; between its sample at 30 s and the one `drive.py` takes just after it removes the throttle at 60 s the disk took 33 to 38 reads. That second sample is at 118.2 to 118.9 MB from boot, short of the 492 MB where the stalls stopped. The log does not show when explorer.exe started, so whether it started under the cap or at full speed is open.

So in this experiment a prefix whose reads ran at one a second for the first 60 s, and at full speed from there, did not stall. A disk that stops every read was not run. The boots that stalled were still capped when their reads stopped. At 5 iops the prefix had read only some 367 MB by the 900 s cap, short of that point, and made the prefix at full speed after.

## The stall

The driver took each stall's captures while it stood: `info registers -a` three times 5 s apart, the kernel's image, data and bss by `pmemsave`, each processor's stack, QEMU's view of each virtio queue and the block statistics, and, by the guest's root shell, every thread's state, wait channel and system call (`dump.sh`). Boot 2's are in [evidence/b2/](5798/evidence/b2/), without the binary image and stacks; the others' are not in the tree, and their directories are no longer on the testing host. In every stall:

* All four processors halted in the idle loop with interrupts on. The big lock's word, claim and lent word were 0, and each processor's lock and interrupt depths 0.
* The virtio-blk driver's queues had every slot free and nothing unkicked. QEMU agreed: on every queue `last_avail`, `shadow_avail`, `used` and `signalled_used` were equal and `inuse` was 0, so every request was answered and its completion signalled. The disk's reads had stopped while the throttle still allowed more.
* All threads slept, none in a disk wait. wineboot.exe waited in `futex_waitv` on 2 words with no timeout, conhost.exe and rundll32.exe on 1 each; services, rpcss, plugplay, winedevice, svchost and lsass waited too, wineserver in `epoll_pwait2`. No explorer.exe ran.
* Wine's log of the prefix (`WINEDEBUG=err+all`) had `nodrv_CreateWindow Application tried to create a window, but no driver could be loaded` and `The explorer process failed to start.` The watcher (`watcher.py`) read it in each stall's guest and released the guest when the line was there; it was there in all 18.

In boots 2 and 3 the guests were held and read further. What was read is in the comments of [issue 5798](https://github.com/toksaitov/rach2/issues/5798) of 11-24, 14:45 to 15:11 UTC+6; the word dumps and the page walk were not kept, and neither the tree nor the testing host has them. Every futex and `futex_waitv` word, 75 in boot 2 and 76 in boot 3, read through `/proc/PID/mem`, held the value its waiter expected, 0 in every Wine object. Boot 2's 75 is also the count in its waiter listing ([evidence/b2/console-after](5798/evidence/b2/console-after)): 70 words in `futex_waitv` and 5 in `futex`. The rest was read in boot 3 alone, since boot 2's guest was released before its pages were walked. The words wineboot.exe, conhost.exe and rundll32.exe waited on lay in `/dev/shm/wine-b2-fsync`, at file offsets 0x26a0, 0x26b0, 0x26d0 and 0x2750. A walk of each process's page table through QMP (`walk.py`; Rach2 has no `/proc/PID/pagemap`) mapped those offsets to one physical page, 0x3f08b000, in the three waiters and in wineserver, which sets and wakes them; each word read 0 there too. In these two snapshots, then, the words held the values the waiters expect, and in boot 3's waiters and waker shared one page. A snapshot shows the values at its capture: it cannot show whether a wake was issued or delivered before it, and the words of the other 16 stalls were not read. The wake-up sequence is unresolved until it is traced.

## Linux

One prefix in the Debian reference guest (Debian 13, Linux 6.12.115, KVM, 4 processors, 8 GiB, virgl), with the same Proton and `one.py`, its page cache dropped and its reads held at 2 MB/s by a cgroup's `io.max` (`linux.sh`), stalled the same way: reads stopped 180 s in, at 527 MB counted from boot, with the same waiters, no explorer.exe and the same log line, and more of Wine's timeouts besides. Its lift of the limit at 900 s was refused, so it stands as one reading, and its raw output is not in the tree. Card 5804 ran the twenty with QMP's throttle: 10 of 10 stalled at 2 MB/s and 10 of 10 made it at 50 iops.

The two systems part at 50 iops, and why is not known: Rach2 stalled in 6 boots of 6 there, Debian in 0 runs of 10. The six are boots 3, 8, 13, 18, 23 and 28. At full speed Rach2 read the cold prefix in requests of about 59 KB (14,343 for 847 MB) and Debian in requests of about 86 KB (9,398 for 806 MB, card 5804's smoke run, [smoke.out](5804/evidence/smoke.out)), which at 50 requests a second are nominal rates of 2.95 MB/s and 4.29 MB/s. An estimate of Debian's average under the cap is 4.57 to 4.63 MB/s in runs 11 to 20: the total of the byte counter, which the driver read after it lifted the throttle, over the time to the lift ([evidence/results.jsonl](5804/evidence/results.jsonl)). Rach2's boots made no prefix under it, so they have no such estimate. The larger requests may account for the difference, since both systems stall at 2 MB/s, but neither system ran at the other's byte rate, so the difference stands unexplained and is not recorded as Proton's.

## Change

None in Rach2. The stall at 2 MB/s is recorded as Proton's because Debian shows the same symptom at that rate, and on no other ground; those at 50 iops and 500 KB/s are not shown to be, since Debian made the prefix at 50 iops and was not run at 500 KB/s. The fault is recorded in [upstream.md](upstream.md#ge-protons-wineboot-waits-for-ever-when-its-desktop-starts-too-slowly).
