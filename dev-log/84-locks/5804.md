# 5804: the disk-throttled cold wineboot on plain Debian, 10 runs at 2 MB/s and 10 at 50 iops

Card 5798 forced the stall of `make_prefix`'s cold `wineboot.exe -u` on Rach2 by throttling the guest's system disk with QMP's `block_set_io_throttle`. This card ran the same load on plain Debian, with the same throttle on Debian's own QEMU, to see whether Linux stalls too. It does: at 2 MB/s Debian stalled in 10 runs of 10, with the log lines of Rach2's stalls, in run 1 the waiters of Rach2's boots 2 and 3, and every futex word read at its expected value, as in the snapshots of those two boots. At 50 iops it made the prefix in 10 runs of 10, but Linux's larger requests give it nominally 4.29 MB/s under that cap against Rach2's 2.95 (Requests under an iops cap), so that step does not compare the same rate, and why Rach2 stalls there and Debian does not is unexplained. This agrees with card 5787's runs under a cgroup's `io.max` ([5787.md](5787.md)).

## The runs

The driver ([scripts/](5804/scripts/)) ran on the testing host on 2026-11-24 from 15:11 to 18:43 UTC+6, as one `etude admit -c` claim of 4 processors and 9 GiB. Each run booted a fresh qcow2 overlay of the Debian 13 reference guest (`tests/graphics/debian-ref`, Linux 6.12.115), under KVM with 4 processors, 8 GiB and the virgl profile. The overlay's base had `/opt/proton` copied byte for byte from the GNU image (GE-Proton11-12) and `rach2-game` from the same image. `one.py` started Weston headless on GL with Xwayland, as `tests/system/games/run.py` does. Once Weston was up, the host set the read throttle on the system disk, and the guest ran `rach2-game --id 2733 --prefix FRESH -- cmd.exe /c exit 0` with `WINEDEBUG=-all,err+all`. The throttle went to 0 when the prefix ended, or at 900 s. A stall was no end within 120 s after that.

| step | runs | stalled | prefix's end | reads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| none (smoke) | 1 | 0 | 8.1 s | 9,398 requests, 806 MB |
| `bps_rd` 2000000 | 10 | 10 | none | stopped at 4,043 to 9,582 requests, 335 to 474 MB |
| `iops_rd` 50 | 10 | 0 | 171.8 to 174.4 s | 9,352 to 9,374 requests, 804 to 805 MB |

Each run's row is in [evidence/table.md](5804/evidence/table.md), and the driver's record in [evidence/results.jsonl](5804/evidence/results.jsonl).

## The stall

`one.py` looked for explorer.exe, and `drive.py` read the disk's counts, every 10 s. In run 1 explorer.exe was there at the samples of 210 and 220 s and gone at 230 s; the reads went on to 320 s, 7,162 requests at 230 s and 9,582 at 320 s, and then did not change until the throttle was lifted at 900 s, when the count rose by three to 9,585. The prefix had not finished 120 s later ([evidence/run1/one.log](5804/evidence/run1/one.log), [evidence/run1/blk.log](5804/evidence/run1/blk.log)).

The other nine runs' `one.log` and `blk.log` are in toksaitov-m's clone on the testing host, in `build/runs/t5804/series`; [evidence/table.md](5804/evidence/table.md) has their figures. Explorer.exe was there in one sample, 160 s, in runs 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 and 9, at 150 and 160 s in run 10, and at 210 and 220 s in run 7, and in the next sample of each it was gone. Under the throttle the disk's count last rose at 170 s in runs 2, 4, 5, 8 and 9, the first sample without explorer.exe, at 360 s in run 6, at 390 s in run 10, at 790 s in run 3 and at 860 s in run 7. At the first sample after the throttle was lifted, 900 s, the count had risen by 118 to 120 in runs 2 to 10, and run 10's went on, by 72 more to 990 s and 3,677 more at 1,000 s. In none of the ten did lifting the throttle complete the prefix: 120 s later each was still a stall.

In every run Wine's log has `nodrv_CreateWindow Application tried to create a window, but no driver could be loaded.` and `The explorer process failed to start.` (`nodrv` and `failmsg` in [evidence/results.jsonl](5804/evidence/results.jsonl)). Run 1's captures are in [evidence/run1/](5804/evidence/run1/):

* QMP's `info registers -a`, taken three times 5 s apart: the four processors halted in the idle loop, or briefly out of it.
* All 58 threads of the account's processes slept. wineboot.exe waited in `futex_waitv` on 2 words with no timeout, conhost.exe and `rundll32.exe setupapi,InstallHinfSection PreInstall` on 1 word each. services, winedevice, svchost, lsass and rpcss waited in `futex_waitv` too, and wineserver in `epoll_wait`. Rach2's boots 2 and 3 on card 5798 had the same set of waiters.
* Each word held the value its `struct futex_waitv` entry expects, 0. The three processes' words mapped to one physical page, the fsync file in `/dev/shm`. In each of the 10 stalls, all 66 or 67 words the account's threads waited on held their expected value.
* The sixth value of conhost's and rundll32's `/proc/PID/syscall` line, `0x1dd7be304d76300`, is a register that `futex_waitv` does not read; the timeout pointer, its fourth argument, is 0.

## Requests under an iops cap

At full speed, Debian reads the cold prefix in 9,398 requests of about 86 KB each, 806 MB, and Rach2 in 14,343 requests of about 59 KB each (loop s's smoke run on card 5798, 847 MB). Those sizes at 50 requests a second are nominal rates of 4.29 MB/s for Debian and 2.95 MB/s for Rach2. Debian's measured average, the bytes of runs 11 to 20 over the time to the throttle's removal, is 4.57 to 4.63 MB/s: run 11 read 804 MB in 176.0 s. Rach2 made no prefix under that cap, so it has no such average. Under it explorer.exe on Debian came up at about 71 s and stayed. Rach2 stalled in 6 boots of 6 at that step, boots 3, 8, 13, 18, 23 and 28 on card 5798, and Debian in none of 10. The difference is unexplained. That the lower byte rate causes it is a reading, not a finding: a cap of about 34 iops would give Debian Rach2's nominal byte rate, and it was not run.
