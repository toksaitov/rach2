# 84-264x: callsmusl's itimer-first-tick under host load

`guest.default.callsmusl`'s row `itimer-first-tick` failed under host load because the handler's reading of the clock came after ticks charged past the timer's expiry, not because the kernel expired the timer at the wrong tick. The row now judges the kernel's two counts that the expiry fixes: the handler's reading shows at least the fourth tick, and each reading taken while no signal had come shows fewer.

## Load

The run, QEMU's threads with it, is pinned to one core of the testing PC beside busy loops of the same priority on that core, so the guest's processor runs in slices of a few milliseconds with gaps of several ticks between them, and each late tick charges the ticks it missed (`sched_account_tick`):

```
core=30
for i in 1 2 3 4 5 6; do taskset -c $core sh -c 'while :; do :; done' & done
RACH2_CASES=guest.default.callsmusl ARCH=i686 taskset -c $core tests/run default
```

With six busy loops, the row as it was failed every run, five of five on each architecture under TCG, at 6, 13, 12, 6 and 8 ticks against 4 on i686 and 7, 9, 9, 7 and 7 on x86-64, as 84-264e and 84-264g saw it fail at host load averages of 47 to 56 and above 11. Without the loops a run passes.

## Trace

A scratch build, not committed, ran the row's code 200 times, alternating ITIMER_PROF and ITIMER_VIRTUAL, and printed for each the ticks of the handler's reading and of the last reading taken before the signal; the kernel logged, at each expiry, the number of ticks the interrupt charged and the timer's place among them. With two busy loops, 13 of the 200 failed the old bound of 4 or 5 ticks, and none of 200 without load. In every try, failed or passed, the last reading before the signal showed at most 3 ticks and the handler's at least 4: the timer expired at the fourth tick charged each time. In 10 of the 13 the timer expired inside a batch of 3 to 5 ticks, whose rest the handler then counted. In the other 3 the batch held 1 or 2 ticks, and 3 or 4 more were charged after the signal was posted and before the handler read the time; the trace's own console line was written in that window and lengthened it.

## Linux

Linux's handler would read past the tick under the same load as well. Its interval timers count one tick for each tick interrupt, which skips the ticks a late one missed (`hrtimer_forward` in kernel/time/tick-sched.c:309, `account_process_tick` in kernel/sched/cputime.c:486-497, the samples of kernel/time/posix-cpu-timers.c:216-217), while its CLOCK_PROCESS_CPUTIME_ID is the scheduler's runtime in nanoseconds (posix-cpu-timers.c:218 and 1658), which grows by the whole of a stall of the virtual processor. So the kernel is not changed; the row is.

## Proof

The fix waits for Codex's review, which runs only in the owner's accounts; then the same load runs the row five times on each architecture.
