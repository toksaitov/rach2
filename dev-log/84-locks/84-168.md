# 84-168: epoll's ready list

An epoll wait that slept scanned every registration twice with interrupts off and could spin for the big lock inside the scan; on the Lenovo 58 of epoll_pwait2's 63 profile samples in 84-151's first loop fell as interrupts came back on after that scan. Now epoll keeps Linux's ready list (fs/eventpoll.c, `ep_poll_callback`, `ep_poll`, `ep_send_events`): each registration has a record on the queue of what it watches, whose wake function puts it on its set's list, and a wait polls only the registrations on that list, with interrupts on, under the set's mutex. [The io Readme](../../rach2/sys/io/Readme.md) states the design; this file holds the audit and the measurements.

## What changed

* Wait queues. A record with a wake function, `func`, stays on its queue through every wake, which calls it with the kinds of change; the plain records go first, then the exclusive ones in turn until one returns nonzero, as `__wake_up_common` (kernel/sched/wait.c:73-97). `wq_entry_add` and `wq_entry_remove` file and take such a record; `wq_wake_free`, Linux's `wake_up_pollfree`, tells each record that its queue is about to be freed, which a thread group's signalfd queue now does. `wq_wake_first`, the mutex's hand-off, passes over records with a function.
* epoll. A set has a mutex, `mtx`, over its registrations, and a spinlock, `lock`, over the ready list, as Linux's `ep->mtx` and `ep->lock`; `ep_nest_mtx` is `epnested_mutex`. A registration polls its target through a copy of the descriptor it was added by, so it reports through its open file whatever became of the number, as Linux's does; the release of the description's last descriptor removes it first (`epoll_release_file`, Linux's `eventpoll_release_file`), called from `fd_release_objects`. A wake while a wait has the registration on its batch marks it, and the batch's end puts it back, which is Linux's `ovflist`. EPOLLEXCLUSIVE is an exclusive record on the target's queue with Linux's `ewake`, and EPOLL_CTL_MOD with it, a set and an event outside `EPOLLEXCLUSIVE_OK_BITS` are EINVAL.
* poll and select. `poll_wait` is Linux's `do_poll`: a round clears `poll_woken` (Linux's `triggered`) by an exchange and scans with interrupts on; the records, filed after the first scan that finds nothing, stay on their queues until the wait ends; a sleep happens only while `poll_woken` is clear, as `poll_schedule_timeout`. `poll_wait_queue` is the same wait on one queue, for epoll_wait, with one exclusive record.
* Gone: the close's letting go of epoll waits (`epoll_waits_let_go`, `poll_let_go`, `poll_quiet`, `poll_lock`, `fd_holds_release_closing`, `fd_close_finish`), the registration's description serial and `fd_ofd_live`, and `epoll_poll_set`. An epoll wait now holds only its set's descriptor.

## The shared-structure audit

Every reader and writer of a registration's fields and of a set's ready list, and the lock that orders each:

| Field | Written by, under | Read by, under |
|---|---|---|
| `ep->items`, `ep->count`, `it->next`, `it->fd`, `it->ofd` | `ep_insert` and `ep_remove`, `mtx` (`ep_nest_mtx` before it for a set) | `ctl_locked`, `epoll_target`, `epoll_close`, all under `mtx`; `registrations` in the host test |
| `it->target`, `it->big`, `it->ep` | `ep_insert` once, `mtx`, before the record is filed | `item_poll` under `mtx`; the target lasts until `epoll_release_file`, which takes `mtx` to remove the registration, so no poll runs on a released target |
| `it->events`, `it->data` | `ctl_locked`'s MOD and a one-shot report in `send_events`, under `mtx` and `lock` | `ep_wake` under `lock`; `item_poll` and `send_events` under `mtx` |
| `it->rdstate`, `again`, `rdnext`, `rdprev`, `ep->rdhead`, `rdtail`, `rdbig` | `ep_wake` under the queue's lock and `lock`; `batch_take`, `batch_end`, `ep_poll_queue` and `ep_remove` under `lock`, with interrupts off | the same; `ep_available` reads `rdhead` with no lock, as Linux's `ep_events_available` reads `rdllist`, and a wake that fills the list then wakes `ep->wq` after a full barrier (`sched_mb`), so the waiter's test after filing its record and the waker's test of `wq_active` cannot both miss |
| `it->wait.next` | the queue's lock (`wq_entry_add`, `wq_entry_remove`, `wq_wake_free`) | wakes, under the queue's lock |
| `it->wait.wq` | `wq_entry_add`; cleared by `wq_entry_remove` and `wq_wake_free`, each under `wq_free_lock` | `wq_entry_remove`, under `wq_free_lock`, so it never takes the lock of a queue freed meanwhile |
| `it->fnext`, `fprev`, `dying`, `ofd->epolls` | `ep_insert`, `ep_remove`, `epoll_release_file`, under `ep_files_lock` | the same; `ep_remove` without `force` leaves a dying registration to the release, as Linux's `ep_remove_safe` |
| `it->set`, `hold_next`, `sub_next`, `ep->holders`, `subs`, `walk`, `height` | `ep_insert`, `ep_remove` (`unhold`, `unsub`), `nests_badly`, under `ep_nest_mtx` | `sets_below`, `sets_above`, under `ep_nest_mtx` |
| `ep->refs` | atomic exchange: the description holds one and each registration one | the last `ep_put` frees the set; a registration a release is taking keeps its set after `epoll_close` |

The wake functions run under the waker's queue lock with interrupts off. A scan now calls every poll method with interrupts on. Each was read: pipes, unix sockets, eventfd, timerfd, the sockets, signalfd, inotify, the event devices, DRM, sync files, dma-bufs and the rest read single words or take their own locks with interrupts saved. The terminal relied on the caller's: `tty_poll` runs the console's pump, which the tick and the serial interrupt also fill, and read the discipline after it, so it now takes interrupts off itself, as `tty_inq` does. A pseudo-terminal master's state changes only in thread context. `ep_wake` takes `ep->lock` under the queue's lock and wakes `ep->wq` under it and `ep->poll_wait` after it, one nesting deeper for each set the wake came through, which the proc Readme's lock order now lists. A queue that is freed while records may be on it was only signalfd's; every other queue a poll names lives as long as the open file, which the release hook outlasts.

## Host rows

`epoll_hosttest` has four new rows, run alone and passing: a one-shot registration; a wait over forty registrations that polls only those on the ready list (one poll for one ready, two after a wake, two when the woken one is no longer ready and one after it leaves the list); EPOLLEXCLUSIVE with a sleeper on each of two sets, which wakes one sleeper and readies one set, and with no sleeper readies both; and EPOLLEXCLUSIVE's EINVAL cases. Two rows changed to Linux's semantics: a registration whose number was closed reports while another number keeps its file, in the same process and in a fork's child. `wait_queue_hosttest entries` checks records with a wake function, and `poll_deadline_hosttest enrollment` a wake during filing that leaves the record on its queue. Changing either expectation makes its row fail. The other host tests that include the changed files compile.

## Measured in QEMU beside Linux

`tests/musl/wakemusl.c` has three new rows over 500 eventfds that never become ready: one epoll_wait with a zero timeout on a set of them and one ready eventfd, one poll of all of them, and the eventfd wake row with the 500 in the waiter's set. QEMU under KVM on the testing host, `-cpu max -smp 4 -m 2048`, by [qrun.py](84-168/qrun.py) and [mkinit.py](84-168/mkinit.py). Rach2 is the x86-64 kernel with the test image, the debug shell typing `exec /musl/wakemusl 20000 50`; "before" is the milestone at dd323d931, "after" that with this task merged. Linux is Debian's 6.12.107 cloud kernel with an initramfs of the same static binary, as Debian boots it and with `mitigations=off`. Two runs of each, 10:09 to 10:10 UTC+6 on 2026-11-13; the output is in [qemu.txt](84-168/qemu.txt).

| Row, us | Rach2 before | Rach2 after | Linux | Linux, mitigations=off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| epoll_wait, 500 idle and 1 ready, a call | 8.046, 8.031 | 0.139, 0.137 | 0.331, 0.325 | 0.096, 0.093 |
| poll, 500 idle and 1 ready, a call | 6.869, 6.882 | 6.690, 6.578 | 8.893, 8.847 | 1.913, 1.917 |
| eventfd write to epoll_pwait2 over 500 idle, median wake to run | 20.619, 20.489 | 5.681, 5.641 | 6.322, 6.442 | 5.200, 5.191 |
| eventfd write to epoll_pwait2, one registration, median | 5.691, 5.671 | 5.691, 5.621 | 6.292, 6.342 | 5.210, 5.190 |
| pipe write to epoll_pwait2, median | 5.681, 5.681 | 5.691, 5.651 | 6.403, 6.412 | 5.240, 5.251 |

A wait over the large set no longer depends on its size: the wake into it is the one-registration figure, where it cost 15 us more before. The rebased head, on the milestone's new mutex at ad9513c38, gave 0.136 us, 6.590 us and a 5.921 us median for the first three rows at 10:15.

poll and select scan as Linux's do, each descriptor once a round. The first round that blocks scans twice before it sleeps, where Linux's scan files the entries as it polls (`__pollwait`) and scans once; every later wake costs one scan, as in Linux. Rach2's poll of 501 descriptors costs 6.6 us against Linux's 1.9 with mitigations off, 13 ns a descriptor against 3.8; this task did not change that cost and did not trace it.
