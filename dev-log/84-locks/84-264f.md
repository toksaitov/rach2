# 84-264f: kvm-clock as Linux keeps it

Rach2 gave KVM one clock page, the boot processor's, and used it only where KVM set the stable bit, as the rate of a TSC that then kept the clock; without the bit the clock went back to the HPET, whose every read under KVM is an exit, 123,229 of them in the default suite ([84-264e](84-264e.md)). The kernel now keeps KVM's clock as Linux 6.12's `kvmclock.c` and `pvclock.c` do, on every processor, with the stable bit or without it, and the vDSO reads it or a TSC rated above it without an exit.

## What changed

* `rach2/sys/arch/x86/kvmclock.c` is new and holds all of it. It gives KVM a `pvclock_vcpu_time_info` for each processor, at the boot processor's first use and at each other processor's start, with MSR_KVM_SYSTEM_TIME_NEW under KVM_FEATURE_CLOCKSOURCE2 and the older MSR under KVM_FEATURE_CLOCKSOURCE alone, and takes the running processor's page back at a stop, a restart and a power-off, as `kvmclock_disable` does. A reading retries on the page's version, scales the TSC by `tsc_to_system_mul` and `tsc_shift` and adds `system_time`. The page's stable bit counts only with KVM_FEATURE_CLOCKSOURCE_STABLE_BIT, Linux's `valid_flags`; without it a reading is never earlier than one any processor made before, Linux's `last_value`, under a lock where Linux uses a compare and exchange.
* The tick's clock reads kvm-clock wherever the pages are kept, and no longer hands the clock to the HPET when the bit is clear or withdrawn. `no-kvmclock` and `no-kvmclock-vsyscall` are Linux's options.
* The boot's real time comes from KVM's wall clock, MSR_KVM_WALL_CLOCK_NEW or its older MSR, plus kvm-clock, as `kvm_get_wallclock` reads it, and from the CMOS clock where KVM gives none.
* PVCLOCK_GUEST_STOPPED is cleared where a reading meets it and the lockup watchdogs are touched, as `pvclock_touch_watchdogs` does, so a guest that its host stopped reports no lockup.
* The vDSO's own reading of the boot processor's page, on x86-64 and in the 32-bit vDSO, with the version's retry and the system call once the stable bit is gone, as `vread_pvclock` does, is as it was; `no-kvmclock-vsyscall` now keeps it from the page.
* The TSC is rated against kvm-clock as Linux rates it: below it without an invariant TSC, above it with one, where Linux's kvm-clock rates itself 299 to the TSC's 300, and then watched against kvm-clock, or left unwatched under `rach2.tscreliable`. A TSC above kvm-clock is the vDSO's clock; one the watch parts falls back to KVM's page at the next tick. `/sys/devices/system/clocksource/clocksource0` names kvm-clock in both files.

Rach2 has no processor hot-unplug and no suspend, so the pages are dropped only at the final stop, and nothing registers them again.

## Evidence

On the testing PC's WSL, QEMU 10.2.1 under KVM nested in Hyper-V, two processors, on 2026-11-14 from 22:38 to 22:55 UTC+6. Rach2 ran `musl/vdsomusl clock=kvm` on x86-64, which makes 100,000 calls of `clock_gettime`, `gettimeofday` and `time` through musl and reads the kernel's count of clock system calls from /proc/stat. Debian 13's kernel 6.12.115, the reference guest of `tests/graphics/debian-ref`, ran the same 100,000 calls through glibc in a traced child, with the system calls counted at its stops. The second and third machines came from two processor profiles added to etude for these runs alone and removed after them.

| Machine | Guest | Clock source in use, of those listed | Clock system calls |
|---|---|---|---|
| `-cpu host,invtsc=on` | Linux | tsc, of tsc kvm-clock hpet acpi_pm | 0 |
| `-cpu max,invtsc=on` | Rach2 | tsc, of tsc kvm-clock hpet | 0 |
| `-cpu host` | Linux | kvm-clock, of kvm-clock tsc hpet acpi_pm | 0 |
| `-cpu max` | Rach2 | kvm-clock, of kvm-clock tsc hpet | 0 |
| `-cpu host,kvmclock-stable-bit=off` | Linux | kvm-clock, of kvm-clock tsc hpet acpi_pm | 66,667 |
| `-cpu max,kvmclock-stable-bit=off` | Rach2 | kvm-clock, of kvm-clock tsc hpet | 100,010 |

The i686 kernel, which maps no vDSO, kept kvm-clock on `-cpu max` as well, kvm-clock of kvm-clock tsc hpet, with 100,014 system calls for the 100,000 calls. In each of Rach2's x86-64 runs the vDSO's clocks agreed with the system calls', and a million readings on one processor and two thousand on each in turn were in order, the unstable machine's through the kernel's last-value guard. `tests/run vdso` passed there under KVM as well, on both architectures, and so did the case under QEMU's emulator on the Mac on both architectures, where no kvm-clock is listed.

The last row differs from Linux by the calls of `time`. Linux's `__vdso_time` and its coarse clocks read the seconds of the last tick from the data page whatever the clock source, so only `clock_gettime` and `gettimeofday` enter the kernel, two calls in three. Rach2's vDSO serves `time` and the coarse clocks only while its clock runs, under `rach2.notsc` as well, where `guest.vdso.syscall` requires a system call for each. That is the vDSO's rule and not kvm-clock's, and this task left it as it was.

Not run: the wall clock's reading beside the CMOS clock's, a guest stopped by its host, and a page whose stable bit KVM withdraws while the guest runs. `kvmclock_hosttest` runs those paths against a model of KVM, and `rtc_hosttest` and `watchdog_nmi_hosttest` their callers.

## After the review (84-264r)

Codex's review of the four commits found no regression without KVM and four faults under it, which task 84-264r fixed.

* The TSC went unwatched on the TSC_ADJUST bit alone. Linux leaves it so because it also checks IA32_TSC_ADJUST at each idle entry and writes back a value that changed (`tsc_verify_tsc_adjust`); Rach2 has no such check, so kvm-clock now watches a TSC rated above it with the bit as without it.
* `rach2.tscreliable` rated a TSC above kvm-clock that `cpuid` does not say runs at one rate. Linux's `tsc=reliable` only turns the watchdog off, and so does the option now: the rating follows `cpuid` alone.
* The boot took KVM's wall clock's seconds and dropped its nanoseconds, so real time could be up to a second off. `rtc_anchor_now` anchors the whole reading against the monotonic clock, read with interrupts still off.
* `guest.kvmclock.calls` took both whether kvm-clock is there and what to expect of the vDSO from the kernel under test, so a failed registration passed as an emulator and a vDSO that never started as a page without its stable bit. The runner now names the machine's accelerator to every guest program in `ETUDE_ACCEL`, and under KVM the row requires kvm-clock listed and, on the x86-64 kernel, the vDSO's clock running with no system call. The machine without the stable bit in the table above is one this case no longer accepts; it was measured once, by hand.

The fixes were compiled and their host rows run; no suite ran on them before their review.
