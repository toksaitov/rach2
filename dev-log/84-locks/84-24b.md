# 84-24b: the kernel threads and the big lock

Task 84-24b (#4665) was filed on 84-7's boot A, whose whole-kernel profile charged 26.9% of SuperTuxKart's samples and 24.4% of 0 A.D.'s to kernel threads (`84-7/boot-a/G/profile.txt` on `task/3984` at f972f469c). This file records what those samples are, what the task changed, and what QEMU measured.

## What the samples are

The whole-kernel profile samples every tick in kernel mode, with or without the big lock, so a kernel thread's share there is its processor time, not its time under the lock. 84-7's reading puts 85% of 0 A.D.'s kernel-thread samples, 20,651, at `kirq_restore <- spin_unlock_irqrestore <- vchan_render <- hda_play_render`: khda mixing the virtual channels with interrupts off, and khda has run without the big lock since 84-6c. The service's own counter agrees, 45.4 s over the two games, 1.78 ms for each of 25,435 services.

The big lock's figures for kernel threads were small and mixed with others'. A kernel thread's own holds counted under `vector 0`, beside other holds begun in no entry, and its holds after each sleep under `take 1`, beside every system call's hold after a sleep. In boot A `vector 0` held 0.73 s and waited 0.92 s in SuperTuxKart, 0.93 s and 0.92 s in 0 A.D.; `take 1` held 3.9 s and 3.4 s and waited 4.1 s and 2.3 s. The two together are at most 15% of SuperTuxKart's 31.8 s held.

The kernel threads on the Lenovo, from boot A's log: kdrm, kacpid, kwlan, kpsmoused, watchdogd, khda, kinputd and ki915. ki915, the i915 submit thread, is EXECBUFFER2's path and 84-24's.

## What changed

* `bkl/calls` counts each kernel thread's own holds on a line of its own, `kthread PID calls wall_us holds held_us wait_us NAME`, and no longer under `take` or `vector 0`, so the lines stay disjoint and the next Lenovo run names each thread that takes the lock (debugfs(5)). The processors' idle tasks count together as `idle`. A reading that sums every line except `take 0` and the ioctl lines, as 84-7's does, now counts a kernel thread's waits after its sleeps among the waits in entries, which `take 1` used to leave out.
* kdrm, the DRM commit worker, gives up the big lock it starts with and takes it only for a driver's delayed work, a hotplug event, and a commit's tail on a device without `DRIVER_KMS_UNLOCKED`, as the ATOMIC ioctl's dispatch decides. Linux's commit work runs the tail on a workqueue under no global lock (`drivers/gpu/drm/drm_atomic_helper.c:1876-1882` and `2099` of 6.12.115). On i915 a nonblocking commit's tail, one for each of Weston's frames, now runs under the device's commit lock alone, as a blocking one already did in the ioctl.
* PulseAudio opens `/dev/dsp` at 48 kHz (`vendor/gnu/desktop/default.pa`). At its default of 44.1 kHz the kernel resampled every frame to the mix's 48 kHz with its windowed-sinc filter of 118 taps in khda; now the frames go through, and PulseAudio converts its clients' rates in user space, as a sound server on Linux does.

## Measured in QEMU

KVM on the testing host, four processors, 1 GiB, virtio-vga, intel-hda, os-real on the x86-64 kernel at 03d03cf26, `-cpu max,+invtsc` so that the kernel's times count. A program built in the guest with tcc wrote 10 s of 16-bit stereo to `/dev/dsp` at each rate; `/proc/audio`'s service line before and after:

| Rate | Services | Service time | Per service |
|---|---|---|---|
| 44.1 kHz | 429 | 149.6 ms | 349 us |
| 48 kHz | 429 | 32.8 ms | 76 us |

The resampling is 78% of khda's service time at 44.1 kHz. On the host, `resample.c` alone converts a second of 44.1 kHz in 8.9 ms and copies a second of 48 kHz in 0.3 ms. Scaled by the same factor, the Lenovo's 1.78 ms a service would fall to some 0.4 ms, and khda's 45.4 s over the two games to some 10 s; the release's Lenovo boot reads the real figure from `/proc/audio`.

`bkl/calls` in the same boots, after 20 s of 8 kHz audio: `kthread 0 0 0 425 6196 613 idle` and `take 1 0 0 467 13418 1221`, and no other kernel thread's line: no other kernel thread took the lock there, khda and kdrm among them. The idle tasks' holds came only while audio played (none in 5 s of a quiet system) and were too short for a profile's sample to find.

The games' harness in QEMU runs them on llvmpipe under a headless Weston, which makes no KMS commit, so it cannot show kdrm's change; the figures for kdrm and for the other threads come from the release's Lenovo boot, which runs the native games with the lock counters (83-13, #4648), now with a line for each kernel thread.
