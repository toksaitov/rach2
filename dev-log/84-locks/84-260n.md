# 84-260n: why Venus stops in machine.venus.piglit-quick-gl on x86_64 under TCG

The release run at dc8ee3986 failed `machine.venus.piglit-quick-gl` on x86_64 under TCG: the case overran its gap bound of 2,400,000 ms, and the host logged `vkr: failed to look up object 59 of type 8`. Two faults stop the case under TCG. The release's failure is QEMU's: a notify the guest writes after a blob is mapped is lost, and the control queue stops for good (below, The lost notify). The release at dc8ee3986 ran the `venus` suite under TCG because 20388c1e1, which puts it in `tools/ci/kvm-suites`, came after it; at the head the suite runs under KVM. The second fault stops a single ring. Its cause is a race between Mesa's Venus driver and virglrenderer's ring thread ([upstream.md](upstream.md#venus-leaves-a-ring-asleep-with-commands-in-it-when-the-guests-clock-read-is-slow)), which Rach2 opened by reading the clock through a system call that reads the HPET. Under KVM the processor has the invariant TSC, the vDSO reads it, and the case passed at 2cd9c3a6e in 969 s. Under TCG the vDSO made the system call, and its HPET read waited on QEMU's global lock. Linux on the same TCG processor takes the TSC as its clock source behind its clocksource watchdog (`Switched to clocksource tsc`, with no `constant_tsc` flag), so its vDSO reads the clock in microseconds. Rach2 now does the same on x86-64.

## The race

`vn_ring_submit_internal` stores the ring's tail, loads its status, and only then reads the clock, and sends vkNotifyRingMESA only where IDLE is set and the time lies outside [`last_notify`, `last_notify` + 1 ms). `vkr_ring_thread` sets IDLE 1 ms after the last command it ran and sleeps if the tail has not moved; a notify that reaches it while it runs is taken and does not restart that millisecond. A submission whose clock read returns late sets `last_notify` late, the thread runs its commands without the notify and goes idle within that late millisecond, and the next submission sees IDLE and is throttled. The driver sends no notify while it waits, so the ring sleeps with commands in it for good.

On every stuck ring of the trace run the host's ring thread went to sleep 0.795 to 0.870 ms after the last notify reached QEMU, with the tail ahead of the head and the status 0x5, IDLE and ALIVE. The thread sleeps 1 ms after the last command it ran, so it ran that command before the notify arrived: it had found the submission's tail by itself, and the notify, taken while it ran, moved nothing. The next submission, after the thread slept, read its clock less than 1 ms after the time the notifying submission had read, and sent no notify. `ring-last-ran` below is when the ring thread last ran on the host, before its sleep:

The ring was read from the host's mapping of the guest's memory, the driver's `vn_ring` from the guest's heap, and the notifies from QEMU's `virtio_gpu_cmd_ctx_submit` trace; the guest's uptime kept pace with the host's clock throughout.

## The clock

Under 8 test processes the default kernel's `clock_gettime` took a median 0.46 to 0.66 ms, its 99th percentile 9 to 16 ms and at most 35 ms; 45% of the calls took over 0.8 ms. `getppid` took a median 28 us, so the cost is the HPET's read, not the big kernel lock. QEMU's virtio-gpu holds the global lock for some 0.8 ms per command, and each RESOURCE_UNMAP_BLOB suspends the control queue for some 30 ms until RCU frees the region, some 2,352 times in 5 minutes.

## The runs

All on the testing PC under QEMU 10.2.1's TCG, with 8 processors, `virtio-vga-gl,venus=on,blob=on,hostmem=4G` and 1,100 of piglit's textureGather tests on zink over Venus, 8 at once:

* the head's kernel: a test stopped in its wait within 300 to 520 s of guest time in each run;
* Debian's Linux as the guest: all 1,100 in some 270 s, no stop;
* the head's kernel under `rach2.tscreliable`: one run stopped one ring at some 200 s, and its processes then stopped in RESOURCE_CREATE_BLOB; a second, traced, finished all 1,100 with no stop;
* the kernel with the watched TSC: of twelve runs, eight finished all 1,100; four stopped the whole control queue, as The lost notify says, and in two of those one ring had stopped by the race some minutes before. The watched TSC narrows the race's window and does not close it.

## The change

`timer_tsc_watch` (`pit.c`) measures a TSC without the invariant bit against the HPET or the PM timer before the first tick, on x86-64 only, where KVM states no rate. The processors' checks of the TSC run against that rate (`tsc_clock_per_tick`), the reference keeps the kernel's clock as before, and the vDSO reads the TSC. `vclock_watch` (`vclock.c`) sets the TSC against the tick's raw clock as Linux's clocksource watchdog does: a second after the start it measures the rate again, as `tsc_refine_calibration_work` does, and over each half second after, a parting of more than 248 us, Linux's margins for the TSC and the HPET together, turns the vDSO's clock off for good.

## The lost notify

In each of the four runs that stopped the control queue, the last batch the guest sent before the stop held a RESOURCE_CREATE_BLOB and a RESOURCE_MAP_BLOB, and QEMU traced no `virtio_queue_notify` after it. In one run, held through QEMU's monitor, QEMU had used and consumed 44,312 descriptors with none in use, while the guest's available index was 44,333, its `avail_event` 44,312, and its own `struct virtqueue` had kicked at 44,333 with 21 heads in use. The guest had written the notify for the 21 commands; the write never reached virtio-gpu. Every later kick then skips the notify by the event-index rule, since the guest's last kick lies past the `avail_event` QEMU published, so nothing moves the queue again.

The map adds a subregion to virtio-gpu's host-memory BAR, which renumbers the sections of QEMU's memory map. Under TCG, a vCPU whose TLB entry for the notify page was filled before that change finds the entry's section by its old index in the new map until its TLB flush runs, and the write goes to another section ([upstream.md](upstream.md#qemu-aborts-in-iotlb_to_section-while-venus-runs-vulkan-under-tcg)). The guest's virtqueue code follows Linux's handshake; nothing in Rach2 loses the write. Under KVM nothing looks the section up this way, and the release's `venus` suite runs under KVM.
