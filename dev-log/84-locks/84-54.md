# 84-54: the takes of the big lock after a switch, and rt_sigprocmask without it

Task 84-54 names the takes of the big lock outside a system call that WineD3D's GL round trip waits on, which 80-27's Lenovo step counted as `take 1`: 3,593 to 4,258 holds a loop, 49 to 59 ms. They are the takes after a switch: a thread that slept inside a call still under the big lock takes it back when it runs again, and the rest of that call is charged to `take 1`. debugfs's `bkl/calls` now divides them by the system call the thread slept in, as `slept` lines (debugfs(5)), and `trip.py` prints them for each loop. The task then runs rt_sigprocmask without the big lock, under a lock of the signal state that stands in for Linux's `sighand->siglock` ([signals](../../rach2/sys/proc/signal.Readme.md)). The manager's split gave epoll to 84-57 and path lookup to 84-56; the first versions of both written here were left out of the milestone branch, and their commits were posted on those tasks.

## Measurements in QEMU

All three kernels booted 80-5's trial root, built from the milestone branch at 2cf7554d8, under KVM on 4 processors with 8 GiB, and ran the bundle's `trip.sh -r llvmpipe` on 2026-11-12 between 21:44 and 22:08 UTC+6, on the testing host while other accounts' loops ran. The base is the milestone branch at 2cf7554d8; the counter adds the slept lines to it; the third is `task/4729` with rt_sigprocmask under the signal state's lock, on the milestone branch at 09f26f6e9, whose three later commits change no code the loop runs. The outputs and debugfs's `bkl/calls` before and after each run are in [84-54/evidence](84-54/evidence/).

| Per loop | Base | Counter | rt_sigprocmask without the big lock |
|---|---|---|---|
| Round trips, ms | 80.5, 69.9, 106.4 | 125.3, 104.9, 105.8 | 117.2, 75.3, 88.8 |
| Waits for the big lock, ms | 11.5, 15.4, 17.4 | 17.0, 23.7, 22.5 | 16.7, 13.3, 16.8 |
| rt_sigprocmask's holds | 4,472, 3,854, 4,202 | 4,394, 4,198, 4,288 | none |

| of those, slept in clock_nanosleep | | 163, 134, 139 | 152, 100, 119 |

The times vary by a third between loops of one kernel, as the host's other work comes and goes, and say nothing about the change; the counts do. The milestone's earlier work has already cut the loop from 80-27's 494 to 605 ms in QEMU: epoll, pipes, futexes and the regions' calls run without the big lock, and `take 1` fell from 80-27's thousands to 116 to 207 holds a loop. What is left of it is nearly all clock_nanosleep (number 230), which still runs under the big lock and takes it back when its sleep ends; Under the counter kernel 10 to 13 more had slept in rt_sigprocmask (`slept 14`).

Over each whole run, the strace and ioctl runs included, rt_sigprocmask made 999,352 calls under the counter kernel with as many holds of the big lock, and 1,010,248 calls with 177,750 holds without it. The holds left are the traced runs': a thread that strace traces takes the big lock at every entry (`syscall_entry_locked`). In the plain loops it took the big lock no time.

## The lock-order check

The same `task/4729` head built as `rach2-lockdep` ran one loop, its strace run and its ioctl run, which passed, from 22:08 to 22:12 UTC+6. `/proc/lockdep` lists `sig_lock` with no orders, as its one checked taker, rt_sigprocmask without the big lock, takes nothing under it. The checker made 475 reports (`/proc/lockdep_stats`), none on the signal code: 481 report lines of locks without a class taken in `fd_table_join` (fs/fd.c) and `vfs_cwd_set` (fs/vfs.c); `mmap_lock` taken with interrupts off once at boot, by crond; HDA's `hda_stream_mutex` taken with interrupts off at a stream's start (`hda_stream_start`); and at power-off an ext2 node and an ext2 mount's metadata lock taken against their order. No base `rach2-lockdep` kernel was run beside it, so this run does not show whether those reports came before this task; none is in code it changed. They are in `84-54/evidence/qemu-lockdep/reports.txt`.

## What the counter showed on the way

The first boot of the counter, on the milestone branch at a791ed4a3, stopped 18 s after boot with `vm: fault: killing pid 114: bad write`, and trip.sh then slept in `wait4` with no child left. The milestone had fixed that fault meanwhile, in e5f011142 (a write fault that another thread's fault had made writable, which killed llvmpipe's Weston), and the counter on 2cf7554d8 ran through.

## Left under the big lock

clock_nanosleep is now the larger part of `take 1` in this loop, about 100 to 160 holds of 5 to 10 us. Its sleep reads the timer list, the restart block and the signal state under the big lock (`sleep_until`, rach2/sys/syscall.c); taking it off is a task of its own. sigprocmask, the old form, and rt_sigaction, rt_sigpending and sigaltstack keep the big lock: the loop does not call them.
