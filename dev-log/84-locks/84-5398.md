# 84-5398: transparent huge pages and the Debian boot's exits under KVM

With transparent huge pages, the Debian guest's boot under Rach2's KVM takes 61432 and 61958 exits, against 90458 and 90589 with them off; its nested page faults fall from 27796 and 27817 to 1679 and 1690, Linux's 1737. QEMU's guest memory is a private anonymous mapping that QEMU marks MADV_HUGEPAGE, so under the default policy `madvise` it faults in 2 MiB pages, and KVM's guest-physical tables (`rach2/sys/kvm/tdp.c`) map each with one 2 MiB leaf, since the kept leaf table names 512 consecutive frames from a 2 MiB boundary. No KVM code changed.

## The measurements

QEMU 10.2.1 on the testing PC under WSL's KVM, `-cpu host` with the `host-svm` profile's features, 2 processors and 4 GiB, Rach2's x86-64 kernel of task/5398 at ad50fbdb94 on the preparation copy's `gnu-test.disk` of 2026-11-18, booted with `rach2.root=ext2 rach2.tscreliable`, the KVM data disk copied to `/tmp/kvm` as the `machine.kvm` cases copy it. [`drive.py`](84-5398/drive.py) logs in at the serial console and, four times, writes the policy to `/sys/kernel/mm/transparent_hugepage/enabled` and runs `sh exits.sh sh kvm-boot.sh -k guests/debian/vmlinuz -i guests/debian/initrd`. The whole console is in [`exits.txt`](84-5398/exits.txt).

| Policy | Exits | Nested page faults (0x400) | I/O exits (0x7b) | Interrupts (0x60) | Boot | `thp_fault_alloc` after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| never | 90458 | 27796 | 54415 | 4015 | 39 s | 0 |
| madvise | 61432 | 1679 | 54243 | 2450 | 26 s | 57 |
| never | 90589 | 27817 | 54399 | 3929 | 46 s | 57 |
| madvise | 61958 | 1690 | 54529 | 2457 | 33 s | 113 |

Each boot with the policy `madvise` made 56 or 57 huge pages at fault, of the guest's 256 MiB; no collapse ran (`thp_collapse_alloc` 0) and no page was split (`thp_split_pmd` 0). The boot times are the host's clock, for the record.

The task's issue gives Linux 6.12 as the host of the same machine 57704 exits with transparent huge pages and 84007 without, and 1737 nested page faults, the manager's figures. Rach2's nested page faults now match Linux's; the rest of the gap, some 4000 exits, is in other exit codes, which this task did not compare.
