# 84-165: a mutex spins while its holder runs, as Linux's does

Rach2's mutex slept at once when it found itself held (`sys/proc/mutex.c:87-107` before this task), so a taker that met a hold of a few hundred nanoseconds paid a sleep and a wake, which for a halted processor is an interprocessor interrupt. Linux spins while the holder runs on another processor before it sleeps (`kernel/locking/mutex.c:352-373`, `441-510`), with the spinners queued so that they do not all pull the lock word's line ([84-163](https://github.com/toksaitov/rach2/issues/4889), Fable's first answer, item 2 and the fix "Mutex"). In 84-157's GL loop the mutex is the GEM mutex between EXECBUFFER2 and ki915's retire, and every other mutex in the kernel takes the same path. This task brings the spin, the queue and Linux's handoff to a sleeper, measures the changed path beside Linux in QEMU, and prepares the Lenovo loop.

## The change

All in `rach2/sys/proc/mutex.c`, with its contract in [the processes Readme](../../rach2/sys/proc/Readme.md):

* The lock word, `owner`, is the holder's `process_t` address, pointer-wide, with the low bit `MUTEX_HANDOFF`; 0 is free. A take is one compare-and-exchange of 0 to the caller (`mutex_take`), so the holder is recorded with the take, as Linux's `__mutex_trylock_common`, and owner tracking is exact. `owner_cpu` is a hint of the holder's processor, written after the take; a spinner that finds it stale, before the holder has written it or after the holder slept and woke elsewhere, corrects it by a look at every processor (`sched_running_cpu`). `arch_atomic_xchg_ptr` and `arch_atomic_cmpxchg_ptr` are the new pointer-wide atomics, `xchgq` and `cmpxchgq` on x86-64 and the 32-bit forms on i686.
* `mutex_spin` is Linux's `mutex_optimistic_spin`: a spinner takes the head of the mutex's queue, then tries the word whenever it changes while the holder runs (`mutex_owner_runs`). It gives up when the holder sleeps, when its hypervisor has preempted the holder's processor (KVM's steal-time flag, `arch_cpu_preempted`), or when the spinner must give way: `need_resched`, a claim of the big lock it holds, or a holder waiting for that lock. These are the tests `sched_spin_on` already made for the sleep lock and the pipe, as Linux's `mutex_spin_on_owner` tests `owner_on_cpu`, `need_resched` and `vcpu_is_preempted`.
* Only a thread that no interrupt switches spins (`sched_spin_pinned`): a kernel thread, or a thread in a system call or a fault. Linux spins with preemption off; Rach2's tick does not switch such a thread, so its processor's place in the queue stays its own.
* The queue is Linux's `osq_lock` and `osq_unlock` (`osq_lock.c:91-236`): a place for each processor, `osq_nodes`, links that name processors as their index + 1 so that 32-bit exchanges serve on both architectures, and Linux's three-step unqueue of a spinner that leaves from the middle. A queued spinner waits on its own place and reads neither the lock word nor the holder; it leaves when the processor ahead is preempted by its hypervisor or it must give way. Only the head reads the word.
* `mutex_wait_cond`, the sleeper's condition, asks for the handoff when it wakes to a mutex taken again, by setting `MUTEX_HANDOFF` in the held word, as Linux's first waiter sets `MUTEX_FLAG_HANDOFF` (`mutex.c:110-121`, `693-699`). A release that finds the flag hands the word to the oldest sleeper under the wait queue's lock (`wq_wake_first` with `mutex_grant`, Linux's `__mutex_handoff`, `mutex.c:225-248`), in one exchange from holder to sleeper, so no spinner takes it between them. A sleeper that has slept once is not starved by spinners.
* `mutex_lock_interruptible` and `mutex_lock_killable` end with the mutex held when it was handed over while their signal arrived, as Linux's waiter that finds itself the owner takes it.

Three differences from Linux remain, each named in the code:

* A sleeper that wakes does not spin again; Linux's first waiter may (`mutex_optimistic_spin` with a `waiter`). It takes the word or asks for the handoff.
* A release with no handoff wakes every sleeper, as before; Linux wakes the first alone. A Rach2 waiter that leaves by a signal does not take the mutex first, so a lone wake that went to it would leave the others asleep on a free mutex. With the spin, sleepers are rare: the QEMU rows below have tens where they had tens of thousands.
* There is no `ww_mutex` path and no `MUTEX_FLAG_PICKUP`; Rach2 has no wound-wait mutex.

The task found one difference outside the mutex and fixed it: a Unix stream receive that may not wait let go of its receive mutex when it found no data, took it again for nothing and woke the writers (`recv_stream` in `sys/ipc/unix.c`). Linux returns EAGAIN with the mutex still held (`af_unix.c:2834-2837`) and wakes writers only when it consumes. The empty receive now takes the mutex once, and only a consumption wakes the writers.

## The shared structures

* `owner` is written only by `mutex_take` (compare-and-exchange 0 to the caller), `mutex_unlock` (compare-and-exchange to 0), `mutex_ask_handoff` (compare-and-exchange that adds the flag to a held word) and `mutex_grant` (exchange to the oldest sleeper under the queue's lock). Each is a locked instruction and a full barrier. A holder's release reads the word, panics unless it is the holder's, and loops on its compare-and-exchange, so a flag added between the read and the exchange is seen. Readers: `mutex_mine` and `mutex_held` compare its owner part with the caller; the spinner compares the whole word with what it read and never follows the pointer, which `sched_spin_on` and `sched_running_cpu` compare with each processor's current thread.
* `owner_cpu` is a hint: written by the taker after its take and set to -1 by `mutex_grant`, read only by spinners, and checked against `sched_running_cpu` before a spinner gives up on it.
* `osq` and `osq_nodes` are touched only by atomic exchanges and compare-and-exchanges and by the stores of `osq_lock` and `osq_unlock`, in Linux's order. A processor's place serves one mutex at a time, since a spinning thread is never switched off its processor and interrupt handlers take no mutex.
* `wq` is touched only through the wait queue's functions under its spin lock: the sleeper files itself before its condition runs with the process blocked, so a release after the condition's test wakes it; `wake_waiters` and `wq_wake_first` take the lock. `wq_wake_first` calls `mutex_grant` under the lock with the first waiter that has a process, dequeues it, makes it ready, and sends any kick after the unlock.
* No other kernel code reads or writes a mutex's fields: grep of `owner`, `owner_cpu`, `osq` and `.wq` on `struct mutex` finds only `mutex.c`, the static initializer of `bcache.c`'s `wb_lock` (now `{0, -1, 0, ...}`), and the host tests' fakes, which were changed with the type.

## The host row

`tests/host/mutex_hosttest.c` compiles `mutex.c` against models of the scheduler's tests, the wait queue and a second processor, which hold the mutex for a countdown of pauses. Its rows:

* A short hold of 40 pauses: 0 sleeps and 40 pauses with the spin, 1 sleep without it. A holder asleep: the taker sleeps at once.
* A holder that stops mid-hold: the taker spins 10 pauses, then sleeps. A stale `owner_cpu` hint still spins.
* Handoff: a sleeper that wakes to a stolen mutex asks for the handoff and is given it on the next release (2 sleeps, the first waiter handed the word); a handoff flag with no sleeper frees the word.
* Signals: an interruptible lock handed over while its signal came returns 0 and holds the mutex; a signal alone is -EINTR; a killable lock is -EINTR and the holder keeps it.
* The queue: the head passes on, the last leaves, a spinner behind a preempted processor leaves, and a spinner leaving from the middle relinks the ones on each side.

It printed:

```
mutex_hosttest: short hold, spin: 0 sleeps, 40 pauses
mutex_hosttest: short hold, no spin: 1 sleeps, 0 pauses
mutex_hosttest: ok
```

## Measured in QEMU

`tests/musl/mutexmusl.c` pins one to four threads to processors 0 to 3; each makes 200,000 `recv(MSG_DONTWAIT)` calls on one end of an empty Unix stream socketpair, which on both systems takes the socket's receive mutex, finds nothing and gives it back with EAGAIN. A row prints the mean time of a call and the threads' voluntary switches, which count the sleeps on the mutex. Run as process 1 it powers the machine off, so one boot is one run.

The testing PC, QEMU under KVM in WSL nested under Hyper-V, `qemu-system-x86_64 -enable-kvm -cpu max -smp 4 -m 2048`, serial console, one machine at a time, on 2026-11-13 between 09:34 and 09:48 UTC+6. Rach2 booted the x86-64 kernel with a KRFFS root holding the binary as `/sbin/init`; Linux was Debian's 6.12.107+deb13-cloud-amd64 with an initramfs holding the same binary, SHA-256 `ce1cfcde4357b3bb...`, as booted and with `mitigations=off`. "Base" is the milestone head this task merged, 122eada15; "mutex" is this task's mutex change on it, 0bc67452b; "unix" adds the receive's change, 95eaffba3. Each is three boots or more; the range is over them. Output and the kernels' SHA-256 are in [84-165/qemu.txt](84-165/qemu.txt).

| Threads | Rach2 base | Rach2 mutex | Rach2 unix | Linux | Linux, mitigations=off |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1, us a call | 0.188-0.207 | 0.181-0.194 | 0.162-0.171 | 0.303-0.317 | 0.070-0.073 |
| 2, us a call | 0.388-0.398 | 0.273-0.302 | 0.228-0.236 | 0.301-0.328 | 0.101-0.103 |
| 2, sleeps | 3143-4036 | 0-4 | 0-1 | 0 | 0 |
| 3, us a call | 0.683-0.750 | 0.698-0.936 | 0.479-0.512 | 0.303-0.322 | 0.134-0.143 |
| 3, sleeps | 18421-18853 | 13-40 | 4-21 | 0 | 0 |
| 4, us a call | 1.057-1.201 | 1.113-1.243 | 0.913-0.984 | 0.304-0.333 | 0.375-0.379 |
| 4, sleeps | 40343-43473 | 63-177 | 19-52 | 0 | 0-6 |

The spin removes the sleeps: of 800,000 calls on four processors, 40,000 slept before and under 200 now, against Linux's none. A short hold now costs no sleep and wake, which is what the host row counts. Two threads became faster than Linux as booted. Three and four did not get faster with the spin alone, since all four threads take one mutex back to back and the call is bound by how long each holds it, not by sleeping; the receive's change shortens the hold by one release and one take, which took 0.2 us off the call at three and four threads.

The kernel before 84-166's scheduler commit, 82d80f11c, gave the same sleeps (1783-7902, 17081-17875, 44027-45599). A first form of the queue, in which queued spinners read the lock word as well as their own place, slept as rarely but was slower at four threads, 1.347 to 1.802 us, since every spinner pulled the line the holder writes; Linux's queued spinners read only their own place, and so does the form that landed.

What remained: at four threads Rach2's call was 0.91 to 0.98 us against Linux's 0.30 to 0.33 us as booted and 0.38 us without mitigations. It is not the mutex, which now sleeps under one call in 4,000. 84-165a, below, traced the rest.

QEMU has no i915, so the GL loop's figure is the Lenovo's: [84-165-lenovo.md](84-165-lenovo.md).

## 84-165a: the empty receive

[84-165a](https://github.com/toksaitov/rach2/issues/4903) traced the empty receive at four threads and brought it to Linux's shape.

### The trace

A trace kernel at the milestone head 92cd6c0eb counted, by lock and call site, each spin-lock take, each take whose first exchange failed and the cycles it then waited, with the mutex's takes, spins and sleeps and the big lock's takes; the call sites are from `addr2line` on its `kernel.debug`. One boot, 2026-11-13 10:26 UTC+6. Per call, in the four-thread row of 800,000 calls:

| Lock | Taken in | Takes | Contended | Cycles waited |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `flight_lock`, global | `transit_enter` | 1 | 37.9% | 183 |
| `flight_lock`, global | `release_in` | 1 | 27.2% | 152 |
| `flight_lock`, global | `transit_leave` | 1 | 18.0% | 119 |
| socket's `state_lock` | `recv_stream`, state and SO_RCVTIMEO | 1 | 19.4% | 67 |
| socket's `state_lock` | `recv_stream`, look for a message | 1 | 4.3% | 6 |
| socket's `state_lock` | `wait_data`, error and shutdown | 1 | 6.1% | 10 |
| socket's `queue_lock` | nested in the two above | 2 | 0 | 0 |
| descriptor table's lock | `fd_get` | 1 | 9.9% | 37 |
| descriptor table's lock | `fd_holds_claim`, the call's end | 1 | 13.1% | 56 |

The receive mutex was taken at once 422,696 times and after a spin 377,257 times, with 47 sleeps; the big lock was taken 22 times in the row, never on the call's path. Every Unix receive, send and message entered one global list of descriptors in flight under `flight_lock` and walked it to leave, whether or not it had descriptors; that was 455 of the 631 cycles a call waited. Linux keeps descriptors in flight on the skb and touches nothing global on a receive that finds no data. The trace is posted on the issue.

### The change

In `sys/ipc/unix.c`:

* A flight enters the registry in the hold of `flight_lock` that first gives it descriptors (`flight_list`, from `get_rights`, `attach` and `take_fds`) and leaves it in one step by its back link (`flight_unlist`, `flight_end`). A receive, a send or a message that has no descriptors takes `flight_lock` no more; `msg_free` frees such a message at once, without the reap list.
* A stream receive looks at its socket once a turn (`stream_look`): the state and SO_RCVTIMEO at the first look, the queue, and with nothing queued the pending error and the shutdown, in one hold of the state lock under the receive mutex, as Linux's `unix_stream_read_generic` (af_unix.c:2785-2860). SO_RCVTIMEO is read under the mutex, where Linux reads it just before; both read it once, before any copy or wait.

A trace kernel with the change counted, per empty receive, one state-lock take, one nested queue-lock take and the two descriptor-table takes, and no `flight_lock`.

### Measured

The same QEMU and binary as above, `mutexmusl` SHA-256 `aaa0aaec16f98d02...`, on 2026-11-13 between 10:45 and 10:50 UTC+6, the kernels booted in turn. "Base" is 92cd6c0eb, "fix" this change, 38fbb1184, on it; Linux is the same Debian kernel with an initramfs holding the same binary. The base is faster than the "unix" column above, since the milestone head moved under it. Output is in [84-165/qemu-165a.txt](84-165/qemu-165a.txt).

| Threads | Rach2 base | Rach2 fix | Linux | Linux, mitigations=off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1, us a call | 0.178-0.184 | 0.099-0.104 | 0.320-0.322 | 0.068-0.079 |
| 2, us a call | 0.240-0.248 | 0.147-0.166 | 0.319-0.328 | 0.096-0.106 |
| 3, us a call | 0.350-0.356 | 0.245-0.283 | 0.321-0.329 | 0.114-0.131 |
| 4, us a call | 0.630-0.654 | 0.498-0.531 | 0.321-0.330 | 0.353-0.366 |
| boots | 4 | 10 | 5 | 3 |

The call is faster at every count, 44% at one thread and 20% at four, and the two-thread row is better than before and than Linux's. The four-thread row is not within 10% of Linux's 0.32 us.

### What is left at four threads

Linux as booted holds 0.32 us from one thread to four because its entry, with Debian's mitigations, spends some 0.25 us of each call outside the socket's locks; with `mitigations=off` its entry costs about what Rach2's does, and its four-thread row is 0.353-0.366 us, three times its three-thread row. Two experiments, not landed, measured the rest of Rach2's gap, three boots each:

* With `fd_get` taking no table lock (unsafe, for the measure alone), four threads gave 0.442-0.460 us. The descriptor table's spin lock, taken twice a call on a table that threads share, costs some 0.06 us at four threads; Linux's `fdget` takes no table lock, an RCU lookup and the file's `f_count`.
* With `raw_spin_unlock` a plain store, as Linux's x86 unlock is, four threads gave 0.540-0.567 us, slower: Rach2's test-and-set spin lock lets the releaser take the lock again before a waiter, where Linux's queued spin lock hands it on in order.

What is left is the per-socket section that four threads pass one at a time, the receive mutex and the state lock over a test-and-set spin lock, and the descriptor table's lock.

## Failures

* The first QEMU runs of Linux printed nothing past its first rows: `mutexmusl` exited as init, Linux panicked and the output was cut. It now powers the machine off as process 1.
* The first form of the queue let queued spinners read the lock word, which Linux's do not; it measured slower at four threads before it was found and changed.
* The first "before" kernel was the milestone head before 84-166's scheduler commit, which "after" carried through the merge; the comparison was run again against the merged head, 122eada15, which gives the table above.
