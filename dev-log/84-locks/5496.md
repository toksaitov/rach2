# 5496: a tracer's stop cost that grew with every thread of the system

5426 measured WineD3D's GL round trip under strace on the Lenovo: 1000 trips took 4057 to 4120 ms on Rach2's release and 859 to 956 ms on Linux 6.12.115, both rows of trip.py, `strace -f -c -w` and `strace -f -e trace=ioctl`, while without strace both take 208 to 313 ms ([5426](https://github.com/toksaitov/rach2/issues/5426)). So each syscall stop cost Rach2 several times what it costs Linux.

## The cause

In an empty QEMU guest the two kernels cost the same per stop: a traced system call, two stops, took Rach2 31.0 to 35.9 us and Linux 29.7 to 36.1 us. The Lenovo's root ran Weston, Xwayland, Wine's processes and their threads, and the cost followed them: with 500 other processes asleep in the guest, the same call took Rach2 70.4 to 76.5 us and Linux still 30.8 to 37.1 us, and the two-thread ping-pong 204 to 209 us against Linux's 78 to 86 us. Two paths of a stop walked every record of the system:

* `wait_find`, which strace's `wait4` runs twice a stop, walked `proc_list` for the caller's children and tracees, where Linux's `do_wait` walks the caller's `children` and `ptraced` lists (kernel/exit.c:1488-1530).
* Each report of the wait summed the child's CPU time, page faults and switches, whether or not the caller gave a buffer for them, and each sum walked `proc_list` again for the process's threads: three walks a report. Linux fills the usage only when the caller asks (`wait_task_stopped`, `wait_task_zombie`) and sums the process's own threads (`getrusage`). strace's `-c` asks for it.

`bkl/calls` showed it: over 20000 traced getppid calls, the holds of `wait4` after its sleep took 23.8 ms alone and 410.5 ms with the sleepers, on the kernel that already walked by index.

Not a cause: seccomp's `SECCOMP_RET_TRACE` works, and with `--seccomp-bpf` strace skips the untraced calls on Rach2 as on Linux (getppid 0.15 against 0.18 us a call), but strace 7.2 uses it only when asked, and trip.py does not ask. The idle path cannot explain it either: the guest halts as Linux's does, and the costs matched with no other process.

## The change

`proc/process.c` keeps three more indexes, each a chain of records doubly linked so that a record goes on its end and comes off in constant time, the first record's back link naming the last:

* by parent, of process leaders alone, as Linux's `children` (`proc_set_parent`, `proc_children`); an exec that makes a thread the leader gives it the old leader's place in its parent's chain (`key_hand` from `proc_exchange_pids`), as Linux's `de_thread` does with `list_replace_init` (fs/exec.c:1123);
* by tracer, of the traced threads, as Linux's `ptraced` (`proc_set_tracer`, `proc_tracees`);
* each process's own records, `threads` in `struct tgroup`, as Linux's `thread_head`, which the sums of its usage walk.

The two hashes are as wide as the pid hash and grow with it, keeping each chain's order. Every writer of `parent_pid` and `tracer` goes through the setters; the kernel threads' own `parent_pid = 0` after `alloc_process` stays, since a new record's parent is 0 already and 0 is in no chain. `wait_find` walks the caller's children and then the threads it traces that are not its children; `ptrace_tracer_gone` walks the chain of its tracees. A wait's report sums its child's usage only for a caller that gave `wait4` or `waitid` a buffer.

## Two traced threads

The two-thread ping-pong is still above Linux's. Eight rounds alone each, at a quieter hour of the host: Rach2 74.71 to 79.82 us under `-c -w` and 73.35 to 78.08 under `-e trace=ioctl`, Linux 67.59 to 71.21 and 63.81 to 67.75; under `--seccomp-bpf`, where no call stops, 57.67 to 59.59 against 57.68 to 59.53. One profiled run on the fix (`ppprof-rach2` in qemu.txt) has the tracer's `wait4` and `ptrace` and the tracees' pipe `read` and `write` waiting 21.4 ms for the big lock over a 367.5 ms run, some 6%: the calls of a stop run under it, and Linux has no such lock. The rest of the gap is not traced.

## The lock judged after the entry stop, tried and taken back

A traced thread runs each call under the big lock from entry to exit (`syscall_entry_locked`), even a call whose handler runs without it, such as a pipe's read and write. 6e487f4cc took the lock only for the tracer's two stops and judged the call again on the number the entry stop left, as Linux's `syscall_trace_enter` returns the number the call then runs (kernel/entry/common.c:28-75). It made the two-thread ping-pong slower, and aaa940351 takes it back. Eight rounds a boot, two boots each, on 2026-11-19 between 21:37 and 21:39 UTC+6 ([5496/relock.txt](5496/relock.txt)), microseconds a round trip:

| strace mode | Base | Change | Linux |
|---|---|---|---|
| `-f -c -w` | 77.98-81.81 | 85.17-92.05 | 68.98-93.65, medians 71.11 and 73.62 |
| `-f -e trace=ioctl` | 76.35-81.13 | 83.25-94.53 | 65.41-74.54 |
| `-f --seccomp-bpf -e trace=ioctl` | 58.50-60.60 | 58.76-65.84 | 58.51-72.30 |

With the change each traced read and write took the lock twice instead of once, and the reader's waits for it rose from 5.2 to 15.9 ms over 5000 round trips (bkl/calls). The cost of the two-thread case is the lock passing between the tracer and the tracees, not the handler held under it.

The same profile shows where the lock goes: over a 415 ms run of the base kernel, the tracer's 80162 `ptrace` calls hold it 178.5 ms, 2.2 us a call, and its 65417 `wait4` calls 26.1 ms, and wait for it 15.7 ms. With one traced thread nothing else wants the lock while the tracer holds it, and a stop costs what Linux's costs (getppid under `-f -c -w`, 27.6 and 28.1 us on the base kernel against 28.5 and 32.9 on Linux); with two, the tracer's hold delays the tracee that runs meanwhile. Linux serves `ptrace` and `wait4` under the tracee's `siglock` and `tasklist_lock`. Rach2's process list, pid hash and signal posting are under the big lock, so taking `ptrace` and `wait4` off it is a conversion of milestone 84's kind, not a change to this task's paths.

## WineD3D in QEMU

The indexed waits cut the median of the full strace row from 2,500.8 to 1,836.0 ms per 1,000 round trips, and the ioctl row from 2,410.7 to 1,732.9 ms. Rach2 still took 66% and 75% longer than Linux in those rows, respectively. Both kernels linked.

On 2026-11-19 from 22:09 to 22:17 UTC+6, QEMU 10.2.1 on the testing PC ran one guest at a time under KVM with four processors, 8 GiB and std VGA with no display. The Rach2 kernels were a4fe27cf4 and the milestone kernel at 0f85834fc8, which also has the ext2 lock-class change for lockdep builds. Both booted 3984's retained GNU trial root through `rach2.rootimage=`, since 84-58's image was gone from its recorded path. Debian's prepared graphics guest booted Linux 6.12.115. All three boots used the same probe bundle and strace 7.2 binary, GE-Proton11-12 and headless Weston on llvmpipe. Each ran `trip.sh -r llvmpipe` three times with a fresh Wine prefix per invocation. All pixels checked and every run reported `TRIPS OK`. The [run output and input digests](5496/wine-qemu.txt) record the figures.

| Kernel | Plain, ms (range of 9) | `trip-gl-strace`, ms | `trip-gl-ioctl`, ms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rach2 before the wait fix | 86.2-104.2 | 2,477.5, 2,500.8, 2,507.1 | 2,409.5, 2,410.7, 2,443.0 |
| Rach2 at the milestone head | 82.8-99.3 | 1,851.3, 1,836.0, 1,812.5 | 1,746.4, 1,732.0, 1,732.9 |

In the full strace row, `wait4` held the big lock for 756 to 769 ms before the fix and 237 to 240 ms after it. On the milestone kernel, `ptrace` still held it for 963 to 979 ms over 431,167 to 433,894 holds. The sum of waits for the lock across all processors was 1,330 to 1,355 ms over the loop. Wine's command thread waited ready for 212 to 222 ms on Rach2 and 19 to 24 ms on Linux. The ioctl row showed the same pattern. The remaining gap calls for taking the process list, signal posting and the tracing paths off the big lock. The Linux-range requirement remains open. The Lenovo's figure still comes from the release or the next trial that boots the head.

## WineD3D in QEMU once the tracer's calls left the big lock

Subtask 5508 ran the same three `trip.sh -r llvmpipe` runs on the head of `milestone/76-display`, 90468670dd, which runs `wait4` (a3f41354eb), the `ptrace` requests (777d69bbd2) and signal posting (824775ac34) without the big lock. The full strace row's median fell from 1,836.0 to 1,696.2 ms per 1,000 round trips and the ioctl row's from 1,732.9 to 1,627.8 ms, still 53% and 65% above Linux's 1,106.0 and 988.3 ms. The setup and inputs were those of the earlier rows, on 2026-11-20 between 02:03 and 02:05 UTC+6, with one guest of another task running beside it ([output](5496/wine-qemu-head.txt)).

| Row | Runs, ms | Big-lock waits, ms | wined3d_cs ran, waited ready, ms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plain | 88.9-110.0 (range of 9) | 6.2-7.4 | 37.9-43.2, 20.7-25.5 |
| `trip-gl-strace` | 1,696.2, 1,677.7, 1,821.1 | 1,116-1,209 | 464.0-501.6, 270.0-289.0 |
| `trip-gl-ioctl` | 1,588.5, 1,663.1, 1,627.8 | 1,078-1,147 | 449.5-469.9, 263.3-282.5 |

The tracer's calls no longer hold the lock: `ptrace` went from 963 to 979 ms over some 432,000 holds to none, and `wait4` from 237 to 240 ms to under 0.1 ms. What holds it now is the tracees. A traced thread runs each call under the big lock from entry to exit (`syscall_entry_locked`), and takes it again after each of the call's two stops. In the full strace row, per run:

* the takes after a sleep, `take 1`: 152,331 to 153,304 holds, 524 to 564 ms; by the call slept in, futex (202) 446 to 468 ms over some 111,000 holds, getrusage (98) 112 to 118 ms, sched_yield (24) 56 to 62 ms, read (0) 47 to 51 ms, rt_sigprocmask (14) 38 to 40 ms and write (1) 37 to 40 ms;
* the calls' own holds: futex 102 to 109 ms over some 47,000 calls, getrusage 42 to 46 ms over some 20,000, sched_yield 19 to 21 ms.

Every one of those calls runs without the big lock when untraced. The ioctl row, where strace still stops at every call, shows the same holds within 5%. Linux stops a traced call and runs its handler under no lock but the ones the handler takes (kernel/entry/common.c:28-75, kernel/signal.c:2345-2470).
