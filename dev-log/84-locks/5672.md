# 5672: flush the KRFFS disk root after a pass of writes

A barrier of the KRFFS disk root is `pc_sync_wait`, which writes the dirty pages of the page cache in runs of up to 32 pages. Each run went through the device's `write`, which flushes the disk's cache before it returns (`ata_write`, `ahci_write`, the FLUSH after a virtio-blk write, FUA on NVMe), so a barrier waited for the medium once for each run it wrote. Linux sends writeback as ordinary writes and flushes once at the durability point (`Documentation/block/writeback_cache_control.rst`, `REQ_PREFLUSH | REQ_FUA` on a jbd2 commit).

## The change

The fault-safe disks have a `write_cached`, their polled write without the flush, which marks a write-through disk `unflushed`, and a `flush_now`, their polled flush alone: ATA, AHCI, NVMe (without FUA), and virtio-blk with its cache on. A partition view passes both through. The root's backing writes its runs with `write_cached` (`krffs_disk_write`), and `pc_sync` calls the backing's flush (`krffs_disk_flush`) at most once per pass. A completed write sets a pending flag, which the flush clears at its start. Writes completed during that flush set the flag for a later pass. A concurrent pass that finds a flush in flight issues its own and reports its own result. `pc_sync_wait` repeats the pass when it meets a pin, and flushes again if the completed write needs it. A failed flush fails the sync with `PC_SYNC_FAILED` and is told to the root's failure hook, which records it for the next fsync, and the next sync flushes again; it halts the root where a failed write would, during the mount and the reap.

Every caller of `pc_sync` keeps its contract, since the flush is inside it: the barrier (`krffs_root_flush`, `pc_sync_wait`), fsync, fdatasync, sync and syncfs of the root, the flusher's periodic pass, the mount's dirty flag and the reap, `krffs_disk_mark_clean`'s two syncs around the clean flag, and the shutdown's. Evictions, the flusher's background writes and the writer's throttle now leave their runs in the disk's cache, and the next sync flushes them, as Linux's writeback does. The buffer cache, devfs's raw writes and every other caller of `write` are unchanged: `write` still flushes, and `transfer` calls it for a fault-safe device. A USB stick and a loop device have neither function and never back the root.

## Measurements

Each boot ran the probe from the debug shell on a private copy of the etude disk, under KVM with `-cpu max`, 128 MiB and one processor, on IDE with QEMU's default cache, so each flush is an fdatasync of the image on the host. A kernel with temporary counters, not kept, counted the root's barriers (`pc_sync_wait` calls), its runs (`krffs_disk_write` calls) and the disk's commands and flushes (`/proc/disk`'s counters), and printed them at the shutdown. The two kernels of a row ran at the same time, on the testing host at a load of 3 to 23. Every assertion of each probe passed in each boot, and each shutdown cleared the root's dirty-mount flag.

| `fdstale` (the fdlock orphans case), i686 | 54,324 | 80,366 | 80,366 | 54,333 | 448.5 s | 300.1 s |
| `fdstale`, x86_64 | 54,324 | 80,366 | 80,366 | 54,331 | 413.2 s | 255.3 s |

A barrier of `fdstale` writes 1.48 runs on average. The flushes fall by about a third and the disk's commands from 160,785 to 134,755. Each `pc_sync` pass makes at most one flush. Mount and periodic writeback also call `pc_sync`. `stressprobe`'s syncs make fewer flushes than barriers, since a barrier that finds nothing written since the last flush sends none.
