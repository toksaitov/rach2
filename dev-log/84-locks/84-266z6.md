Linux on the same Lenovo fails the test in the same way in the same setup, four piglit tests at a time through GBM; the reference's pass came from a rerun of the test alone. The expectation now keeps Linux's result of the run Rach2's case repeats. Rach2 has no fault here, and no delay of a waiter enters the failing check.

## What the test checks

piglit's `timestamp-get` reads the GPU's clock two ways: `glQueryCounter(GL_TIMESTAMP)` then `glGetQueryObjecti64v(GL_QUERY_RESULT)`, the timestamp the render ring writes when it reaches the query's PIPE_CONTROL, and `glGetInteger64v(GL_TIMESTAMP)`, which iris reads at once through I915_REG_READ of the render ring's timestamp register. It takes the difference of two consecutive queries as the query's overhead and of two consecutive reads as the read's, sets its tolerance to their sum and 3 ms, and then checks two orders: a query and then a read, and a read and then a query. Either difference over the tolerance is `too big difference`.

## The two failures

Rach2's output, from the issue:

    glGet overhead: 32 us
    glQuery overhead: 55177 us
    Test: first glQuery, then glGet
    Test: first glGet, then glQuery
    time 1 = 3711833 us
    time 2 = 3811383 us

Linux's, the test's row in step 9's `tests/graphics/ref/lenovo/piglit/results.json.bz2`, Debian's Crocus through GBM, four tests at a time, at 20:11:18 UTC on 2026-11-08:

    glGet overhead: 25 us
    glQuery overhead: 28283 us
    Test: first glQuery, then glGet
    Test: first glGet, then glQuery
    time 1 = 40401704 us
    time 2 = 40503637 us
    too big difference

Both fail the second order, by 99.5 ms on Rach2 and 101.9 ms on Linux. There time 1 is the register read before the query and time 2 the timestamp the GPU wrote on reaching the query. The GPU writes time 2 before the batch's breadcrumb and its interrupt, so the wait for the query's result, its fence, interrupt and wakeup, cannot change it. The difference is the time from the read to the render ring's reaching the query's batch: the batch's submission and the other tests' batches ahead of it on the ring, which runs every context's batches in order on Haswell. The query overhead, 55 ms here and 28 ms on Linux, counts the same queue once more between two queries.

The tests that ran beside Linux's, by their times in the same file, were `arb_texture_view@rendering-formats`, which draws for 5 s, `arb_timer_query@query gl_timestamp`, `arb_timer_query@query-lifetime` and two of `arb_transform_feedback2`. The 38 s of dEQP-VK reruns that step 9 ran beside its piglit run ended at 20:07:51 UTC, three minutes before, so Linux failed beside piglit's own tests alone, as Rach2's case runs. Run alone in step 9's rerun, the test passed on Linux with a query overhead of 187 us.
