# 84-264e: where Rach2's time goes under KVM

Under KVM in WSL on the testing PC, Rach2's `default` suite on x86-64 took 1686.6 s of summed case time against 718.8 s under TCG, and made 63.2 million exits, 97% of them port I/O that QEMU answers in user mode. Two causes hold nearly all of them. The PS/2 mouse probe spins on the i8042's status port until each of about six waits times out, and takes 3.02 s of every boot under KVM against 0.12 s under TCG: about 400 s of the suite, 24%. The 8250 serial port, which carries the harness's console and stages the probes' files into the guest, makes 22.2 million exits, about 209 s, 12%. PCI configuration, the VGA cursor, the HPET counter, interrupts and every in-kernel exit together make under 1%. Debian 13 under the same KVM and QEMU makes 327,482 exits for a boot, most of them its firmware's and its serial kernel console's, 148 a second idle, and 21,605 for 512 MiB of direct disk I/O on virtio-blk with 200,000 lines over SSH on virtio-net. It changes no code.

## The runs

All on the testing PC's WSL, QEMU 10.2.1 (Debian 1:10.2.1+ds-1ubuntu3.3), WSL's kernel 6.18.58.1, on 2026-11-14 from 21:18 to 22:00 UTC+6, at host load averages of 13 to 56 from other accounts' runs. The tree was milestone/76-display at b3d9c8687.

* `tests/run x86_64:default` (138 cases, each a boot of the test image with one processor, 128 MiB, an RTL8139 and the serial console, no disk) under TCG, then under KVM with [84-264e/qemu-system-x86_64](84-264e/qemu-system-x86_64) first on PATH, which reads KVM's per-vCPU statistics through QMP's `query-stats` every 0.2 s ([poll.py](84-264e/poll.py)). The statistics cost no exits of their own: `signal_exits` stayed at 26 over the run.
* The same suite under KVM with QEMU's `memory_region_ops_read` and `_write` trace events, counted by region, address, size and direction ([count.awk](84-264e/count.awk)), with `ext2`, `ssh` and `diskrate` beside it for the disks and the network. A traced exit costs more, so the time-bounded spins below read fewer times under the trace; the other counts do not depend on it. A run of the default suite with `kernel-irqchip=split`, which moves the PIT and the PIC into QEMU, found 8,899 accesses to them in the 100 of its 138 runs whose counts were kept whole, and in the traced run the port accesses that reached QEMU, 41.66 million, were no fewer than KVM's port exits, 41.51 million, so KVM answers almost none of Rach2's port I/O itself.
* The Debian reference guest, `tests/graphics/debian-ref/debian-ref.sh run -c 1 -m 2048` under KVM with the same wrapper and trace: a boot to SSH and power-off running `true`, the same with `sleep 30`, and the same with 256 MiB written and read back with O_DIRECT, 200,000 lines printed over SSH and 64 MiB hashed.
* The cost of one exit, from [bios.S](84-264e/bios.S): a 64 KiB firmware image that performs one operation a million times and leaves through `isa-debug-exit`, three times each, less the empty image's 0.14 to 0.18 s. KVM counted 0.99 million exits for each.
* [analyze.py](84-264e/analyze.py) sums a run's statistics and counts by case, and [casetimes.py](84-264e/casetimes.py) a history stream's case times.

A time is the count by the cost of its kind above, but for the i8042, whose waits end on the clock. The exits counted hold about 627 s of KVM's 1686.6 s; the rest of KVM's 967.8 s over TCG was not traced here.

The mouse probe: each boot logs `mouse: started` at 0.09 to 0.12 s and `mouse: ready` 3.02 s later under KVM, 0.116 s later under TCG, in every console kept. `ps2_wait_mouse_output` in `rach2/sys/arch/x86/ps2mouse.c` and `ps2_wait` in `i8042.c` read the status port with no delay until 100,000 reads (`PS2_TIMEOUT`) or 0.5 s (`PS2_TIMEOUT_US`) have passed. Under TCG a read is a function call and 100,000 come in a few milliseconds; under KVM each is an exit, so each wait that the device does not answer lasts 0.5 s, about six a boot. Linux's `i8042_wait_read` waits 50 us between reads and gives up after 10,000.

The serial port: every byte the harness sends the guest is an LSR read and a data read, and every byte the guest writes an LSR read and a data write. The data reads are the probes' files that `etude-guest --stage` takes over the console.

One boot, the shortest case (`dirtest`, 4.7 s of QEMU): 189,544 exits; 159,399 i8042 status reads, 24,655 serial accesses, 2,106 PCI configuration accesses, 1,162 HPET reads, 824 VGA cursor writes, 400 reads of port 0x80, 1,514 in-kernel exits of the other kinds and 20 HLTs.

Idle: the restart probe, idle for most of its 89.4 s, made about 17,200 exits more than the shortest case beside its port I/O, about 200 a second, of them 60 HLTs.

## Debian 13 under the same KVM

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| boot to SSH and power-off | 20.9 | 327,482 | 208,213 | 23,592 | 1,830 | 83,521 |

| the same with the disk, SSH and hash work | 24.4 | 349,087 | 214,767 | 23,738 | 2,207 | 89,532 |

Its boot's accesses that reach QEMU: 102,053 serial (the kernel's console on ttyS0: 64,360 bytes and 30,703 LSR reads), 52,869 PCI configuration, 18,809 VGA ports, 20,914 APM control, 5,624 RTC, 5,423 port 0x92, 3,186 virtio-blk ISR reads by the firmware and 3,035 MSI writes; its 255,224 VGA memory writes are coalesced by QEMU and are not exits. Idle it adds 4,223 exits in 28.6 s, 148 a second. Its whole workload adds 21,605 exits: virtio's notifications go to KVM's ioeventfds and never reach QEMU, and the work's only accesses there of note are 6,268 coalesced VGA writes and 3,540 APM control accesses.

## What it orders

By the gain each measures in the suites above:

1. The mouse probe's waits (filed as 84-264k): 2.9 s of every boot under KVM, about 24% of the default suite.
2. virtio-console (84-264h): 22.2 million exits, about 12% of the default suite, and 2.0 million in `ext2`.
3. virtio-blk (84-264i): none in the default suite, which has no disk; 879,433 IDE exits in `ext2` and 3.2 million in `diskrate`, about 8 and 30 s.
4. kvm-clock (84-264f): 123,229 HPET reads in the default suite, 1.2 s.
5. virtio-net (84-264g): 7,959 RTL8139 accesses in the default suite and 3,447 in `ssh`, under 0.1 s.

## Limits

* No root on the testing PC's WSL from a team account, and no `perf` for its kernel, so KVM's in-kernel exits are counted by KVM's statistics and not split by MSR or exit code, and no exit is timed on the host; the times are the counts by the measured cost of their kind. The in-kernel exits other than port I/O are 1.8% of the default suite's and 25% of Debian's boot.
* `procprobe` (ticks-counted-once) and `smptlbmusl` failed under KVM in each run, and `callsmusl` (itimer-first-tick) and `smptlbmusl` under TCG, at host load averages of 47 to 56; they were not traced.
