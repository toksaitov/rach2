# 84-56: path lookup for openat and newfstatat without the big lock

`openat` and `newfstatat` already walked a path without the big lock when the name cache held every name of it (`resolve_cached`, after Linux's walk under RCU, `filename_lookup`, fs/namei.c:2651-2653). In WineD3D's GL round trip under trip.sh most of their remaining holds were paths that the cached walk gave up on: every path under a trial boot's `/data`, which is the old root left as a nameless mount and entered by name, and each `..` in a link's target, such as Wine's `dosdevices/c:`, a link to `../drive_c`. The cached walk now enters a nameless mount by its name from its parent's root, as the full walk's `mount_named` does, and climbs a `..` to the directory it came down through, as `follow_dotdot_rcu` climbs to `d_parent` (fs/namei.c:1968-2006): from a covering mount's root to the directory the covered one is in, from a nameless mount's root to its parent's root, and at the caller's root it stays. The kept directory is trusted only while no unlink or rename has begun since the walk started (`vfs_name_seq_still`), as Linux checks the child's `d_seq` (fs/namei.c:1994).

## The measurement

QEMU on the testing PC under KVM, four processors and 8 GiB, booted with the t3513 trial root from a data disk (`rach2.rootimage=`), as 80-27's Lenovo trial boots, running `tests/system/games/trip.sh -r llvmpipe -n 3`. The holds by entry are the differences of `bkl_hold_entry` in `/proc/stat` across each plain loop of 1,000 round trips, from the run's `trip-gl-N.loop.txt`.

Three kernels, all on the base of the baseline: the head with the copy-on-write fix below (before), with the cached walk's `..` and nameless mounts (walk), and with the failed open's description ended without the big lock as well (walk and fd). Per plain loop, holds and microseconds held:

| walk | 52.9, 42.1, 51.4 | 291 / 2,315; 182 / 2,555; 253 / 2,400 | 0; 0; 0 | 18,570 / 10,383; 17,515 / 10,181; 19,459 / 11,742 |
| walk and fd | 56.3, 46.8, 46.0 | 116 / 2,408; 104 / 2,436; 112 / 2,583 | 0; 0; 0 | 19,771 / 11,285; 15,996 / 10,368; 18,007 / 11,247 |

The round trip did not move beyond its spread between runs, 42 to 60 ms in each kernel, against the 638 to 884 ms 80-27 recorded for this loop in QEMU before the stack's earlier work; the waits for the big lock over all entries are 7 to 12 ms a loop in every run. On this setup the two calls were a small part of the lock's use; the Lenovo, where 80-27 counted 1,117 to 1,490 `openat` holds a loop, is measured by 84-58.

A scratch kernel that recorded the caller of each big-lock take inside `openat` accounted for every hold of a loop: after the walk, 90 to 92 were opens that went to the locked path, 73 to 201 the end of the reserved description of an unlocked open that failed, most of them `ENOENT` for a name the cache holds as missing (`.Xdefaults-rach2`, looked up by every Wine process at each X connection), and 24 inotify's report of an open on `/data`, which wineserver watches. The second went because `ofd_end_big` asked whether any record lock existed anywhere, and Wine and Mesa hold some; a description whose slot was never published can hold none, so `fd_free` of a slot still reserved now ends it without the big lock, as Linux's failed open gives its descriptor back under the table's lock alone (`put_unused_fd`, fs/file.c:569-575, from fs/open.c:1423).

## What stays under the big lock

Counted by path over a loop, from the same scratch kernel's counters:

* `/proc` and the other synthetic types: about 75 opens a loop are trip.py's own watcher reading `/proc/PID/comm`, `/proc/PID/task/TID/stat` and `status` and `/proc/stat`, the measurement itself; 7 to 8 are Wine's writes of thread names to `/proc/PID/task/TID/comm`, and 2 to 5 other reads, such as Xwayland's of `/proc/PID/cmdline`. These hold about 23 us each, most of the 2.4 ms a loop that `openat` still holds. procfs builds each file's text under the big lock from process records that other entries change under it, in `fs/procfs.c` (9,908 lines) through `synthfs.c`'s walk (`lookup_rest`); taking that off is work of its own beyond this task, which the issue says.
* An open for writing or with `O_CREAT`, `O_TRUNC`, `O_PATH` or `O_TMPFILE`: Mesa's shader cache opens `mesa_shader_cache_db/part0` with `O_RDWR|O_CREAT` 6 times a loop.
* inotify's report of an open on a watched file system (`open_install_unlocked`), about 24 a loop.
* A path relative to a descriptor, a `..` above the directories the cached walk came down through or after an unlink or rename since it began, a link whose target the inode does not hold, and every type but ext2. None of these came up in the loop.

## A fault met on the way

The baseline run did not start: trip.sh's private Weston on llvmpipe died with a bad write (error 0x7) in two of three starts, on the head and on the kernel of 80-27's trial. Two threads' copy-on-write faults on one page raced, since faults run without the big lock: the second found the entry already made writable by the first and copied the page again. `cow_fault` now leaves an entry that is present and writable to be run again, as Linux's `handle_pte_fault` calls `do_wp_page` only for an entry that is not writable (mm/memory.c:5799-5805). Weston then started five times of five.

## Build

Both kernels linked with `make ARCH=i686 rach2` and `make ARCH=x86_64 rach2` at each commit. The cached-walk rows of `tests/host/fixtures/krffs_open.c` were run alone. No upstream fault was found.
