# 84-159: the 250 Hz conversion after j137

The six findings are fixed in the task branch. The complete pre-change inventory, posted on the issue before the code, is [84-159/tick-uses.md](84-159/tick-uses.md). It covers 179 source files, including declarations, comments, device clocks and occurrence counters returned by the searches, and lists the missing duration boundaries and every rate divisor separately. No fault in another project's code was found.

* Scheduler: a slice event requires an eligible competitor that selection could take. Lower-priority work under RR arms none. An expired event sets the reschedule request once instead of asking for another immediate event. A selection that keeps the current thread refreshes its slice. The host rows record timer requests and cover lower priority, equal priority, expiry and affinity changing before selection.
* Calibration: TSC counts per tick are the full product of the measured TSC counts and reference frequency divided by the measured reference counts times HZ. HZ/4 is never truncated. APIC phase scales before dividing by HZ too.
* Durations: wireless management, BSS expiry, BlockAck, fragments, rekeying, ath9k calibration, recovery and rfkill periods convert milliseconds to ticks where they enter arithmetic. BlockAck's TU timeout rounds up directly to ticks. The beacon interval's millisecond conversion also splits its product to keep a channel countdown from overflowing 32 bits. Netlink reports age and inactivity in milliseconds, last-seen boot time in nanoseconds and connected time in seconds, saturating each 32-bit duration in its own unit. The shared tick_msecs conversion, TCP's ISN rate, TTY and neighbour service intervals and the diagnostic readbacks keep multiplication ahead of division. The original literal wireless fixtures declare their one-millisecond clock with TICK_HZ=1000; production stays at 250.
* Timer calls: getitimer truncates positive nanosecond remainders to whole microseconds. alarm rounds the nanosecond remainder itself, preserving a positive remainder below one microsecond.
* Forcewake: the base fixture selects its uncore register lock in the publication stub. A publication while it is held traps at the call, before recording the deadline. The row checks the timestamp separately.

Both kernels compiled and linked. The affected host fixtures and the existing calibration fixture compiled and linked without being executed. No suite, host test, style check or guest boot was run by hand. The git hooks run at commit and push. Review and the manager's proof remain to be done.
