# 84-5256: Chromium's dropped frames on the Lenovo's 1080p H.264 and AV1 plays

The continued native run at 3ffd553f6 (run 1115-114117, the installed release on the Lenovo) failed `video/browsers` on two of Chromium's twelve plays by dropped frames, H.264 1080p and AV1 1080p. Both drops are inside the play-to-play spread that Linux's own run on the same machine shows, and the judge's rule could not tell them from it. `video/browsers` now judges a clip's median against Linux's most plus that spread, which `tests/graphics/ref/lenovo-video/spread` gives by browser. No fault was found in what Rach2 does for the decoders' threads, and none is claimed; what the measurements do not settle is said below.

## The run

Chromium decodes every clip in software (FFmpegVideoDecoder, VpxVideoDecoder, Dav1dVideoDecoder), so its drops measure processor time and frame timing. Three counted plays of each clip, 300 frames a play, both systems on a headless Weston of their own with the GL renderer:

| Clip | Rach2 | Linux | Old judge |
|---|---|---|---|
| H.264 720p | 0, 25, 0 | 0, 8, 0 | pass |
| H.264 1080p | 10, 4, 1 | 0, 0, 0 | fail, median 4 over 0 |
| VP9 720p | 29, 6, 9 | 12, 5, 2 | pass |
| VP9 1080p | 0, 0, 0 | 2, 6, 0 | pass |
| AV1 720p | 11, 2, 20 | 24, 24, 3 | pass |
| AV1 1080p | 24, 0, 16 | 0, 7, 6 | fail, median 16 over 7 |

Chromium dropped 157 of 5,400 counted frames on Rach2 and 99 on Linux; Firefox 137 and 134. Linux's warm-up plays, which the judge does not count, dropped 0 on every Chromium clip but AV1 720p's, which dropped 17.

## The spread

* Linux's own three plays of one Chromium clip range by up to 21 frames (AV1 720p, 24, 24 and 3), and of one Firefox clip by up to 22 (AV1 1080p, 0, 22 and 2).
* The judge failed a clip whose median of three plays exceeded the most of Linux's three. For two systems that drop alike, with untied counts, that happens in 4 of the 20 orders of the six counts, one clip in five, so a run of six clips fails in some three runs of four. Ties at zero lower that, but a clip Linux played three times without a drop, as H.264 1080p, then fails on any median above zero.
* Rach2's earlier Lenovo plays of the same clips, from 76-17b's second trial (`dev-log/76-display/lenovo-desktop/trial-2/`, on the desktop's compositor, before this milestone's lock work): H.264 1080p 2, 2, 2 and, in the traced play, 3, 2, 3; AV1 1080p 0, 1, 6, whose median passes against Linux's 7.

## What is not settled

Rach2's nine Lenovo plays of H.264 1080p each dropped 1 to 10 frames, where Linux's four, the warm-up's included, dropped none, and Linux's H.264 720p dropped 8 in one play of four. Three plays a side cannot tell whether that is a lasting difference of a few frames in 300 or the spread of one Linux run. Of the paths the issue names, timer slack is Linux's: `prctl`'s `PR_SET_TIMERSLACK` and a sleep that ends at the latest expiry of its range, as Linux's hrtimer_nanosleep does (`sleep_slack`, `rach2/sys/syscall.c`); timed waits are armed on each processor's event timer, not rounded to the tick (`rach2/sys/proc/Readme.md`, Design). The vsync clock is each side's headless Weston's own repaint timer. The scheduler does not read nice values (`rach2/sys/proc/Readme.md`, Scheduling), where Linux weighs a thread's share of a processor by its nice value; which nice values Chromium's threads had in these plays was not read, and no measurement here ties a drop to it. A Lenovo run of each clip with more plays on both systems would settle it.

## The change

* `tests/graphics/ref/lenovo-video/spread`: `chromium 21` and `firefox 22`, each the widest range of one clip's counted plays in that browser's run of `linux.tar.gz`, written as the gate's file is, with its measurements in its comment.
* `judge.py video` takes SPREAD after LIMITS: a median over the reference's most by no more than the browser's spread is printed as `judge: spread: ...` and is not worse. A clip that drops as 76-17b's first trial did, 172 to 181 a play, still fails.
* `gpuvideo.sh browsers` passes it, and `gpu.cases` puts it in `video/browsers`'s workspace.

On this run's plays the judge now passes all twelve, and judges H.264 1080p and AV1 1080p as `judge: spread: chromium-h264-1080p.mp4: dropped a median of 4 frames, the reference at most 0, within Linux's spread of 21` and the same for AV1 at 16 against 7. Without SPREAD it still fails them as before.
