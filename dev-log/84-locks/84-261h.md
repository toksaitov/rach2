# 84-261h: Chromium's WebGL chunks under QEMU's emulator

The release ran `machine.virgl.webgl-chromium-webgl1-00` and `-01` on x86-64 under TCG, since `tools/ci/kvm-suites` did not list `virgl`, and both failed: the browser ran no page, and the log ended with Chromium's `Failed to send GetTerminationStatus message to zygote`. Those lines come from `webgl.sh` ending the browser, not from a crash. The runner ends a browser that posts nothing for 120 s, and Chromium's start under TCG took longer than that between two events.

## Cause

Under TCG every clock read of Rach2's x86-64 kernel was a system call that read the HPET under a global lock. `timer_calibrate` measured the TSC only for KVM's stable clock, `rach2.tscreliable` or an invariant TSC, and QEMU's `-cpu max` under TCG, an AuthenticAMD family 6 model 6, sets no invariant bit (CPUID 0x80000007 EDX bit 8). With no rate the vDSO's clock stays off, so `clock_gettime` enters `rtc_monotonic`, takes `rtc_mono_lock` and reads the HPET. Under TCG an HPET read is an MMIO exit that takes QEMU's big lock, which the main thread holds while it renders for virgl, so the eight processors read the clock one at a time.

Linux 6.12 measures the TSC there. `unsynchronized_tsc` (`arch/x86/kernel/tsc.c:1270-1296`) takes the TSC as one clock on every processor for a constant TSC, a reliable one, any Intel processor, or a machine of one package, and the clocksource watchdog (`kernel/time/clocksource.c:422-560`) then checks it against the reference every 0.5 s and drops it once the two differ by more than the two uncertainty margins, some 250 us. Debian under the same TCG machine chose `tsc` as its clocksource.

## Fix

Two tasks of this milestone made the same two changes while this one traced the cause, and they are the fix.

* 84-260n and the tasks after it (84-261x, 84-262h, 84-262n) have `timer_tsc_watch` measure a TSC that nothing vouches for, against the HPET or the PM timer, for the vDSO alone, and `vclock.c` watch it against the tick's clock on each online processor in turn, as Linux's clocksource watchdog does, so that a clock read under TCG no longer enters the kernel. This task measured an earlier rule of its own, the TSC taken as Linux's `unsynchronized_tsc` takes it, an Intel processor or one package, and checked over 0.5 s windows; the row above marked "first rule" is that kernel's, and its commits did not reach the milestone branch.
* 84-261s lists `virgl` in `tools/ci/kvm-suites`, so that the release runs the suite under KVM, where its references in `tests/graphics/ref/virgl-wsl` were taken.
* This task adds `guest.vdso.watched`, which boots the TCG machine with no clock option and requires the vDSO to read the TSC and make no clock system call by the kernel's count.

## The watched TSC under Chromium

Chunk 00 under TCG at the milestone branch's head with the watched TSC passed twice, 247 of 250 pages each time. In the first run, beside six other QEMUs at a load of 12, the case took about 21 minutes, and the vDSO's clock stopped partway: /proc/stat's `vdso_tsc` was 1 at the start and 0 at the end, with 11,483,019 clock system calls. In the second, at a load of 5, it took 494 s, kept the TSC and made 1,775. Without KVM the watch's parting is the one path that stops the clock, but a passing case keeps no console, so the first run's log line, with the TSC's and the HPET's nanoseconds, is lost.

Reading the watch found one way it parts a TSC that is right. The HPET's counter is read as 32 bits at 100 MHz, and the tick's clock extends it past its wrap at each tick, so a stretch of 43 s with no tick loses 42.9 s from the tick's clock, as `pit.c` says. A span that holds such a stretch of 43.4 s ends half a second long by the tick's clock and 43.4 s long by the TSC; the watch clamped the TSC's count to twice the span and parted the clock. Linux skips an interval that either clock found longer than WATCHDOG_INTERVAL_MAX_NS, the greater of `cs_nsec` and `wd_nsec`, and the watch now begins such a span again unjudged. `vclock_hosttest`'s new row of that stretch fails on the old watch and passes on the new one. Whether the first run's stop was this stretch is not known.

During the release up to five virgl guests ran at once on the testing PC, while `tests/graphics/Readme.md` speaks of one at a time; this task did not change that.
