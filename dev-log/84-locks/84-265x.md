# 84-265x: when the host's D3D12 device went in the two webgl2-06 crashes, and how Debian's chunk 6 ended

Both Rach2 runs of `machine.virgl.webgl-chromium-webgl2-06` that ended in QEMU's fault in Mesa's D3D12 driver ([84-265r](84-265r.md)) lost the host's D3D12 device at page 1662 or 1663 of chunk 6, some 160 s before the fault, at the second of the NVIDIA driver's event 153. Mesa, virglrenderer, QEMU and the guest drew on until the next browser's first GL context, with black readbacks and no error. Debian's chunk 6 reference had its engine error in its last 15 s, after its first pass had run the same pages clean, and made no GL context after it. Rach2 has nothing to fix: the removal is the host's, and the guest does what Linux does after it.

## What was read

The two kept workspaces of task #5184 (the release's rerun and loop a's run at the branch head) hold the console, QEMU's standard error, the QMP capture and the guest disk's overlay; there is no `out.tar.gz`, which the case packs only after the runner ends. The GNU root's `/tmp` is on that disk, so the overlay holds the runner's work directory. The overlay's backing image belongs to another account and could not be opened, so a copy of the overlay was made standalone with `qemu-img rebase -u -b ''` and its ext2 file system read from the 64 KiB clusters it holds: the directory block of the runner's `RUNNER_WORK` names `events`, `port`, `attempt`, `profile`, `first` and `again`, and every block of `events` (293,317 and 295,880 bytes), `first` and `attempt` is in the overlay.

`server.py` stamps each event with the guest's `time.monotonic()`, its uptime. Each console logs the RTC at uptime 0.008 s, to the second: 21:58:44 UTC, 03:58:44 UTC+6 on 2026-11-15, in the rerun, and 22:30:32 UTC, 04:30:32, in loop a's run. Windows' event times are loop v's, read on #5183.

## When the device went

Wall times are UTC+6, and each may be up to 1 s later for the RTC's second.

| | rerun | loop a |
|---|---|---|
| last page whose readbacks pass | 1662 `deqp/functional/gles3/shaderbuiltinvar.html`, pass 104779, ends at uptime 484.143, 04:06:48.1 | 1659 `deqp/functional/gles3/samplerobject.html`, pass 2353, ends at 545.013, 04:39:37.0; 1660 `samplerstatequery.html` and 1661 `shaderapi.html`, which read back no image, pass |
| first page that fails from its first subtest | 1663 `shadercommonfunction.html`, starts at 484.144, fails from `common.abs.float_lowp_vertex` | 1662 `shaderbuiltinvar.html`, starts at 545.490, 04:39:37.5, fails all 30 of its rendered subtests |
| NVIDIA's event 153 | 04:06:48.267 | 04:39:38.046, with a watchdog live dump |
| the harness's `done` | 643.36, 04:09:27.4 | 706.268, 04:42:18.3 |
| QEMU's fault | 04:09:32 | 04:42:24 |

Every page before 1662 has Debian's status and counts in both runs. From the device's loss to the end of the first pass, 37 and 38 pages that pass in Debian's reference fail, most with "expected: 0,255,0,255 was 0,0,0,0", and the pages run fast, 0.2 s for most conformance2 pages. `attempt` is empty and `again` exists, so the runner had begun `run_pages` for the 63 and 64 failed pages: QEMU faulted as the second Chromium started, 4.6 and 5.7 s after `done`.

The reset was not a timeout of work submitted 2 s before it: the NVIDIA driver's journal holds a GPU memory page fault and a graphics engine class error for these crashes, and the guest's readbacks ran right up to the page change, which a stuck queue would not allow ([84-266a](84-266a.md)). Debian's first pass ran every page to 1676 clean.

## What QEMU, virglrenderer and the guest did

No record of the command stream exists: QEMU ran without virgl tracing, and the guest's kernel logs no commands. QEMU's standard error is the one line "D3D12: Removing Device." in both runs, and QEMU prints `virtio_gpu_virgl_process_cmd: ctrl ..., error ...` for each command virglrenderer refuses, so no command failed at the virtio level from boot to the fault. The guest's console has no kernel message after the boot's but the block request pool's at 11 s. The guest saw no error and no lost context, and ANGLE drew on, as Linux does after a reset in [`ref/virgl-wsl/lost`](../../tests/graphics/ref/virgl-wsl/Readme.md), where glreset draws on with no error and wrong pixels.

`/usr/lib/wsl/lib/libd3d12core.so` holds, beside "D3D12: Removing Device.", messages that name a removal's cause: "Removing device due to driver error.", "Removing device due to bad UMD error.", "Removing device due to driver-reported app error." and "UMD reported device removed.". None was printed. The task gives the line as 0.6 s before the fault; where that was timed is not in the workspace, so whether the line came at the removal or as Mesa's reset in `d3d12_context_create` released the device is not settled here.

## How Debian's chunk 6 ended

`tests/graphics/ref/virgl-wsl/webgl-chromium-2.0.1/record-06` is whole, from `runner` to `kernel_after`, with `list fetches failed 0`, on NVIDIA's 621.01. Its guest booted at 03:52:39 on 2026-11-11 (`btime 1794347559`), and its `kernel_after` CPU line sums to 4625.22 CPU-seconds over 8 CPUs, an uptime of 578 s, 04:02:17: `runner_finish` reads those counters after the last browser is stopped. NVIDIA's event 153 at 04:02:02 is uptime 563 s, 15 s before. The browser of the again pass is the run's last: after it the runner stops it, runs `events.py` and reads the counters, and starts no Chromium. So no GL context was made after the removal, Mesa never reset the screen, and no buffer went back to a freed cache manager.

`chromium-2.0.1-06.pages` has 26 pages that fail twice, and pages 1659 to 1676 pass. In its again pass the counts of 1702, 1717, 1719, 1736 and 1737 equal Rach2's after the removal and those of 1723 (1486 passed and 119 failed, against 1431 and 174) do not; since none of these pages is known to change its counts with the device, this does not place the removal within that pass.
