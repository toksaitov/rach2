# 84-183: a shared descriptor table looked up without its lock

84-165a measured the descriptor table's spin lock on the empty Unix receive: `fd_get` and the end of the call (`fd_holds_claim`) each took it on a table that threads share, so every thread's lookup wrote one shared line, and four threads' receive went from 0.498-0.531 to 0.442-0.460 us a call with the lock removed unsafely ([84-165](84-165.md#what-is-left-at-four-threads)). Linux's `fdget` takes no table lock: a table whose count is 1 is read as it is (`__fget_light`, fs/file.c:1114-1118), and a shared one is read under RCU, the file's reference taken by an increment that refuses 0 between two reads of the slot (`__fget_files_rcu`, fs/file.c:930-1003), the old array freed after a grace period (`expand_fdtable`, fs/file.c:207). This task brings both, with the grace periods they need.

## The change

A minimal RCU, `rach2/sys/lib/rcu.c`, after Linux's Tree RCU without `CONFIG_PREEMPT_RCU` ([rcu.Readme.md](../../rach2/sys/lib/rcu.Readme.md)):

* A read section is a section with preemption off, which a Rach2 kernel thread is already outside its sleeps and service points, so `rcu_read_lock` and `rcu_read_unlock` are compiler barriers.
* Each processor counts its quiescent states in a cache line of its own: 2 at each `schedule` and service point and at a tick in user mode, 1 as its idle halt begins and again as it ends, so an odd count is an idle processor, as Linux's `rcu_watching_snap_in_eqs` reads one (kernel/rcu/tree.c:306).
* `call_rcu` appends to its processor's list; the grace-period thread, `rcu_sched`, moves every list to the grace period it begins, reads the counts, waits until each processor online at the beginning has moved, been idle or gone offline, and runs the lists in order. `synchronize_rcu` waits for a callback of its own and returns at once while one processor is online, as Linux's `rcu_blocking_is_gp`.
* No preemptible RCU and no expedited grace period; the Readme's Limits say so.

The lookup and the end of a call, in `rach2/sys/fs/fd.c`, with the contract in [the fs Readme](../../rach2/sys/fs/Readme.md):

* A call holds what it looks up by a bit in its thread's map (`struct fd_hold_map`), which a close or `dup2` by another sharer reads under the table's lock, as before. `fd_get` on the caller's shared table now reads the slot's own entry, sets the bit by a locked compare-and-exchange (`hold_set`), and reads the entry again, open and still the number's, with no lock. A close stores `FD_FLAG_CLOSING` by a locked exchange before it reads the maps (`fd_close_claim`), and so does `dup2` for an open entry it replaces (`gone_make`), so of the two at once, either the close or `dup2` finds the bit or the second look finds the mark. A slot found changing, a number that leads to a spare entry `dup2` made, and a call that keeps an entry `dup2` replaced take the lock, as before.
* An entry read without the lock is judged by its flags and reservation before its type. A release clears the type first (`slot_clear`), and a `dup2` into a free slot writes the type last (`slot_fill`), so a half-cleared or half-filled entry is never taken for open.
* `fd_holds_claim` clears each bit by a compare-and-exchange and then reads the slot's mark (`slot_left`): a close that marked it after that read found the bit clear and released the slot itself, and a `dup2` that read the bit first moved it to a hold on the entry it replaced, so only a slot found marked or leading to a spare takes the lock (`hold_drop_locked`), as Linux's `fput` at a call's end takes none.
* A map never shrinks. A growth past a sharer's map replaces it under the lock with a wider copy, 512 descriptors at least so that two threads' maps seldom share a line: it marks the old one stale by a locked exchange before it copies it, so a bit the thread changed first reaches the copy and a thread that finds the mark after its change makes it again in the new map under the lock; the old map is freed by `call_rcu`.
* Every change of a word of a map is one locked compare-and-exchange (`hold_word_change`), since `gone_make` clears the bit of another thread's hold under the lock while that thread changes its other bits without it.

Linux's increment-not-zero has no counterpart: Rach2's call holds a slot, not a file reference, and the second look plays its part. Linux's `__fget_light` was already here (`fd_table_alone`).

The first form of this task, by the loop that held it before, was written against the `dup2` that answered `EBUSY` for a held slot; the milestone branch meanwhile gained Linux's `dup2`, which swaps a spare entry in and keeps the old one for the calls that hold it. The lookup was rebuilt on that: it reads only the slot's own entry, since a release may free a spare under the lock, and `gone_make` now marks the old entry closing before it reads the holds, where it had marked it after, which a lookup between the reading and the mark would have passed with a hold nobody saw.

One fault was met and fixed: `fd_table_fit` grew a shared table without widening the sharers' maps, so a hold past the old width was never set and a close by another sharer could release what the call used (it now grows through `fd_table_resize`, as every other growth does).

## The shared structures

The descriptor table, `fd_table_t`, and what the threads that share it keep:

| Field | Written by | Read by |
|---|---|---|
| `chunks[k]` | `chunks_reach`, under the lock, before the `cap` that covers it, by a volatile store; freed only in `fd_table_free` when no process has the table | lookups without the lock after `cap`, everything else under the lock |
| `cap` | `chunks_reach`, under the lock, volatile, after its chunk | `fd_table_cap` without the lock; `/proc`, the node holders' scan and `files_take` under the lock |
| `moved[k]` and its elements | `gone_make` (array and element, volatile, after the spare is filled) and `slot_settle` (element to NULL), under the lock | `fd_table_entry` and `slot_home_unlocked`, `slot_left` without the lock: a non-NULL element sends them to the lock |
| `spares`, `gone` | `gone_make`, `fd_free_after`, `slot_settle`, under the lock | under the lock alone |
| `refs` | share, put and copy, under the lock | `fd_table_alone` without the lock, which reads 1 only for the caller's own table |
| `next_free` | allocation and release, under the lock | under the lock alone |
| `sharers`, `fd_sharer_next` | `fd_table_join`, `fd_table_share`, `fd_table_put`, under the lock | `fd_held_elsewhere`, `gone_make`, `sharers_fit`, under the lock |
| entry `flags` | close, `dup2`, release and `fd_set_flags`, under the lock; the closing mark by a locked exchange | lookups and `slot_left` without the lock, volatile, before `pending` and `type` |
| entry `pending` | `fd_alloc_from`, `fd_install`, `fd_free`, under the lock | lookups without the lock, after `flags` |
| entry `type` | the opener while `pending` is set; `slot_fill` last; `slot_clear` first; under the lock | lookups without the lock, last |
| entry's other fields | the opener while `pending` is set, `fd_table_repoint` under the lock | the holding call, through the entry's address, after its second look |
| thread's `fd_held` pointer | `held_fit`, under the lock | the thread without the lock, in a read section; `fd_held_elsewhere` and `gone_make` under the lock |
| map `bits` | the thread by compare-and-exchange without the lock, or plainly under it; `gone_make` by compare-and-exchange under the lock | close and `dup2` under the lock; the thread |
| map `stale` | `held_fit`, by a locked exchange, under the lock | the thread after each change of a bit |
| thread's `fd_holding` | the thread alone, before the bit it covers | close and `dup2` under the lock; the system call's exit |
| thread's `fd_gone` | `gone_push` (volatile, after the hold is filled) under the lock; the thread pops | the thread without the lock; `holds_forget` under the lock |

Outside `fd.c`, `files_take`, the KRFFS node holders' count, `/proc/PID/fd`'s listing and link and the exec's staging read `cap`, `refs` or entries, each under the table's lock or on the caller's own unshared table; the system call's exit reads the thread's own `fd_holding` and `fd_gone`; `free_process` frees a thread's map after `fd_table_put` took the thread off the sharers under the lock.

## The host rows

`tests/host/rcu_hosttest.c` drives the grace-period thread's steps, one host thread playing four processors: a reader holds a grace period through the others' quiescent states and its own kernel ticks and a user tick ends it; one processor's callbacks run in order and one queued after a grace period began waits for the next; an idle processor is not waited for and one that left its idle wait is; a processor gone offline is waited for no more and its callback runs. It printed `rcu_hosttest: ok`.

`tests/host/fd_hosttest.c` links `rcu.c` and gives the atomics of its own, which run the other sharer just before or just after the exchange that sets or clears a hold:

* a close before the lookup's hold releases at once and the lookup fails; one after leaves the release to the lookup's call; a close just before or just after the call's end clears the hold leaves the release to it or releases at once;
* a `dup2` before the lookup's hold sends the lookup to the copy and leaves the old entry to the `dup2`'s call; one just after the hold moves it to the old entry, which the lookup's call releases at its end;
* a growth between the look and the hold replaces the map, the hold is set again in the new one, where the close finds it, and both sharers' old maps are freed only after a grace period; a call's end across a growth clears the hold in the new map too.

Each was run against the code with one part taken out: without the second look the close row and both `dup2` rows fail; without setting the hold again in a replaced map the close finds no hold; with the old map freed at once the growth rows fail. The test printed `fd_hosttest: pass: ...` with the code whole.

## Measured in QEMU

84-165's `mutexmusl` and setup: the testing PC, `qemu-system-x86_64 -enable-kvm -cpu max -smp 4 -m 2048`, serial console, one machine at a time, the binary as the init of a KRFFS root for Rach2 and of an initramfs for Linux, Debian's 6.12.107+deb13-cloud-amd64, on 2026-11-19 between 10:27 and 10:28 UTC+6. "Base" is the milestone head this task rebased onto, 6fb5a7354; "fix" is this task's change on it. Other work ran on the host, a team account's suite under KVM and a TCG guest, at a load average of 5 to 6, the same for every kernel, which were booted in turn. A third round, as the load rose to 9, slowed every kernel, base's one-thread row from 0.11 to 0.43 us, and is kept in the output but not used. Output is in [84-183/qemu.txt](84-183/qemu.txt).

Four threads' empty receive is 0.415-0.436 us in six boots of seven, at or below 84-165a's 0.46 us without the lock, and 0.505 us in the seventh, whose 127 mutex sleeps were twice the others'; base gave 0.606-0.711 us beside it; 84-165a's change alone had given 0.498-0.531 us on 2026-11-13. Three threads went from 0.38-0.40 to 0.21-0.23 us, faster than Linux as booted and near its 0.13-0.19 us without mitigations. Four threads are within 0.02 to 0.11 us of Linux without mitigations, 0.328-0.412 us here.

With the table's lock gone, the receive mutex is what four threads queue on: its sleeps went from under 20 to 39-127 a row of 800,000 calls, since a thread now reaches it sooner. The spin lock under the mutex and the socket's state lock are 84-182's, Linux's queued spin lock; it is not on the milestone branch yet, so the row with both waits for it.

## Failures

* The first form of the lookup, written before the milestone branch's `dup2` changed, no longer applied: the branch had been rebased and the new `dup2` writes the old entry's closing mark after it reads the holds. The lookup was rebuilt on the new `dup2`, and the order in `gone_make` changed.
* The third QEMU round ran into other work on the host and slowed every kernel; it is not used.
