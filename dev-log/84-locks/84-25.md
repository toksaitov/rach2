### What still holds and waits

SuperTuxKart's 3.8 s of waits in entries, by line of `calls`:

* `syscall 16`, ioctl: 1.69 s, in 1,249,823 holds for 696,748 calls. Of its commands, GEM_BUSY (`0xc0086457`) waited 0.61 s, MADVISE (`0xc00c6466`) 0.47 s and SYNCOBJ_WAIT (`0xc02864c3`) 0.39 s.
* `syscall 271`, ppoll: 0.42 s; `vector 0`, holds begun in no entry: 0.40 s; `syscall 7`, poll: 0.19 s; `read`: 0.14 s; the idle tasks: 0.13 s.

The ioctl's waits are its two takes for each unlocked DRM call. `ioctl_fd` takes the big lock for every DRM description (`syscall.c:2723-2728`), since `file_ops_unlocked` refuses a description that keeps its device node's inode in `dev_mnt` (`syscall_fs.c:5586-5595`); `drm_ioctl` then gives it back for a `DRM_UNLOCKED` row and takes it again after the copy out (`drm.c:989`, `1026`). GEM_BUSY and MADVISE are `DRM_UNLOCKED` and make two holds a call each (656,534 holds in 328,267 MADVISE calls); SYNCOBJ_WAIT's row in `drm.c:840` has no `DRM_UNLOCKED`, so it runs locked throughout, where Linux 6.12's DRM core runs every row without a global lock. Entering the DRM dispatcher unlocked, with the dispatcher taking the big lock itself for a row that needs it, would remove both takes from every unlocked call, and marking the syncobj rows unlocked would remove SYNCOBJ_WAIT's hold.

The big lock's longest holds by line, SuperTuxKart: the idle tasks 1.31 s, `vector 32` (the tick) 1.25 s, ioctl 1.21 s, `take 1` 1.06 s, `syscall 232` (epoll_wait) 0.61 s, `syscall 162` (sync) 0.51 s, `write` 0.47 s. 0 A.D.: `vector 32` 2.75 s, `take 1` 1.50 s, ioctl 1.32 s, epoll_wait 0.99 s, `write` 0.77 s, `vector 0` 0.70 s, `syscall 56` (clone) 0.64 s, `syscall 262` (newfstatat) 0.63 s.
