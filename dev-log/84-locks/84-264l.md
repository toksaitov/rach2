# 84-264l: procprobe's tick rows on a loaded host

procprobe's `ticks-counted-once` failed under KVM in each run of the default suite that 84-264e made on a loaded host. Its sum, idle and busy, left out the ticks the kernel counts as steal. KVM's steal-time record, which `kvmsteal.c` registers, puts every tick the host keeps from the guest's processor there, and /proc/uptime counts it with the rest. The check now adds /proc/stat's iowait, irq, softirq and steal columns to its sum, Linux's arithmetic, in which the columns from user to steal sum to the time each processor has been up. Under the same load the busy half then counted too few ticks, since a spin of 600 ms of the clock was charged mostly steal; the spin now runs for 600 ms of the thread's own CPU time, which a stolen tick does not count, so busy reaches its 60 ticks however much the host takes.

## The runs

`guest.default.procprobe` alone under KVM on the testing PC's WSL, on 2026-11-19 between 11:57 and 12:11 UTC+6, with 40 or 64 shell loops spinning on the host for the length of each run. The case boots one processor. Counts are in hundredths of a second over the rows' sleep and spin, as the probe printed them; "other" is iowait, irq, softirq and steal, of which only steal is ever nonzero on Rach2.

| Probe | Arch | Host load | Uptime | Idle | Busy | Other | Old sum's drift | New sum's drift |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sum fixed, clock-bound spin | x86-64 | 14 to 30 | 122 | 45 | 13 | 64 | 64 | 0 |
| both fixed | x86-64 | 44 | 149 | 59 | 60 | 30 | 30 | 0 |
| both fixed | x86-64 | 41 to 49 | 126 | 60 | 60 | 7 | 6 | 1 |
| both fixed | x86-64 | 49 to 53 | 153 | 60 | 60 | 33 | 33 | 0 |
| both fixed | i686 | 3 to 31 | 318 | 58 | 61 | 200 | 199 | 1 |
| both fixed | i686 | 31 to 46 | 240 | 58 | 60 | 123 | 122 | 1 |

The old check allows 4 ticks for each processor, so each of these runs would have failed it. In the first run, with the sum fixed and the spin still bound by the clock, `ticks-busy-enough` failed with 13 busy ticks against its 20.

## Linux

Linux charges a tick that the steal clock covers to steal and to no task (`steal_account_process_time` and `account_process_tick`, kernel/sched/cputime.c:253-269 and 486-492 in 6.12.115), as Rach2's `sched_account_tick` does, so the old sum of idle and busy and the clock-bound spin would fail on Linux under the same load. No Linux guest was run for this.

## Left

Nothing in the kernel: the missed-tick accounting (`accounted_ns` in `sched_account_tick`) and the steal column were already in place.
