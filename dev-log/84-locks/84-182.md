# 84-182: the spin lock hands itself on in order, as Linux's queued spin lock

Rach2's spin lock was a test-and-set word: its releaser could take it again ahead of every waiter, and every waiter spun on the one line the holder writes. 84-165a measured it ([84-165](84-165.md), "What is left at four threads"): a plain-store release alone made the four-thread empty Unix receive slower, since the releaser took the lock again first. [84-182](https://github.com/toksaitov/rach2/issues/4912) gives the lock Linux's queued design, measures it beside Linux in QEMU, and keeps Linux's test-and-set waiters for a guest whose processors the host may preempt.

## The change

In `rach2/sys/lib/spinlock.c`, with its contract in `spinlock.h` and its design in [spinlock.Readme.md](../../rach2/sys/lib/spinlock.Readme.md):

* The word is Linux's (`qspinlock_types.h`): the locked byte, the pending byte and a tail that names the last waiter's processor and nesting level. The take is one compare-and-exchange of 0 to 1, the release one store of 0 to the locked byte (`arch_store_u8`, a new call of `arch.h`, a plain store after a compiler barrier on x86).
* The slow path is Linux's `queued_spin_lock_slowpath` without its paravirtual hooks: the first waiter sets the pending bit and takes the lock with one half-word store (`arch_store_u16`) when the holder lets go; the others queue on places of their own, four for each processor, one for each level of waits that may nest; only the pending waiter and the queue's head read the word.
* A try takes only a word that is wholly 0, so it fails while any processor waits, as Linux's `queued_spin_trylock`.
* A waiter calls `smp_service` on every turn, as the test-and-set waiter did, since a holder that drops a translation on every processor waits for each one's answer. A wait nested in that service, or in an NMI, for the lock its own outer wait is for, stops with a panic; the queue would never reach it.
* Under a hypervisor, CPUID leaf 1's ECX bit 31, waiters take the lock by test-and-set instead (`arch_spin_virt`), unless KVM's `KVM_HINTS_REALTIME` says each processor is the guest's alone. This is Linux's `virt_spin_lock` (arch/x86/include/asm/qspinlock.h:88-110), which a guest uses when it has no paravirtual spin lock (arch/x86/kernel/paravirt.c:56-60, arch/x86/kernel/kvm.c:1120-1166). The task's first form queued there too; the overcommitted rows below are why it changed.

The interface, lockdep's calls, `spin_lock_nested`, the irqsave forms and every caller are unchanged.

## The shared structures

* `locked`, the lock word, is written only in `spinlock.c`, by locked instructions (the take's, the try's and the slow path's compare-and-exchanges, which on x86 are full barriers) and by three plain stores: the release's store of the locked byte, the pending waiter's half-word store that takes the lock and clears the pending byte, and the undo of a pending bit that a waiter set when it found a queue. Each plain store writes bytes no other processor writes at that moment: the locked byte is the holder's, and the pending byte is the pending waiter's until it takes the lock. The tail's half changes only by compare-and-exchange, which keeps the low half as it finds it.
* Outside `spinlock.c`, the word is written only to make a free lock: the static initializers (`{0}`), `spin_lock_init`, and `wait_queue_init_key` (`proc/wait_queue.c:212`), each where the lock's record is made, and memory allocated zeroed. A grep of `.locked` and `->locked` over `rach2/` finds no other user of a spin lock's word; the other hits are `struct qnode`'s and the mutex queue's own `locked` (`proc/mutex.c`), `pty.c`'s pty lock flag and `ext2_batch.c`'s plan. `wait_queue.c:53-80` writes 1 and 0 only in its host model (`WAIT_QUEUE_HOSTTEST`). No assembly reads the word.
* The host tests that link `spinlock.c` are `spinlock_hosttest.c` and `lockdep_hosttest.c`, which got `arch_store_u8`, `arch_store_u16` and `arch_spin_virt`. Of the others, 133 link `compat/spinlock_host.c` and the rest stub the calls themselves, which keep 0 and 1 in the same field and never meet a pending bit or a tail; their checks of `!lock.locked` after the kernel's code read their own stubs.
* `qcpus`, the places: a place's `next` is written by the waiter behind it, after that waiter's own place is set; its `locked` by the waiter ahead, once, to make it the head; its `lock` and the processor's `depth` by its own processor alone, an interrupt or NMI on it putting `depth` back before it returns. A place is reused only after its processor's wait at that level ended. Tail codes name processors as their number + 1 in 14 bits; `NR_CPUS` is 64.
* `q_virt` is -1 until the first slow path reads `arch_spin_virt`; every processor reads the same answer, so two that store it at once store the same.
* The panic stops the other processors where they are, inside a wait too, and a stopped waiter keeps its place, so the lock passes none behind it. The panic path takes no spin lock after the stop: `console_lock` and `smp_serialized` answer at once while panicking (`kio.c`, `proc/smp.c`), and klog and pstore's RAM writer take none. A stopped processor could already hold any lock, which is why that path takes none.

## The host rows

`tests/host/spinlock_hosttest.c` runs `spinlock.c` with each waiting processor a call on a stack of its own (`compat/fiber_host.c`) that yields at every turn. Its rows: three waiters take the lock in the order they came, whatever order they are resumed in, and the releaser's try fails; a try fails while the lock is handed to a waiter that has not run, while that waiter holds it, and while a queued waiter has not run, and takes the free lock; each waiter, pending, at the head and behind it, answers a TLB request and keeps its place; two waiters asked inside their waits to take a second lock queue for it at the next level and keep their places in the first; a nested wait for its own outer wait's lock panics; and, with `q_virt` set, of two test-and-set waiters the one that runs takes the lock while the other's turn never comes.

Built with the runner's flags under ASan and UBSan, it printed `spinlock_hosttest: ok`. Against the test-and-set lock of the milestone head, nine checks failed:

```
spinlock_hosttest: fail: order: the releaser took the lock again ahead of its waiters
spinlock_hosttest: fail: order: the waiters took the lock out of the order they came in
spinlock_hosttest: fail: trylock: a try took the lock from the waiter it was given to
spinlock_hosttest: fail: trylock: a try took the lock ahead of the waiter queued for it
spinlock_hosttest: fail: tlb: a waiter that answered lost its place
spinlock_hosttest: fail: nested: lock_b did not go to its first waiter
spinlock_hosttest: fail: nested: lock_b's nested waiters took it out of the order they came in
spinlock_hosttest: fail: nested: lock_a's waiters lost their places to the nested waits
spinlock_hosttest: fail: same: a nested wait for the lock its own wait is for did not panic
spinlock_hosttest: 9 failed
```

With `q_virt` left 0 in the last row, the queue, its three checks failed, the first "virt: a waiter that runs did not take the lock ahead of one that does not".

## Measured in QEMU

84-165a's harness: `tests/musl/mutexmusl.c`, the same binary (SHA-256 `aaa0aaec16f98d02...`), pins one to four threads to processors 0 to 3, each making 200,000 `recv(MSG_DONTWAIT)` calls on an empty Unix stream socketpair, and prints the mean time of a call. The testing PC, `qemu-system-x86_64 -enable-kvm -cpu max -smp 4 -m 2048`, one machine at a time, on 2026-11-19 between 09:25 and 09:40 UTC+6, the kernels booted in turn. Rach2 is the x86-64 kernel at milestone/76-display 73b2e0738 with the old lock ("base"), with the queued lock that queues in a guest too ("queue", as bare metal runs it), and with the hypervisor's test-and-set waiters ("virt", as the task ships it); Linux is Debian's 6.12.107+deb13-cloud-amd64, which in this QEMU reports "kvm-guest: PV spinlocks enabled". Output and SHA-256 of every kernel are in [84-182/qemu.txt](84-182/qemu.txt).

The queue, as the Lenovo runs it, takes the four-thread call 3% under the old lock's mean. The kernel as shipped runs the test-and-set waiters in QEMU, and is 4% over it at four threads, 2% under at three, and within 3% at one and two. That is the plain-store release, Linux's, on a test-and-set lock, which 84-165a measured on the old lock as 7% slower at four threads: the releaser's next take finds the line still its own. The task's bound, a four-thread call no slower than now in QEMU, holds for the queue and not, by 4%, for the guest's test-and-set waiters; the rows below are why the guest has them.

### The lock's own figures

The scratch counters of the manager's 84-182 trace, keyed by lock and call site, in three more kernels built the same way, three boots each (in [qemu.txt](84-182/qemu.txt)). Every row's waits fall on one lock: the descriptor table's, taken twice a call, in `fd_get` and in `fd_holds_claim` at the call's end. The socket's state lock, taken once a call under the receive mutex, waited for nothing in any row. At four threads, 1,600,000 takes a row:

| Kernel | us a call, traced | Takes that waited | By the pending bit | By the queue | Cycles waited a call | Cycles a wait |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base | 0.577-0.625 | 48.6-51.1% | | | 956-1065 | 983-1044 |
| queue | 0.671-0.700 | 84.0-90.7% | 7.8-9.3% | 74.7-82.9% | 1553-1755 | 898-967 |
| virt | 0.613-0.639 | 44.1-46.3% | | | 1094-1243 | 1240-1342 |

The queue's waits are shorter each but nearly every take waits, since a releaser hands the lock to the next waiter instead of taking it again while its line is still its own; untraced it is the fastest at four threads, and traced the slowest, as each of its far more numerous waits reads the cycle counter twice. Linux takes no lock of the descriptor table on this call: `fdget` reads the table under RCU and counts the file's references with an atomic (fs/file.c:1107-1132), and its only spin lock on an empty receive is the socket's state lock under `u->iolock`, as Rach2's (net/unix/af_unix.c:2827-2872). Debian's kernel has neither `CONFIG_LOCK_STAT` nor `CONFIG_LOCK_EVENT_COUNTS`, so it gives no counts of its own.

### Overcommitted

The same boots with QEMU's every thread pinned to two host threads, so that the guest's four processors are preempted by turns, as on the testing host while suites fill it:

| Threads | Rach2 base | Rach2 queue | Rach2 virt | Linux |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 0.207-0.220 | 0.221, 98.743, 201.519 | 0.210-0.220 | 0.452-2.599 |
| 4 | 0.252-0.273 | 10.624, 21.982, 213.262 | 0.257-0.303 | 0.606-2.955 |
| boots | 3 | 3 | 3 | 5 |

The queue made five of its six rows 40 to 950 times slower than the old lock's mean: a waiter whose processor the host has preempted holds up every waiter behind it until the host runs it again, and the lock's holder hands the lock to it all the same. Linux's comment over `virt_spin_lock` names this ("fair locks have horrible lock 'holder' preemption issues"); Linux in this QEMU uses its paravirtual queue, whose waiters halt and are woken by the releaser's hypercall, and without that uses the test-and-set waiters, which Rach2 now does. With them the overcommitted rows are the old lock's.

## What is left

* The four-thread call is bound by the descriptor table's lock, which Linux does not take: in the traced virt kernel its waits are 1,094 to 1,243 cycles a call at four threads, 0.26 to 0.30 us of 0.61 to 0.64. 84-165a measured `fd_get` without the lock at 0.442-0.460 us. Linux's lookup under RCU is work of its own, not this task's.
* Rach2 has no paravirtual spin lock. Linux's under KVM halts a waiter after a bounded spin; Rach2's waiters must answer `smp_service`'s requests while they wait, which a waiter halted with interrupts off would not, so it is no small port.

## Failures

* My first boots of the three kernels ran beside another account's suite run of fourteen suites, at a load average of 9 to 13, and the same kernel's four-thread row moved by half between boots. I waited for the host to quiet and measured again; only the later boots are in the tables.
* The task's first form queued under a hypervisor too, and its commit went to the task branch before any boot. The overcommitted boots showed the stall, and the hypervisor's waiters followed. Its commit message gives the worst slowdown as 700 times; the rows above give 950.
* The manager's hand-over comment described its diff as the unfinished queued lock; the lock was already committed on the task branch, and the diff was the scratch trace, which this task used, keyed by call site as well, for the figures above.
