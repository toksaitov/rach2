# 84-5279: an ext2 inode freed twice under piglit's load

The release candidate 948779b3b5 panicked twice on the Lenovo on 17 November under piglit's shader_runner shard. The netconsole caught the second at 20:52:43 (UTC+6): `ext2: free of inode 202793, whose bit is clear`, then `panic: stopped: sleeplock: a give by a thread that does not hold the lock`, from shader_runner. The task's issue has the disk's state after the reset and `e2fsck -fn`'s reading of it.

## Cause

`em_reap` in `rach2/sys/fs/ext2.c` frees the dying nodes that last puts under a file lock leave on `em->reap`. Since 63f7fb3282 of 12 November, which let ext2's read, open, release and put run without the big lock, a caller without the lock looked at the list without it and took the lock only when it found a node, but then ran its loop of pops whatever it had found. `node_final` adds to the list under the big lock. So a caller that found the list empty, and then found a node that another processor queued in between, popped it without the lock, while a holder of the lock could pop the same head. Both ran `node_free` on one node: the first freed the inode and the node, and the second freed the inode again, found its bit clear, and gave back the lock of a node already freed. The callers without the big lock are the operations on names and the writers of a file's data, at their last unlock (`ext2_unlock_node`, `em_finish`, `em_finish_node`). Codex found the cause and the manager traced it against the code; the comment on the issue has the trace.

The fix takes the big lock before `em_reap` looks at the list a second time or takes a node. A caller without the lock that finds the list empty returns, and a node queued after its look is the next caller's, as one queued after a caller with the lock ends already was.

## The load

`tests/musl/inodechurnmusl.c` runs processes of threads in one directory. Each thread keeps a file open for writing, renames it over one of 32 shared names while it still writes it, and closes it a few steps later, so that the file is unlinked while its writes wait in the batch and a drain under another file's lock puts it for the last time; beside that it replaces, unlinks, opens and holds, stats, truncates and links the shared names. It reads the kernel's log at its end and fails on a free whose bit was clear. It is the case `machine.ext2.probe-inodechurnmusl`, on four processors for ten seconds, and the native case `musl/inodechurnmusl`, for thirty seconds on the installed root.

The runs below boot the i686 kernel built with `RACH2_LOCKDEP` under QEMU with KVM on the testing host, from the debug shell on an ext2 root of 256 MiB that holds the program, mounted to write back, three at once in three shapes.

## Results

At 948779b3b5, unchanged, the load ran for 600 seconds on 8 processors with 8 processes of 4 threads, on 4 with 4 of 4 under `rach2.blocklag=1`, and on 4 with 4 of 3: about 1.07 million operations in all, with no fault. The race's window is the time between two loads of the list's head, with no instruction between them, and in QEMU an interrupt landed there in none of the runs.

The window was then widened in a build of 948779b3b5 that is not committed, `84-5279/widen-948779b3b5.diff`: 50 microseconds after a look that found the list empty without the big lock, and 200 microseconds, logged, between taking the head and unlinking it when the lock is not held, with a log line at each 256th node queued. The load queued some 170 nodes a second. Each of four runs failed within 4 seconds of the load's start, one on 4 processors with 4 processes of 4 threads and three in the shapes the fix ran in below:

* `ext2: free of inode 18, whose bit is clear`, then `panic: stopped: sleeplock: a give by a thread that does not hold the lock`, the Lenovo's two lines, with the trace `sleep_lock_give` from `node_free` from `em_reap` from `ext2_unlock_node` from `file_unlock` in `sys_write`.
* `ext2: free of inode 25, whose bit is clear`, and then inode 45 popped twice without the lock and freed twice, then the same panic.
* The same panic from `em_finish_node` under `sys_write`, which gave back the lock of the writer's own node, freed under it.
* `panic: stopped: unrecoverable CPU exception in kernel mode`, after a pop without the lock.

The same widening on the fix, `84-5279/widen-fix.diff`, ran for 600 seconds, on 4 processors with 4 processes of 4 threads, with 8 of 2, and with 4 of 3 under `rach2.blocklag=1`: about 1.14 million operations and 281,000 nodes queued, 150 times the longest time to failure, with no pop without the big lock, no inode or block freed twice, no panic and no lock-order report. The fix unwidened ran in those shapes for 300 seconds, about 608,000 operations, with the same result.

## Host row

None was added. The old `em_reap` reads the list's head twice with no call between the reads when the first read finds it empty, so the host tests' model, whose processes take turns only at a call the model makes (`compat/park_host.c`, `compat/bkl_held.c`), cannot put another processor's queueing between them, and a row against the fix would pass on the old code too. The widened guest runs above stand in for it.

## The panic's trace

`k_panic` now logs `panic: Call Trace:` and the return addresses of the panicking thread's frames after its reason, through `platform_show_stack`, which the watchdog's reports already used; `addr2line -e` of the build's `kernel.debug` names them. The traces above come from it.
