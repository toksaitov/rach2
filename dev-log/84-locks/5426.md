# 5426: WineD3D's GL round trip on the installed release

The manager's loop on the Lenovo's installed release 7a2168e3c2, on 2026-11-19 at 13:15 UTC+6, took 311.3 ms for 1000 round trips, against 84-157's 281.6 ms and Linux's 224 to 251 ms. `wined3d_cs` had 133 involuntary switches against 84-157's 61, 37 of them by wineserver and 31 by ki915; the figures are on #4890 to #4894 and in the manager's checkout under `build/runs/t4890r`. That loop did not sample the GPU's clock. This task separated the clock from the scheduler on the Lenovo: the round trip follows the GPU's clock on Rach2 and on Linux alike, and at a matched clock Rach2's is within Linux's range, so no fix is needed (The Lenovo's figures, below).

## What the code and the earlier runs settle

The nearest measured kernel is 84-176's kernel B. Kernel B, built from `task/4904` at 7ed47a392, already held 84-164, 84-165, 84-166 and 84-168 with 84-176's park, and its three loops on 2026-11-16 took 225.9, 203.8 and 280.5 ms at a median 500, 450 and 200 MHz ([84-176.md](84-176.md)). Between kernel B and the release the kernel took 90 commits. Four change paths the loop runs:

* d856b1e27, i915's runtime power management, which kernel B left out. It lets the device sleep only after 10 s with no hold (`I915_RPM_DELAY_MS`), and the GT holds the device from each unpark to its park (`i915_request.c`), so the device cannot sleep inside a loop.
* 1b66ea097, which holds a DRM device awake across each of its ioctls (`drm_dev_pm_get` and `drm_dev_pm_put` in `drm.c`). For i915 that is `pm_runtime_get_sync` and `pm_runtime_put`, two sections under the record's spinlock and a read of the monotonic clock a call. While the GT or a lit pipe holds the device, the put never reaches zero and never queues the idle work.
* 2914ab9ae, which returns a heap class's empty page without walking the class's free blocks (`kheap.c`), on the free of every request and every other object the loop's calls allocate.
* 92c25b492, a wait for the big lock at a return to user mode, taken only when the thread has loopback datagrams to deliver, which this loop does not send.

The others are the release's unbind and reset, the display's flips and FIFO, vkms, virtio-gpu, DRM leases, the DP AUX and I2C devices, the page cache's invalidation, and debugfs.

The release's 311.3 ms loop is slower than kernel B's loop at 200 MHz. Linux on the same machine took 360.6 ms at 200 MHz, and 311.1 ms in a loop whose 200 MHz was inferred from the time from submission to the interrupt (84-163). Two conditions also differ from kernel B's run. The release uses its installed root in place of 84-58's trial root, and the release's loop ran with the whole-kernel profile.

### GPU clock and preemption

Linux 6.12.115's `intel_rps_irq_handler` queues `rps_work` on RPS events. The work uses the current request, the direction of the last adjustment and the soft limits to move the request. A down-threshold event steps it down and a down-timeout can take it to RPn. `intel_rps_park` requests idle while the GT sleeps and leaves the next request at least RPe if it parked above idle. Its early return at idle leaves the next unpark at RPn. `intel_rps_unpark` clamps that saved request to the limits, and `intel_rps_boost` can queue an increase for a waiter. 84-176 measured no waitboosts or up events during the GL loop. The release carries the corresponding park, unpark and work in `i915_rps.c`. Read the actual and requested clock together with RPS work, parks and unparks to distinguish a request that stayed low from a low actual clock under a higher request. [Linux i915 RPS](https://github.com/gregkh/linux/blob/v6.12.115/drivers/gpu/drm/i915/gt/intel_rps.c)

Linux's `select_task_rq_fair` looks for an idle processor near the target. On the chosen run queue, `check_preempt_wakeup_fair` updates the current thread's weighted virtual runtime and asks for a switch when `pick_eevdf` selects the woken thread. Selection uses eligibility and virtual deadline. A current eligible thread can keep its protected slice, and `do_preempt_short` can cancel that protection for an eligible shorter slice with an earlier deadline. On the release, `sched_wake_preempt` asks for selection when an ordinary wake lands on a busy ordinary processor, subject to its policy and pending-request checks. `sched_select` alternates a marked wake with the head of the queue when both turns have work. It has no eligibility or virtual deadline test. An ordinary wake can therefore interrupt a running `wined3d_cs` within its slice, or another thread can take the processor after a reschedule request already sent. [Linux fair scheduling](https://github.com/gregkh/linux/blob/v6.12.115/kernel/sched/fair.c)

The GPU interrupt signals the fence and then kicks `ki915` through `retire_kick` when it signalled the last request to a listener. A retire or park deadline can also wake that worker. The switch counter records the next thread chosen while the command thread remains runnable. It records neither the wake that requested the switch nor the interval until the command thread runs again. The release has 31 switches to `ki915`, down from 43, and 37 to wineserver, up from 1, out of 133 total. Its command thread's ready wait fell from 44.5 to 14.4 ms while its measured run time rose from 159.0 to 184.0 ms. Linux's 228.7 ms plain loop in 84-163 switched `wined3d_cs` out involuntarily 262 times and waited ready 10.2 ms. The seven Linux switches quoted in 84-157 belong to the main thread. These figures show that switch count alone cannot account for the extra 29.7 ms. The release trace has no timestamp for a wineserver or `ki915` wake before each switch, and no GPU clock sample.

## The step

One session on the Lenovo, run by a loop of the owner's ([5426/scripts/](5426/scripts/)):

* On the installed release, with no reboot: `run5426.sh DIR 500` runs three loops of `trip.sh -n 3 -t wined3d_cs` with the kernel profile as the manager's loop ran it, then two loops of `trip.sh -n 2 -t wined3d_cs` with the GPU's clock held at 500 MHz through `gt_min_freq_mhz` and `gt_max_freq_mhz`, which it puts back on every exit. 84-176's `sample4904.py` reads the clock every 20 ms through both, and i915's frequency, RPS, engine and runtime power files are read before, between and after.
* On Debian 13.7 on p5, one boot: `lin5426.sh DIR 500 PROTON` runs the same five loops with the same sampler.

`boot5426.sh` stages and restores the Debian reference boot. It uses 80-2's GRUB modules, menu and sector-run reader, with the vendored mtools running on Rach2. The boot partition's images stay on the Lenovo and its digest is checked before staging, after writing the GRUB modules and after restoring the release boot partition.

84-176's `windows4904.py` gives each loop's clock from its window; on Linux, whose `i915_rps_boost_info` has no counters of RPS's work and parks, it gives the clock alone.

How to read it:

* The three free loops give the round trip against the actual clock's median and range, beside kernel B's and Linux's at the same clock. Each group starts with a fresh home and creates its prefix before its first loop. Compare like positions and say whether the 311.3 ms result recurs.
* The two held loops compare the systems at one clock. Read the actual samples before treating the limits as a held clock. A gap that closes at the held clock, together with a reproduced gap at unequal clocks, gives the clock's effect. A gap still present at the held clock needs the time split: command and main thread run and ready times, SYNCOBJ_WAIT sleeps, and on Rach2 the switched-to threads and the whole-kernel profile. The systems' profiling differs. If a residual gap needs another measurement, read it also with Rach2's whole-kernel profiling off before attributing it to a scheduler policy.
* `power/runtime_status` stays `active` through the run, and `runtime_suspended_time` does not grow, unless the device sat idle 10 s between the runs.

## The Lenovo's figures

The manager ran the step under the Lenovo's lock on 2026-11-19, as written on `task/5426` at 3b55011b65: on the installed release 7a2168e3c2 from 20:19 to 20:23 UTC+6, and on Debian 13.7's 6.12.115 on p5 from 20:55 to 20:57, both with headless Weston and no sound. Every runner exited 0 and put both limits back to 200 to 1000 MHz; the held loops ran at limits of 500 to 500 MHz. Rach2's log showed no reset, hang, wedge, fault, lockup or panic. The boot partition read its original SHA-256, c01ac5dd..., after each Debian boot, and the release booted from its committed candidate. The first Debian boot, at 20:40, ended at once, since the bundle's files kept the Mac's owner and `trip.sh` refuses a directory that root does not own alone; the second boot's run is the one below.

Each loop is 1000 WineD3D GL round trips, with the GPU's actual frequency sampled every 20 ms and read by `windows4904.py`. Held loops ran with `gt_min_freq_mhz` and `gt_max_freq_mhz` both at 500.

Beside them, the earlier loops: kernel B took 225.9, 203.8 and 280.5 ms at a median 500, 450 and 200 MHz ([84-176.md](84-176.md)); the release's 13:15 loop took 311.3 ms with no clock sampled, its `wined3d_cs` running 184.0 ms, waiting ready 14.4 ms and switched out involuntarily 133 times; Linux's plain loop in 84-163 took 228.7 ms with 262 involuntary switches and 10.2 ms ready. The command thread's run times of the ten loops above are in the evidence; the manager's reading quotes only the switches and the ready wait.

The cause is the GPU's clock. A loop that RPS leaves at 200 MHz takes about 300 ms on either system: Rach2's free 0 took 299.1 ms and Linux's free 2 took 312.6 ms. At 500 MHz both take 208 to 235 ms. With the clock held at 500 MHz, Rach2's 235.4 and 225.8 ms lie inside Linux's 208.2 to 247.8 ms. The 311.3 ms that opened this task is a loop at RPS's low clock, which Linux shows too. The preemptions of `wined3d_cs` do not set the figure: at a matched clock Rach2's involuntary switches and ready wait are of Linux's order, and on both systems they vary between loops more than the round trip does. The Done holds without a change to the kernel: the round trip is within Linux's range at a matched clock.

Outside the comparison, the same loop under strace took 4057 to 4120 ms on Rach2 and 859 to 956 ms on Linux; that gap is #5496.

The evidence, both transcripts, the clock samples, the frequency dumps, the loop snapshots and the boot records, is kept in the manager's checkout on the Mac under `build/runs/t5426`. It is not committed, since the boot records carry the home network's addresses.
