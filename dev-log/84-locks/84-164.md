# 84-164: i915's retirement in the submitter, and ki915's pass that only tries the GEM lock

84-163's reading (Fable's answers on #4889) found that on the Lenovo the GPU's interrupt signals the fence and then kicks `ki915`, whose retire pass took the GEM lock with a sleeping take and held the big lock for the whole pass, while EXECBUFFER2 left retirement to it. 84-151's loop counted 98 EXECBUFFER2 sleeps on the GEM lock and 136 preemptions of `wined3d_cs` by `ki915`; 84-157's trial, with the GT no longer parked after each call, counted 3 sleeps and 43 preemptions, with `wined3d_cs` waiting ready 44.5 ms and the big lock's waits at 48.1 ms over the loop. This task gives Rach2 Linux's division of the work.

## Linux

* The submitter retires its own timeline: `i915_request_create` retires the oldest request before it makes a new one (`drivers/gpu/drm/i915/i915_request.c:1037-1040`), and `eb_request_add` retires the timeline's completed requests after it queues the request, short of the previous one (`gem/i915_gem_execbuffer.c:3058-3065`, `3104-3106`).
* The retire worker kicked from the interrupt only tries each timeline's mutex and skips a held one, "either it is being retired elsewhere or about to be" (`gt/intel_gt_requests.c:64-87`); its periodic pass tries them the same way and counts a held one busy (`144-145`). Nothing requeues a skipped timeline but the next pass, once a second.
* The interrupt only signals fences and queues the retire (`gt/intel_breadcrumbs.c:170-262`). The worker holds no global lock.

## What Rach2 does now

* Request creation already retired every engine under the GEM lock before this task (`i915_request_create_room`, at 84-157's trial head too), so the issue's "EXECBUFFER2 never retires" holds only for the file `i915_gem_execbuffer.c`. What was missing is the retire after the add: `eb_submit` now calls `i915_request_retire_engine`, which retires the completed requests before the new one on its engine, without the pass that signals the rest, whose fences the interrupt signals. The request itself is never retired in its own call, as Linux stops short of it.
* `ki915`'s passes, the kicked one and the one each second, go through `retire_pass` with `worker` set: the GEM lock is only tried (`i915_gem_trylock`, on `mutex_trylock`, `sys/proc/mutex.c:78`) and the pass is skipped when another thread holds it. A holder that builds a request retires the engine itself, at the creation and after the add; what no holder retires waits for the next pass, within two seconds, as on Linux. The pass of each second now clears `unretired`, parks the GT and trims the pool's stale shadows all under the one lock it took, so that a shadow's last put joins `gem.freed` and the worker never sleeps on the lock in a free.
* Once it has the GEM lock, the worker lets the big lock go for the pass and takes it back just before it gives the GEM lock back. The GEM lock keeps the device on the worker's list meanwhile, since `i915_unplug` takes it. The frees of `gem.freed` that `i915_gem_unlock` runs therefore run under the big lock in the worker, as they did; the retirement itself, the request walk, the releases of the holds, contexts and fences, RPS's retire, the park and the trim, runs without it.

## The shared-structure audit

Requests leave an engine's list only in `engine_retire`, under the GEM lock and the engine's `lock` together, and are added only in `i915_request_add`, under both as well. So a reader that holds either lock sees a list no one shortens under it, and the big lock guards no part of the list. Every caller of a retire holds the GEM lock:

| Caller | GEM lock | Big lock |
|---|---|---|
| `ki915`'s kicked pass and pass each second (`retire_work`) | tried; skipped when held | let go for the pass |
| `i915_request_create_room`, from EXECBUFFER2 and the kernel's requests | held by the caller | none in EXECBUFFER2 |
| `eb_submit` after the add (`i915_request_retire_engine`) | held | none |
| `i915_request_wait` after its wait | taken back after the wait | the caller's |
| `i915_gt_wait_for_idle`, from eviction and debugfs | taken by `retire_pass` | none in EXECBUFFER2's eviction |
| the default state's recording | held | the caller's |
| a file's close (`i915_gem_context.c`) | taken by `retire_pass` | held, from `drm_node_close` |
| debugfs's `DROP_RETIRE` and `idle_within` | held | the caller's |
| the unplug and the wedge (`engine_retire(..., 1, 0)`) | held | the caller's |

Every reader of the lists holds one of the two locks:

* the interrupt's `i915_request_signal`, `i915_request_throttle_target` and debugfs's engine dump hold the engine's lock;
* `last_on_chip` in the heartbeat, the error capture's walks and `engine_rewind` in a reset run under the GEM lock that `i915_hangcheck` takes; `last_on_chip`'s comment said the big lock and now says the GEM lock;
* `ring_wait_for_space`, `i915_gt_wait_for_idle` and debugfs's `idle_within`, which read `requests_tail` or walk the list and then take a reference before they sleep, hold the GEM lock.

The one part of a retire that reaches state of the big lock's is the last put of an imported buffer: a request's hold puts the import, its free under the GEM lock defers it to `gem.freed`, and `free_deferred` puts the dma-buf, whose release puts the exporter's object. For virtio-gpu that runs `vgpu_obj_free`, which counts `unrefs` and sends a command under the big lock. EXECBUFFER2's own releases already reached it without the big lock, so this was a fault before this task, which the worker's new pass would have widened: `prime_release` now takes the big lock for the put when the exporter's device lacks `DRIVER_KMS_UNLOCKED` and the caller does not hold it. i915 sets the flag, so a buffer i915 exported and imported takes nothing.

The remaining structures a retire touches without the big lock already have their own locks: the fence callbacks' lists their fence's lock, `submit_lock` the awaits and the retire kick, RPS's boost count the frequency lock, the pool `pool_lock`, the contexts their atomic counts, and the device list, which the worker walks under the big lock, the GEM lock held across the pass. The fences a request holds, of i915, virtio-gpu, vgem, sw_sync, the display, arrays and chains, have no `release` of their own but the free and their children's puts.

## Measured

QEMU has no i915, so the changed path runs only on the exec host test's chip model, as 84-157's did. Its new row `retire_in_caller` makes a Crocus call with a listener on its syncobj, takes the interrupt, and runs the worker with the GEM lock marked as another thread's (`compat/mutex_none.c` now tells the test's own hold from another's): the worker leaves the request on the engine; the next call retires it itself and leaves its own request alone; and with the lock free the worker's kicked pass retires at once. Run alone with the same test file:

* on this task's driver the whole exec test passes, `park_per_call` still counting no park, unpark, unmask or new shadow over eight waited calls and 24 heap blocks;
* on the milestone's head the rows before it pass and the new row stops at the mutex model's trap, where the old worker's sleeping take meets the other thread's hold: the sleep that 84-151's loop counted 98 times.

How many of `ki915`'s 43 preemptions of `wined3d_cs` and how much of the big lock's 48.1 ms this removes, the model cannot say: it has one thread and no big lock. That is the Lenovo's step, [84-164-lenovo.md](84-164-lenovo.md).

## What stays

* `ki915` is still woken once a call, by the request's signal to its listener, as Linux's retire worker is queued by `signal_irq_work`. While another thread holds the GEM lock its pass is a failed try; while the pass holds it, a call that needs it still sleeps, as a submitter sleeps on Linux's timeline mutex.
* The kick and the heartbeat still run under the big lock, and `ki915` is still not switched by the tick (`no_tick_switch`); placement and preemption of the woken thread are 84-166's.
