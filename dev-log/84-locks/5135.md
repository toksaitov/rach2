# 84-264a: x2apic's stall in machine.kvm.unit-tests-nested (task 5135)

machine.kvm.unit-tests-nested failed in the release of 2026-11-14, at 5473dd9d1, with "it ran past its gap bound of 2400000 ms" after the 56th test, while `x2apic` printed nothing for 40 minutes. `x2apic` alone is not slow here: it takes 556 s, less than under Linux. What stopped it in the release was a lost NMI in Rach2's KVM, found on 11-19 by the forcing load below: `take_nmis` queued an NMI without asking for its injection, so an NMI that came between the run loop's look at KVM_REQ_NMI and its look at KVM_REQ_EVENT stayed queued for ever, and `test_sti_nmi` waited for it. Since dbdf4eb9b (11-15) upstream's 30 s limit ends such a stall as the failure the list expects, and the release's rerun passed with it. Run at the milestone's head as the release ran it, the case stopped at its 7th test with the same message, from a different fault: a QEMU process deadlocked in Rach2's kernel on its address space's lock, `mmap_lock`, because a sleeper on a wait queue could test its condition before its filing on the queue reached the processor that released the lock. That fault came with the queued spin lock of 11-19, and the fix is in `rach2/sys/proc/wait_queue.c`.

## x2apic alone

At the milestone's head, `x2apic` alone in the case's setup (an x86-64 Rach2 on `host-svm` under the testing PC's KVM, running kvm-unit-tests under its own KVM) ran its 56 subtests in 556 s with no time limit and failed the two subtests the lists record, `nmi-after-sti` and `TMCCT should not be reset to TMICT value`. The subtests took, from the test's start:

| Subtest | Ended at | Took |
|---|---|---|
| up to `PV IPIs testing` | 5 s | 5 s |
| `nmi-after-sti` (fail) | 31 s | 26 s |
| `multiple nmi` | 177 s | 146 s |
| `pending nmi` | 555 s | 378 s |
| the timer, ID and APIC base subtests, `TMCCT should not be reset to TMICT value` failing | 555 s | under 1 s |

`pending nmi` makes 100000 rounds of an NMI whose handler sends ten more, each an ICR write that exits to the KVM, and two NMIs a round, each with the intercept of its handler's IRET and a single step over it, since neither host has virtual NMIs at this depth. The exit counts grew steadily through it: some 145,000 MSR writes, 26,000 IRET intercepts and 13,000 #DB exits a minute. Linux 6.12 as the host takes 640 to 849 s for the test (81-25), so Rach2 is not slower.

## The stall

The whole of `unit-tests.list`, run as the case runs it but with `x2apic` given no limit, stopped at its 7th test, `hypercall`, with no output for 1900 s and the same gap-bound failure as the release. The test's VM made no exit in that time and its QEMU's monitor refused connections. The kernel's hung-task report named two of QEMU's threads in state D in `wq_wait_event_nointr`:

* the main thread, in a read fault of a file mapping: `page_fault`, `shared_fault`, `share_around`, which lets `mmap_lock` go across the file's `around` and takes it again to read (`callback_end`), in `down_read`;
* another thread, in `mmap`: `mmap_install`, `regions_begin`, `mmap_write_lock`, in `down_write`.

Neither held the lock: the reader had let it go, and the writer waited for it. Nobody else held it either. A release had found the queue empty and woken nobody.

## The cause

`down_read` and `down_write` sleep in `wq_wait`, which files the thread on the rwsem's queue under the queue's spin lock, lets the lock go and then tests the rwsem's state. `up_read`, `up_write` and `mutex_unlock` change the lock's word by an atomic exchange and then read the queue with no lock (`wake_waiters`, `wq_active`), waking only a queue with a sleeper on it (0f119d0775, 11-13). That is sound only when the sleeper passes a full barrier between its filing and its test, as Linux's `prepare_to_wait` does through `set_current_state`'s `smp_store_mb`. The spin lock's release was an exchange when `wq_wait` was written, and its comment relies on it; since the queued spin lock (b6f52f0720, written 11-13 and on the milestone branch since 11-19) the release is one store, as Linux's `native_queued_spin_unlock`. x86 lets a later load pass an earlier store to another address, so the sleeper's load of the rwsem's state could be served before its stores to the queue were visible: it saw the lock held, while the releaser, after its exchange, saw no sleeper. The sleeper then slept for ever, and every thread that wanted the lock after it queued behind it.

The release's kernel, 5473dd9d1, had the unlocked reads of the queue but released the spin lock by an exchange, so this fault cannot explain its stall; nor does its console hold a hung-task report, which a thread blocked in `down_read` for 120 s would have made. Its `x2apic` was therefore running, not blocked. Each of the test's three NMI subtests spins until the NMI it sent arrives, with no limit of its own, so one NMI lost between the two KVMs would hold it for ever; one run of the test at the head, 556 s, lost none, and the cause stays open.

## The fix

`wq_wait` passes a full barrier (`sched_mb`) after its filing and before each test of the condition, and `wq_poll_add` and `wq_entry_add` after theirs, for the pollers and epoll's records, whose callers test readiness after them. The three wakers that read the queue with `wq_active` after a change ordered only by a spin lock's release, `ahci_release`, `ata_unlock` and the console's `console_deliver`, pass one before the read. The comments in `mutex.c`, `sleeplock.c` and `pipe.c` that named the queue's unlock as the sleeper's barrier now name `wq_wait`'s barrier.

## The load

`mmapstress`, below, was compiled with the GNU root's GCC in the case's machine and run for up to 900 s in place of the corpus. It has four threads that map a 4 MiB file, read every seventh page, which faults each page around and so lets `mmap_lock` go and takes it again, and unmap it, and four that map, touch and unmap 64 KiB of anonymous memory. On the kernel before the fix, every one of its nine threads, and the `writeback` thread, was in state D in `wq_wait_event_nointr` within 120 s, the reader's trace the same as QEMU's main thread's. On the kernel with the fix it ran the whole 900 s and passed, 44,535,228 rounds, each thread's count rising every 30 s: the file threads about 1,110 rounds a second each, the anonymous ones about 11,250.

```c
/* mmapstress.c: contend for one address space's mmap_lock, for SECONDS,
 * and report a thread that stops. */
#include <fcntl.h>
#include <pthread.h>
#include <stdio.h>
#include <stdlib.h>
#include <string.h>
#include <sys/mman.h>
#include <time.h>
#include <unistd.h>

#define NT 8
#define FLEN (4u << 20)

static volatile unsigned long count[NT];
static int fd;

/** Map the file, read a page in seven, which faults each around, and unmap
 * it: reads of the lock that let it go and take it again, and writes. */
static void *filer(void *arg)
{
    long i = (long)arg;

    for (;;) {
        volatile char *p = mmap(NULL, FLEN, PROT_READ, MAP_SHARED, fd, 0);
        unsigned long o, s = 0;

        if (p == MAP_FAILED) {
            perror("mmapstress: mmap");
            exit(1);
        }
        for (o = 0; o < FLEN; o += 4096 * 7)
            s += (unsigned char)p[o];
        (void)s;
        munmap((void *)p, FLEN);
        count[i]++;
    }
    return NULL;
}

/** Map, touch and unmap 64 KiB of anonymous memory: writes of the lock. */
static void *anon(void *arg)
{
    long i = (long)arg;

    for (;;) {
        char *p = mmap(NULL, 1 << 16, PROT_READ | PROT_WRITE, MAP_PRIVATE | MAP_ANONYMOUS, -1, 0);

        if (p == MAP_FAILED) {
            perror("mmapstress: mmap");
            exit(1);
        }
        p[0] = 1;
        munmap(p, 1 << 16);
        count[i]++;
    }
    return NULL;
}

int main(int argc, char **argv)
{
    static char buf[65536];
    unsigned long last[NT], total = 0;
    int still[NT];
    pthread_t t;
    long i, secs, s;

    if (argc != 3) {
        fprintf(stderr, "usage: mmapstress FILE SECONDS\n");
        return 2;
    }
    secs = atol(argv[2]);
    fd = open(argv[1], O_RDWR | O_CREAT | O_TRUNC, 0600);
    if (fd < 0) {
        perror("mmapstress: open");
        return 1;
    }
    memset(buf, 0x5a, sizeof(buf));
    for (s = 0; s < (long)(FLEN / sizeof(buf)); s++)
        if (write(fd, buf, sizeof(buf)) != (ssize_t)sizeof(buf)) {
            perror("mmapstress: write");
            return 1;
        }
    for (i = 0; i < NT; i++) {
        last[i] = 0;
        still[i] = 0;
        if (pthread_create(&t, NULL, i % 2 ? anon : filer, (void *)i) != 0) {
            fprintf(stderr, "mmapstress: no thread\n");
            return 1;
        }
    }
    for (s = 1; s <= secs; s++) {
        sleep(1);
        for (i = 0; i < NT; i++) {
            unsigned long c = count[i];

            still[i] = c == last[i] ? still[i] + 1 : 0;
            last[i] = c;
            if (still[i] >= 30) {
                printf("mmapstress: stuck: thread %ld (%s) at %lu after %ld s\n", i,
                       i % 2 ? "anon" : "file", c, s);
                fflush(stdout);
                return 1;
            }
        }
        if (s % 30 == 0) {
            printf("mmapstress: %ld s:", s);
            for (i = 0; i < NT; i++)
                printf(" %lu", last[i]);
            printf("\n");
            fflush(stdout);
        }
    }
    for (i = 0; i < NT; i++)
        total += last[i];
    printf("mmapstress: pass: %lu rounds in %ld s\n", total, secs);
    return 0;
}
```

## Reading the code for the release's stall

On 11-19 the chat ordered the cause found by reading the code and the one failure's evidence, with no soak. The reading, at the milestone's head and checked against the release's kernel 5473dd9d1, whose `svm.c`, `vlapic.c` and `vcpu.c` differ from the head's only in Xen's hypercall, timers and upcall and in halt polling, names no cause.

`x2apic` is `apic.flat` at kvm-unit-tests 5a221342, and its waits with no limit of their own are these: `test_sti_nmi`, where vCPU 0 waits for each NMI it sends vCPU 1; `test_multiple_nmi`, where vCPU 0 waits for the fixed IPI 0x44 that follows vCPU 1's NMI, and each side for the other's counter; `test_pending_nmi`, where vCPU 0 waits for two NMIs to itself; `test_apic_timer_one_shot`'s `while (!lvtt_counter)`; the TMCCT loops of `test_apic_change_mode`; and `on_cpu`'s `while (!ipi_done)`, which waits for a halted vCPU 1 to take the IPI. Each was read against the code that serves it.

* The timer's waits cannot last. `tmcct` reads 0 for good only when `expire_ns` is 0, after a one-shot count ran out; in periodic mode it reads between 1 and the period. `wait_until_tmcct_common` reads TMCCT first and skips its loops when it is 0, so a count that ran out before the switch to periodic mode fails "TMCCT should have a non-zero value" and goes on. The one-shot interrupt's deadline is armed on the host's event timer before every entry (`svm_timer_arm`), and a processor that runs a vCPU keeps its tick, which stops only in the idle task (`sched.c`), so a lost event costs one tick at most.
* A halted vCPU's wake is not lost. `lapic_kick` sets the request and wakes the queue with `wq_wake`, which takes the queue's lock, so the one-store release of the spin lock that lost the rwsem's wake does not reach it: the waker either sees the sleeper filed or changed IRR or `nmis` before the sleeper's test.
* A running vCPU's kick is not lost. `req_set` is a locked exchange, `run_enter` marks the vCPU IN_GUEST and passes `sched_mb` before it reads the requests, and the fast path reads them again after each exit (`fast_ends`). `smp_kick` sends no interrupt only to a processor whose `idle_poll` is CPU_POLL_ON, which only the idle task sets while it is current, and which `schedule` clears before it switches to another thread.
* The NMI window follows Linux 6.12's: the delivery masks NMIs and arms the IRET intercept (`svm_inject`, `load_dirty`, as svm_inject_nmi), the intercept records the IRET's RIP (iret_interception), the next exit at another RIP unmasks (svm_complete_interrupts), a queued NMI behind an intercepted IRET or a shadow steps the guest with TF and RF (svm_enable_nmi_window), the step's #DB ends it (db_interception), an injection under the step ends the step and exits after the delivery (svm_vcpu_run), and NMIs are queued as process_nmi limits them. A stall needs NMIs masked with neither the intercept nor the step armed; every path that masks them marks the events dirty, whose load arms the intercept, and the intercept is cleared only by its own exit, which sets `awaiting_iret`. No state was found in which an NMI stays queued with no window armed.

What would settle it is the evidence of the rerun that stalled, at 4a039f6d9 on 11-15: its consoles and disk copies, 36 MiB, kept in toksaitov-m's clone in `build/tmp/unit-tests-nested-rerun-evidence`. The corpus writes `x2apic`'s log on the guest's disk, and its last line names the subtest that stalled. Neither toksaitov-d nor toksaitov-v can read that clone.

## The subtest that stalled

The owner read `x2apic`'s log back from the raw clusters of the stalled run's kept guest-disk overlay (comment on the task, 11-19). Its last line is `PASS: PV IPIs testing`, so the stall is in `test_sti_nmi`, the next subtest, which prints nothing until its end. It has three waits with no limit: `on_cpu(1, update_cr3)` and `on_cpu_async(1, sti_loop)`, where vCPU 0 waits for a halted vCPU 1 to take the fixed IPI 0x20, and the loop of 30,000 NMIs, where vCPU 0 sends vCPU 1 an NMI and waits until `sti_loop` copies the handler's count into `nmi_counter`. The log cannot tell them apart. Each round of `sti_loop` runs `sti; testb $0, (p); nop; cli` inside the IPI's handler, with `p` stepping a page at a time through the low 128 MiB, so an NMI that comes while `testb` is in the STI shadow waits for the NMI window's single step.

Read against Rach2's KVM at the milestone's head, each wait ends:

* `test_pv_ipi` before it delivers nothing: `min` is 0xFFFFFFFF, above every APIC ID, and the second half's `min` wraps to 63, so `kvm_lapic_pv_ipi` sends no IPI, as Linux's `__pv_send_ipi` returns 0.
* The IPI to a halted vCPU 1 is not lost. `accept` sets IRR, then `lapic_kick` sets KVM_REQ_EVENT and calls `wq_wake`, which reads the queue under its lock; `wq_wait` files the halted thread and marks it BLOCKED under the same lock and tests `wake_cond`, which reads IRR with the guest's IF, after a full barrier. The halt poll tests `wake_cond` on each round.
* An NMI to vCPU 1 in `sti_loop` is queued and injected. `accept` adds to `nmis` and kicks with KVM_REQ_NMI and KVM_REQ_EVENT; `take_nmis` keeps one while NMIs are masked; `svm_inject` injects it when neither NMI blocking nor a shadow holds it, and otherwise `nmi_window` arms the step, or leaves the window to the IRET intercept while the handler has not reached its IRET. The step's #DB is always intercepted, ends the step and asks for the injection. A nested page fault in the shadow keeps the step armed.
* `p` passes 0xA0000 to 0xBFFFF, which is no memory under kvm-unit-tests' `-nodefaults`, so a `testb` there in the shadow is an MMIO read that QEMU answers and the emulator completes. `x_commit` ends the shadow without asking for an injection, where Linux's `toggle_interruptibility` raises KVM_REQ_EVENT, but the step stays armed, so the NMI goes in after one more instruction: a delay, not a stall.
* An injection while the step is armed ends the step and exits after the delivery, by the self-IPI in `svm_enter`, as Linux's `svm_vcpu_run`.

So reading Rach2's code names no cause. The outer layers, WSL's KVM under Hyper-V, are where the evidence points, though it does not prove it. Neither inner host has a virtual NMI there, so both open the NMI window by stepping the guest with TF. Linux 6.12 as the inner host fails this subtest by delivering an NMI inside the shadow, which needs an outer layer to lose the shadow or misplace the step, and it did not finish `xapic` in 900 s after this subtest, nor `debug` after its single step under MOV SS blocking ([81-25.md](../81-kvm/81-25.md)). Both are single steps through an interrupt shadow, the path that `test_sti_nmi` drives some 30,000 times.

What would settle it, when the stall appears a second time, is the forcing load AGENTS.md names: `test_sti_nmi` alone in the case's machine, repeated, with Rach2 tracing vCPU 1's `nmi_queued`, NMI blocking, `awaiting_iret`, `nmi_ss` and each exit's code, RIP and interrupt shadow once no NMI has gone in for a second. That trace shows whether Rach2 holds a queued NMI with no window armed, or arms the step and gets no #DB back from the outer layers.

## The forcing load and the cause

The stall's second appearance made the forcing load due (the owner's comment of 11-19). It ran in toksaitov-v's clone on the testing PC, as the case `machine.kvm.unit-tests-nested` with `RACH2_KVM_UNIT=x2loop.list`, whose `corpus.sh` handed the list to `x2loop.sh` below: two of `x2apic`'s machines at once inside the case's Rach2, each run up to the `nmi-after-sti` line, the end of `test_sti_nmi`, then stopped and started again, so that four L2 vCPUs share L1's two. A machine with no such line after 300 s is a stall. The kernel carried a trace that is not committed: each vCPU kept a ring of its last 32 runs (the exit's code, RIP, interrupt shadow and EXITINTINFO, the event injected, and before the entry `nmi_queued`, NMI blocking, an NMI under injection, `awaiting_iret`, `nmi_ss`, the shadow, TF, the IRET intercept and IF), and when a VM had injected no NMI for 10 s, the exit path of any of its vCPUs logged each vCPU's state and its last 16 runs, every 30 s until the next NMI.

On the milestone's head with the trace, both machines stalled in `test_sti_nmi` within 12 minutes, after 11 rounds of 91 to 109 s each, the stalled one's log ending at `PASS: PV IPIs testing` as the release's did. The trace of each, 220 s and 160 s after its last NMI:

vCPU 1 has one NMI queued, NMIs unmasked, no NMI under injection, no IRET awaited, no single step armed, and no KVM_REQ_EVENT (0x200 is the steal time's request). It runs `sti_loop` on, exiting only for the host's ticks (code 0x60), and every run's record shows the NMI queued (bit 0 of `f`) and nothing injected; vCPU 0 spins at 0x400da8 waiting for `nmi_counter`. Nothing in that state ever asks for an injection, so the NMI waits for ever.

How it gets there: a sender's delivery adds to the APIC's `nmis` and sets KVM_REQ_NMI and KVM_REQ_EVENT. The run loop takes KVM_REQ_NMI in `kvm_lapic_prepare`, through `take_nmis`, before it takes KVM_REQ_EVENT and calls `svm_inject`. An NMI that arrives between the two looks has its KVM_REQ_EVENT taken by an injection that finds nothing queued; its KVM_REQ_NMI cancels the entry (KVM_REQ_NOENTER), and the next pass's `take_nmis` queues it with no request left to inject it. Linux's `process_nmi`, which `take_nmis` follows, ends by asking for an injection whenever an NMI is pending (`arch/x86/kvm/x86.c:10076-10077`); `take_nmis` did not. `test_sti_nmi` sends 30,000 NMIs, each the moment vCPU 1 has taken the last, so it meets the window more than any other test; the load of a release's other cases, or a second machine sharing L1's processors, makes the vCPU thread's pass through the loop long enough to meet it within minutes. Linux as L1 did not need comparing: the cause is Rach2's.

The fix (b30112ce0): `take_nmis` sets KVM_REQ_EVENT when an NMI is queued, as `process_nmi` does. The host row "a queued NMI asks for its injection" in `kvm_irq_hosttest.c` delivers an NMI, takes its KVM_REQ_EVENT away as the early injection would, and requires the queueing to ask for the injection again: it fails without the fix and passes with it. It covers VMX too, which shares `take_nmis`.

The same load on the kernel with the fix and the trace ran its whole 3058 s: 55 rounds, none stalled and none ended early, each 86 to 160 s, 107 s at the median: "kvm-corpus: result: pass: 55 rounds, 0 ended early, in 3058 s".

```sh
#!/bin/sh
#
# x2loop.sh: run kvm-unit-tests' x2apic to the end of its test_sti_nmi, the
# subtest that stalled in the release, again and again, PARALLEL at once,
# and on a stall print the test's last lines and the kernel's NMI trace
# (task 5135's diagnostic, not a case).
#
# Usage: x2loop.sh LIST
#
# LIST has the lines `parallel P`, `seconds S` and `limit L`: P machines at
# once (1), for S seconds (3000), a stall being a machine with no
# nmi-after-sti line after L seconds (300). Output is corpus.sh's progress,
# `kvm-corpus: done: N of M`, a line a round, and a last line
# `kvm-corpus: result: pass: ...` with no stall, else `... fail: stall`.

list=$1
val()
{
    v=$(awk -v k="$1" '$1 == k { print $2 }' "$list")
    echo "${v:-$2}"
}
par=$(val parallel 1)
secs=$(val seconds 3000)
limit=$(val limit 300)

work=
trap 'for f in "$work"/pid.*; do
        [ -f "$f" ] || continue
        p=$(cat "$f"); kill -TERM -- "-$p" 2> /dev/null; kill -KILL -- "-$p" 2> /dev/null
    done
    wait
    [ -z "$work" ] || rm -rf "$work"' EXIT
trap 'exit 129' HUP
trap 'exit 130' INT
trap 'exit 143' TERM
work=$(mktemp -d "${TMPDIR:-/tmp}/x2loop.XXXXXX") || exit 1
cp -R /opt/kvm-unit-tests "$work/kut" || exit 1
echo 0 > /proc/sys/kernel/nmi_watchdog 2> /dev/null

# launch I: start machine I in a process group of its own.
launch()
{
    (cd "$work/kut" && ACCEL=kvm,kernel-irqchip=split TIMEOUT=0 exec perl -e \
        'setpgrp(0, 0); exec @ARGV or die' ./x86/run x86/apic.flat -smp 2 \
        -cpu qemu64,+x2apic,+tsc-deadline) > "$work/log.$1" 2>&1 < /dev/null &
    echo $! > "$work/pid.$1"
    date +%s > "$work/t0.$1"
}

# finish I: stop machine I.
finish()
{
    p=$(cat "$work/pid.$1")
    kill -TERM -- "-$p" 2> /dev/null
    sleep 1
    kill -KILL -- "-$p" 2> /dev/null
    wait "$p" 2> /dev/null
    rm -f "$work/pid.$1"
}

start=$(date +%s)
target=$((secs / 30 * par))
rounds=0 early=0
i=1
while [ "$i" -le "$par" ]; do
    launch "$i"
    i=$((i + 1))
done
echo "kvm-corpus: tests: $target"
while :; do
    sleep 1
    now=$(date +%s)
    i=1
    while [ "$i" -le "$par" ]; do
        if [ ! -f "$work/pid.$i" ]; then
            i=$((i + 1))
            continue
        fi
        t0=$(cat "$work/t0.$i")
        if grep -q 'nmi-after-sti' "$work/log.$i"; then
            rounds=$((rounds + 1))
            echo "x2loop: round $rounds: machine $i: $((now - t0)) s:" \
                "$(grep 'nmi-after-sti' "$work/log.$i" | tr -d '\r')" >&2
            echo "kvm-corpus: done: $rounds of $target"
            finish "$i"
            [ $((now - start)) -ge "$secs" ] || launch "$i"
        elif ! kill -0 "$(cat "$work/pid.$i")" 2> /dev/null; then
            early=$((early + 1))
            echo "x2loop: machine $i ended early after $((now - t0)) s:" >&2
            tail -n 5 "$work/log.$i" >&2
            finish "$i"
            [ $((now - start)) -ge "$secs" ] || launch "$i"
        elif [ $((now - t0)) -ge "$limit" ]; then
            echo "x2loop: stall: machine $i after $((now - t0)) s, round $((rounds + 1))" >&2
            tail -n 8 "$work/log.$i" | tr -d '\r' >&2
            # A second look at the stalled machine, 35 s on.
            sleep 35
            dmesg | grep 'diag: ' | tail -n 150 >&2
            echo "kvm-corpus: result: fail: stall after $rounds rounds"
            exit 1
        fi
        i=$((i + 1))
    done
    [ -n "$(ls "$work"/pid.* 2> /dev/null)" ] || break
done
echo "kvm-corpus: result: pass: $rounds rounds, $early ended early, in $(($(date +%s) - start)) s"
exit 0
```
