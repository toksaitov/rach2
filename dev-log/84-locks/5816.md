# 5816: IGT's sync_multi_consumer hung, possibly on a poll whose wake was lost

`machine.igt.sw_sync-virtio-vga` on x86-64 stopped at `progress stage 2` in the release's WSL run at 0626d3bd80 (`full-84-1124-2155`, 2026-11-25 00:38 UTC+6). The kernel can lose a poll's wake, which is certain from the code, and the guest's memory has the state such a loss leaves. That this run lost one is inferred: the hang was not reproduced.

## The capture

IGT 2.0's `sync_multi_consumer` (`tests/sw_sync.c`, the tarball `vendor/gnu/igt/sources.txt` pins) starts as many consumers as processors, at most 8: two here. Consumer `t` makes a fence at point `i * 2 + t`, polls it with a timeout of 1000 ms (`sync_fence_wait`), checks that the counter is the point, and posts a semaphore. The main thread waits for a post, adds 1 to the counter and 1 to the timeline. A consumer whose fence wait fails, by a timeout, an error event or a poll error other than the two it retries, or whose counter is wrong, leaves without posting and without closing its fence, and the main thread, which waits with no timeout, then waits for ever.

The memory image was taken at 01:04, 25 minutes after the stop, and is kept with the run. The figures below were read from it, the runner's line with `strings`, except the consumers' counter, which was read at 01:03 from the memory of the hung guest, which still ran, as the thread listing was, and the 1.162 s, which is the difference of two of them. Neither the image nor the listing is in the repository.

* The timeline's value is 5163 and the counter 5163. Its one pending point is 5164, not signalled, with two references, the timeline's and its sync file's: so it was made by consumer 0 and left open. No thread is on the fence's queue, and both consumer threads are gone. That consumer 0 polled it first is inferred.
* Point 5163 is signalled, without error, and has the one reference of its sync file, which the thread that made it, consumer 1, left open; how that thread came to leave is inferred. Its stamp is 6.403074 s since boot.
* igt_runner's line for the subtest is stamped 5.241433 s, on the same clock (CLOCK_BOOTTIME). The runner takes the stamp when it prints the subtest's start packet. Point 5163 was signalled 1.162 s after that line.
* Steal time sums to 0.714 s on processor 0 and 0.762 s on processor 1 over the guest's 25 minutes, so no such delay of one vCPU lasted a second. It does not count a vCPU thread asleep in the host or a stop of the whole WSL machine.

A lost wake could have left this state so, a possible reconstruction, since the image shows neither when either poll began nor the wake that ended it. The wake of point 5162's poll is lost, and consumer 0 sleeps on to its timeout. Consumer 1 waits for 5163, which cannot come until consumer 0 posts, and its poll, begun within microseconds of consumer 0's, times out with it: it finds 5163 unsignalled and leaves. Consumer 0's scan after its sleep finds 5162 signalled, so it posts, and the main thread advances to 5163. Consumer 0 then polls 5164, which nobody can reach since consumer 1 is gone, until its own timeout, and leaves too.

## The fault

`poll_rec_wake` (`io/poll.c`) marked the poller woken and `wq_ready_deferred` (`proc/wait_queue.c`) then read its state, with no full barrier between; `poll_sleep` does the mirror of it, with one:

| waker, `poll_rec_wake` and `wq_ready_deferred` | poller, `poll_sleep` |
|---|---|
| | `state = PROC_BLOCKED` |
| | full barrier |
| `poll_woken = 1`, into the store buffer | reads `poll_woken`: 0 |
| reads `state`: not BLOCKED, so no wake | `schedule`, asleep |

An x86 processor may make the waker's load before its earlier store reaches the other processor, so both reads may be of the old values: the waker takes the poller for running and the poller takes the mark for clear. A kernel built from the same sources, not the run's own binary, has no locked instruction between the two (`poll_rec_wake`: `movl $1,0x20e4(%rax)`, a call, `cmpl $3,0x4(%rdi)`); the queue's lock, taken before the store, leaves the mark and the state's read unordered between themselves. The wake of the fence is not repeated, so the poll can sleep until its deadline, a signal, or another wake of a queue it is on. Linux's `pollwake` stores `triggered` and calls `try_to_wake_up`, which passes `smp_mb__after_spinlock` before it reads the state of a task that is not the caller.

The function records came with f1ff815000 and poll's mark with 3575844e4d, both of 84-168 (2026-11-13). Every poll, ppoll, select and epoll_wait since had the fault where it files a record whose wake can race with the wait before it sleeps; a descriptor ready at the first scan files none.

## The fix

`wq_ready_deferred` passes `sched_mb` before it reads the state.

## Ruled out by reading

* ddc8e95f0e, the poll of a sync file without the big lock, did not make the fault.
* `dma_fence_signal` marks the fence before it wakes its queue, poll's second scan follows the filing of its records, and the timeline's calls run under the big lock.
* `wq_wake`'s other records are safe: a sleeper of `wq_wait` sets its state under the queue's lock, the lock's acquisition follows the waker's store of its condition, and a full barrier follows the sleeper's filing.
* The other wake functions, epoll's and virtio-console's, read no sleeper's state after a store of their own. `sig_wake`, `ptrace_signal_wake` and an exiting child's wake of its parent (`exit.c`) pass a barrier or take a lock between the two.
