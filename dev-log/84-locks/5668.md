# 5668: the fdlock orphans case that ran past its bound

`guest.fdlock.orphans` failed three times in full runs as "it ran past its case bound of 300000 ms: mint-control is missing: nothing reported it": under TCG on i686 and x86_64 at 3c5c4ab4b, and under KVM on the ia32 profile at 439ea92b6. Nothing hung. The probe was still working when etude stopped it: its run makes some 80,000 disk flushes, the case's disk passed each of them to the host as an fdatasync of the image, and on the loaded testing host they took longer than the bound. The case's disk now keeps its flushes from the host (`flush` false, `cache=unsafe`), and a run passes in 42 to 44 s where it took more than 300.

## The consoles

All three kept consoles end alike. `fdstale` writes its report records only at its end (`report_at`), so the console's one `out` frame, `@etude 1 begin`, says nothing about how far it got. The four `process: exited with status 0` lines that follow are its four forked children (reader, holder, moved-file reader, working-directory child), so the probe was past `directory_case`; in a passing run on this host the leak loops after them take some 38 of its 39 seconds. The probe's processor time when etude stopped it, which etude-guest reports from wait4, was 11.4 s under TCG and 27.6 s under KVM, of the 298 s since its children ended, and etude-guest answered the cancel at once. So the probe did not spin, and nothing else in the guest stopped: it slept most of the time.

The history agrees. The passing attempts of the same week took 181 s (TCG) and 242 s (KVM) of the case's 300 s bound, against 40 s for the same probe on a disk that keeps its flushes from the host.

## The cause

A traced kernel counted the page cache's writeback runs by caller over one run of the probe: 80,382 runs, every one from `pc_sync`; none from the flusher, the writer's throttle or eviction. The disk counted 80,382 flushes and 160,820 commands, a write and a flush for each run.

`pc_sync` is called that often because it is the KRFFS disk root's barrier. `kernel.c` mounts the root with `pc_sync_wait` as its flush, and `krffs_barrier` (rach2/sys/fs/krffs.c) calls it wherever the protocol orders a write on the medium before the field that publishes it: around each resize, allocation, free and name change. The leak loops write 48 MB as 12,288 writes of 4 KiB that grow 768 files, with an unlink or a rename for each file, so a run of the probe passes tens of thousands of barriers, and each writes back the dirty pages of the root, a flush after each run of pages (`ata_write`). That is the design of KRFFS's crash ordering, and the count is the same in a pass.

Each guest flush is an fdatasync of the image on the host unless the disk is `cache=unsafe`. On the testing host's WSL root, at a load of 6, an fdatasync of 4 KiB took 3.0 ms at the median and 5.7 ms on average (257 ms at most, 300 tries). Booted by hand on a copy of the etude disk under TCG, one run of the probe took:

| Disk | Run |
|---|---|
| `cache=unsafe` | 34 to 44 s, 8 boots |
| the host's flushes | 547 s |
| the host's flushes, each 2 ms later | 655 s |

## Forcing it

The load that makes the trigger certain is a host whose flushes are slow. The runs after were made on the change before its rebase onto the milestone branch, which has it as d275b10da. A shim preloaded into QEMU for this task, and not kept, waited 4 ms in each fdatasync and fsync before making it, between the host's own median and mean, and the case ran through `tests/run fdlock` with `RACH2_CASES=guest.fdlock.orphans`, `-S 1` and `-R` repetitions.

| Profile | Before, at 596b15155 | After, at the change |
|---|---|---|
| i686, TCG | 2 of 2 past the bound, 301 s | 3 of 3 passed, 44 s |
| x86_64, TCG | 2 of 2 past the bound, 301 s | 3 of 3 passed, 42 to 43 s |
| ia32 profile, KVM | 2 of 2 past the bound, 301 s | 3 of 3 passed, 37 s |
| i686, KVM | 2 of 2 past the bound, 301 s | 2 of 2 passed, 36 to 37 s |
| x86_64, KVM | 2 of 2 past the bound, 301 s | 2 of 2 passed, 36 to 37 s |

The i686 and x86_64 rows under KVM ran before with the old case file put back in the tree. Each failure before had the release's message and a console like the release's: the begin record, four child exits, and nothing until the cancel at 300 s.

Under the same probe with `cache=unsafe`, 80 rounds of `fdstale` alone and 120 rounds beside a process that appended and fsynced a record every 50 ms, as etude-guest does for its commands, all passed on i686 under TCG, as did 89 such rounds on the ia32 profile under KVM; none stopped.

## The change

`tests/cases/guest.fdlock.json` gives both disks of the family `flush` false. The orphans case claims nothing about what survives a failure of the host, and each write still reaches the host's page cache. The raw case boots under `rach2.blocklag=10`, which tests/system/Readme.md already said keeps its disk's flushes from the host, though its profile did not; it passed once on each architecture under TCG after the change. tests/system/Readme.md, tests/system/suites.mk and tools/etude/Readme.md say why.

## Left as it is

A run of this probe flushes the disk 80,382 times for 48 MB, because each barrier of the root flushes after each run it writes. One flush after the barrier's last write would order the medium as well, and on real hardware, where a flush costs a millisecond or more, it would save most of the time this workload spends. That is a change to KRFFS's write-back, not needed by the case, and is not made here.

The other cases that boot the etude disk and write much through the root, `guest.stress` and the `blockprobe` cases, keep their flushes too; their counts were not measured.
