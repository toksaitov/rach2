# 84-163: what stands between the GL loop's 281.6 ms and Linux's 224 to 251

84-157 left WineD3D's GL round trip at 281.6 ms per 1000 trips on the Lenovo, against Linux's 224 to 251 ms of 80-27. This task measured on the machine, by Fable's six measurements on #4889, what holds the difference, so that the fix tasks 84-164 to 84-168 are weighed by it. It changes no code.

The answer: the GPU's clock. On 2026-11-13 Rach2's GPU ran at 200 MHz, its lowest, through every sampled loop, and its loops took 302.2 to 319.7 ms. Linux's GPU ran at a median 450 and 500 MHz in its two fast loops, 233.7 and 253.7 ms, and at 200 MHz throughout its slow one, 360.6 ms, the same day on the same machine. At 200 MHz Linux is no faster than Rach2; Linux's six loops of the day spread from 228.7 to 360.6 ms. None of the four parts Fable named holds the gap: per trip at 200 MHz Linux spends 128 us from the request to the GPU's interrupt, 14 us to the wake and 8 us to the run, and a sleeping SYNCOBJ_WAIT costs Rach2 126 to 132 us against Linux's 119 to 126 us asleep. Why Rach2's frequency does not rise is the next question; it is not among 84-164 to 84-168.

## The six measurements

1. **84-157's split.** Over its 281.6 ms loop wined3d_cs ran 159.0 ms and waited ready 44.5 ms; it slept in EXECBUFFER2 3 times; SYNCOBJ_WAIT took 2,994 calls in 104,070 us, 998 of them asleep, so 98 to 104 us a sleeping call, the bound depending on whether the 1,996 others took 0 or 3 us; the profile's `kernel 0` had 25 samples. Rows 87 (unlink, 98 calls, 6.3 ms), 257 (openat, 300, 5.0 ms) and 83 (mkdir, 49, 1.1 ms) are present in wined3d_cs's counts, 73 (flock) below the 16 longest, and ioctl 0x400c645f, GEM_SET_DOMAIN, once for 10.3 ms against 84-151's 24.5.
2. **Two loops back to back.** 310.2 then 302.2 ms. The warm loop has no unlink, mkdir or flock and 6 openat in wined3d_cs, against 98, 49 and 300 cold; the 10.4 ms SET_DOMAIN stays in both. Item 4's one-off costs are 8 ms, not the 20 to 45 that would make them the gap. The same kernel took 281.6 ms at 08:45 and 302 to 320 ms at 09:38; its GPU's frequency then was not read.
3. **Linux's run delay.** wined3d_cs's schedstat over each loop, which trip.py now reads on Linux as on Rach2: 3.4 to 20.5 ms waited ready in six loops, against Rach2's 26.9 to 42.2 in four today and 44.5 in 84-157's. Rach2's thread waits 15 to 25 ms longer a loop, mostly behind preemptions: ki915 preempted it 52 to 57 times a loop.
4. **Per-trip timestamps.** Linux, from tracefs on the monotonic clock ([84-163/linux/split-trace.txt](84-163/linux/split-trace.txt), [split-linux2-trace.txt](84-163/linux/split-linux2-trace.txt)), both traced loops at 200 MHz: of wined3d_cs's 1,001 and 1,005 sleeps, 999 and 1,002 were woken from an i915 interrupt. Per such trip, medians (means): the request's add to the interrupt's entry, the GPU's work and the interrupt's delivery, 127 and 128 us (142); the entry to `sched_waking`, 14 and 15 us; `sched_waking` to `sched_wakeup` 5 and 6 us; `sched_wakeup` to the switch-in, the ready wait, 2 and 3 us (3.3 and 18.0). Asleep 99 and 107 us (119 and 126). Linux's i915 signals its fences without the `dma_fence_signaled` event, so the signal itself is not in the trace, and the `sys_enter_ioctl` events filtered to wined3d_cs recorded nothing, so Linux's SYNCOBJ_WAIT has no time a call. Rach2 has no event tracer: no timestamp of the submission, the interrupt's entry, the signal or the switch-in, and no interrupt count per processor. What it gives is totals: SYNCOBJ_WAIT 2,977 and 2,994 calls of 131.2 and 121.9 ms, 992 and 990 asleep, so 126 to 132 and 117 to 123 us a sleeping call; and the ready wait above.
5. **Pinned.** `taskset`'s call works on Rach2: sched_setaffinity bound every tripprobe thread, wined3d_cs among them, to processors 1 to 3 as it appeared, 0.5 s before the loop. The loop took 310.8 ms, no faster, and wined3d_cs waited ready 42.2 ms. Placement off the boot processor is not the gap.
6. **The GPU's frequency.** Above. Both systems read `gt_act_freq_mhz` of card0, Rach2's from the CAGF field as Linux's.

The i915 interrupts are all on processor 0 under Linux too: 1,009 to 1,017 a loop, every one on processor 0, in each of its four sampled or traced loops. Rach2 took 1,004 and 1,006 over the window.

## Which part holds the gap

None of the four. At the same 200 MHz the parts compare as follows:

* GPU time and the interrupt's delivery: 128 us a trip on Linux at 200 MHz. Rach2 cannot split it out, but its whole sleeping SYNCOBJ_WAIT, 117 to 132 us, is no longer than Linux's sleep, 119 to 126 us, which spans the same GPU work. At 450 to 500 MHz the GPU's share would shrink, and Linux's loops at those clocks, 233.7 and 253.7 ms, are 57 to 77 ms shorter than its traced loop at 200 MHz, 311.1 ms: the gap to Rach2's 302 to 320 ms is that.
* Interrupt latency to the wake: 14 us on Linux from entry to the wake, with every interrupt on processor 0, as on Rach2.
* Wake latency: 5 to 6 us on Linux.
* Ready wait: 3 to 18 us a trip on Linux, 3.4 to 20.5 ms a loop; Rach2's 26.9 to 42.2 ms a loop is 15 to 25 ms more, about 20 us a trip. It is the one CPU-side difference left, and it does not show in the loop's time at 200 MHz, where Rach2 is as fast as Linux.

So the loop's gap is the GPU's clock: Linux's RPS raised the frequency in two of the three loops it was read in, and Rach2's stayed at RPn in both. What keeps Rach2's at RPn, the up-threshold's interrupts, their evaluation interval or the work that answers them in `i915_rps.c`, was not read here.

## The fix tasks

* 84-164 (retire in the submitter): ki915 still preempts wined3d_cs 52 to 57 times a loop, part of the ready wait above, which is about 20 us a trip more than Linux's.
* 84-165 (mutex spin): EXECBUFFER2 slept 1 to 3 times a loop; nothing to gain in this loop.
* 84-166 (preempt or place a woken thread): the ready wait is the measured target, 15 to 25 ms a loop. Pinning off processor 0 did not change it.
* 84-167 (spread MSIs): Linux delivers every i915 interrupt of the loop to processor 0 too; no difference in this loop.
* 84-168 (epoll): epoll_pwait2 had 40 to 47 whole-kernel samples a loop, as in 84-151 and 84-157; Linux's time there was not measured.

None of them moves the loop by the 50 to 80 ms that the frequency does.

## Not measured

* Rach2's GPU frequency during 84-157's 281.6 ms loop, and Linux's during the first session's loops.
* Why Rach2's RPS stays at RPn under this load.
* Linux's SYNCOBJ_WAIT time a call, and its signal's own timestamp.
* Rach2's interrupt count per processor, which its /proc/interrupts does not give.
