# 83-12: a 64 MiB blob's map and the held blobs' release

83-5b's trial on the Lenovo measured two rows of `nocopy` slower than Linux's baseline: a blob of 64 MiB made, mapped and touched in 88.3 ms, against Linux's 52.5 to 58.1 ms, and 32 held blobs of 64 KiB unmapped and freed together in 23.1 ms, against 15.9 ms ([83-5](83-5.md), Results). The cost of the first grew with the pages: 1.1 to 1.2 ms for blobs of 4 KiB and 64 KiB, 5.2 ms for 1 MiB, 25.2 ms for 16 MiB and 88.3 ms for 64 MiB, some 5 us a page. The second was 0.72 ms a blob, as was each serial unmap and free of a small blob, 0.66 to 0.76 ms; the testing PC's rehearsal, three hypervisors deep but on a processor about twice as fast, freed the 32 in 14.0 ms, so the cost was the processor's work in Rach2, not round trips to the VM.

## Where the time went

Read from the code along the path of one blob, from `VIRTGPU_RESOURCE_CREATE_BLOB` to the touch of its last page, and from `munmap` and `GEM_CLOSE` to the last frame given back.

* The touch faulted once for each page. A mapping of a blob of host memory is a device region (`vm_device_map`), and each of its pages came at its own fault (`device_fault`): an exception, the regions' lock let go, the big lock taken, the object asked for the page, both locks taken back, the region found again and the entry set. A blob of 64 MiB took 16,384 such faults, with the big lock contended by the VM's QEMU threads, which take it for their exits. Linux maps such a blob whole at `mmap` (`virtio_gpu_vram_mmap`, `io_remap_pfn_range`, drivers/gpu/drm/virtio/virtgpu_vram.c:32-67), and i915 maps a whole object at its first fault (`vm_fault_cpu`, `remap_io_sg`, drivers/gpu/drm/i915/gem/i915_gem_mman.c:253-295).
* Each blob's free walked every address space. `vgpu_obj_free` took the blob's mappings away with `paging_zap_device`, which visits every record on the process list, takes its space's regions' lock to write, moves its count of zaps and walks its regions; each thread has a record, so QEMU's space, with its dozen threads and hundreds of regions, was walked a dozen times for each blob. A mapping holds a reference to its object, so none can be left when the object is freed, and Linux's GEM free unmaps nothing: `drm_gem_vm_open` takes the reference (drivers/gpu/drm/drm_gem.c:990-995) and `virtio_gpu_vram_free` sends UNMAP_BLOB and UNREF alone (virtgpu_vram.c:6-25). Where Linux does unmap an object, `unmap_mapping_range` visits each region that maps it once, through the file's tree of regions (mm/memory.c:3755-3774).
* The page list took a round trip through the VM for each 124 runs. RESOURCE_MAP_BLOB's answer was written to the half of the command's page after the request, 2048 bytes, room for 124 runs, so a blob whose 16,384 pages the VM's allocator scattered took up to 133 answers, each a doorbell, a wake of the VM's backend, its `VGPUSHM_IOC_PAGES` and a doorbell back; a blob of 4 KiB, whose make takes several such round trips, was made, mapped and touched in 1.2 ms on the Lenovo.
* KVM read each page's frame with a walk from the top. `kvm_gpamap_new` found each page's region and walked the maker's four levels of tables for it (`paging_user_ram_frame`), and the transport gathered the runs of each answer into an array copied whole at each answer. Linux's VFIO pins a batch of pages a call at a time (`vaddr_get_pfns`, `vfio_pin_pages_remote`, drivers/vfio/vfio_iommu_type1.c:551-590 and 597-713).
* VFIO and the IOMMU are not on the blob's path: the VM's memory is pinned and mapped in the domain when the VM starts, ten mappings of 532,544 pages in 83-5b's run, and a blob's map and free change neither. KVM's tables are not either once the VM has touched the pages: the first blob of 64 MiB, whose pages the VM had not used before, took 111.9 ms, and the next seven 85 ms each.

## What changed

* A device object whose `page` never sleeps sets `whole` in its `struct vm_device_ops`, and the first fault in its region maps every page of the region not yet present, 64 at a time under the checks the one page has: the region the same and no zap since (`device_map_rest`, rach2/sys/mm/paging.c). Virtio-gpu's blobs of host memory set it, both those mapped from the VM's pages and those in QEMU's host-visible window, as Linux maps both whole.
* `vgpu_obj_free` lets a blob's frames go without the zap (`vgpu_blob_pages_drop(..., 0)`); the reset, which may meet live mappings, still zaps, as does KVM's taking back of the frames.
* `paging_zap_where` changes each address space once in a walk, marking it with the walk's number (`mm->zap_walk`), and a walk that starts again after waiting for a lock takes a new number and zaps every space again, since a space it changed before the wait may have faulted the object back in while the big lock was free (83-12.1, from round 130's review).
* RESOURCE_MAP_BLOB's answer is written to 15 response pages, room for 3836 runs, so a blob of 64 MiB in scattered pages takes 5 answers; 15 pages and the request fit the shared region's chain of 16 descriptors when the heap cannot give them as one block, and when the heap has not got them, or the chain cannot carry them, the answer goes to the command's own page as before. The VM's backend answers up to 64 KiB (`VD_RESP_MAX`), at most 4092 runs after the answer's 56-byte header, below the 4096 `VGPUSHM_IOC_PAGES` gives at once. The transport grows its array of runs by doubling.
* KVM reads each part of a map, the run of pages one slot holds, in one walk of the maker's tables: the region found once for each it crosses and each leaf table once (`paging_user_ram_frames`).
* `nocopy serial` prints the most page faults one buffer's touch took, and `bed-shm.sh`'s `measure` fails when one took more than 16, since a call per page shows as a count, not only as a time.

### Readiness

Why no path this trial adds can leave the Lenovo stopped. Everything else the step runs ran in 83-5b's trial on 2026-11-12, at 0d639415e, whose code this branch carries: the trial root, the GPU's release to vfio-pci and its return, translation on dmar0, KVM under VMX with the GPU assigned, ivshmemd and virtio-gpu over the shared region, KVM's maps of the VM's pages, and QEMU's death with maps live. No register sequence, interrupt or hardware path is new.

1. The whole-region fault (`device_map_rest`, rach2/sys/mm/paging.c). It runs only in a fault of a region whose object sets `whole`, virtio-gpu's blobs of host memory alone, after the faulting page is mapped as before. It is a loop over the region's pages, 64 at a time, ended by the region's end, by the first page the object has not got, by a table that cannot be had, or by a change of the region or a zap while the locks were let go; it waits for nothing but the regions' lock and the big lock, which the one-page fault already takes in the same order (`callback_begin`, `callback_end`), and the object's `page` there is an array lookup (`host_phys`, `kvm_gpamap_frame`) that never sleeps. A frame KVM takes back meanwhile is not mapped: its revoke zaps, which moves every space's count of zaps, and each batch is mapped only when the count is as it was.
2. The zap walk (`paging_zap_where`). The same locks, taken in the same order, for fewer spaces in a pass; a walk that waits starts again with a new number and zaps every space again, as the walk before this task did after each wait, and ends after a pass that waits for nothing.
3. The free without a zap (`vgpu_obj_free`). It removes work: no mapping of a freed object can exist, since each holds a reference to it. The reset and KVM's revoke still zap.
4. The page list in 15 response pages. The same RESOURCE_MAP_BLOB, waited for as before until the answer comes or the device dies, and every pending command fails at the VM's death (83-3b.4), which `bed-shm.sh`'s bounds bring about; the chain of 16 descriptors is checked before it is sent (`virtqueue_chain_fits`), and an answer that cannot have its pages goes to the command's page as before. The VM's backend only answers up to 64 KiB.
5. KVM's walk of the maker's tables (`paging_user_ram_frames`). It reads entries and waits for nothing; it refuses the same pages the one-page reading refused.

Every wait of the run is `bed-shm.sh`'s, bounded as in 83-5b's step (Readiness, 83-5.md), and any exit of `bed-shm.sh` kills QEMU and has `gpu.sh` give the GPU back. The rehearsal: `machine.bed-shm.nocopy` in QEMU on the testing PC, below, runs every path above three hypervisors deep on Venus over lavapipe, the revoke and the leak check among them.
