## The WebGL scene

[`webgl.html`](../../bed/measure/webgl.html) draws a grid of 32 by 32 lit cubes turning, in one instanced draw of 36,864 vertices, over a full-window quad whose fragment shader runs 24 rounds of a hash, the same work in every frame, and posts each frame's time between `requestAnimationFrame` callbacks. It checks `getError` once after its first frames and posts the error with `isContextLost()`, and a `webglcontextlost` listener posts the loss, so that a lost context fails the scene by name.

On Debian. In the graphics tests' Debian reference guest under `virtio-vga`, Chromium 156 under a headless Weston with Pixman, the first form of the page, the grid drawn from one vertex buffer of all its cubes, lost its context one or two frames after its first draw. The size of the buffer decides: 294,912 bytes or more loses it, 73,728 draws, and 36 vertices drawn out of the large buffer fail as the whole does. That is Chromium's or ANGLE's fault on llvmpipe, recorded in [upstream.md](upstream.md#chromium-on-llvmpipe-loses-a-webgl-context-after-a-large-vertex-buffer). The page now draws one cube's 36 vertices 1,024 times with `ANGLE_instanced_arrays` from buffers of 864 and 8,192 bytes; Debian's Chromium drew it 299 frames at 93.25 ms a frame on "ANGLE (Mesa, llvmpipe (LLVM 19.1.7 256 bits), OpenGL ES 3.2)", its read pixel `[25,25,64,255]`.

On Rach2 the context is lost at the first frame, and the cause is not found. Chromium 156 on Rach2's GNU root, ANGLE over the same Mesa's llvmpipe, under the harness's headless Weston with Pixman as the graphics tests run it, reports `CONTEXT_LOST_WEBGL` with `isContextLost()` true at frame 1 for every page tried: the scene, a page that only clears, a 256 by 256 canvas, the default context attributes, no depth test, a trivial vertex shader, one triangle, one attribute. Neither the GPU process's sandbox off, nor the GPU in the browser's process, which crashes nothing, nor `LP_NUM_THREADS=0`, nor the test account in place of `desktop`, nor `/dev/udmabuf` closed to the user changed it, and `MESA_DEBUG` printed no Mesa error. What differs from Debian in what was read: Chromium's log on Rach2 has "No suitable EGL configs found", which Debian's lacks, and `eglinfo` differs only in Rach2's GBM platform offering `EGL_ANDROID_native_fence_sync`. On the same root, Chromium with `--use-angle=swiftshader` draws the scene 299 frames at 96 ms, and Firefox on llvmpipe at 75 ms. So the loss comes with ANGLE's GL backend over Rach2's Mesa, and where in it is not known. The first comparisons to make are the `webgl` suite's Chromium cases on Rach2, which run the same browser under the same Weston, and ANGLE's EGL config query on Rach2 beside Debian's. Until it is found the scene's rows on Rach2 fail, as a user's page would.

* The session's stop waited for `su` alone. `su` and `dbus-run-session` were gone while the old Weston, on the dead VM's card, still held `wayland-0`'s lock, so the new Weston failed at once ("unable to lock lockfile /run/user/1001/wayland-0.lock", "fatal: failed to add socket") and the session ended. * `wait -n -p ended` leaves its name unset when a signal ends it, so a SIGHUP while the supervisor waited ended it under `set -u` ("line 129: ended: unbound variable"), and init restarted the whole service. The name is set again after the wait.

The case's own fallback client then waited without a bound on the old Weston, which accepted it and never drew. It now runs as `drawn`, which kills glmark2 once its output has not grown for 120 s.

What QEMU cannot show, the session's return to Rach2's driver on the GPU and the pipe's CRC, is the `return` check of `bed-shm.sh -w -g`, which runs on the Lenovo; reading it for the step found its pattern for Crocus's renderer line wrong, `Intel` where Weston logs `Mesa Intel(R) HD Graphics 4400 (HSW GT2)`, which is fixed.

### Readiness

Why nothing the trial adds can leave the Lenovo stopped, as the `machine` skill's Trial boots asks.

What this trial runs there for the first time:

1. The harness itself, `measure.sh`, as root reading `/proc/stat` and running the scene as `desktop`: glmark2-wayland, piglit, Chromium and SuperTuxKart, each of which has run on Rach2's Crocus under the graphics suites. It writes only OUT, which stays root's while `desktop` writes the scene's files there through its group, reads those files only through copies `desktop` makes into root's `OUT/taken`, removes an earlier run's names without changing their owner, and sets no time limit. A result copy runs in the background under the exit trap, which ends it with TERM and CONT, KILLs it 5 s later or as soon as it has gone, and does not wait to reap it, so neither a FIFO substituted after its plain-file check nor `desktop` stopping or tracing its reader can hold off TERM. The trap ends the scene's half the same way, TERM and KILL 15 s later, and does not wait to reap it either, so `desktop` tracing that half cannot hold off the stop; a scene that does not end is stopped as step 13 says.
2. The session following its display (`rach2-desktop`, this task): when the card it draws on goes away, as when `gpu.sh` takes the GPU or the VM that drew it dies, the supervisor starts the session again headless with GL on llvmpipe, and when a card appears again, on it, or on the GPU at once when `gpu.sh` gave it back before the old session had gone; a card that went away and came back under the same name still counts as gone. It is the supervisor's shell and Weston's own starts, with no kernel code; a stop of the session waits until its whole group has gone, and kills what is left after 10 seconds. The worst a fault here does is leave the session ended, which the console and SSH survive and SIGHUP starts again.
3. The vtest rung's guest with the GPU (`vtest-rach2.sh -g`, this task): the bed-vtest Debian guest started through `gpu.sh` on a raw copy of its overlay, its server on the GPU's render node, measured through the relay, then killed by `gpu.sh`'s SIGUSR1 and the GPU given back, the same `gpu.sh` path as 82-4b's.
4. `bed-shm.sh -w` on the GPU, with figures and four loads: the session on the region's virtio-gpu card, the VM's virgl drawing on the HD 4400 through the VM's i915 and Crocus, which the VM's backend now uses for GL as well as for Venus, the VM killed under the load with four fenced submissions pending, then the session on llvmpipe and back on Rach2's driver. ivshmemd and the region's transport are 83-3b's and 83-5b's; the death and its failed waits are 83-3b.4's, which ran in `machine.bed-shm`. Each load and figure runs in a process group of its own, which bed-shm.sh ends after the death and on any exit.
5. Debian 13.7 running the harness: its glmark2, piglit, Chromium and SuperTuxKart, which 80-2's session ran on the same boot.
6. Kernel code: `virtio_gpu_copied_bytes` in `/proc/stat`, which the harness reads. Rach2's virtio-gpu counts each transfer's bytes when its command is freed answered OK, under a spinlock of its own, and reads the TRANSFER3D, COPY_TRANSFER3D and RESOURCE_INLINE_WRITE commands of each submission on a virgl context it copies in, within the submission's own length, for their boxes, each box's resource found by binary search in a sorted copy of the submission's buffer list, so that a submission costs its commands times the logarithm of its buffers. It changes nothing the device is sent, and it fails no submission: one whose sorted copy cannot be allocated counts nothing; a submission on a Venus or native context is not read. On the Lenovo it runs only in step 14, where virtio-gpu binds over the region.

Every wait is bounded. `gpu.sh` runs with `-w 0`, so the bounds are its callers': vtest-rach2.sh's 600 s of the guest's console's silence before its server is ready; bed-shm.sh's 600 s of silence of the VM's console and QEMU's processor time for the bind and the session's start on the region's card, 600 s for a harness load's start, 300 s for the load's doorbells and for the session's start after the death, and 120 s of silence of the fallback client's output. The harness's scenes have no bound of their own; each run below is watched as step 13 says. Any exit of bed-shm.sh or vtest-rach2.sh, by its end or by SIGTERM, even while a scene runs, ends the scene's process group, kills QEMU and has `gpu.sh` give the GPU back; any exit of `measure.sh` ends its scene's group.

What the step cannot vouch for before it runs: the WebGL scene on Rach2. Chromium's WebGL on ANGLE over Mesa's GL loses its context at its first frame on Rach2, in QEMU and so far everywhere it was tried ([The WebGL scene](#the-webgl-scene)); its rows will show the failure until that is found, and the `-l webgl` load ends at once, so its death row is of an ended load.
