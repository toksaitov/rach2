# 83-2: the forward direction

83-2a's half of the forward direction ([bed/forward](../../bed/forward/Readme.md)), on the testing PC. A Debian guest of Debian's QEMU on Rach2's x86-64 GNU root draws on Rach2's GPU through QEMU's own virtio-gpu, by virgl and by Venus with blobs, and its pictures are right: the triangle drawn through virgl has the checksum of the guest's llvmpipe and glmark2's scene validates, at 52 and 63 frames a second in the case's two runs; vkcube's two frames through Venus are the reference's, at 36.57 and 33.78 presents a second. The guest's processor there is QEMU's emulator, since Rach2 is itself a guest on the testing PC and a guest of its KVM there does not finish Debian's boot. Two faults of Rach2's KVM that the attempt showed are fixed here, a third, which hung one start in six in SeaBIOS, is the one 81-3 found and fixed on the stack's branch meanwhile, and one end of a start under it, seen once, is open; two faults in the `venus` runs are other projects' and happen on Linux in the same way. Debian in Rach2's place, with the same QEMU, gave 42 frames and 47.09 presents a second and answered SSH after 29 to 33 s, where Rach2 took 38 to 68. Those differences are measured here and their causes are not traced; 83-7 compares the rungs. The Lenovo's half, under Rach2's KVM on its own GPU, is 83-2b's.

## The machines

* The testing PC is an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X3D with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4080 SUPER, under Windows and Hyper-V. Its Linux is Ubuntu 26.04.1 in WSL 2, Linux 6.18.58.1-microsoft-standard-WSL2, with QEMU 10.2.1 (`1:10.2.1+ds-1ubuntu3.3`), virglrenderer 1.2.0 (`1.2.0-2ubuntu2`) and Mesa 26.0.8. WSL has GL on the GPU through Mesa's d3d12 driver and no hardware Vulkan driver, so its Vulkan is lavapipe.
* Rach2 is the x86-64 GNU root at the task's head, a guest of the PC's KVM with 8 processors and 12 GiB, on the test runner's display profile `virgl`, which gives it the PC's GPU through virgl, or `venus`, which gives it Venus on the PC's lavapipe and GL on its llvmpipe. Its Mesa is Debian's 25.0.7-2+deb13u1 with the Venus driver of 26.1.6-1~bpo13+1, and its QEMU is Debian's 10.0.13 (`1:10.0.13+ds-0+deb13u1`) with `libvirglrenderer1` and `virgl-server` 1.1.0-2 ([the KVM layer](../../vendor/gnu/kvm/Readme.md)).
* The inner guest is the graphics tests' Debian 13 [reference guest](../../tests/graphics/Readme.md#the-reference-guest), Linux 6.12.115 with the same Mesa and the same Venus driver, on q35 with 4 processors and 4096 MiB under `-accel tcg -cpu max`, its disk a snapshot.

### What the figures say

* The emulator costs more than the graphics path: glmark2 falls from 76 frames a second to 42 to 53, and vkcube from 105 to 111 presents a second to 30 to 47. The rates on the testing PC so measure QEMU's emulator beside virtio-gpu. The rates under Rach2's KVM are the Lenovo's, 83-2b.
* Against Debian in its place, Rach2 answered SSH after 38 to 68 s where Debian took 29 to 33 s; it presented vkcube at 30.5 to 36.6 a second against 47.1; and it drew glmark2 at 52 to 63 frames a second against 42. No cause is traced for any of the three, nor for the cases' second runs, which answered SSH after 38 and 40 s where the first took 66 and 64: the merged branch and the machine's other load both differ between them. Each figure is one run, or two, under an emulator on a machine with other load, and the two rigs differ in the disk's bus and in where the overlay is kept, as said above.
* During the guest's disk transfers Rach2 logs `block: request pool: waiting for a slot: the device holds its share`. That is the block layer's ordinary wait: a disk may have half of the pool's 16 requests in flight, and a caller that finds it so waits ([the block layer](../../rach2/sys/block/Readme.md)). Whether that bound is part of the later SSH answer was not measured.

## What stood in the way on Rach2

* A guest of Rach2's KVM is at the third level on the testing PC, under Hyper-V and the PC's KVM, and Debian does not finish its boot there. With `forward.sh -a kvm`, on the kernel with the two fixes below, GRUB wrote its first bytes to the console 57 s after QEMU started and the kernel its first line after 136 s; all four processors came up; `Run /init` came at 145 s of the guest's clock and systemd at 264 s; the wait for the EFI partition's device then ran out, its check, `/boot/efi` and `local-fs.target` failed for it, and Debian was in emergency mode 12 minutes after QEMU started ([kvm-third-level.txt](83-2a/kvm-third-level.txt)). A run before the fixes went the same way: the kernel's first line after 2.5 minutes, systemd at 258 s, emergency mode after 14 minutes. So did one on the head with the stack's branch merged, which has the fix of the run pages below: the console's first bytes after 77 to 88 s, the kernel's first line after 3 minutes, systemd at 296 s, the same wait ran out, and this time the one for `/dev/ttyS0` with it, and emergency mode after 15 minutes; the guest's kernel also reported an RCU stall on its second processor at 534 s, and Rach2's log had no line of `kvm:` but `kvm: ready: svm`. The guest's kernel could not calibrate its TSC against the PIT, the HPET or the PM timer (`tsc: HPET/PMTIMER calibration failed`), marked it unstable and took the HPET for its clock, which QEMU emulates, so that each reading of the time leaves the guest for QEMU. Rach2's KVM offers no paravirtual leaf and so no kvm-clock, which a guest of Linux's KVM reads without leaving ([its limits](../../rach2/sys/kvm/Readme.md#limits)). What share of the boot's time that is was not measured. The runner so has `-a tcg`, which the cases pass, and `kvm` stays its default for a Rach2 on hardware. The [testing skill](../../.agents/skills/testing/SKILL.md) has the same rule for every third-level guest of the PC.
* Rach2's KVM answered two of the guest's MSR accesses with #GP where Linux's does not, and Debian's kernel logged each with a stack trace: `unchecked MSR access error: RDMSR from 0xc0010010`, AMD's SYSCFG, read with the MTRRs, and `WRMSR to 0xc001001f`, AMD's NB_CFG, set on each processor brought up. Linux's KVM reads the first as zero and drops the second, with the other registers of its two lists (`kvm_get_msr_common` and `kvm_set_msr_common`, `arch/x86/kvm/x86.c` of Linux 6.12.115). `kvm_emul_msr` now answers both lists as Linux does, with rows for them in `kvm_vmx_hosttest`; the guest's console has neither line since. The MSRs that Linux's KVM keeps a value for, HWCR among them, were not compared.
* Rach2's KVM lost the vector of a SIPI that came while its INIT still waited. The vector was kept in the vCPU's record of events, which the INIT's reset of the vCPU clears; a vCPU that found both waiting took the INIT and then started at vector 0, at address 0, where the sender meant its trampoline. SeaBIOS sends the two with nothing between them. Linux's KVM keeps the vector in the APIC, and `vlapic.c` now does. A row of `kvm_irq_hosttest` sends both before the vCPU looks; it fails on the old code and passes on the new. The fault was found by reading, after a start under `-a kvm` whose console stayed empty for nine minutes while the threads of the first and the fourth vCPU ran and the other two slept. That start's registers were not read, so it is not shown to have been this fault.
* One start in six under `-a kvm`, on the kernel with the MSR fix, hung in SeaBIOS: the console was still empty after 100 s. The first processor ran zeros between 0xD8000 and 0xDE7FF in 32-bit mode, in a loop that SeaBIOS's code at 0xE008B enters by a call into that range; the others were at reset. The range is where SeaBIOS copies its 32-bit code once it has found the host bridge and set its PAM registers, which make 0xC0000 to 0xDFFFF RAM. Here the PAM registers were zero and QEMU still had its ROM there, though the bridge answered the monitor's own read with its identity, 0x29c08086. The PCI address latch at port 0xCF8 held 0xffffffff, where SeaBIOS writes addresses with bit 31 set and the low two bits clear. So SeaBIOS's search for the bridge failed on port I/O that did not reach QEMU as the guest made it ([kvm-post-hang.txt](83-2a/kvm-post-hang.txt) has the monitor's answers). 81-3 found the cause on the Lenovo while this task ran, by the same signs, SeaBIOS's first write to port 0xCF8 arriving as 0xffffffff, the shadow RAM left locked and the guest running zeros ([81-3.md](../81-kvm/81-3.md#the-run-pages-frames)): a vCPU's port I/O page was mapped into QEMU at the frame after its run page's, and the two pages, the heap's, are neighbours in virtual memory only, so that QEMU took an OUT's bytes from another owner's frame and put an IN's answer there. The stack's branch has the fix, which reads each page's frame from the kernel's tables. With it merged, 13 starts of the same guest under `-a kvm`, 12 of them one after another in one boot of Rach2, each wrote to the console within 100 s. The family's first run, under `-a kvm` on the kernel before the fixes, also failed with an empty console, after 600 s of silence; whether it was this hang, the lost SIPI vector or neither was not read.
* Rach2's ordinary kernel takes no write to a disk's node ([disk(4)](../../man/man4/disk.4)), and Debian does not boot from a disk it cannot write: its root stays read-only and nothing can be made in a home directory. The runner opens the disk with `snapshot=on`. QEMU puts the snapshot's overlay in `/var/tmp`, which the GNU root does not have ("Could not open temporary file '/var/tmp/vl.I48WY3': No such file or directory"), so the runner sets `TMPDIR` to its output directory.
* With `venus=on`, virglrenderer runs the guest's Vulkan calls in `/usr/libexec/virgl_render_server`, which is in Debian's `virgl-server`, a package nothing in QEMU's closure depends on. The KVM layer now stages it, with `virgl_test_server` and its page.

## What is open in Rach2's KVM

One start under `-a kvm` on the testing PC ended in a way that is not explained. It does not touch the family's cases, whose guest runs under the emulator, and is for the KVM milestone to trace, on SVM at the third level, where it was seen.

* QEMU's end by itself, once, on the kernel with the MSR fix: at about 405 s of the guest's clock, after `local-fs.target` had failed and while the guest loaded its AppArmor profiles, QEMU was gone, its messages empty, and Rach2's kernel had logged nothing. The runner starts QEMU with `-no-reboot`, under which a guest's reset ends QEMU without a message; whether the guest reset was not seen, and that run's console was not kept. The run on the kernel with both fixes passed that point and was stopped by hand after 15 minutes, in emergency mode; the run with the stack's branch merged passed it too. The kernel of that end had the run pages' fault above, under which QEMU's answer to a guest's IN went into another owner's frame; whether the end came of it is not known.

## What other projects' code does

Two things in the `venus` runs look like faults of Rach2 and are not. Each has its entry in [upstream.md](upstream.md).

* [Mesa's Venus driver leaves its thread key's destructor behind when it is unloaded](upstream.md#mesas-venus-driver-leaves-its-thread-keys-destructor-behind-when-it-is-unloaded). Rach2 kills the render server's worker for each of the guest's Vulkan contexts when the context ends, `vm: fault: killing pid 199: bad read at 0x7eb375a563c0 (err 0x14)`: an instruction fetch at offset 0x363c0 of `libvulkan_virtio.so`, in memory the loader unmapped when the worker destroyed its last instance. The thread's end calls the destructor of a key that the driver made and never deleted. On Debian in Rach2's place the same workers die the same way, `vkr-ring-2[1171]: segfault at 7f20071273c0 ip 00007f20071273c0 ... error 14` ([linux-venus.txt](83-2a/linux-venus.txt)), and a program of 131 lines does it alone ([venus-tls.c](83-2a/venus-tls.c), [venus-tls.txt](83-2a/venus-tls.txt)). The worker has done its work by then, and the pictures are right.
* [virglrenderer unmaps a blob by what it is now, so one that a virgl context imported stays mapped](upstream.md#virglrenderer-unmaps-a-blob-by-what-it-is-now-so-one-that-a-virgl-context-imported-stays-mapped). The guest's kernel logs `*ERROR* response 0x1200 (command 0x209)` eight times in the scene, once for each of vkcube's swapchain images, which Weston's virgl context imports; QEMU 10.2.1 on Ubuntu answers the same eight ([unmap-trace.txt](83-2a/unmap-trace.txt)). Rach2's own virtio-gpu driver logs the same answer from the PC's QEMU, `virtio_gpu: command 0x209: error 0x1200`, eight times when QEMU on Rach2 ends, since Rach2 is then the guest whose Venus blobs its QEMU imported into a virgl context.

## The subtasks' checks

* 83-2a.1 asks for glmark2's one scene with an in-guest checksum. glmark2 gives no checksum; the scene is checked by glmark2's own `--validate`, which compares a frame with its reference picture, and the checksum is the triangle's, read back in the guest and equal on llvmpipe and virgl. The device is `virtio-vga-gl` where the plan says `virtio-gpu-gl`: Debian's GRUB under SeaBIOS resets on a display without a VGA.
* 83-2a.2 asks for vkcube off screen, read back. vkcube presents to a Weston of the scene's own on the guest's card, with QEMU's display headless, and the graphics tests' `vkcube.sh` reads its 300th and 345th frames back inside the guest; both have the CRC-32 the reference guest has on the PC's lavapipe.
* 83-2a.3 is the family `machine.bed-forward` with the two cases, which `tests/run -N bed-forward` lists and which milestone 83's checkpoint names as `bed-forward:virgl`.

## Running it again

On the testing PC, from the tree's top, with the x86-64 GNU image built:

```
RACH2_QEMU_ACCEL=kvm ARCH=x86_64 RACH2_CASES=machine.bed-forward.virgl tests/run bed-forward
RACH2_QEMU_ACCEL=kvm ARCH=x86_64 RACH2_CASES=machine.bed-forward.venus tests/run bed-forward
```

The first run makes the guest's image, `build/tests/cases/bed-forward/guest.qcow2`, from the reference guest's prepared image. On a Rach2 on hardware the runner is given the disk, the key and the scenes by hand, as [its Readme](../../bed/forward/Readme.md) says, and `-a kvm` is its default.
