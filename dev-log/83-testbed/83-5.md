# 83-5: buffers without copies

83-5a's half of the reversed path's buffers, on the testing PC. A Venus buffer that the VM's lavapipe makes is mapped into a Rach2 program from the VM's own pages: the VM's module pins it and answers its page list, Rach2's KVM resolves the pages through the VM's memory slots and maps their frames into the program, and what the CPU writes there the VM's GPU reads, and what the GPU writes the CPU reads, with nothing copied. Buffers the VM cannot export are refused, and when the VM is killed with a mapping live the program's next touch raises SIGBUS and every page Rach2 held is given back. The case is `machine.bed-shm.nocopy` ([tests/kvm](../../tests/kvm/Readme.md)); 83-5b does the same on the Lenovo's hasvk.

## The machines

* The testing PC is an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X3D under Windows and Hyper-V. Its Linux is Ubuntu 26.04.1 in WSL 2, Linux 6.18.58.1-microsoft-standard-WSL2, with QEMU 10.2.1 (`1:10.2.1+ds-1ubuntu3.3`), which runs Rach2 under WSL's KVM.
* Rach2 is the x86-64 GNU root at the task's head, Linux 6.18.0-rach2 to `uname`, under `host-svm` with 4 processors and 6 GiB. Its QEMU is Debian's 10.0.13 (`1:10.0.13+ds-0+deb13u1`), which runs the VM under Rach2's KVM: three hypervisors deep.
* The VM is the test bed's Debian 13 ([bed/vm](../../bed/vm/Readme.md)) with 2 processors and 2 GiB, its backend `vgpu-backend` over virglrenderer 1.1.0-2+rach21, Venus on lavapipe (LLVM 19.1.7, 256 bits), and its module for the doorbells and the page lists. The region the two share is 32 MiB, of which the transport's heap is 8189 pages.

## Against Linux

The plan's baseline on the same PC is Linux one hypervisor deep, QEMU 10.2.1 on WSL's lavapipe with the module: 7.2 to 31.0 ms to map a blob and 2.4 to 3.6 ms to unmap it. Rach2's unmap and free, 1.1 to 3.3 ms, is as fast as Linux's. Its make, map and touch is slower, 26 to 271 ms for a blob of up to 1 MiB, and grows little with the size up to 1 MiB, so its cost is in the commands and answers between Rach2 and the VM's backend, three hypervisors deep, more than in the pages; a 64 MiB blob, whose 16,384 pages the VM's kernel pins and lists, takes 1 to 2.2 s.

Once mapped, a buffer is memory of the program's like any other. Through a 64 MiB mapping the CPU read at 61 to 114 percent of its rate in its own memory and wrote at 54 to 80 percent; Linux's baseline, a Linux VM's hasvk on the Lenovo, gives 96 to 105 percent of the host's own. The cause of the slower writes is not traced; 83-5b measures the same on the Lenovo's GPU.

A copy costs a command and the device's copy each way: a GL buffer of 1 MiB with its backing in the region took 173 to 538 ms to reach the VM and 9.5 to 37 ms to come back, where the mapped buffers need neither. A blob in the region itself needs no answer from the VM's kernel and is made in 0.3 to 6.5 ms, but the region is 32 MiB, shared with every command, and holds a blob's pages until the VM answers its UNREF; the VM's own pages have no such bound.

## Faults found and fixed

* Virtio-gpu's waiter spun under the big lock. `cmd_wait` slept on the command's fence, which the answer to a later command signals, the device's timeline being in queue order, before this command's own answer is reaped; the wait returned at once, so the caller looped in the kernel holding the big kernel lock, and the VM's QEMU threads, which take it for each exit, could not run to make the answer. The five-second bound on each command ended the loop and hid it; once RESOURCE_MAP_BLOB waited without the bound, the machine stopped. Samples of each processor's instruction pointer through the outer QEMU's monitor put one processor in `cmd_wait` and its fence and clock calls and the others in `arch_relax` and `bkl_acquire`, waiting for the lock, with none in user mode. The waiter now sleeps on the queue's wake, which the transport's thread raises as it reaps.
* RESOURCE_MAP_BLOB had the five-second bound of every command, which a 64 MiB blob passes three hypervisors deep: the largest make, map and touch took 8.5 s. Linux's `virtio_gpu_vram_map` waits for the answer without a bound, and Rach2's does too.
* The shared region's heap refused a 4 MiB blob in the region with ENOMEM, for two causes, which a trace of the heap's takes and gives showed. `vgshmprobe` makes, maps, touches and frees eight such blobs in turn, some 10 ms in all, and a blob's pages go back to the heap only once the VM answers its RESOURCE_UNREF, tens of ms later three hypervisors deep: the copy's buffer before them and the eight blobs, 9216 pages, were asked of a heap of 8189 within a few ms. Virtio-gpu now waits, when the transport's memory is full and UNREFs are in flight, until one is answered, and refuses only when none is. The heap also takes first fit from a rover that moves past each take, so that the few pages commands hold are spread across it: with six blobs in use, 6171 pages taken, no run of the 2018 pages left free was 1024 long, and the seventh blob took runs of 355, 3 and 666 pages; a backing that finds no single run now takes the free runs in turn, and each of its pages goes back alone.
* `vgshmprobe` gave PIPE_RESOURCE_CREATE its fields in the wrong order and virgl's map flags the wrong bits; virglrenderer's errors named both.
* The VM's serial console lags its work by seconds three hypervisors deep, so a check that grepped the console for the backend's refusal at once missed it; `bed-shm.sh` now waits for the line. The backend's line for each piece of a page list filled that console, and is gone.

Two things seen once are not traced. The `virgl` case's doorbell check failed in one run, with 2 wakes and 1 ioeventfd signal; and the VM's render server logged "vkr: vkFreeMemory resulted in CS error" a few times in some runs, which failed no check and left no map.

### Readiness (83-5b.1)

Why no path the trial adds can leave the Lenovo stopped, as the `machine` skill's Trial boots asks.

What has run on the Lenovo: the GPU's release to vfio-pci, translation on dmar0, KVM under VMX with the GPU assigned, QEMU's death by SIGKILL under load and the bring-up from a reset, every count back and no DMA fault, in 82-4b's second run on 2026-11-11 with `gpu.sh` and the same QEMU, 10.0.13 with `kernel-irqchip=split`; ioeventfds and irqfds under VMX in the same run, whose `/proc/stat` counted up to 23,753 ioeventfd signals from the guest's virtio devices and 1,358,251 irqfd raises from the GPU's messages ([`82-4/second/`](../82-assign/82-4/second/)); hasvk on Rach2 itself (78-11c). Venus on hasvk under Rach2's KVM is 83-2b's step, which has not run yet. What this trial runs there for the first time:

1. ivshmemd and Rach2's virtio-gpu over the shared region (`rach2/sys/virtio/shmem.c`, 83-3b), with its doorbells through KVM's ioeventfd and irqfd. It has run only on the testing PC, under SVM, in `machine.bed-shm`. Its waits: each command five seconds, as Linux's virtio-gpu, but RESOURCE_MAP_BLOB, which waits for its answer without a bound, as Linux's `virtio_gpu_vram_map`, sleeping on the queue's wake; every pending command fails when the VM dies (83-3b.4), so a VM that stops answering is ended by `bed-shm.sh`'s bounds below and its waits with it.
2. KVM's map of the VM's pages into a program (`rach2/sys/kvm/gpamap.c`, 83-5a): the page list resolved through the VM's slots into frames of QEMU's memory, each held by a reference, and taken back on the end of QEMU's address space, a slot's change or the VM's end. It reads no register and waits on nothing; it is code of the architecture-neutral KVM layer, which VMX shares with SVM.
3. That map beside VFIO's pins of the same memory. When QEMU dies, the end of its address space takes the map back first, with the program's mapping, and drops the map's references; VFIO's pins of the VM's memory stay past it (`PIN_KEEP_PAST_END`, 82-4b's fix) until the device's release has reset the GPU and the container has gone. The two hold references of their own on the same frames, so neither frees a frame the other holds. The rehearsal below ran this end in QEMU with QEMU's edu device given to the VM through `gpu.sh`.
4. The VM's i915 driving the GPU from a guest of other packages than step 11's: the test bed's Debian 13, the same kernel, 6.12.115+deb13.1, and Mesa, 25.0.7, with virglrenderer 1.1.0-2+rach21 rendering Venus on hasvk. What the VM does with the GPU reaches the host only as DMA through dmar0, counted and refused if it leaves the VM's domain, and as interrupts through remapped entries, as in 82-4b.

Every wait is bounded. `gpu.sh` runs with `-w 0`, so the bounds are `bed-shm.sh`'s: the wait for virtio-gpu's bind ends when QEMU exits or when for 600 s the VM's console stays silent and QEMU uses no processor time; each wait for a line of the VM or of a program ends after 30 to 120 s of the same silence; a figure runs until its program ends and prints ivshmemd's counts and the VM's last lines every 30 s, so a stalled one shows, and the step stops it (step 9). Any exit of `bed-shm.sh`, by its end or by a signal, kills QEMU and has `gpu.sh` give the GPU back, as its trap does.
