# The VM exits of a load against Linux's

Task 80-94 (issue #4798) named, from the guest's side, the VM exits Rach2 makes in QEMU with KVM where Linux makes fewer, and brought the largest kinds to Linux's form. 80-92 measured Crysis's island load at four times Linux's exits and five times its interrupt-window exits. This task measured the same kinds on small loads that each make one kind of kernel work and on a tar of GE-Proton's tree through a pipe, which reads many files as the game reads its archives. On 2026-11-13, on the tar, Rach2 made 1,975,881 exits at the task's start and 503,209 at its end, against Linux's 317,872 and 330,957; its interrupt-window exits fell from 81,972 to 21,949, against Linux's 4,192 and 4,237. A second session the same day brought the tar to 450,184 exits and the idle machine from 81,018 exits in 10 s to 20,706, against Linux's 4,511; its interrupt-window exits stayed at 18,293, and the section on the second session has its figures. Crysis's load itself was not measured again here. What is left is larger than this task and is listed in the last section.

## The measurement

[vm.py](80-94/vm.py) boots Rach2's kernel on the GNU root of `gnu-test.disk`, or Debian's 6.12 kernel with that root in a chroot, as 80-92's `crysis-load.py` boots them, with a 64 MiB ext2 disk, `tools.ext2` beside the script, that holds [load.c](80-94/load.c) built static (`gcc -O2 -static -pthread`). It runs a script's lines one at a time, over the serial console on Rach2 and over SSH on Linux, and prints KVM's counters of the VM over each, from QMP's `query-stats` of the vCPUs. KVM names the halts, the port and MMIO accesses, the external interrupts and the interrupt windows; the rest, printed as `unnamed`, is mostly MSR accesses, which under KVM without APIC virtualization include every x2APIC register access: the EOI at 0x80b, the ICR at 0x830 and the timer's count at 0x838. QEMU runs with KVM in WSL 2 on the testing host, nested under Hyper-V, with four processors and 4 GiB. Rach2 boots with `rach2.exits`, which this task added: debugfs's `exits` counts each processor's MSR accesses by register, `cpuid` by leaf, port accesses by port and halts, and the interprocessor interrupts sent by kind, and [diff.py](80-94/diff.py) prints the difference of two readings. [loads.txt](80-94/loads.txt) is the set of loads, [exits.txt](80-94/exits.txt) the one with the readings, and [linux-msr.txt](80-94/linux-msr.txt) the Linux run that counted MSR writes by register through the `msr` tracepoints' histograms and the interrupts by kind in `/proc/interrupts`.

## The fixes

* The idle wait polled before every halt under any hypervisor, and each look at the local APIC's request register in the poll, every 10 us, read an x2APIC MSR, which is an exit. Linux loads cpuidle-haltpoll only where KVM hints that the guest's processors are its own (KVM_HINTS_REALTIME) or `cpuidle_haltpoll.force` is given (drivers/cpuidle/cpuidle-haltpoll.c:96-111), and QEMU gives no such hint. The wait now polls under the same two conditions (`arch_idle_poll`). The tar's exits fell from 1.98 million to 795,000 and its unnamed exits from 1.61 million to 394,000. The futex hand-offs, which the poll took without a halt, now halt as Linux's do: 97,258 exits in 0.34 s, against Linux's 141,586 in 0.77 s.
* Every end of interrupt was a write of the EOI register. Linux gives KVM a word for the paravirtual end of interrupt (MSR_KVM_PV_EOI_EN, arch/x86/kernel/kvm.c:392-401): KVM sets it when it injects an interrupt whose end needs nothing of it, and the guest ends that interrupt by clearing the word in one instruction. Rach2 now does the same (`lapic_eoi`, `kvm_steal_cpu_setup`). A plain read and store of the word hung `tar` and the readahead thread in the state D, since KVM may take the word back between the two; the exchange fixed it. The tar's EOI writes fell from 198,000 to 67,000, and the busy unmaps' from 62,000 to 3,400.
* KVM sets the word only at an entry that injects an interrupt and takes it back at any exit before the end (arch/x86/kvm/lapic.c:3217-3299), and the tick's handler wrote the timer's count, an exit, before its EOI, so the tick still made an EOI exit on every processor every millisecond. The timer's interrupts and the interprocessor interrupt now end first, as Linux's `sysvec_apic_timer_interrupt` and its other system vectors end theirs (arch/x86/kernel/apic/apic.c:1053, arch/x86/kernel/smp.c:248-270). The spinning threads' 20,387 EOI writes in 5 s fell to none, and their exits from 70,480 to 50,491.
* A histogram of the kicks by their callers' return addresses, from a kernel that walked the frame pointers at each kick and was never committed, gave 121,000 of the tar's 212,000 kicks to `sleep_lock_give`: a sleeping lock's waiter slept at once and every give woke every waiter with an interprocessor interrupt, the pipe's lock between `tar` and `cat` above all. Linux's mutex spins while its owner runs (kernel/locking/mutex.c:350-385, include/linux/sched.h:2200-2207). A waiter now spins while the holder is the current thread of the processor it took the lock on, that processor is not halted in an idle wait and KVM's steal-time record does not mark it preempted (`sched_spin_on`). Counted by reason, the waiters that still slept had found the lock passing to a waiter ahead, 41,800, or held the big lock, 33,500, against 15,000 whose spin succeeded. A waiter now spins on while the lock passes and no waiter sleeps, and spins with the big lock unless another processor has claimed it or the holder's processor waits for it (`bkl_spin_ok`). The tar's kicks fell to 121,000 and its exits to 505,000.
* A pipe's write woke the readers after each page and a read woke the writers after each buffer; Linux wakes them once for a call, after its lock (fs/pipe.c:399-404, 575-602). Rach2 now does the same. The tar's kicks from the write fell only from 60,500 to 56,300: `tar` writes 56,000 records of 10 KiB, and `cat` sleeps between them, so each write still wakes it once.

* An idle processor ticked 1000 times a second at two exits a tick, its halt and the timer's count. A processor other than the boot one now stops its tick while its idle task waits, as Linux's NO_HZ idle does (kernel/time/tick-sched.c:1211-1245): a tick taken in the wait moves the next tick on by half the watchdog's sample period, 2 s, so that the watchdog's timer still runs and the buddy check sees the count move, and the switch to a thread starts it again on its phase (`timer_idle_exit`). The end of each wait charges the ticks skipped as idle, and `/proc/stat` gave each processor 1,000 idle ticks over the 10 s. An idle processor's tick also stole ready threads from busy queues; a busy processor's tick now kicks a stopped one when its queue holds a thread that one may take (`sched_idle_balance`), as Linux's nohz_balancer_kick does. The boot processor's tick polls devices (`timer_own_work`, `timer_locked_work`) and keeps ticking: the 20,706 exits of the idle machine are its 10,000 halts and count writes.
* A message interrupt ended after its chain, and the chain's MMIO reads and kicks exited first and took the paravirtual EOI back. It now ends before its chain, as Linux's handle_edge_irq acknowledges an edge first (kernel/irq/chip.c:787-838), and a message that comes during the chain, once a handler turns interrupts on or on another processor, is run by the run under way (`irq_dispatch_edge`). The tar's EOI writes fell from 31,375 to 5,064. The timer's divide register, written at each change of the timer's vector, is now written at its first count only, which took 7,637 writes of it from the tar.
* Each interprocessor interrupt was one write of the ICR for one processor. A TLB shootdown, a kernel flush and the kicks of a wake of many now send one message in logical mode for each x2APIC cluster, as Linux's x2apic_cluster does (arch/x86/kernel/apic/x2apic_cluster.c:39-77). The busy unmaps fell from 126,682 exits to 86,147.
* A wake of a thread still current on its processor, between its marking as blocked and its switch away, kicked that processor, whose interrupt came during its switch with interrupts off. The thread's own selection finds it ready and it runs on, as Linux's ttwu_runnable sends no interrupt (kernel/sched/core.c:3756-3780), so it is now kicked only where it halts in place, with no idle task or the scheduler off. The tar's kicks fell from 113,248 to 105,353; its interrupt-window exits did not change.

The futex hand-offs vary from run to run, and the stopped tick makes them slower: 28,699 to 34,109 exits in 0.09 to 0.10 s with every idle tick kept, against 53,731 to 87,273 in 0.15 to 0.23 s with the tick stopped, against Linux's 141,586 in 0.77 s. With the tick stopped, KVM's halt polling ends 7,000 to 9,000 halts against 15,000 to 24,000, so more wakes wait for the host to run the vCPU again. Turning `sched_idle_balance` off did not change the figures. Linux stops its idle tick too, and the change stays.

## 80-102: the end of interrupt before the handler

Task 80-102 (issue #4828) was split from this one to move a message interrupt's end of interrupt before its handler, as Linux's handle_edge_irq acknowledges an edge first (kernel/irq/chip.c:787-838), and to check every handler that then runs with its vector no longer in service. The second session had already made the move (`irq_dispatch_edge`, above); the task added the check, a count of the ends of interrupt by how they went, and Linux's count of the same.

Rach2 now counts, under `rach2.exits`, each end of interrupt: `eoipv`, through KVM's paravirtual word, and, by vector, `eoitaken`, a write of the EOI register because an exit between the router's start and the end took the word back, and `eoiunset`, a write because the word was not set when the router began (`lapic_eoi_enter`). Linux's guest-side `msr` tracepoints miss its EOI writes, since `native_apic_msr_eoi` writes the register with `__wrmsr` (arch/x86/include/asm/apic.h:215-218), so both systems were counted from the host too: [vm-hosteoi.diff](80-94/runs/80-102/vm-hosteoi.diff) adds to `vm.py` a histogram of KVM's `kvm_msr` event by thread for writes of the EOI register and of the TSC deadline, and the guest's own count of its TSC-deadline writes by processor names which of the host's threads are its vCPUs. On the tar, at the milestone's head with the counters (`rach2g.out`, `linuxc.out` and the `khist-*.txt` beside them in [runs/80-102](80-94/runs/80-102)):

| | Rach2 | Linux |
|---|---|---|
| exits | 444,352 in 4.72 s | 331,840 in 5.55 s |
| interrupts injected | 136,915 | 83,336 |
| EOI writes, all processors | 5,907 | 1,323 |
| EOI writes, boot processor | 1,717 | 146 |
| interrupt-window exits | 16,812 | 3,801 |

The host counted 5,991 EOI writes of Rach2's vCPUs over an earlier run whose guest counted 5,993, so the two sides agree. The boot processor's 27,800 EOI writes when the task was filed are 1,717. Of Rach2's 5,907, 24 are `eoitaken`: 15 of the interprocessor interrupt, 6 of AHCI's message, 2 of the serial port's line and 1 of the tick. A handler's register access or kick before the end no longer costs the word. The other 5,883 are `eoiunset`: 5,042 of the interprocessor interrupt, 532 of AHCI's message and 307 of the tick. KVM sets the word only for an interrupt it delivers while no other waits in the request register, none is nested in service and the I/O APIC need not hear of its end (`apic_sync_pv_eoi_to_guest`, arch/x86/kvm/lapic.c:3273-3291). A kernel that read the request and in-service registers before each such write ([irr-diag.patch](80-94/runs/80-102/irr-diag.patch), not committed; `rach2f.out`, whose `eoiback` is `eoitaken`) found another interrupt waiting at 6,890 of 7,245 of them and two in service at none. Interrupts pile up behind one another where the guest holds them off, and Rach2's 16,812 interrupt-window exits against Linux's 3,801 count those stretches; its kicks, 105,000 against Linux's 47,000 in the second session's count, add to the interrupts that wait. The EOI writes left are fixed with those, by 80-103 (#4829), the interrupts-off stretches, and 80-101 (#4827), the read-ahead's kicks, and not in the end of interrupt; each task's run reads them in `eoiunset`.

Every handler of a message vector, read with its calls, a call graph of the x86-64 kernel's direct calls by address and the callbacks it reaches through pointers. A handler that turned interrupts on would let the vector's next message, or a lower vector's, come inside it, which the end before the handler no longer holds off; `irq_dispatch_edge` runs a message that comes during its vector's chain once the chain returns, on this processor or another, so no chain runs inside itself.

* `ahci_handler` (`block/ahci.c`): each port under its lock with `spin_lock_irqsave`, completes the commands and wakes their waiters, writes IS. Interrupts stay off.
* `nvme_handler` (`block/nvme.c`): reaps each controller's completions and starts the next commands under `cq_poll_lock` and `sq_lock`. Interrupts stay off.
* `i915_irq_handler` (`drm/i915/i915_pci.c`): `i915_irq_handle` with the device's in-flight count held around it. Interrupts stay off.
* `hda_irq_handler` (`audio/hda.c`), on a message or its line: the stream status under `stream_lock`, then `wq_wake_all` or `hda_service_inline`. Interrupts stay off.
* `vp_slot0` to `vp_slot15` (`virtio/virtio_pci.c`): `virtio_interrupt_queue` or `virtio_interrupt_config`, whose only callbacks are virtio-gpu's `cmd_done` and `vgpu_config_changed`, and the fence callbacks `dma_fence_signal` runs from them. Interrupts stay off.
* `rtl8168_isr` (`network/driver/rtl8168.c`): masks the card, serves its rings, unmasks. Interrupts stay off.
* `ath9k_msi` (`network/driver/ath9k/intr.c`): `ath9k_intr`. Interrupts stay off.
* `msi_isr` (`vfio/irq.c`): `eventfd_signal`. Interrupts stay off.
* `fault_isr` (`arch/x86/iommu/hw.c`): `vtd_fault_all`. Interrupts stay off.
* `usb_msi_handler` (`usb/usb.c`), on a message or its line: `usb_pump`, which saves and restores the flag. Its `usb_core_step` reaches `usbdisk_step`, whose `settle` runs a removed stick's teardown when the interrupt did not come in a system call, and the teardown's `bcache_device_gone` takes a mutex and may sleep there, with interrupts on. The end of interrupt has gone out by then, so the sleep holds back no vector, where with the end after the chain it held back the tick and every vector below the controller's on that processor; a message that comes meanwhile is run by the sleeping run once it returns, and the timer's pump drains the rings in the meantime. Linux tears a disk down in a thread.

The lines the I/O APIC routes, PCI's INTx lines among them, still end after their chain, as Linux's handle_fasteoi_irq ends a level line (kernel/irq/chip.c:687-736). Linux acknowledges an ISA edge line, the keyboard's, the serial port's or the clock's, before its handler, and Rach2 ends it after; the tar made 4 EOI writes for those lines, all the serial port's. The router's and the xHCI handler's comments said a message ended after its chain, and now say otherwise. No fault was found in another project's code.

## What is left

These are traced, with the counts of the tar at the end of the second session against Linux's, and none is fixed:

* Kicks, 105,000 against Linux's 47,000 function-call interrupts, and the halts that follow them, 116,000 against 78,000. The first session counted the pipe's writes waking `cat` 56,000 times, the AHCI completions waking their waiters 17,600 times, and `kreadahead` woken by `fc_queue` 14,800 times and waking its readers 15,600 times. Linux reads ahead in the reader's own context and submits the window's reads without waiting for them (`page_cache_async_ra`, mm/readahead.c), so a read ahead costs it the one wake of its completion. Here `fc_fill` waits for the disk, so a window read ahead goes to the worker, which costs three wakes. Submitting a window's reads without waiting, with the pages locked until their completion, is the size of a milestone. Task 80-101, below, did it.
* The boot processor's tick, 1000 a second while the machine is idle: its periodic work polls the network device, USB, the console's serial port, the TCP timers and the rest each tick, where Linux's drivers keep timers of their own, its tick runs at 250 Hz and every idle processor stops it. Moving each poll onto a timer of its own, so that the boot processor's tick can stop, is the size of a milestone.
* The EOI writes, 5,907 against Linux's 1,323 in 80-102's section: interrupts KVM delivers while another waits get no paravirtual end, and they follow the interrupt-window exits and the kicks.
* The interrupt-window exits, 18,293 against 4,192. A kernel that counted the return address of every interrupt taken in kernel mode, and was not committed, put 13,767 of them at the instruction that turned interrupts back on: 6,283 at the end of `schedule`, after the switch, whose tail takes the big lock back at the incoming thread's depth with interrupts off, called from `wq_wait`; 3,531 at a spinlock's release, under `fc_job_read`, `ahci_submit`, `pipe_wake`, `wq_wake_all` and `req_take`; and 870 at `wq_wake`. Which part of `schedule`'s switch holds the interrupts was not measured; 80-103, below, measured it and cut the count.
* The timer's count, 24,042 writes in the tar: every tick of a busy processor counts the one-shot timer again, as Linux's tick programs the TSC deadline at 250 Hz where this tick runs at 1000 Hz. The timer's entry, 4,527 writes, changes between the tick's vector and the vector between ticks, where Linux has one.
* Crysis's load itself: 80-92's harness runs it on the testing host with the game's disk.

## 80-100: the pipe's wakes of its reader

Task 80-100 (#4826) was to wake a pipe's reader with a sync wake placed as Linux's `wake_affine` places one, so that `tar`'s writes would stop kicking `cat` on another processor. Measured on 2026-11-13 at the milestone's head b649e41ea, Linux places the reader where Rach2 already does, and the gap in kicks comes from how often the reader finds the pipe empty, which no change of the wake path tried here closed. No code was changed. [80-100](80-94/80-100) has the scripts, the probes, which were never committed, and the readings. `vm.py` and `tools.ext2` were rebuilt as the second session's, with [rcat.c](80-94/80-100/rcat.c) beside `load`: it reads its input with a 128 KiB buffer, as GNU `cat` does, and prints its reads by size and its voluntary switches from `getrusage`, which count its sleeps.

* Linux does not move the reader. Over the tar into `cat`, a histogram of `sched_migrate_task` counted 8 moves of `cat`, and `sched_wakeup` put its 30,415 wakes on processors 2 and 1 and `tar`'s 20,477 on processor 0 (`linux-place.out`). A sync wake makes the waker's processor the target when it runs one task (`wake_affine_idle`, kernel/sched/fair.c:7461-7466), but `select_idle_sibling` then returns the wakee's previous processor when that one is idle and shares the cache (fair.c:7962-7970), and QEMU's four processors share one L3 (`shared_cpu_list` 0-3). Rach2's `sched_choose` takes the previous processor when it is idle too. The two differ only when the previous processor is busy and no processor is idle, which this load does not reach, so the sync placement was not added.
* The reader sleeps more often. The first tar after boot, on two boots of Rach2 and one of Linux, and the three that follow it from the page cache:

| | Rach2, first | Linux, first | Rach2, cached | Linux, cached |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| time | 4.77 and 4.80 s | 5.37 s | 0.97 to 1.02 s | 0.92 s |
| reads of `rcat` | 91,719 and 92,771 | 109,075 | 113,280 to 116,392 | 106,488 |
| its sleeps | 60,424 and 61,393 | 40,117 | 74,620 to 76,036 | 52,834 |
| kicks, or function-call interrupts | 119,121 and 119,665 | 57,974 | 78,178 and 78,423, a boot's mean | 52,897 |
| exits | 468,067 and 469,281 | 341,204 | 166,071 to 169,107 | 108,542 |

  Linux's `rcat` slept 37,630 times of 37,638 in `pipe_read`, waiting for data, and once on the pipe's mutex (`stack-l.out`), so nothing in Linux's wake keeps its reader awake: it finds the next record already in the pipe more often, in 63,000 of 101,000 reads against some 31,000 of 92,000 here. `tar` writes 157,000 records of 10 KiB, and Rach2's takes 5 to 11% longer than Linux's over the cached tree. Which part of its record makes the reader find the pipe empty was not measured.
* Rach2 moves threads where Linux does not. A probe (`probe.patch`) counted, in the first tar, 38,931 and 39,030 wakes placed on a processor other than the last, and 47,989 and 48,532 steals (`rep-base-*.out`). A steal mostly takes a thread woken onto an idle processor before that processor, kicked, leaves its halt. Linux's balance takes no task from a queue with one runnable (`detach_tasks`, fair.c:9626-9640), and a wake goes only to an idle processor with nothing queued (`idle_cpu` and `available_idle_cpu`, kernel/sched/syscalls.c:202-235). Both rules together (`steal-probe.patch`, `rep-fix-*.out`) cut the moves to under 30, but the first tar's exits rose to 511,075 and 517,370, its halts from 128,723 and 130,358 to 165,521 and 170,507, and its time to 5.52 and 4.98 s, while its interrupt-window exits fell from 17,078 and 17,864 to 11,778 and 10,756; the cached tars rose to 173,018 to 179,471 exits. The second rule alone (`rep-free-1.out`) gave 482,963 exits in 5.90 s on the first tar, and 151,477 to 153,297 in 1.14 to 1.16 s on the cached ones. A steal runs the woken thread on a processor that is already awake and spares that processor a halt. Neither rule was kept.
* An idle processor not yet halted is kicked with an interrupt. A second probe (`kick-probe.patch`, `probe-r.out`) found 2,171 of the first tar's 117,891 kicks sent to a processor that ran its idle task and had not yet halted, and 48 to a busy one. Its interrupts are off there, so each costs the sender's write of the ICR and the receiver an interrupt-window exit. Linux sends none: its idle loop marks itself polling from its start to just before the halt (`do_idle`, kernel/sched/idle.c:290, and `default_idle_call`, idle.c:111), and a wake of a polling processor only sets its need to reschedule (`set_nr_if_polling`, kernel/sched/core.c:970-983). The window is in the idle loop's interrupts-off region, which 80-103 (#4829) owns, and it was passed to that task.

## 80-101: reading ahead in the reader's own context

Task 80-101 (#4827), on 2026-11-13, made a window read ahead is now submitted from the reader's own context without waiting, as Linux's `page_cache_async_ra` submits it (mm/readahead.c), in place of the `kreadahead` workers. `block_read_frames_async` starts a read into frames and calls its completion from the interrupt that ends it; ext2's `read_frames` takes that completion and starts each run of blocks with it; the file page cache inserts the window's pages locked (`FC_LOCK`) and the completion unlocks them, or drops them on an error, and wakes a hashed queue of 64, as Linux's `folio_end_read` wakes its folio's. A reader that finds a locked page waits on its queue; one that finds no page reads ahead, as `page_cache_sync_ra` does, and reads the page itself only when the window could not start it. A punch waits for reads in flight over the sectors it frees (`block_settle`). The asynchronous read's wait for room in the request pool no longer writes a line to the serial console, which cost 8,900 port exits in the tar, and ext2 reads a file's last page as the disk has it, zeros past the size, as Linux's `do_mpage_readpage` reads the last block whole, where splitting it off made a second request for nearly every file and 26,000 MMIO exits.

This host's `gnu-test.disk` has no `/opt/proton`, so the GE-Proton11-12 tree from `build/cache/77-7a`, 1.6 GB in 6,167 files, went on the 6 GiB tools disk at `/t/proton`, and `vm.py` matched that disk's size for Linux's chroot. [readahead](80-94/readahead) has the scripts and the readings: `base.out` at the milestone branch's head, `async.out` with the change, `linux.out` Debian's kernel, `count.out` a kernel that counted the kicks sent in interrupt context in the `ipi call` slot and was not committed, and `race.out` three readers of the same 623 files at once, one of which stops after 1 MB.

## 80-104: the paravirtual interrupt and TLB flush

Task 80-104 (#4830) was to send interprocessor interrupts and TLB shootdowns as Linux does under KVM. 80-94 had already brought in x2APIC cluster mode; this task added the other two of Linux's paths, in the guest and in Rach2's own KVM. Measured on 2026-11-13 with `vm.py` in the testing host's copy, `/home/testing/rach2`, at the milestone's head c311ddec6 and with the task's changes, neither changes the exits of these loads, as expected with four vCPUs in one cluster and a host with idle processors. [80-104](80-94/80-104) has the scripts and the readings: `m.txt` and `m2.txt` the loads, `base-*` the head's kernel, `fix-1` and `fix-off-*` the first form of the change, `fix2-*` the form kept, and `*-off-*` boots with QEMU's `kvm-pv-ipi=off,kvm-pv-tlb-flush=off`.

* Under KVM_FEATURE_PV_SEND_IPI an interrupt to two or more processors is KVM_HC_SEND_IPI, one hypercall for each span of 128 APIC IDs on x86-64 or 64 on i686, as Linux's `kvm_send_ipi_mask` (arch/x86/kernel/kvm.c). An interrupt to one processor stays one write of the x2APIC command register, as Linux sends a reschedule or a single call with `apic->send_IPI`: KVM takes that write on its fast path, in the exit handler with interrupts still off (`handle_fastpath_set_msr_irqoff`, arch/x86/kvm/x86.c), where a hypercall takes the whole exit. The first form sent every set through the hypercall, and the tar's 112,486 hypercalls in `fix-1.out` were its single kicks, which `smp_kick_many` sends as sets; one hypercall replaced one write, and the first tar's exits did not change.
* Under KVM_FEATURE_PV_TLB_FLUSH a shootdown sends no request to a processor whose steal-time record says KVM preempted it, once a compare-and-exchange has added KVM_VCPU_FLUSH_TLB there, as Linux's `kvm_flush_tlb_multi`; KVM flushes that processor's TLB as it enters it again. Rach2's KVM now offers the feature, takes `preempted` with a compare-and-exchange and flushes the guest's TLB at the entry when it finds the mark, as Linux's `record_steal_time`.
* The busy unmaps' 20,000 shootdowns, each to three processors in the one cluster, became 20,000 hypercalls in place of 20,000 writes of the command register: 85,247 to 85,897 exits against 85,418 to 86,104 at the head, and Linux's 84,161. How many shootdowns found a processor preempted was not counted; the host's 32 threads ran these four vCPUs and one other guest.
* The tar made 446,870 to 453,614 exits on its first run, against 435,267 to 448,465 at the head, and 154,572 to 160,295 on each run from the page cache, against 147,122 to 159,729; the first tar sent 286 to 401 hypercalls and the cached ones none. The kept kernel with the two features off made 445,980 and 449,600, and 152,993 to 158,699 cached, so the features account for none of the difference, and the head's kernel spans it from boot to boot, as the first session found: over three cached tars the guest counted 209,452 kicks and 204,934 halts on one boot of the head's kernel and 222,450 and 218,224 on another, each kick one write of the command register.

The two paths save exits where a set spans clusters, above 16 vCPUs, in xAPIC mode, where each target is a write, and where the host preempts vCPUs, none of which these loads reach. The tar's difference from Linux is the one the section on 80-101 leaves, which 80-97 and 80-103 own.

## 80-108: the reader's spin

Task 80-108 (#4850) was to find which part of `tar`'s 10 KiB writes makes Rach2's reader find the pipe empty more often than Linux's, and to bring the reader's sleeps, the kicks and the cached tar's time within Linux's. No part of the write does: a write merges its first 2 KiB into the newest buffer when it finds one, and wakes the reader once, after its lock, when the reader sleeps, as Linux's `pipe_write` wakes it once when the pipe was empty. The reader finds the pipe empty more often because it wakes twice as fast as Linux's, and so takes the one record its wake was for before the writer, slowed by the exit its own wake costs, has written the next. A blocking read now spins for the next write, for at most 4 us, while the last writer runs on another processor (`pipe_read_spin`), and the cached tar sleeps 6,207 to 6,819 times against Linux's 54,747 to 55,738 and takes 0.62 to 0.65 s against Linux's 0.88 to 0.90 s. Measured on 2026-11-13 on the testing host, at the milestone's head 2f1f6d3ec and at the fix, and again at the head d5328f63b that the fix was rebased onto. [80-108](80-94/80-108) has the scripts, the probe, which was never committed, and the readings.

The measurement is 80-100's: 80-94's `vm.py`, with its tree set to the account's clone and root's password read from `krffs/rach2_demo_password`, boots each kernel on `gnu-test.disk`, or Debian's 6.12 with that root in a chroot, with `load` and 80-100's `rcat` on the tools disk. `rach2.txt` and `linux.txt` run a first tar into `rcat`, two tars into `/dev/zero`, which time `tar` alone, and three cached tars into `rcat`; `summ.py` prints them. The probe (`probe.patch`, read by `pp.py`) counts, on the pipe `rcat` reads with 128 KiB reads, its writes and reads, the writes that found it empty and those that woke the reader, and log2 histograms in TSC cycles, at 4,192 MHz, of the time from a wake to the reader's return from its sleep, of the writer's period, of the waking write and of the wake itself, and of the time from the reader's sleep to the wake that ends it (`probe4.out`); `probe3.out`, from the patch with the scheduler's lock timed where it is now `(void)t0`, times that lock in place of the sleep. `probe2.out` is the patch without either. On Linux, synthetic events of the `hist` triggers time the same spans: `sched_waking` to `sched_switch` of `rcat` (`linux-tr2.out`) and its switch out to its next `sched_waking` (`linux-tr3.out`). A trace of each write as well (`linux-tr.out`) slowed `tar` until its reader slept 79,689 times, once a record, and was set aside.

* The writes are Linux's. Of a cached tar's 157,290 writes, 112,612 found the pipe empty and 74,713 woke the reader, which slept 74,683 times (`probe2.out`). A write that wakes the reader takes 4 to 8 us in 70,262 of them, 2 to 8 us of it in the wake, and the writes' period has one peak at 1 to 2 us and another at 4 to 8. The scheduler's lock was taken at once in 75,511 of 75,694 wakes, and the kick, one write of the x2APIC's command register and so one exit, took 2 to 4 us in 60,020 and 4 to 8 us in 13,228 (`probe3.out`). Linux's wake is the same write of the command register: its function-call interrupts, 55,018 to 55,905 in a cached tar, match its reader's sleeps.
* The reader wakes faster. From the wake to the reader's return from its sleep, Rach2's took 3.9 to 7.8 us in 68,745 of 74,713 wakes; from `sched_waking` to the switch to `rcat`, Linux's took 8.2 to 16.4 us in 52,517 of 54,764. Both readers then find the pipe empty a little before the next write: Rach2's was woken 0.5 to 2 us after it went to sleep in 65,708 of 75,559 sleeps, Linux's 0.5 to 4 us after its switch out in 44,495 of 54,913. Linux's reader, slower to come, finds the next record already written in nearly half its reads: 49,777 of its 104,230 reads take two records, against 24,738 of Rach2's 115,155. Why Linux's wake takes the longer was not traced.
* A spin bridges the gap. A sleep costs the writer the kick's exit, which delays its next record, and the reader a halt, while the next write comes a microsecond or two after the reader looks. The reader now spins with the pipe's lock given back, once a read, while the writer of the last write is current on another processor that is not in its idle wait and not marked preempted (`sched_spin_on`, the sleeping lock's test after Linux's `owner_on_cpu`), and sleeps if nothing comes in 4 us, which is about what the kick costs the writer, so that a spin that fails costs at most twice the sleep. Splice and tee note their writer too. A bound of 10 us gave the same cached figures over seven tars, 0.63 to 0.68 s and 6,042 to 6,387 sleeps (`spin10.out`, `cold-spin10.out`), and 4 us over four, 0.64 to 0.68 s and 6,263 to 6,882 (`cold-spin4.out`); each `cold-*` file holds the second of two boots.

## 80-103: the interrupt-window exits

Task 80-103 (issue #4829) traced the interrupt-window exits 80-94 left, 17,019 on the tar against Linux's 4,192, and cut them to a median of 6,797 in six boots, against 17,210 at the milestone head, eac147e85, in six boots of the same order. What is left follows the tick, which runs 1000 times a second where Linux's runs 250: a kernel built with `TICK_HZ` 250, measured and not committed, made 1,718 and 1,900 on the tar with this task's changes, within Linux's, and 11,549 and 12,102 without them. 80-97 (#4801) brings the tick to Linux's rate.

An interrupt-window exit is an interrupt KVM holds for a vCPU that runs with interrupts off, taken at the instruction that turns them on again. [trace.patch](80-103/trace.patch), which applies to eac147e85 and was not committed, adds to `rach2.exits` a debugfs file `irqsites`: each interrupt taken in kernel mode, by the interrupted instruction, the return addresses of the two frames above it and the interrupt's class (the IPI, the timer, or a device's vector); the cycles of each phase of `schedule` (the run queues' lock, the selection, the lock's release and the wait for `on_cpu`, `switch_context`, the tail's take of the big lock); for an interrupt that lands at the end of `schedule`, the phase in which the kick it answers was sent or the tick fell due, and the kick's delay; each kick's target's state when it was sent; and the big lock's waits by caller. [sites.py](80-103/sites.py) prints the difference of the two readings [win.txt](80-103/win.txt) takes around the tar. The runs are 80-94's `vm.py` with its paths set to the clone, in QEMU with KVM on the testing host, whose TSC counts 4.19 GHz. The patch grew from run to run, and the readings below were taken with the parts it had then; [runs/trace13.txt](80-103/runs/trace13.txt) is a reading of the whole patch at the end.

At the milestone head, in a traced run of 19,195 window exits, 12,206 interrupts landed at `kirq_restore`: 6,127 at the end of `schedule` called from `wq_wait`, 1,267 at `ahci_submit`'s release of the port's lock, 887 at `fc_job_read`'s, 807 at `pipe_wake`'s and 405 at `wq_wake_all`'s. From the run queues' lock to the restore, a switch of a caller with interrupts on held them off for 10,700 cycles, 2.6 us: 44% in the selection, 23% in the lock's release and the wait for `on_cpu`, 18% in `switch_context` and 15% in the tail's take of the big lock. The interrupts were not sent in any of those phases. Of those that landed at the end of `schedule` in a run of 16,631, 4,416 were kicks sent before the switch began and 1,058 ticks; in another, every one of 5,121 kicks counted there had been sent while its target halted, and had taken 8 to 16 us to arrive, where most kicks take 4 to 8 us to reach a vCPU here, nested under Hyper-V. Two changes followed.

* A wake that put a thread on an idle processor's queue kicked it each time, until that processor switched away from its idle task, so the first kick woke it and the second came during the switch, with interrupts off. Linux's resched_curr sends nothing to a task whose TIF_NEED_RESCHED is set (kernel/sched/core.c:1101-1102). `sched_preempt_cpu` now sets the idle task's `need_resched` with the kick and asks nothing while it is set; the processor's selection clears it under the run queues' lock and takes every thread queued meanwhile. The traced tar's window exits fell to 11,743 and its kicks from 108,952 to 92,628.
* Of the 4,003 kicks still landing at the end of `schedule`, 3,714 came to a processor whose halt a device's interrupt, the disk's completion, had ended, and 3,994 had been sent before that interrupt returned: the idle task runs with interrupts off from such an interrupt to its switch (`_irq_router`), and a wake meanwhile kicked it. Linux's idle task keeps its polling bit set while it is current, outside the halt itself (kernel/sched/idle.c:257, 282-290), so that resched_curr sets its flag and sends nothing. The interrupt that ends an idle halt now marks the processor's `idle_poll` CPU_POLL_ON (`sched_idle_awake`), which `smp_kick` turns to CPU_POLL_KICKED without an interrupt, as it does for the poll before a halt; the switch to a thread clears the mark under the run queues' lock, and the next halt clears it and returns at once when a kick came. Kicks landing at the end of `schedule` fell to 270, and the traced tar's window exits to 6,581.

* The tail of `schedule` takes the big lock back with interrupts off, 15% of the switch's region.
