# 80-7b: the native games on the Lenovo

Task 80-7b plays SuperTuxKart's and 0 A.D.'s fixed replays on the Lenovo's HD 4400 through Crocus, on the panel, with sound at volume 1, and sets their frame times beside Linux's on the same machine with the IOMMU's translation off (80-2); and it adds an i386 game, Debian's `supertuxkart:i386`, which plays the same race through the i386 Mesa on the x86-64 kernel. Its parts, its check in QEMU, the readiness note for its trial and the step come first; the results go at the end.

## The i386 game

Debian's `supertuxkart` 1.4+dfsg-5+b1 for i386, the version `native.list` pins for amd64, so that its benchmark race is the 64-bit game's. [`vendor/gnu/games/i386.list`](../../vendor/gnu/games/i386.list) names it, and `make ARCH=x86_64 gnu-i386` resolves it with the i386 layer's list against the i386 indices of the same snapshot. Its data, `supertuxkart-data` (628 MB, no architecture), is the games layer's amd64 closure's, so the step leaves it out with `GNU_DEBIAN_UNFOLLOWED=supertuxkart-data`, a variable `fetch_debs.sh` gained for this. The closure grew from 138 packages and 108.4 MB to 168 and 122.5 MB; of the 30 new, 25 are Multi-Arch `same` libraries (SDL2, OpenAL, libcurl's GnuTLS build, harfbuzz, shaderc and the rest of the game's), and the others the game itself, `libopenal-data`, `lsb-base`, `sysvinit-utils` and `x11-common`, which the amd64 closures supply.

dpkg keeps one architecture's `/usr/games/supertuxkart`, so `install_games.sh` puts the i386 one beside it as `/usr/games/supertuxkart.i386-linux-gnu`, the name `mesa-utils-bin` gives its i386 programs, once the games closure has `supertuxkart` at the same version, and refuses the root when a library it needs is not in `/usr/lib/i386-linux-gnu`. `games.sh` plays it as the game `supertuxkart-i386`, and the `games-native` family has the case `machine.games-native.supertuxkart-i386`. The i386 `vulkaninfo`, which 79-8b staged without a page, got its alias on the way.

## The step's script

[`tests/system/games/lenovo.sh`](../../tests/system/games/lenovo.sh), as root on the trial root, plays each game's replay twice, one run after another, through `games.sh -d` as `desktop` on the root's own desktop session: Weston's kiosk-shell on the DRM device with its GL renderer and PulseAudio on `/dev/dsp` (`vendor/gnu/desktop`), fullscreen at the panel's 1366x768. Linux's runs used cage on the same panel, SDL on Wayland without libdecor and Gallium's HUD dump; `games.sh` gives the games the same arguments, environment and window. Mesa's shader cache is shared by a game's two runs, so that the first starts cold and the second warm, as Linux's did. The volume is set to 1 before each game and again 5 s and 30 s after its start, and read every 10 s; the GPU's clock, `gt_act_freq_mhz`, is read every second; the kernel's log is kept after each run, for the lines of `rach2.trace=enosys`.

Each run of SuperTuxKart and 0 A.D. is judged against the mean of Linux's two Wayland runs: the median, 90th and 99th percentiles at most 10% over Linux's. The i386 game's rows are printed beside the 64-bit game's Linux numbers and not judged, since Linux ran no i386 game.

| Game | Linux's median ms | p90 | p99 | 110%: median | p90 | p99 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SuperTuxKart | 14.72 | 16.96 | 19.14 | 16.19 | 18.66 | 21.05 |
| 0 A.D. | 17.06 | 19.40 | 36.135 | 18.77 | 21.34 | 39.75 |

## The check in QEMU

The root the step boots, `desktop-test.disk` (os-real-gnu.ext2 on an MBR disk), booted under KVM on the WSL host with 4 processors and 8 GiB, `rach2.trace=enosys`, HDA with no audio backend, and the payload staged over SSH as the step stages it. Weston's session came up as the Lenovo's does, kiosk-shell on the DRM device as `desktop`, with its GL renderer; the GPU is QEMU's, so the frame times say only that each game reached its end, and no gate is read from them.

* `virtio-vga-gl` with virgl on the host's RTX 4080 SUPER, Weston's "GL renderer: virgl (D3D12 (NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4080 SUPER))". The small form, `lenovo.sh -n 2 -s 640x400 -t 10`, played all six runs in 2.5 minutes: SuperTuxKart at 121 and 128 frames a second, 0 A.D. at 41 and 40, the i386 SuperTuxKart at 121 and 122, `lenovo-games: result: pass`. The volume read 100:100 before the script and 1:1 at every reading after; the shader cache held no file before the first run and 101 after. `freq` is `none`, since virtio-gpu has no `gt_act_freq_mhz`.
* The full form, as the step runs it, played SuperTuxKart's 90 s race five times at 112 to 158 frames a second, 0 A.D.'s 150 s window four times at 38 to 44, and the i386 game's race once at 130, across three boots. 0 A.D.'s rows read `fail` against Linux's numbers (medians of 21.25 to 24.34 ms against the gate's 18.77), which on virgl through D3D12 says nothing of the Lenovo; one SuperTuxKart row read `fail` on its p99, 21.88 against 21.05.
* The fake HD 4400 (`-device edu` with `rach2.i915fake`, the `crocus-fake` case's chip), which no display is attached to, so Weston ran headless on its GL renderer, `RUNNER_RENDERER=gl`, through `games.sh` alone. The i386 `eglinfo.i386-linux-gnu -B -p surfaceless` named "Mesa Intel(R) HD Graphics 4400 (HSW GT2)" for core, compatibility and ES contexts, and the i386 SuperTuxKart played its 10 s race through Crocus, 3455 frames, `renderer Mesa Intel(R) HD Graphics 4400 (HSW GT2)`, through the 32-bit ioctl translation of `ia32_ioctl_drm.c` and `ia32_ioctl_gpu.c`; the 64-bit game played 2900. Under Weston's Pixman renderer both games fell back to llvmpipe, since Pixman offers clients shared memory alone.
* No run logged a missing call: the kernel's log of every boot, 141 to 145 lines and never turned over, has no `not implemented` line.

One boot of the full form stopped in the kernel during SuperTuxKart's second run, at uptime 267 s: `isr: kernel exception: general protection (vector 13, err 0x0, rip 0xffffffff801d83c9)`, which `addr2line` puts in `kmalloc`, `rach2/sys/mm/kheap.c:512`, where the head of a class's free list is followed to the next block. A general protection fault there means that the free block's link held a non-canonical address: a block was written after it was freed, freed twice, or overrun by its neighbour. The nine full-form runs after it, on the same build, reached their ends, six of them on a kernel that poisoned each freed block, kept its freer's address, refused a second free and checked the poison and the link at each allocation; it found nothing. The log names no subsystem and the exception handler prints no registers, so the writer is not known; the fault goes to the manager with this record, and the step keeps the kernel's log of each run.
