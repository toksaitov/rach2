# 80-97: the dynamic idle tick

The local APIC runs scheduler ticks and timed events on one vector. An idle scheduler task stops its tick for a long wait, keeps the earliest real event armed, and restarts the tick when it leaves idle. Guests with an independent reference clock use a 250 Hz scheduler; native and legacy paths keep their existing 1000 Hz rate. Clock, timeout and CPU-accounting units remain milliseconds. The manager added the guest rate to this task under the owner's fast-VM decision in the task's comment of 2026-11-13.

The final event-based comparison passed after the non-notifying-timer fix: Rach2 took 20,743 local timer interrupts and 151,830 VM exits, against Linux's 23,092 and 180,229. The engine reported 8.43 s against 8.75 s. In the separate ten-second idle measurement Rach2 took 650 exits, against Linux's 3390 and 3405 in the two reference runs. Both kernel links and the new functional probes passed.

## The timer

`apic_hr_entry` classifies a physical interrupt as a scheduler tick, an event, both, or a stale interrupt. A coincident event and tick run once each. An event earlier than the tick cannot consume that future tick through the early-tick allowance. The common scheduler phase is a multiple of its period on the monotonic clock; skipped ticks do not move that phase. The first interrupt of an eligible continuously busy guest converts its periodic device too. The four-second catch-up bound stays independent of the guest's four-millisecond scheduler period.

TSC-deadline mode uses IA32_TSC_DEADLINE where CPUID and the measured TSC rate allow it. The LVT write is ordered before its MSR. Other APICs use the divided count. Long hardware intervals are capped at ten seconds and rearmed. Native machines and machines without an independent reference retain their old fallbacks. The one vector needs no repeated LVT change. The hardware expiry cache is invalidated by an interrupt and changes only with a real device write.

A pass batches its requested times. Requests from the scheduler idle wait remain desired metadata until final stopped preparation, retained entry, or the switch to a thread. The optional enabled-IRQ poll is skipped when its desired expiry or mode differs from the installed device. A failed stopped preparation merges held requests before committing the running timer. This prevents an unarmed halt and avoids programming a deadline which selection immediately replaces. The final commit still respects the unchanged-expiry cache.

The optional halt poll follows the KVM hint or explicit boot option, as 80-94 made it. A running tick stays through that poll. A guest halt shorter than one scheduler period without a logical tick predicts one more short halt and retains the running tick, following the [idle governor's retention choice](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.12/drivers/cpuidle/governors/menu.c#L381). This uses the preceding halt, not Linux's full menu governor. A retained tick clears the prediction, including during a poll, so a quiet CPU stops on its next entry. The prediction requires a confirmed guest one-shot timer; a periodic timer has no reliable last-due token. An already stopped tick remains stopped.

## Deadlines and accounting

The boot processor's services expose their next deadlines through `idle_timer.h`. The earliest service is armed while busy too. The service event runs unlocked work, tries the big lock for locked work, and retries a failed try in one millisecond. A fine-clock expiry which precedes its coarse tick retains that saved target until the coarse count reaches it. Another CPU can publish an earlier target under the big lock and kick CPU0; the IPI installs it without taking that lock or querying devices. An atomic exchange consumes the notification before reading the target. An unrelated IPI changes no service event.

Polling activations are discovered at the next scheduler pass or idle preparation. Published polling work keeps its requested cadence; sleeps, alarms and timerfds explicitly arm their owner. Polled network devices, USB and active HDA streams retain their required service interval. The one-second maintenance bound extends the reference before the PM timer's 4.7-second wrap and carries NTP through its seconds. The load average keeps its five-second sample phase. HDA projects the elapsed part of its jack countdown under the stream lock. iTCO advances its feed and open countdowns by elapsed millisecond units, including zero elapsed time, rather than by invocation count.

Whole-tick waits keep the first conversion of a live target, with its tag moving through holds and replacement. Paired phase/clock readings bound the error of a host stop during conversion. If slew puts the smooth clock past a target whose raw tick has not advanced, the next raw edge is armed instead of an already-past event. Global timer passes may expire another CPU's timer; only its owner rearms the future event.

Idle and steal accounting catches up once from elapsed time. A remote `/proc/stat` reader projects a stopped span without mutating the owner's counters. An interrupt returning to idle leaves the tick stopped; only an actual selection leaves it. Ready work marks the idle task for selection, is checked before halt, and is consumed by selection under the scheduler lock. Service points cannot switch inside post-halt idle work. Busy processors offer stealable work to stopped idle processors, using 80-94's balance kick. SRCU grace periods wait on readers and require no scheduler tick.

Every CPU arms its enabled watchdog sample at an absolute monotonic deadline. Tick and idle callbacks sample by elapsed time. Settings changes kick parked CPUs. The hard-lockup buddy check remains active on idle CPUs and has no broad idle exemption. The merged KVM PV EOI implementation is 80-94's shared completion word; this task's earlier equivalent implementation was replaced during integration.

## Proof

Only the task's new model rows and unattended probes ran. No existing host rows, suites, CI or standalone style checks ran; git hooks ran as required.

* `timercal_hosttest --idle`: empty and stale idle events, full intervals, tick/event coincidence, early events inside tick slack, TSC deadline, native fallback, a busy guest's first one-shot conversion, common 1 ms and 4 ms phases, slew/coarse lag, stable whole-tick conversion, a host stop between readings, declined and failed idle preparation, deferred retained events and final commitments.
* `sched_hosttest --idle-only`: projected idle/steal readings, catch-up once, the first busy tick, load phase, stopped state across IRQs, ready work before/during preparation, guarded service points, a wake caught by the open poll, a stopped or dirty timer skipping that poll, retained short waits, a tick clearing their prediction in the halt or the poll, and service kicks absorbed while awake or polling before selection and retained entry.
* `process_timer_hosttest --event-owner`: future events stay on their owner; another CPU may expire one already due.
* `kvmguest_hosttest`: independent KVM feature setup, separate EOI-word registration and local/remote steal-time reads after merging 80-94.
* `itco_hosttest --elapsed-only`: zero and irregular elapsed spans, exact open expiry and projected deadlines across tick wrap. The USB recorder's stubs were updated and compiled, without running its existing rows.

[nohzprobe.c](80-97/nohzprobe.c) is a freestanding PID 1 and powers the guest off. Its four-CPU i686 and x86-64 boots use the PM timer with HPET disabled. It pins itself to CPU0, settles, arms a 100 us periodic `SIGEV_NONE` timer, then sleeps six seconds with every user thread blocked. It checks that the non-notifying timer advances lazily and deletes it. It checks clock advance and a physical-timer bound, then poll, futex, timerfd and blocked real-alarm waits. It forces 4096 one-millisecond waits under a +500 ppm slew, bounds the total physical events and restores the rate. Finally it lowers the watchdog threshold, stalls CPU0 with interrupts off for five seconds, reads accounting and powers off. On both architectures an idle CPU3 reported CPU0's deliberate hard lockup, the write returned, `NOHZPROBE OK` was printed and poweroff completed. The physical timer row includes real events as well as scheduler ticks; the model separately verifies that a stopped CPU serves no scheduler tick.

The final four-CPU PM-reference boots are [i686](80-97/evidence/boot-i686.log) and [x86-64](80-97/evidence/boot-x86_64.log). They include the final rebase and the non-notifying-timer fix. The same probe before that fix is [the failing x86-64 boot](80-97/evidence/none-before-x86_64.log); after it, the six-second idle interval took 72 physical timer interrupts on i686 and 67 on x86-64. [The measurements](80-97/evidence/measurements.csv) name each measured kernel by SHA-256; [the archive](80-97/evidence/runs.tar.gz) keeps the raw evidence. 

## The measurements

The Crysis measurements use 80-92's harness, the same warmed prefix, DLL and save, four vCPUs, four llvmpipe threads, 640x480 and 4 GiB, under nested KVM on WSL. Debian's reference is 6.12.115 with HZ=250 and NO_HZ idle behaviour. Host compaction proactiveness was 20. The fixtures are read through private overlays. The game closes and each VM powers off. Each run has an admission claim.

The scratch harness prepends `/proc/interrupts` to its existing snapshots. [qmpstats.py](80-97/qmpstats.py) reads that VM's vCPU KVM counters through its own socket at the harness's host markers. Optional histograms use an owned trace instance. A private QEMU executable changes only the vCPU thread-name format to `N48`, for attribution among concurrent VMs; both kernels use it. The instrument is removed afterwards. Counter windows include the harness's snapshots and its one-second detection granularity; they are not exactly the engine's loading interval. No subtraction is made from a VM's total.

`timer_interrupts` sums every local timer interrupt: the old split tick and event vectors, or this task's shared vector, and Linux's LOC row. TSC-deadline writes are the named VM's writes of MSR 0x6e0. These are distinct from all VM exits. The CSV retains every captured run, including failures and incomplete counter captures. The archive holds the selected original snapshots, QMP replies, histograms, kernel hashes, command lines and output.

## Failures found during this task

These are this worker's failures first, taken from its output and the diagnosis it checked against the source.

* The first whole-tick conversion could repeatedly arm a past fine-clock target while the coarse count was still short during slew. N3 forced the fault under the real load. The raw-edge guard fixed it; the 500 ppm probe and new model force the same condition.
* Repeated conversion of one live tick could move its desired expiry by the read latency. A host stop between phase and clock readings could move it much farther. A retained target tag and bracketed readings fix both; the new rows force each case.
* The idle-service query initially polled non-notifying POSIX timers every millisecond. The forced 100 us `SIGEV_NONE` probe took 4,129 timer interrupts in six idle seconds and failed its bound. Excluding only `SIGEV_NONE` preserves service for ignored signals and departed thread targets.
* The initial open poll could miss an IPI delivered before the poll marker was published. Interrupt/IPI entry now ends an open poll, and the persistent idle ready flag is checked again before halt. Only selection clears that flag.
* The initial stopped path did not arm a watchdog sample on every CPU, and a broad idle exemption could hide a real stall. Both were removed. The deliberate CPU0 hard stall is detected from idle CPU3 on both architectures.
* Invocations were still used as iTCO's clock; HDA's deadline query omitted elapsed time since its last service. The elapsed countdown and projected deadline fix them.
* A service whose fine expiry preceded its coarse tick could lose its future target; the event now retains it. The first busy service IPI helper also ran for unrelated kicks; a consumed notification flag now gates it.
* Deferring idle writes required all return paths to commit, including retained idle, failed preparation, and selection with a running tick. A deferred event also requires skipping the enabled-IRQ poll. The new rows cover an older nonzero hardware target and held requests on failure, as well as an empty device.
* I kept the coarse one-second counter markers through too many comparisons after their window variation was visible. The final inotify observer removes that ambiguity and preserves the old readings.
* Early diagnostic QMP capture assumed one VM in debugfs, then a copied observer had the wrong argument count. QMP through the VM's own socket replaced the assumption. The first memory-dump attempt used `command_line` instead of QMP's `command-line` and captured no site counters; N36 has valid replies. The first idle wrapper made its output directory as root; its permission failure was fixed before either measured idle case ran.
* A unified-vector probe initially kept the old scheduler-only interrupt bound and failed at 72/73 physical events. The shared row also counts the real events previously on a separate vector; its physical bound is now 128, with the no-scheduler-tick assertion in the model. The first i686 probe was linked at the x86-64 load address and was relinked at 0x08048000.
* One merge dropped the deadline-mode helper and duplicated old stopped-state code and model stubs; both links and model compilation exposed them before a boot. Another build exposed a collision with HDA's existing `jack_seen` array. Those integration errors were corrected. Hooks rejected long comments and two large functions; the comments were wrapped and the functions split. One failed sync log was overwritten before inspection, so no cause is assigned to that exit status. A final SSH command used nested quotes and was rejected by the Windows shell before running; piping a script corrected it. A later rebase initially duplicated a model counter and omitted the brace between two rows; model compilation exposed both before any row ran.

N34 took 15.27 s and 302,345 exits, with three USER_HZ steal ticks. That reading is retained, and no cause is assigned from the timing alone. Linux L14 took 21.13 s with 2634 steal ticks, 26.34 CPU-seconds, and is retained as a host-steal outlier. Neither is used to claim that the final head passed. The old baseline's early GNU boot also encountered the ext2 open-handle fault already fixed by the milestone's existing e3ac67c65; this worker merged that fix before measuring B3.

Fable, Codex and Muse supplied technical diagnoses. Their claims were checked against the code. The measurements above are this worker's. No fault in another project's code was found.

## Repeating the measurement

Use the warmed root, data, runtime, key and DLL fixtures prepared by 80-92. Keep the task's symlinks in the host copy's `build/runs/task4801/load`, and copy the kernel under measurement to `build/runs/task4801/kernel-integrated`. [measure.sh](80-97/measure.sh) is the exact final wrapper: run it as root on the testing host from this clone, with `RUNS` set to unused N names for Rach2 and L names for Linux. It claims admission for every VM, serializes this work directory, starts the owned QMP observer and reaps it at exit. It refuses a turn whose output already exists. The kernel and inputs remain read-only behind overlays. The archive records their hashes and QEMU arguments.

For the standalone boot, build the appropriate probe at 0x400000 on x86-64 or 0x08048000 on i686 with the cross compiler's freestanding static flags. Stage it as `/sbin/init` in a private KRFFS image, then boot the matching kernel with four CPUs, `-machine pc,hpet=off -cpu max`, no default devices, and serial output. The probe itself powers off. Existing suites remain for the reviewed checkpoint.

## Final rebase

The rebase also takes the milestone's later KVM batched IPIs, deferred TLB flushes and 80-103's awake-idle marker and repeated-kick suppression. Its conflict resolution preserves the measured timer tree and those incoming changes. A halt wake leaves the awake marker set; an interrupt during an enabled-IRQ poll marks it kicked. A published service target whose kick was absorbed is installed on both kicked-return paths, before selection can leave idle or retain a running tick. New model rows force both cases. The existing coincidence row still expected separate vectors; its assertions now describe one shared expiry, and were compiled without running the existing rows.

The last rebase also preserves 80-110's one-kick balancing claim and its interrupt window on both cancelled-wait paths, before which this task still installs an absorbed service target. Its new existing rows were adapted to the elapsed idle model and compiled, without running them. Only this task's `--idle-only` rows ran again. The final kernel links include the extracted return-to-user helper and the other incoming filesystem and block fixes; no existing cases ran on those changes.

The milestone push hook also exposed a redundant network-console deadline condition and missing host-model stubs for the new 64-bit divisor, monotonic clock and watchdog settings kick. The condition now uses ordered branches; the DRM fuzzer, software-display model and NMI model supply their existing clocks and host arithmetic. Those affected models were compiled without running any existing rows.
