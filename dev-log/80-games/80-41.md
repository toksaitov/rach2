# 80-41: the games' kernel time, work per call against waits on the big lock

80-7b's runs on the Lenovo with the GPU's clock pinned at 1000 MHz (`80-7.md`, the first run of 11-11) spend twice as much system time as user time, wait 112 s on the big lock in 90 s of SuperTuxKart and 195 s in 150 s of 0 A.D., and hold it in DRM ioctls for 32 to 65 s a run. This task splits that time: what each hot call costs on Rach2 against Linux on the same machine, which is work to fix in this stack, and what is waiting on the lock while another processor holds it, which goes to the manager for the owner's decision on the lock. No lock change is made here.

## What 80-7b's build counts, and what it lacks

The trial build 1d49ce855 counts, since boot, each entry's holds of the lock and their time (`bkl_hold_entry` in `/proc/stat`, the 16 longest), the sum and the longest of the waits, and the oldest hold an interrupt has found with its stack. It does not count:

* the waits by the entry that waited, only their sum;
* the calls of each system call and their time from entry to return;
* the ioctls by request: every DRM call is one entry, system call 16;
* where the kernel spends its time under the lock: there is no profile.

Commit 1a5825c41 adds them in debugfs, `bkl/calls` and `bkl/profile` ([debugfs(5)](../../man/man5/debugfs.5), [the process subsystem](../../rach2/sys/proc/Readme.md)). Its changes are counters under the lock and a sample taken by the tick: no register of any device, no wait and no mapping. It picks clean onto 1d49ce855 and onto 261ab3ae5, 80-40's rerun build; on 1d49ce855 the kernel is 4,103,248 bytes, 91,056 under the slot. One boot in QEMU on the Mac (TCG, four processors, the os-real image, `ls -lR /` on each) filled both files: 26 system calls, the page fault and tick vectors and two ioctl requests counted, 17,763 samples and none lost, the hottest `krffs_memory_read` under `krffs_brief`; the times read 0 there, since TCG gives no invariant TSC, and are measured on the Lenovo.

## The step

Two boots of the Lenovo, each under its lock, by a loop of the owner's, after the manager's go on #3540 for the build below: Rach2's trial root with the counters, then Debian 13.7 on p5 for Linux's reference. The first takes the screen and plays SuperTuxKart's sound at volume 1; the second takes the screen, with no sound driver. Comment the notice on #3540 ten minutes before the first reboot:

```

First a trial boot of Rach2: the desktop's Weston on the panel, then SuperTuxKart and 0 A.D. each play their replay once, fullscreen, SuperTuxKart's music and effects at volume 1, 0 A.D. muted; two reboots put the release back. Then Debian on p5: the same two replays under cage with no sound, and a reboot back to Rach2.
```

### Reading

For each game, from Rach2's `calls` and Linux's `calls.txt`, both over the game's whole run:

* the time per call of each system call and ioctl request that holds the lock for more than 1% of the run: Rach2's `held_us / holds` and `wall_us / calls` against Linux's `@ns / @calls` and `@ins / @icalls`;
* the share of the run spent waiting: the sum of `wait_us` over the game's run against its length times the processors it kept busy, and `/proc/stat`'s `bkl_wait_us` between `stat-before` and `stat-after`;
* the user and system time: `/proc/stat`'s `cpu` line on Rach2, the game's `utime` and `stime` (fields 14 and 15, in clock ticks) between `pid-before` and `pid-last` on Linux, each over the frames of the run.

Rach2's profile by function, against the kernel the step booted:

```
K=$T/build/x86_64/rach2/kernel.debug
p=$R/out/supertuxkart/profile
awk 'NR > 2 { print $1, $3 }' $p > $R/pcs
x86_64-elf-addr2line -f -e $K $(cut -d' ' -f1 $R/pcs) | paste - - | cut -f1 | paste - $R/pcs |
  awk '{ s[$1] += $3 } END { for (f in s) print s[f], f }' | sort -rn | head -n 40
```

and the same with the caller, the profile's second column, for the leaf functions such as copies. Linux's `@prof` gives its own by function.

Each per-call excess is a fault with its place in the code and Linux's way, filed as a subtask of #3540, compile-only. The table below and on #3540; the lock's share to the manager with the figures.

### Where the kernel spends its time under the lock

`bkl/profile` by function, against the booted kernel's `kernel.debug` (the files `profile-functions` and `profile-callers` in [80-41/rach2/](80-41/rach2/)). SuperTuxKart: 82,088 samples, 8,645 lost to the full table; 0 A.D.: 128,371, 17,713 lost.

* Most samples land where interrupts come back on: `arch_irq_enable` in `_syscall_handler`, `kirq_restore` in `schedule` and in `spin_unlock_irqrestore`, 58% of SuperTuxKart's kept samples and 77% of 0 A.D.'s. A tick that arrives while interrupts are off is taken at the instruction that turns them on, and a processor spins for the big lock with interrupts off, so the samples at the system call's enable and at `schedule` are mostly the end of a wait for the lock; one sample stands for a wait of any length. The 24,319 of 0 A.D.'s samples at `spin_unlock_irqrestore` are work done with interrupts off under some spinlock, whose caller the two frames of a sample do not name.
* `drm_handle_find` under `drm_gem_object_lookup`: 17,520 samples, 24% of SuperTuxKart's, and 4,579 of 0 A.D.'s. A file's handles were a list sorted by id, walked by every lookup.
* `find_cmd` under the command parser: 3,848 of SuperTuxKart's and 8,576 of 0 A.D.'s, 7.8%. Every command of a batch that differs from the one before scanned every descriptor of the engine.
* `fd_ofd_access` under `open_install` and `mm_exe_open`: 316 and 658 samples. Every open for writing and every exec walked every open file description in the system.
* The rest is spread thin: `vm_lower` under `vm_find`, the balanced tree of a space's areas, 1,116 and 1,327; `k_memset` and `k_memcpy` some 500 to 800 each.

### The faults, each fixed in this task

* The DRM handle table (#3686, a55cbf915): `drm_handle_find` in `drm_gem.c` walked a sorted list. Linux keeps `object_idr`, a lookup by index. Now a table indexed by id with the lowest free id kept as a hint: lookup and removal are one load, insertion amortized constant. Its 17.5 s of SuperTuxKart's samples come to some 640 us for each of the 27,243 EXECBUFFER2 calls against their 933 us of excess, less what GEM_BUSY's and GEM_MADVISE's own lookups took, which is their excess.
* The command parser's lookup (#3687, 1db6d0611): `find_cmd` in `i915_cmd_parser.c` scanned every table. Linux hashes the descriptors by `cmd_header_key`. Now the same hash, built at the engine's first batch, chained so that the first match in a bucket is the last match of the scan; a check outside the tree confirmed it gives the scan's descriptor for every header of 4,194,304, on all four engines. It accounts for some 140 us of SuperTuxKart's excess in each EXECBUFFER2 and 170 us of 0 A.D.'s.
* The ETXTBSY check (#3688, 7541ee806): `fd_ofd_access` in `fd.c` walked every open file description. Linux counts in `i_writecount`. Now a count of each file's writers and executables, by its mount base and node, in a hash table.

### Not fixed here, to the manager with the figures

* The big lock itself. In SuperTuxKart's 112.5 s of run on four processors the lock was held 68.4 s, 61% of the time, and the processors waited 109.6 s for it: 39.5 s in entries from programs, 19% of the 207.1 processor-seconds of user and system time, and 70.1 s on returns from an idle wait, a processor woken to run a thread that spins first. In 0 A.D.'s 173.6 s it was held 111.7 s, 64%, and waited for 183.9 s: 78.2 s in entries, 22% of 353.4 busy processor-seconds, and 105.7 s on returns from idle. System time is 2.0 and 1.8 times user time on Rach2 (whole system: SuperTuxKart 137.4 s against 69.7 s, 0 A.D. 226.4 s against 127.0 s); the game alone on Linux spends 7.6 s against 47.7 s and 20.4 s against 143.0 s, 1.2 and 1.5 ms of system time a frame.
* The HD Audio interrupt holds the lock 626 to 668 us each time, 4.4 s and 6.3 s a run: it renders each period in the interrupt, mixing the open streams and converting the rate under the lock (`hda_service_stream`, `hda_play_render`). Linux's `azx_interrupt` only acknowledges and calls `snd_pcm_period_elapsed`, and PulseAudio mixes and resamples in user space into the buffer it maps. Moving the render out of the kernel is a change of the audio path's design, not a fix of one call, so it is not filed as a subtask here.
* The timer tick holds the lock 39 to 41 us a tick, 8.6 s and 12.9 s a run. The boot processor's tick runs some sixteen periodic services and the HDA's fill; which costs what is not known, since the profile is taken by the tick and cannot sample the tick itself. Linux's tick takes no global lock.
* EXECBUFFER2's remaining excess in 0 A.D., some 700 us a call after the two faults above, and mmap at 144 us against 16 and newfstatat at 22 us against 2.6, are not explained by these counters: the profile's two frames cannot name the callers of `spin_unlock_irqrestore`, and nothing ties a sample to its system call. A profile of four frames, or one keyed by the entry, would.
