# 80-112: GNU cat's splices of the tar's pipe

80-110 (#4859) found that the cached tar of `/opt/proton` into GNU `cat` made 73,000 kicks and 74,000 halts against Linux's 54,600 and 54,700, while the same tar into 80-100's `rcat` made some 7,300 halts: 80-108's spin, which keeps a pipe's reader from sleeping while its writer runs on another processor, took effect for `rcat` and not for `cat`. GNU `cat` does not read the pipe at all. Two changes bring it to 6,480 to 7,142 halts and 6,111 to 6,711 kicks, under Linux's, and the tar from 0.97 to 1.02 s to 0.61 to 0.66 s, against Linux's 0.99 s. `rcat`'s figures do not move.

## The cause

coreutils 9.12's `cat` copies with splice(2) whenever it has no format option and its input is not a regular file of 32 KiB or less (`splice_cat`, src/cat.c:557-660): it makes a pipe of its own, raises it to 512 KiB (`increase_pipe_size`, src/splice.h), splices its input into that pipe and then that pipe into its output, so the tar's pipe is emptied by a pipe-to-pipe splice. Linux 6.12 waits there in `ipipe_prep` (fs/splice.c:1668-1693), as `pipe_read` waits. Rach2's wait, `pipe_link_ready`, slept at once: 80-108 had put the spin in the read alone.

A splice was also dispatched under the big lock, where read(2) and write(2) are not, and the spin's test refuses a holder of the big lock while any other processor has claimed it or the writer's processor waits for it (`bkl_spin_ok`, after Linux's mutex, whose owner never spins while it holds what a waiter needs). With the spin added to `pipe_link_ready` and the splice still under the big lock, the cached tar made 64,769 to 69,366 halts. Which claim refused each spin was not traced; with the splice off the big lock the halts fell tenfold.

## The changes

* `pipe_read_spin` gives back the locks of both pipes of a tee or a splice, and the waits of a tee or splice for an empty source pipe (`pipe_link_ready`) and of a splice from a pipe to a file or socket (`pipe_wait_data`) spin once before they sleep, as a read does.
* splice between two pipes and tee run without the big lock, as read and write do, since `pipe_tee` takes only the pipes' locks and takes the big lock itself for a signal (`pipe_signal_pending`) and SIGPIPE (`pipe_sigpipe`); a splice between a pipe and anything else takes the big lock around its work (`splice_file`).
