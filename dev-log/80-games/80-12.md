# Crysis on the Lenovo's GPU

Task 80-12 launched the 64-bit Crysis on the Lenovo's own GPU for the first time, on 2026-11-11 between 00:35 and 01:59 UTC+6, in eight attempts on two trial boots. The game loaded the island and played on the panel through Crocus at 22 to 33 frames a second, and Linux's flyby ran at 18 to 27 frames a second where Linux runs 45. Two faults were fixed on the way: the x86-64 kernel had outgrown its boot slot, and a page flip did not wait for the GPU to finish its buffer. The frame stalls and the game's sound gaps follow the big lock, which the owner kept for the next milestone. The figures below are from the files in `80-12/evidence/` and, for the raw logs, `build/runs/task3426/lenovo/` on the Mac.

## The kernel slot

Before attempt 1, `bootctl kernel` refused the branch head's kernel: `'/data/trial2653/kernel-7445e788' is larger than any slot`. It was 4,331,496 bytes, the slot 4,194,304 (`SLOT_MAX` in `utils/bootctl.c`, `SLOT_BYTES` in `tools/image/mkdisk_grub.sh` and `utils/rach2-install`), so no install of that head could have put its kernel on the machine. Of the file, 3,739,880 bytes are what GRUB loads; the rest is the symbol and string tables that `--strip-debug` keeps. The attempts ran on the same kernel stripped, whose two load segments are byte for byte the unstripped file's. The lasting fix, commit `d68dca4e8` (79-12.5), drops the local symbols from the kernel a loader reads, which nothing in the kernel or the tests reads and `kernel.debug` keeps, and fails the link for a kernel over the slot. The x86-64 kernel is now 3,966,720 bytes, 227,584 under the slot; it grew 266,216 bytes between 80-6 and this head, so a larger slot will be needed soon.

## Tearing (80-12.a)

The owner saw tearing in attempts 1 and 2. Weston passes no `IN_FENCE_FD` for its own composited frame, and Rach2's flip waited only for that fence, so a flip could scan out a buffer the GPU was still drawing. Linux's `drm_gem_plane_helper_prepare_fb` gives a plane with no in-fence the write fences of its framebuffer's objects, for every GEM driver and for i915 through `intel_prepare_plane_fb`. Commit `62d23f2c1` does the same in the DRM core's commit (`implicit_fences` in `rach2/sys/drm/drm_atomic.c`), with a host row in `drm_kms_hosttest.c` that fails without it.

On the machine the fix acts on most frames. The commit worker `kdrm`, which runs a nonblocking flip once its fences signal, had switched once from boot to the session's start, and 11,555 times during attempt 6, which drew 8,370 frames (`kdrm.txt`); before the fix each of those flips went to the screen at once. The flyby's frame rate did not change (attempts 6 and 8 against 2 and 5). Whether the tearing is gone to the eye has not been seen: nobody watched the screen during attempts 6 to 8, and Weston's read-back shows the composited frame, not what the panel scans out.

## Frame stalls (80-12.b)

The owner saw frames freeze at times and thought of shader compiles, or locks. The numbers rule out the disk and point to the big lock; they make compiles unlikely without ruling them out.

* Shaders: Mesa's shader cache, `~/.cache/mesa_shader_cache_db`, is written and kept between runs on the trial root (attempt 1 wrote 56,700 bytes and the later attempts read it), and the space allocated to it, as `du -sk` reads it, did not grow during attempts 6 to 8. That alone does not show that no shader was compiled, since a write inside the blocks already allocated leaves it unchanged, and no reading of the compiler or of the cache's own counters was taken. The stalls also fall at different places in each loop and the first loop has the fewest (13 frames over 100 ms in attempt 6, against 56, 15 and 41), the opposite of what compiles would give.
* Disk: in attempt 8 the load read 1,025 MiB from the data partition and the first loop 307 MiB, but the second to fourth loops read 1, 2 and 1 MiB and hold most of the stalls.
* The big lock (`stalls.txt`): in the readings of loops 2 to 4 that hold a frame over 150 ms, the lock's waits are 2.17, 2.42 and 2.27 s a second across the four processors in attempts 6, 7 and 8, against 1.57, 1.92 and 1.71 in the readings whose frames all stay under 60 ms. Idle time falls from 12, 3 and 10% to 4, 1 and 4%, and system time rises to 79, 85 and 78%. The entries whose holds grow in the stalled readings are `epoll_pwait2` (about twice), page faults and `openat`, beside the GPU's `ioctl`, which stays the longest holder. A stall is a moment when the game's other threads take the lock more and the render thread's submissions wait for it.

The figures above came from the first form of `80-12/stalls.py`, which had two faults since fixed. It took an entry's growth as its whole total when the entry first entered the sixteen that `/proc/stat` lists, so an entry new to that list in a reading was charged with all its holds since boot; and it averaged each reading's processor split with equal weight, though the readings differ in length. The lock's waits, the frame rates, the cache and the disk are not affected. The hold rates and the processor percentages above may be, by an amount not known: the samples and frame times they came from were not committed, so they were not computed again.

Over the whole of attempt 6 the lock's waits grew by 679 s in about 470 s, and the processors were 17% in user code, 59% in the kernel and 24% idle. In 15 s of the first loop of attempt 7 the game made 502 `openat`, 622 `newfstatat` (295 of them failing), 297 `getdents64` and 8,987 `read` calls (`file-calls.txt`), Wine's lookups and the game's streaming, each of which takes the lock. Linux has no such lock, and the owner kept it for the next milestone.

## Sound gaps (80-12.c)

HDA's counters show no underrun and no missed period in any attempt, so the kernel kept the hardware fed. PulseAudio's log shows the game's own stream running dry: 142 times in attempt 6 and 444 in attempt 7, whose log carries times. In attempt 7, 1.45 of these came a second in the readings that hold a frame stall and 0.71 in the others. The gaps are the game's sound thread missing its deadline in the same stalls as the frames. Linux's flyby on this machine played into ALSA's dummy card and was not read for gaps.

## The volume (80-12.d)

A change reaches the kernel's mixer only through PulseAudio's sink volume, which its OSS sink writes to `/dev/mixer`, as Linux's PulseAudio writes ALSA's mixer through its ALSA sink (`volume-paths.txt`). With the game's stream playing in attempt 7, the sink set to 3% made the mixer read 2. The game's stream set to 150% left it at 1, since PulseAudio keeps a stream's volume apart from the sink's (`flat-volumes` is off). XF86AudioRaiseVolume and XF86AudioLowerVolume sent to the game's window changed nothing, and the kernel has no path from a volume key to the mixer. The sink set back to 1% wrote 0, PulseAudio's rounding, which `upstream.md` records. Which program set the sink to 86 in attempt 2 is not known; that run's PulseAudio log was not verbose. The watcher now logs each change with its time and leaves it.

## The file cache

The kernel keeps at most 1 GiB of the file data that `read` brings in, the buffer cache's half of a kernel heap that is an eighth of memory, with 15 GB of the 16 free (`file-cache.txt`). Music.pak, 543 MiB, came from memory on a second read; Objects.pak, 1.6 GB, came from the disk on every read and pushed Music.pak out. Linux would keep both. So each load reads its 1 GiB from the disk again, at about 85 MiB/s, which is part of the load's 41 s against Linux's 19.7 s. It is not the stalls' cause; it is a limit Linux does not have, reported to the manager on the task to place.
