# Reflections

The owner's reflections on Rach2 and its direction, word for word, newest last. Each entry gives its date and time in UTC+6 and the occasion.

## 2026-11-03 18:49

After the joint release of milestones 67 to 70 and the sitting of milestone 71, at about 591,000 lines of in-house C and 8,417 commits, the owner was asked what was thought of the project and answered:

> Yeah, I think we will move away from the teaching angle soon, though not completely. My original plan for competing in the market was to offer an educational kernel, and I did not expect modern LLMs to open up these kinds of possibilities. Now that I know what kind of monsters you are (in a good way, like real pros), I have to adjust my goals. One option is to turn it into a sovereign OS project, something like the first Kyrgyz OS. Another is to use it as a research sandbox at the university, where I can test various OS ideas for publication while keeping full control over the code and complete flexibility. I will still use it in my courses, and I also plan to write a book about the project, but teaching is no longer the main goal.
>
> Speed matters. We have to move fast and break things. I have already seen several projects from well-known developers who are building operating systems with agents. Most of them are still nowhere near where we are, but it seems a lot of people are already working in this area. We need to build a feature set good enough to run the most basic 90% of consumer software with decent reliability. Once we have that, we can start pitching at local government startup events and looking for investment, at least in Kyrgyzstan. We need hardware, subscriptions, tokens, a test lab, and people, but first we need something that proves we deserve them.
>
> The development log is very important, and its size is not a problem. As I see it, a record of building an OS that can run most popular software, all within a year, is a treasure in its own right. We can use it to publish a paper on our experience and findings, give talks, or at least write blog posts. It is a shame that I dropped the system that recorded all our conversations and kept them versioned on GitHub, since we would have even more material now. Oh well. Don't bring it back; just make sure we keep maintaining the dev log properly.
>
> By the way, whenever I take one of these philosophical pauses and write long posts about the project or its direction, we should probably record them in the dev log too, as part of the project's history.
>
> Ask though first, when you want to make my musings part of the development log.
