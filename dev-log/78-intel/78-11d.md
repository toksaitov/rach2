# WebGPU's first-frame stall on the Lenovo

Task 78-11d finds and removes the stall of 180 to 300 ms that Chromium's WebGPU picture showed at its second or third frame in every run of [78-11b](78-11b.md)'s trial, which left its 480 frames 3 to 5% slower than Linux's. This record holds what is known before the Lenovo is reached, the tools that measure it, and the step that measures Linux and then Rach2 on the same machine with the same scripts. The step ran on 2026-11-12 ([Results](#results)).

## What 78-11b left

The picture's recorded frame times (`78-11b/runs.json`, runs 2 to 7) give, by `stall.py`'s own reckoning of the slowest gap among the first 16 frames:

| Run | Gap | After frame | Steady rate from frame 10 | Gaps over 25 ms after frame 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 183.2 ms | 1 | 59.51 FPS | 2 |
| 3 | 216.6 ms | 2 | 59.01 FPS | 6 |
| 4 | 200.0 ms | 1 | 59.38 FPS | 3 |
| 5 | 216.7 ms | 2 | 59.13 FPS | 6 |
| 6 | 300.0 ms | 1 | 59.13 FPS | 5 |
| 7 | 299.8 ms | 1 | 59.13 FPS | 6 |

Each gap is a whole number of the panel's 16.7 ms frames: 11, 13, 12, 13, 18 and 18. Linux's six picture runs in run2's steps 9 and 9b gave 59.2 to 60.1 FPS for the whole picture, and the stored reference's 480 frames took 8.0098 s; the page those runs used was not kept, so whether its time began before its first frame, and so would have counted such a gap, is not known.

`/proc/PID/wchan` read empty on that trial because its kernel had no wait channels: it was built from task/2660 at 41f483a6, and 80-20's `31d324763`, which gave `wchan` and `syscall` Linux's contents, is not an ancestor of that commit; it reached the milestone branch at 07:33 UTC+6, twenty minutes before that boot. The kernel the step below boots has both files.

That kernel also lacked 80-19's `12077d61e`, which lets the boot processor's tick end without the big lock when another processor holds it. Without it, a program on the boot processor could run nothing for as long as the other processors kept the lock busy, which 80-19 measured in QEMU at up to seconds of a copy ([80-19](../80-games/80-19.md)). A Chromium starting its GPU process, renderer and compositor at once is such a load, so that fault may account for the stall, or for part of it; that is not shown, and the step measures it on the kernel that has the fix.

## The tools

* The picture page, `tests/graphics/browser/hasvk.html`, keeps beside each frame's time when its callback ran and returned, its setup's phases, its time origin in the epoch's milliseconds, and a user timing mark of each of its first ten frames. A wait inside the page's own calls shows as a late return; a wait for the next frame as a late start.
* The browser runner's `webgpu-frames` and `webgpu-trace` modes make `webgpu-hasvk`'s readback and draw its picture without judging the pixels or the sandboxes; `webgpu-trace` keeps Chromium's own trace of the picture page over its debugging protocol, with each thread's tasks, the GPU process's, the compositor's and Dawn's work and Mojo's and Wayland's messages.
* `tests/graphics/lenovo/threadwatch.c` logs each change of each watched thread's state, wait channel and system call, of the GPU's clock, of the i915 interrupt's count and, on Rach2, of the big lock's and the timer's counters in `/proc/stat`, about once a millisecond. On this testing host it made a pass over eight threads in 0.35 ms. Its reads of `/proc` take Rach2's big lock, so it is itself a load there; the runs without it measure what it costs.
* `tests/graphics/lenovo/stall.py USER OUT RUNS` starts hasvk.py's private Weston, presents 300 vkcube frames, and runs the picture RUNS times, in turn bare, beside threadwatch, and beside threadwatch with Chromium's trace. `summary.json` and `stall.txt` give each run's gap and every thread's state through it. The same script runs on Debian and on Rach2, so the comparison is like for like. The [graphics Readme](../../tests/graphics/Readme.md) describes the files.

## Trial readiness

This task changes no kernel or driver code: the page, the browser runner, the sampler and the driver script. The kernel the trial boots is the milestone branch's head as merged at the step. Its kernel code that has not run on the Lenovo is other tasks', since the last kernel that did, 78-11b's at 41f483a6:

* 80-19: the boot processor's tick without the big lock (`12077d61e`, `rach2/sys/arch/x86/isr.c`), and splice's span copies and `F_SETPIPE_SZ` (`be49efeea`).
* 80-20: `/proc/PID/syscall` and `wchan`, each wait naming itself (`31d324763`), which threadwatch reads at about a thousand reads a second for each thread.
* 76-39: the PS/2 port's protocol fallback, the touchpad's resync and its sysfs files (`ceb702a3c`, `1fe39a5af`, `61796a4cf`), which drive the Lenovo's i8042 and its pad.
* The kernel kept without its symbols (`4f4f81d7d`), VmFlags' `ac` and `nr`, the page cache's read past the end, the tty's echo and master writes, and the read and write refusals of a directory and an epoll set.

The manager reads these and says whether each has had the review a trial needs. The step's own work runs as programs: Weston, Chromium and vkcube through the paths 78-11b ran, and a reader of `/proc` and `/sys` files. Its waits are the browser runner's, which watch the browser's process, and threadwatch ends at its signal or after 600 s. It owns the screen for its boot and plays no sound.

### Linux

```sh

The result is the six lines, `summary.json` and `stall.txt` in the archive. `debian-restore.txt` must give p1's hash equal to `p1-before.sha256`, and `after-debian.txt` the same release and slots as `before.txt`.

If Debian's runs show a gap like Rach2's, 150 ms or more after frame 1 or 2 in most runs, the stall is Linux's too: record the numbers here, release the lock and finish the task without the trial. Otherwise go on.

### Reading the result

`stall.txt` names, for each sampled run, every thread's state before the gap and each change through it. On Rach2, a thread that shows `R` with no change while the gap lasts, `P` lines of the sampler, or a `bkl_wait_max_us` that grows by the gap's length, mean the processors were held by the big lock. A thread asleep in a named wait through the gap, with the page's `callback_end_ms` before it and Chromium's trace showing which of its threads waited, names the call that is late; Chromium's trace says what the GPU process and the compositor were doing meanwhile. If the bare runs do not stall and the sampled ones do, the stall is the sampler's load and the bare runs are the measurement. The cause found in Rach2 is fixed in this task; a trial of the fixed kernel needs a new go, as the machine skill says.

## What the remaining frames wait on

The archives of the step, `debian-out.tar.gz` and `rach2-out.tar.gz` (kept on the Mac in `build/runs/t3504/` until this task closes), hold each run's `result.json` and the traced runs' Chromium traces, which the record's files above do not.

The page is not late in its own calls: on both systems each callback returns within 1 ms of its entry. The frame time the next callback is given is late, which means the compositor sent no frame for those vsyncs. The traces show why. The page's `createShaderModule` and `createRenderPipeline` return at once in the renderer, and the GPU process's main thread, `CrGpuMain`, runs them in its first large `DawnCommands` task, which holds it through the first frames. Display compositing and `SwapBuffers` run on the same thread, so no frame is presented until that task ends.

| Traced run | `DawnCommands` | first pipeline | second pipeline | Tint's SPIR-V per shader |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Debian, run 2 | 43.0 ms | 23.7 ms | 17.2 ms | 0.5 to 0.8 ms |
| Debian, run 5 | 29.3 ms | 16.3 ms | 10.9 ms | 0.5 to 0.8 ms |
| Rach2, run 2 | 83.5 ms | 52.5 ms | 28.2 ms | 0.5 to 0.9 ms |
| Rach2, run 5 | 90.4 ms | 53.9 ms | 30.9 ms | 0.5 to 0.9 ms |

The task creates two shader modules and two render pipelines; one pair is the page's, and the trace does not name the other's caller. Each pipeline is compiled by Mesa's hasvk inside `vkCreateGraphicsPipelines`, after Tint has written its SPIR-V. Rach2 takes 40 to 60 ms longer, which is the two to three frames it loses beyond Linux's one or two. Tint's own work, pure computation in the same process, takes the same time on both, and so does `DirectRenderer::DrawRenderPass` (median 109 us on both over the run), so the processor runs at the same speed; the i3-4030U has no turbo. The sampler shows the GPU process's main thread runnable through the whole task and charged about 90 ms of processor time over its 112 ms of observed running, so the thread computes or runs in the kernel; it does not wait.

The kernel's share is the likely difference. Over a whole bare run (runs 0 and 3), Rach2 charged 637 and 508 ticks of system time against Debian's 297 and 273, with less user time, 714 and 693 against 817 and 799 ticks. In run 0 the big lock was waited for 1.77 s summed over the processors, and held longest by `epoll_wait` (0.57 s over 58,004 holds), the take after a switch (0.47 s), `openat` (0.31 s over 8,663 holds, 36 us each), interrupts outside a call (0.22 s), the timer (0.14 s) and `recvmsg` (0.11 s). These are whole-run totals; they do not say which of them falls inside the compile, and Mesa's compile itself makes few calls of that kind. A page fault and the calls that change the regions run without the big lock, so the fault path's cost would not show in those counts.

The sampler could not split the thread's time: it logged user plus system time as one number, and Rach2's `/proc/PID/stat` gave 0 for the fault counts, which the kernel keeps. Both are fixed on this task: the stat file gives the minor and major faults as Linux does, and threadwatch logs `UTIME/STIME/MINFLT`. `stall.py` now also zeroes debugfs's `bkl/calls`, `bkl/long` and `bkl/tick` before each bare run on Rach2, starts `bkl/profile` in its whole-kernel mode, and keeps the four after the run.

## The profiling step

One Rach2 boot, no Debian: three runs, bare, watched and traced, of the same page. It reads:

* from the watched and traced runs' `threadwatch.txt`, the GPU process's main thread's `UTIME/STIME/MINFLT` on its lines on each side of the trace's `DeviceBase::APICreateRenderPipeline` events, which give the compile's system time and faults;
* from the bare run's `after/bkl_profile`, named by `tests/system/games/bklread.sh` with the build's `kernel.debug`, the kernel's samples by entry and function, and from `bkl_calls` the big lock's holds by call and thread.

If the compile's system time accounts for the 40 to 60 ms, the profile names the kernel path, which this task then fixes and a second trial measures against Linux's figures above. If it does not, the compile's time is user time and the cause lies in what the kernel gives the process, such as the page size or the memory type of its mappings, which the profile's fault entries and `/proc/PID/smaps` of the GPU process then decide.
