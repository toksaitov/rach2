# 78-24: the panic's short report and its 30 seconds

The owner's spec of 11 November, at 15:53 and 15:57: the panic picture stays, with the character whole; a short report a person can photograph and type goes below it or over its bottom-left corner, never over her; the full record goes to the serial port, netconsole and the saved record; and the panic waits a configurable number of seconds, 30, before the machine resets.

## What the kernel does

* `panic_timeout` is 30 when the boot line has no `panic=` (`PANIC_TIMEOUT_DEFAULT`, `rach2/sys/panic.h`), the owner's choice over Linux's 0. `panic=0` halts and `panic=-1` resets at once, as before. The candidate's `panic=10` of 78-22 is gone from `tools/image/mkdisk_grub.sh` and `utils/rach2-install`, so every line of the menu, the fallback's and recovery's among them, waits 30 seconds and resets. A fallback that panics on every boot therefore resets every 30 seconds plus a boot, where 78-22 left it halted; the owner asked for the timeout on every boot.
* The screen takes no record during a panic (`klog_screen_stop`). `k_panic` writes `panic: stopped: REASON`, each line of the report as `panic: report: LINE`, and `panic: rebooting in N seconds..` to the ring, the serial port and netconsole, calls `k_panic_dump` to save the crash record, and then draws the picture and the report cell by cell, white on the picture's blue, so that nothing scrolls and no escape sequence from the log reaches the screen.
* The report, from the top: `rebooting in N s`, counted down once a second of the wait, or `halted` or `rebooting`; `crash record saved` when `k_panic_dump` returns nonzero; `build` and the commit, twelve digits with a `+` when `rach2/` or `mk/` differ from it (`build/rach2/head.h`, which `rach2/Makefile` rewrites only when the head changes, so a new commit compiles `panic.c` alone again); `cpu N  pid P`; `cmd NAME` with every byte outside printable ASCII as `?`; for an exception, `ip`, `addr` for a page fault's or a double fault's CR2, and the exception's name with its error code when the processor pushes one (`k_panic_note_fault`, `k_panic_note_address`, set by `isr.c` and `paging.c`); and `panic: REASON` last, as Linux's panic ends with its reason. The process is read only when that is safe: a fault while it is read panics again, and the second report leaves it out.
* Placement: below the picture, a row apart, when the rows allow (128 by 48 and larger); else over the bottom-left corner, two columns in and a row above the bottom, with a blank cell between each line and the character's first cell on its row. When a line does not fit, the picture moves up over its two blank first rows, and then the report takes the last row too. On 80 by 25 and on the Lenovo's 85 by 24 every line fits with x86-64's 18-digit addresses; a line that fits nowhere is cut where she begins.

## Not done, and why

* Symbol and offset for the instruction pointer. The kernel has no symbol table: x86-64's has 12,210 text symbols with 177 KB of names, and the kernel, 3.90 MB, sits 290 KB under the 4 MiB slot of a boot partition. The report gives the address and the build head instead, which `addr2line -e build/x86_64/rach2/kernel.debug` resolves at that commit.
* `crash record saved` waits for 78-23 (#3585), which sets `k_panic_dump`; until then the line never shows.

## Measured

* `panic_hosttest`, run alone: the report on the text console at 80 by 25 and 80 by 24 and on framebuffers of 85 by 24 and 128 by 48, with every cell of the character in her color and nothing written over it.
* QEMU by hand, `rach2.rescue rach2.stackoverrun=fork`, two processors, VGA text: on i686 with `panic=0` the screen read `halted`, `build 15b24118cdb4`, `cpu 0  pid 1`, `ip 0x001159b0`, `addr 0xc001bdf0`, `double fault` and `panic: double fault: a kernel stack ran out`, the picture unmoved; on x86-64 the same with `ip 0xffffffff80118762` and `addr 0xffffc0000002fdd8`, the picture moved up two rows. With no `panic=`, the i686 screen read `rebooting in 27 s` three seconds into the wait, and the port had `panic: rebooting in 30 seconds..`.
* The QEMU cases that panic: `tests/run panic` names `panic=5` or `panic=-1` itself; `machine.memory.stackoverrun-*` end the machine as soon as the panic's record is out; `machine.smp.nmi` and `machine.smp.exception` now boot with `panic=0`, so that both processors stay halted, and read the report on the screen.
