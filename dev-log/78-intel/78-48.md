# 78-48: HDMI audio on Intel

Task 78-48 drives HDMI and DisplayPort audio on Haswell as Linux 6.12.115 does: the ELD made from the EDID, the connector's `audio` property, the transcoder's audio enable and clocks in i915, the audio component that joins i915 to HD Audio, and the display's HD Audio controller and HDMI codec, which play on `/dev/dsp1` and list their card and ELDs in `/proc/asound`. The code cites the Linux lines it follows; the files' opening comments list them.

## What was built

* The ELD. `drm_edid_to_eld` makes a connector's ELD from the cleaned EDID with the EDID property, as Linux's `drm_edid_to_eld` (drm_edid.c:5606-5725): the monitor's name, the first CTA extension's audio descriptors, up to 15, its speaker allocation, and HDMI's latencies and AI bit. A connector without an EDID has its ELD cleared.
* The property. HDMI and DisplayPort connectors have `audio`, Linux's `force_audio_names` (intel_connector.c:215), `auto` by default; a new value is a modeset, as `intel_digital_connector_atomic_check` (intel_atomic.c:115). `force-dvi` drives an HDMI sink as DVI.
* i915. `intel_audio.c` computes `has_audio` and the ELD with its A/V sync delay, enables the transcoder's audio output after the planes, with the ELD valid bit clear, programs `HSW_AUD_CFG`'s pixel clock and N, disables it before the planes go, and notifies the component of the port. A pipe the firmware lit with audio has its audio turned off at the bind.
* The component. `acomp.c` joins the display's side and the codec's side when both have registered, and carries Linux's `get_power`, `put_power`, `get_cdclk_freq`, `sync_audio_rate` and `get_eld`, and the display's notification of a port.
* The codec. `hda_hdmi.c` drives 8086:0A0C, 0C0C and 0D0C, which `hda.c` now leaves to it: the controller's PCI bits and BCLK, the codec's converters and pins of three device entries, the vendor bits, the ELDs, one playback stream with Linux's pin and converter choice, infoframe, channel slots and IEC958 bits, refilled from the timer tick, and `/dev/dsp1` through `dsp.c`'s new `dspdev_open_driver`.
* `/proc/asound`. `cards`, `card0/id` and `card0/eld#C.N` in Linux's text, from the codec's state.

## inject-audio

IGT's `kms_hdmi_inject` inject-audio forces an HDMI connector on with IGT's audio EDID, sets a mode and reads `/proc/asound/card*/eld#*` for an entry with `monitor_present 1`, `eld_valid 1` and `monitor_name IGT`. It cannot run in QEMU: QEMU has no Intel display, so no HDMI connector exists to force on, and no Haswell display audio controller or HDMI codec, so no ELD file exists to read; the i915 driver binds in QEMU only to the fake chip, which has neither. The case's two halves are checked on the host instead: `i915_hdmi_hosttest.c` runs the display's half on the HD 4400's model, through the component to an ELD named IGT, and `hdmi_audio_hosttest.c` runs the codec's half, from that ELD to the `eld#0.0` text the case reads. Its first run on the Lenovo, with a check of the infoframes on a monitor, joins the owner's sitting; `tests/graphics/igt-kms-trial.txt` leaves it out until then.

## Measured

* Both kernels link; the x86-64 kernel is 3,966,496 bytes, under 4,194,304.
* `i915_hdmi_hosttest.c` and the new `hdmi_audio_hosttest.c`, run alone with the host tests' flags, each with the i686 and then the x86-64 arch headers, print `ok`.
* No suite was run and nothing was booted, as the task orders.

## Differences from Linux

* No DisplayPort MST, so each pin's second and third device entries stay empty.
* `/dev/dsp1` plays one stream of two channels with no mix, and nothing records; `/proc/asound` lists only the HDMI card.
* The stream is refilled from the 4 ms tick with a buffer of at least 40 ms, where Linux takes the stream's interrupt.
* The firmware's audio state is turned off at the bind, not adopted.
