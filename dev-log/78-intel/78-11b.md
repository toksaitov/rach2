# hasvk presenting and WebGPU on the Lenovo

Task 78-11b prepares vkcube and Chromium's WebGPU on hasvk under Weston on the HD 4400. The implementation is [`tests/graphics/lenovo/hasvk.py`](../../tests/graphics/lenovo/hasvk.py); its interface and output are in the [graphics Readme](../../tests/graphics/Readme.md). The trial ran on 2026-11-11 under the manager's go and passed: vkcube presents at the panel's rate, and Chromium's WebGPU names hasvk's Intel gen-7 adapter, computes and draws right, as on Linux. Its results are under Results below and its evidence in [`78-11b/`](78-11b/).

## References

The [stored Linux references](../../tests/graphics/ref/lenovo/Readme.md) include the native hasvk vkcube runs under Weston, 300 then 900 FIFO frames twice, and Chromium's compute, render and picture results with `--ignore-gpu-blocklist`. The rates are 60.07 and 60.06 FPS for vkcube and 59.93 FPS for the WebGPU picture. These are the discovery agents' measurements, read from their original output. This task needs no Debian boot.

The step uses Debian's Mesa 25.0.7 and Chromium 156, the panel's 1366x768 mode, Weston's GL renderer and kiosk shell, hasvk alone at Vulkan 1.2, and an ordinary account in the seat, video and render groups. It records the rates beside Linux's. vkcube's held frames must differ and both be panel-sized. The WebGPU adapter must be native Intel gen-7; 1024 compute words must be right; its rendered pixel must be RGBA 255,128,0,255; the picture's centre and corner must match Linux's in both the canvas and the captured panel within one byte. Chromium's diagnostics must name the Intel Vulkan adapter, and its process sandbox policy and binary version must match the stored Debian sandbox case. The reference's picture ended after eight seconds, giving 480 frames; this step draws those 480 frames and records the elapsed time.

## Results

The notice was posted at 07:42:41; the release was read at 07:42 from the candidate slot, its kernel `0effe5ac...` in both slots; the four staged files matched their local digests at 07:51; the trial was armed at 07:51:38 and booted at 07:53:00, 18 minutes after the notice. The boot took trialroot with `/dev/loop0` at `/` and the image path above, adopted pipe A at 1366x768 and registered i915 on card 0, with no ring, wedge, DMA or register fault (`boot-dmesg.txt`). The release was back at 08:16:52, both slots measured again, and the lock released at 08:17.

The first run, at the go's merge, passed (`hasvk-first.log`, `first/`):

* vkcube: hasvk named the HD 4400 as an integrated GPU in each of the four timed runs; frames 300 and 345 were captured at 1366x768 with different CRCs, 0x45d19f75 and 0x0b5bf495.
* WebGPU: the adapter was Intel gen-7, not a fallback; 1024 compute words and the rendered pixel 255,128,0,255 read back with no error; the canvas and the panel's capture matched Linux's centre 95,80,80 and corner 25,25,76.
* `chrome://gpu` lists Dawn's Vulkan backend on "Intel(R) HD Graphics 4400 (HSW GT2)" and says "WebGPU: Hardware accelerated" and "Sandboxed: false" for the GPU process, as Debian's does. The renderers and the storage service run under seccomp mode 2 in their own user, PID and network namespaces, and the GPU and network processes without seccomp or namespaces, the same policy as the stored Debian case on the same Chromium 156.0.8078.79.
* Rates, Rach2 beside Linux: vkcube 61.72 and 59.92 FPS against 60.07 and 60.06; WebGPU's 480 frames in 8.2785 s, 57.98 FPS, against 8.0098 s, 59.93 FPS.

The kernel logged no GPU reset, wedge, VM or VT-d fault through all seven runs (`dmesg-final.txt`).

The second run, on the same boot, failed its check that Dawn names hasvk: `chrome://gpu` had been read while its "Dawn Info" section was still empty. The reader waited only for the page's sandbox line, which comes before Dawn's adapter list. It now waits for "Vulkan backend" as well, as the Venus case already did; the third and later runs passed with it (`hasvk-third.log`).

The WebGPU total is slower than Linux's in every run, 8.23 to 8.40 s for the 480 frames (`runs.json`). From the second run on the page keeps each frame's callback time. Each run shows one stall of 180 to 300 ms at the second or third frame, then a steady rate of 59.0 to 59.5 FPS over frames 10 to 479, with three to seven later frames each one to three vblanks late. Linux's six picture runs in the discovery's steps 9 and 9b gave 59.2 to 60.1 FPS, so the steady rate is within Linux's spread and the difference in the total is that first stall. A Mesa shader cache kept across two runs did not remove it (216 ms in the warm run), so it is not hasvk compiling the shader. Its cause is not traced: Rach2's `/proc/PID/wchan` reads empty, and a sampler of the threads' `/proc` state ran at 30 to 100 ms a pass, too coarse to resolve it. The Linux page's source was not kept, so whether its eight-second window began before its first frame, and so would have counted such a stall, is not known.

vkcube's first pair includes the run with an empty shader cache, so its difference gives 60.8 to 61.7 FPS; the second pair gives 59.92 to 60.01 FPS in every run.

Correction: the first run's shader cache was not empty. Weston shared the clients' cache and was writing it as `guest-env.sh` removed it; the removal failed and left 9 files (`first/environment.log`), and the first run's WebGPU record names 1280x800 where the page drew at 1366x768. [first/corrections.md](78-11b/first/corrections.md) has both and what now prevents them. The second and third runs' environment logs were not kept, so whether their caches were empty is not known.

## Trial readiness

It requests no Vulkan version override. The kernel paths it combines have already run in the display trial [78-10d](78-10d.md), the ordinary-user and recovery trial [78-6c](78-6c.md), and the hasvk trial [78-11c](78-11c.md). The latter passed its 15 smoke cases and matched Linux's 6,431 sampled cases, including the timeline and sync-file paths used by presentation. The display trial matched Linux's CRCs at all thirteen modes.

The commands retain the existing bounded forcewake, ring, reset, AUX, power and display waits described in those trials' readiness notes. This task adds no hardware path for a new chip-model test. Its own checks need real rendering: the fake i915 backend completes a batch without drawing, so it cannot prove these pixels. The new Python files and the page's JavaScript were compiled; no suite, host test or boot was run in preparation. The manager must review this combination before its first presenting boot. The go covers this task's commits on the milestone branch as built, under the machine skill's rule.

The step owns the screen for its boot, draws the cube and triangle, and plays no sound. It ends the boot session's Weston, keeps seatd, starts a private Weston through that service, and runs each program as `desktop`. Its children and private scratch are removed on failure as well as success, and its output is retained as evidence. A failure before SSH answers leaves the machine for the owner's return; record it and tell the manager, without asking the owner to attend.

## The step

### Build and payload

Merge the current stack into the task branch before building; do not rebase the pushed task branch until the hardware step has passed and the task is ready to merge. In the following commands `T` is this checkout, and `R` is its evidence directory. The Linux build copy is private to this task; the build uses the testing host's shared caches.

```sh
T=$(pwd)
```

### Take the machine and verify the release

```sh
LOCK=$(git rev-parse --git-common-dir)/../build/flow/lenovo
mkdir "$LOCK" && echo 2660 > "$LOCK/issue"
L() { ssh -i "$T/krffs/rach2_demo_ed25519" -o BatchMode=yes -o ConnectTimeout=5 -o UserKnownHostsFile=/dev/null -o StrictHostKeyChecking=no -o ServerAliveInterval=5 -o ServerAliveCountMax=3 root@192.168.0.106 'test "$(cat /sys/class/net/eth0/address)" = 28:d2:44:7e:e0:af && '"$*"; }
back() { i=0; until L true 2>/dev/null; do i=$((i+1)); [ "$i" -lt 180 ] || return 1; sleep 2; done; L 'mixer vol 1'; }
L 'cat /etc/rach2/release; bootctl status; cat /proc/cmdline' > "$R/before.txt"
```

If the lock cannot be taken, stop here and hand the task back. Every `L` command checks the Lenovo's station address before a read or write. Read `before.txt`: the release must be booted from the candidate slot with no trial armed. The preceding hasvk trial left release bb151e3ba there, its 2,845,120-byte kernel SHA-256 `0effe5acc26e1bf2f20b87b73e92ad8a9d769b708086b5ba700873f7e95e2cad` in both slots. If a release has replaced it, take that release's size and hash from its record before proceeding. The next read assumes the candidate has one run, as `before.txt` must confirm; for a fragmented candidate concatenate the sector runs that `bootctl status` lists, in order.

```sh
L 's=$(bootctl status | sed -n "s/^bootctl: candidate: first sector \([0-9]*\),.*/\1/p"); dd if=/dev/bootpart bs=512 skip=$s count=8192 2>/dev/null | head -c 2845120' > "$R/release-kernel"
shasum -a 256 "$R/release-kernel"
RACH2_MEASURE_SLOT=candidate RACH2_MEASURE_KERNEL=$R/release-kernel sh tools/dev/machine_identity.sh "$R/identity-before.txt" ssh -i "$T/krffs/rach2_demo_ed25519" -o BatchMode=yes -o UserKnownHostsFile=/dev/null -o StrictHostKeyChecking=no root@192.168.0.106
```

```sh
L 'mount -t ext2 /dev/disk0p6 /data && mkdir -p /data/trial/t2660 && df -k /data'
L 'cat > /data/trial/t2660/release-kernel' < "$R/release-kernel"

L 'tar -xzf - -C /data/trial/t2660' < "$R/graphics.tar.gz"
L 'chmod 755 /data /data/trial /data/trial/t2660; mkdir -p /data/trial/t2660/tmp; chmod 1777 /data/trial/t2660/tmp'
L "sha256sum /data/trial/t2660/release-kernel /data/trial/t2660/kernel-$KS /data/trial/t2660/root-$IS.img /dev/shm/bootctl; sync" > "$R/staged.sha256"
```

Read the free space before copying: the 16 GiB image and evidence must fit. Compare each whole SHA-256 with the local files, including the release kernel. Then, after the notice's ten minutes:

```sh
L "bootctl kernel /data/trial/t2660/kernel-$KS && umount /data && sync"
L "sh -s /trial/t2660/root-$IS.img rach2.i915" < "$T/dev-log/78-intel/78-10d/trialroot.sh" > "$R/armed.txt"

```

Read `armed.txt` before the reboot: `saved_entry: fallback`, `next_entry: trialroot`, the root image path above and `rach2.i915`, with no `rach2.i915.disable_display`. Read `trial.txt` and the boot log before running: booted trialroot, `/dev/loop0` at `/`, p6 at `/data`, the trial image path whose SHA-256 in `staged.sha256` matches the local `root.sha256` (a trial root has no `/etc/rach2/release`), the adopted 1366x768 pipe and i915 registered on the Intel card. Stop on a new ring failure, wedge, DMA fault or register timeout. A boot that does not answer in `back`'s six minutes is reported on the task and to the manager; do not send another write to it.
