# 78-14b: video on the GPU on the Lenovo

The task's review of the video rings and their parser tables against Linux 6.12, and the trial it prepares on the Lenovo's HD 4400: clips decoded through VA-API, each frame's hash compared with a software decode and with Linux's; both browsers playing 76-17a's clips at 720p and 1080p with their drops counted and each decoder named; and a decode on the video ring while piglit hangs the render ring, recovered as on Linux. Linux's numbers are taken in the same step, under Debian on the same machine. The results go at the end.

## Review note (78-14b.1)

Read against Linux 6.12.115's `drivers/gpu/drm/i915`: `gt/intel_ring_submission.c`, `gt/gen6_engine_cs.c`, `gt/intel_engine_cs.c`, `gt/intel_gt_irq.c`, `gt/intel_reset.c`, `i915_cmd_parser.c` and `gem/i915_gem_execbuffer.c`, beside `i915_ring.c`, `i915_request.c`, `i915_irq.c`, `i915_cmd_parser.c` and `i915_gem_execbuffer.c`. No difference was found; nothing was changed.

### The rings

* The engines' table in `i915_ring.c` is Linux's for Haswell, register by register against `intel_gt_regs.h` and `i915_reg.h`: vcs0 at `GEN6_BSD_RING_BASE` 0x12000 with its status page's address in `BSD_HWS_PGA_GEN7` 0x4180, its user interrupt `GT_BSD_USER_INTERRUPT` (bit 12) in the GT bank, its reset domain `GEN6_GRDOM_MEDIA` (bit 2) and its fault register 0x4194; vecs0 at `VEBOX_RING_BASE` 0x1a000 with 0x4380, `PM_VEBOX_USER_INTERRUPT` (bit 10) in the PM bank, `GEN6_GRDOM_VECS` (bit 4) and 0x4394.
* Both start, stop and resume through the code the render and blitter rings use, Linux's `xcs_resume` for every legacy ring: the ring stopped, every status-page write masked in `HWSTAM` (the render ring alone keeps bit 0), the status page set and the TLB flushed, the ring's start, the PPGTT's registers, `RING_VALID` waited for, then `STOP_RING` cleared. The render ring's workarounds are the only per-engine step, as in Linux.
* Commands, from `gen6_engine_cs.c`: the invalidation before a request is `gen6_emit_flush_vcs` on vcs0, `MI_FLUSH_DW` with `MI_INVALIDATE_TLB` and `MI_INVALIDATE_BSD`, a store-index post-sync write to the scratch dword 0x80, and `gen6_emit_flush_xcs` on vecs0, the same without `MI_INVALIDATE_BSD`; the flush after it the same without the invalidations. The breadcrumb is `gen7_emit_breadcrumb_xcs` word for word: the flushing store of the seqno, the 32 stores of `GEN7_XCS_WA`, an empty `MI_FLUSH_DW`, `MI_USER_INTERRUPT`. A batch starts as `gen6_emit_bb_start` does on every ring but the render ring, which alone takes `hsw_emit_bb_start`: `MI_BATCH_NON_SECURE_I965` unless the batch is secure.
* Interrupts: vcs0's is enabled as `gen6_irq_enable`, the ring's `IMR` and then the GT bank's; vecs0's as `hsw_irq_enable_vecs`, the ring's `IMR` and then the PM bank's `PMIMR`. `PMIER` holds the bit from the handler's registration on, as `gen5_gt_irq_postinstall` sets it at install. The handler reads and clears `GEN6_PMIIR` after the display's identity and dispatches the bit to the ring, as `ilk_irq_handler` and `gen6_rps_irq_handler` do; the frequency's events share the bank, and `GEN6_PMINTRMSK` masks only those, as in Linux.
* Selection: `I915_EXEC_BSD` takes vcs0 whatever its BSD ring bits, since Haswell has one video ring, and `I915_EXEC_VEBOX` takes vecs0; BSD ring bits with another ring are refused, as `eb_select_legacy_ring` does.

### The parser tables

* The video ring's tables are `gen7_common_cmds` and `gen7_video_cmds`, the video enhancement ring's `gen7_common_cmds` and `gen7_vecs_cmds`, row for row: opcode, mask, fixed length or length mask, action, and each bit check of `MI_STORE_DWORD_IMM`, `MI_FLUSH_DW` and `MI_CONDITIONAL_BATCH_BUFFER_END`. `MFX_WAIT` is `MEDIA_INSTR(1, 0, 0, 0)` under `STD_MFX_OPCODE_SHIFT`, taken as one dword, as Linux does for its biased length.
* A command no table holds takes its length from `gen7_bsd_get_cmd_length_mask` on both rings: 0x3f for an MI command, 0xffff for a media command of opcode 6 and 0xfff for the others, 0xff for another render client's, and 0, which refuses the batch, for any other client.
* Neither ring has a register allowlist, as in Linux, where `intel_engine_init_cmd_parser` gives `reg_tables` to the render and blitter rings alone: a batch on either that loads or stores any register is refused.
* Both rings parse whenever the call gives the batch's length, as `eb_use_cmdparser` on Haswell, whoever calls; the shadow runs as a secure batch, as `eb_parse` dispatches it.

### What only the hardware shows

Under Linux, i965's decoding batches pass these same tables on this machine, since Linux runs the parser on Haswell's video ring for every caller; the step's Debian part shows it again by decoding at all. A command the tables refuse fails the call with `EINVAL` and never reaches the chip, so a table that differed would show as a decode that fails, not as a hang.

* What runs on the chip for the first time is i965's decoding: MFX command streams on vcs0, from an ordinary user's context, each through the parser, whose shadow runs as a secure batch from the global GTT, where the aliasing PPGTT puts every buffer at the same address. A decode that hangs the video ring is a hang of that kind: its context is banned after Linux's count, its calls fail with `EIO`, and the other rings go on; if a reset fails the GPU is wedged, programs get errors, and the processor, the disk and SSH go on, so the put-back step still runs.
* i965's other calls are GEM objects, their CPU maps through the legacy `GEM_MMAP` (fd4f22c46, which `vainfo` ran on the fake chip in 78-14a), domains, waits and `EXECBUFFER2` on the render, blitter and video rings; Crocus has made all of these but the legacy map on this machine since 78-5c. The map is memory and touches no register.
* A program that waits on the GPU sleeps interruptibly and can be killed; the heartbeat finds a hang whether or not the submission worker runs.
* The display: the trial binds with the display on, as 78-12b's trial did, where Weston, the browsers and Crocus ran on this same bind. The step makes no mode set; its Westons are headless.
* Not tried in QEMU: the fake chip completes a batch without reading it, so a decode there tests nothing of the chip, and neither vahash nor any VA client but the browsers is in the image. The parser's tables and the rings' commands are checked above against Linux line by line, and `tests/host/i915_video_hosttest.c` submits through both rings against the host model of this chip and judges batches through their tables as Linux's parser does.
* What is left: a fault that stops the processor on a path not read here leaves the machine down until the owner is home, as for any trial. To tell where, the step reads the log after the boot, runs `vainfo` alone, then `decode`, whose clips come one at a time with a file for each, then `browsers`, then `hang` last, since it hangs the GPU on purpose, reading the log after each.
