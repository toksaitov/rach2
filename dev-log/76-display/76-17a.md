# 76-17a: the browsers under Weston against Debian

Task 76-17a: Chromium 156.0.8078.79 and Firefox ESR 153.6.0 on llvmpipe under a headless Weston in QEMU, each case judged against the Debian reference guest's on the same packages: the sandboxes, HTTPS pages, the six video clips, a chunk of the WebGL suite, WebGPU on SwiftShader, sound in three tabs and a GPU process restart. The cases are the `browser` family, `tests/cases/machine.browser.json`, and the `video` and `webgl` families' runners under it; [the graphics Readme](../../tests/graphics/Readme.md#tests) describes them. Everything matched Debian but Firefox's dropped frames under the references' load, which the owner took as a known limit of the stack.

## Results

The runs were the authors' own, of single cases, on the testing PC under KVM on 2026-11-10, by the owner's Codex loop and then a team member's loop; the figures below are theirs, from the task's issues, and each run's output stays in its loop's task worktree on the testing PC.

### The big lock

The cause is the kernel's one big lock, which every system call, fault and interrupt takes. `/proc/stat` now counts the waits for it, `bkl_wait_us` and `bkl_wait_max_us`, its longest hold, and its holds by the kernel entry that took them, `bkl_hold_entry` (6c548b219, f20d5d920, c58ea2c11; proc(5)); a graphics runner's record keeps `/proc/stat` before and after its run.

* In the twelve-clip runs the waits came to most of the guest's system time: Chromium's H.264 clip at 1080p had 66.9 s of system time and 38.4 s of user time on 4 processors, and 48.9 s of the system time were waits, the longest 88 ms against a frame's 33 ms. Firefox's VP9 and AV1 clips had 180.6 to 410.4 s of waits a run and single waits of 165 to 889 ms.
* In the run of 16:14, about 250 s on 4 processors, the processors waited 225 s, the longest wait 0.71 s. The sixteen entries listed held the lock for about 118 s: the timer tick 33.0 s in 601,000 holds, futex 27.7 s in 1,075,000, a take after an idle halt 16.6 s, page faults 13.9 s in 1,960,000, sendmsg 5.7 s, vector 48 3.6 s, mmap 3.2 s, openat 2.8 s, write 2.6 s and poll 2.1 s. No single hold was long, the longest 0.70 s. A hold counts until the processor next returns to user mode or halts, so the tick's and futex's shares include the kernel half of the process they switch to.
* Firefox's decoder threads make about 4,300 futex calls and 7,800 page faults a second. Linux serves them on all processors at once and Rach2 one at a time.
* Venus is limited the same way: part 1 of the dEQP-VK fraction took 534 to 590 s in six runs against the Debian guest's 401 s, with the system time equal to the lock's waits, 2,403 to 2,763 s on 8 processors (76-17a.12).

### The owner's decision

Taking futex, page faults and the timer tick out from under the big lock changes every kernel entry and is work of a milestone's size. On 2026-11-10 at 16:27 UTC+6 the owner decided that the display stack does not wait for it: Firefox's video under full host load is a known limit of this stack, and the lock goes to the next milestone, beside nested virtualization.

The limit is recorded where its contract lives, in [the graphics Readme's Limits](../../tests/graphics/Readme.md#limits). [`tests/graphics/runners/video.limits`](../../tests/graphics/runners/video.limits) names Firefox's six clips, and `judge.py video` prints a named clip's drops over Debian's most as `judge: limit:` without failing the case, in the `video` family and the `browser` family alike. The H.264 clips are named with the VP9 and AV1 ones: they were over Debian's most in their one measurement, by the same cause, and the checkpoint's slice plays Firefox's H.264 clip at 1080p. The clips are named whatever the host's load, since no run measured Firefox on an idle host. A named clip's count of plays, frames shown, media errors and decoder are still judged, and so are Chromium's drops and every other case, so a regression there fails as before.
