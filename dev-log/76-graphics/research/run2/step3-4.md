## How each suite runs

Every run sets `LP_NUM_THREADS` to the guest's CPU count, and every runner that runs tests in parallel uses as many jobs as CPUs, as Mesa's CI and the suites' own runners do by default. Each runner is in `scripts/guest/`.

* dEQP-VK (`run-deqp.sh`, `deqp-run.py`): Mesa's deqp-runner is a Rust program and crates.io is not an allowed source, so a small Python runner does what it does: every K-th case of the caselist from the first (deqp-runner's `--fraction`), groups of 500 consecutive cases, one `deqp-vk --deqp-caselist-file=...` process per group, as many at once as CPUs. A process that dies mid-case marks that case Crash and the rest of its group goes on in a new process; a case silent for 300 s is killed and marked Timeout. Arguments: `--deqp-log-filename=/dev/null --deqp-log-images=disable --deqp-log-shader-sources=disable --deqp-shadercache=disable`, with `MESA_VK_IGNORE_CONFORMANCE_WARNING=1` as Mesa sets. dEQP-VK 1.4.1.1 has no `--deqp-wsi-type` option (it stops with "Failed to parse command line"); its window-system cases are named per platform (`wsi.wayland.*`, `wsi.xcb.*`, `wsi.xlib.*`, about 2,900 each), so both a Weston 14 headless compositor (`WAYLAND_DISPLAY`; Mesa's lavapipe jobs also start Weston) and `Xvfb :35` (`DISPLAY`) run beside it. Each group's wall time is read on a 1 s join loop.
* piglit (`run-piglit.sh`): `piglit run quick RESULTS --test-list LIST -j CPUS` under `xvfb-run -a -s '-screen 0 1280x1024x24'` (Mesa's llvmpipe job runs piglit under Xvfb), every K-th test of `piglit print-cmd --format '{name}' quick` from the first as the test list; piglit's default per-test process isolation; no timeout of its own. piglit's `--test-list` reader cuts each line at `#`, so the 72 of the 44,333 names that hold a `#` (the first met was `spec@arb_uniform_buffer_object@execution@shared-#...`, which piglit read as `...@shared-` and stopped on with a KeyError) cannot be listed; they are left out of every list and counted in `info.txt`. piglit counts subtests in its totals, so its "total" is larger than the number of tests listed.
* vkd3d-proton (`run-vkd3d.sh`): its own `tests/test-runner.sh -j CPUS -o LOGDIR ./d3d12`, one process per test, stress tests left out; the per-test logs give the counts.
* Wine's Direct3D tests (`run-wine.sh`): each unit in its own process, one after another, as winetest runs them, under GE-Proton11-12's Wine in Xvfb, with Proton's prefix made once by `proton run` (which copies DXVK's and vkd3d-proton's DLLs into it). `proton run` starts a program through its `steam.exe`, whose standard output the test's output never reaches, so the units run with `proton runinprefix`, which starts Wine directly but skips Proton's prefix setup and with it the DLL overrides. The overrides Proton's `run` sets (its `proton` script, lines 1458 to 1499) are given by hand, `WINEDLLOVERRIDES="d3d11=n;d3d10core=n;d3d9=n;dxgi=n;d3d12=n;d3d12core=n"`, and `WINEDEBUG=+loaddll` shows `d3d9.dll`, `d3d11.dll`, `dxgi.dll`, `d3d12.dll` and `d3d12core.dll` loaded "native" from the prefix, as under `run`; without the overrides they load "builtin" (WineD3D). `d3d10.dll` and `d3d10_1.dll` are Wine's own over DXVK's `d3d10core.dll`, as Proton leaves them. Each unit is killed after 600 s, dxgi's after 120 s (below). `PROTON_USE_WOW64=1`, `DXVK_LOG_LEVEL=none`, `WINEDEBUG=-all`. The same units run a second time on Wine's own Direct3D (`WINE_D3D=wined3d`, suite name `wined3d`): `WINEDLLOVERRIDES="d3d11=b;d3d10core=b;d3d9=b;dxgi=b;d3d12=b;d3d12core=b"`, and `+loaddll` shows `wined3d.dll`, `opengl32.dll`, `d3d11.dll` and `dxgi.dll` loaded "builtin" for d3d11 (WineD3D on its default OpenGL renderer, over llvmpipe), and Wine's `libvkd3d-1.dll`, `libvkd3d-shader-1.dll` and `d3d12.dll` for d3d12 (over lavapipe). Every summary line a unit prints is summed, since a unit's child processes print their own (d3d11 prints one of 4 checks from a child before its own of 562,594). A unit counts as a crash when it exits with a code other than its failure count, or prints no summary.
* WebGL (`run-webgl.sh`, `webgl-server.py`): the suite's own harness page, `webgl-conformance-tests.html?version=V&run=1&postResults=1`, which runs every page in turn in one iframe and, with `postResults=1`, posts its start and its whole result text to the server; a small Python server serves `sdk/tests`, logs each test page fetched (the run's sign of progress) and keeps the two posts. The harness times a page out after 20 s without a result. A subset is given through the harness's own `include=` filter, one anchored pattern per page. Chromium 156 on Wayland in Weston 14 headless, as step 2 ran it: `--ozone-platform=wayland --no-first-run --no-default-browser-check --password-store=basic --start-fullscreen` and a fresh profile, with `--ignore-gpu-blocklist`, which llvmpipe needs for WebGL (step 2), and four switches Chromium's own WebGL conformance runner uses or that keep a test page from being throttled: `--disable-gpu-process-crash-limit --js-flags=--expose-gc --disable-background-timer-throttling --disable-renderer-backgrounding`. Its GPU process runs without its sandbox here, as step 2 traced.

## Side runs at 8 CPUs: threads, compiler and memory

Run in the same boot after the timed list (`scripts/guest/extras8.sh`, 02:19 to 02:49; `out/step3/8/x-*`), each suite cold, with Mesa's skip lists. From the dEQP-VK run at `LP_NUM_THREADS=0` on, the runner kills a case after 60 s of silence instead of 300 s, since a hung `synchronization.implicit.timeline_semaphore` case otherwise added up to 300 s to the run's wall time (it hung in each of the four 300 s runs: in `x-lp8` it was `write_ssbo_vertex_read_copy_buffer.buffer_16384.2222`). WebGL's wall time is read in 5 s steps by its runner, so the harness's own time is given for it.

| Setting | dEQP-VK every 27th (96,119 after Mesa's skips) | piglit every 5th | WebGL 2.0.1 every 20th (144 pages), harness |
|---|---|---|---|
| `LP_NUM_THREADS=8` | 472.0 s, of which 230 s waiting on one hung case (plateau from 240 s) | 99.3 s (timed list) | 117.1 s |
| `LP_NUM_THREADS=2` | 222.8 s | 97.9 s | 116.3 s |

| `LP_NUM_THREADS=8`, `GALLIVM_PERF=nopt` | 184.6 s | 92.7 s | |

* With as many test processes as CPUs, llvmpipe's and lavapipe's own rasterizer threads buy nothing: piglit and WebGL ran within 3% of one another at 0, 2 and 8 threads, and dEQP-VK reached its hung case at 240 s with 8 threads against 221 to 223 s for the whole run with 0 or 2. ## Step 4: the plan

* Each guest is the timed guest's machine with 8 CPUs and 8 GiB, on its own throwaway overlay of `deb34.qcow2` (`build/cache/run2/step3-4/s4/gI.qcow2`), SSH on port 12500 + I. `scripts/wsl-step4.sh TAG K 8 LP LOAD` starts K at once, waits until all answer, starts the host monitor, and then starts the same load in every guest within a second, so that their phases line up, the hardest case for the host; `scripts/wsl-step4-end.sh TAG K` collects each guest's times and kernel log and removes the overlays.
* The load (`scripts/guest/load.sh`), the same in every guest, cold: dEQP-VK every 100th case after Mesa's skips, piglit `quick` every 20th test, WebGL 2.0.1 every 20th page, one after another. It holds both kinds of suite step 3 found: dEQP-VK and piglit keep every CPU of the guest busy, WebGL keeps one or two busy. vkd3d-proton is left out: its time is one single-threaded test, and its memory would cap the guest count before the CPUs did.
* Threads capped: `LP_NUM_THREADS=0` in every guest, since step 3 found no gain from llvmpipe's threads when the suite already runs a process per CPU; one run at 5 guests with `LP_NUM_THREADS=8` shows what the cap is worth under contention.
* Slowdown: each guest's time for the whole load, and for each suite, over the same time with one guest alone. The guest counts: 1, 2 and 5 as the brief asks, then more until the slowdown passes 2.
* The first one- and two-guest runs (`out/step4/k1-lp0`, `k2-lp0`, 04:26 to 04:35) still ran the `timeline_semaphore` cases; one hung in a guest of the two-guest run (its dEQP-VK took 109.0 s against 66.5 s in the other guest), so both were run again with the skip list in `guest/step4-skips.txt` (`n1-lp0`, `n2-lp0`). The one-guest run's repeat gives the run-to-run spread: 202.5 s for the whole load in `k1-lp0`, 208.5 s in `n1-lp0`, 3% apart.
* Beside WSL's own monitor, Windows' counter of each logical processor's run time, `\Hyper-V Hypervisor Logical Processor(*)\% Total Run Time`, was read every 5 s through PowerShell (`scripts/win/lprun.ps1`, into `out/step4/TAG/lp.txt` on the Mac; nothing written on Windows), since WSL's view cannot show what else runs on the machine. Between runs it read 1 to 4%: nothing else ran.

## Admission limit and LP_NUM_THREADS

* `LP_NUM_THREADS=0` in every test guest. The suites that keep the guest busy already run one process per CPU, and llvmpipe's own threads then buy nothing alone and cost 4 to 5% with 5 guests (step 4). At 8 CPUs alone, piglit and WebGL ran within 3% at 0, 2 and 8 threads, 0 the fastest, and dEQP-VK at 8 threads took 240 s to reach the point where only its hung case was left, against 221 and 223 s for the whole run at 0 and 2. vkd3d-proton's long test builds 512 pipeline state objects on one CPU at 100% with 8 threads allowed, so llvmpipe's rasterizer threads do not help it either (not run at 0).
* The admission limit on the testing PC: guests whose CPUs a suite keeps busy (dEQP-VK, piglit) may hold 32 guest CPUs at once, the PC's thread count: 4 guests of 8 CPUs, where those suites ran 1.62 and 1.82 times as long as alone and the mixed load 1.40 times. At 40 guest CPUs (5 guests) the CPU-bound suites pass two. A guest whose suite keeps 1 or 2 CPUs busy (WebGL, Wine's tests, vkd3d-proton's long test) counts 2 CPUs whatever its size. Guests of 4 CPUs were not run side by side; counting CPUs, 8 of them make the same 32.
* Memory does not bind first: 8 GiB per guest, 16 GiB for vkd3d-proton at 8 jobs, so 4 guests take 32 to 64 GiB of WSL's 125 GiB.
* Etude's admission today reserves memory alone ("processors and disk are shared, not reserved", `tools/etude/Readme.md`, `etude admit`). Holding graphics guests to 32 CPUs needs the runner of 73-16 to claim processors for them, as native collection cases already declare `cpu_millis`: 8,000 for a CPU-bound suite in a guest of 8 CPUs, 2,000 for a light one, against a capacity of 32,000.

## Findings

1. dEQP-VK whole would take 1.7 hours at 8 CPUs and 2.8 at 4 (estimates from a 1-in-27 sample); Mesa's own lavapipe fraction, every 27th case after its skip list (96,119 cases), takes 221 s at 8 CPUs, 378 s at 4 and 600 s at 2. Changes 74-4 and every Vulkan Done on lavapipe: they hold that fraction, kill a case after 60 s of silence, and count the `timeline_semaphore` groups as flakes, since all 11 runs that ran them had one of those cases crash or hang.
2. CPU-bound suites (dEQP-VK, piglit) took 1.62 and 1.82 times as long with 4 guests of 8 CPUs side by side (32 guest CPUs on the PC's 32 threads), 2.00 and 2.25 with 5, 3.46 and 3.70 with 8; the mixed load passed two at 7 guests (2.12). The PC gives these suites the work of 18 to 20 unshared CPUs. Contention changed no result. Sets the admission limit: 32 guest CPUs of CPU-bound graphics suites at once (4 guests of 8), a light suite counted as 2; 73-16's runner must claim processors for them, which etude's admission does not reserve today.
3. `LP_NUM_THREADS=0` costs nothing alone (piglit and WebGL within 3% of 2 and 8 threads, 0 the fastest) and saves 4 to 5% with 5 guests against 8 threads. Sets `LP_NUM_THREADS=0` in every test guest.
4. The WebGL suite does not speed up with CPUs (2.0.1 whole in 2,810 s, 1.0.4 in 473 s at 8 CPUs; within 6% at 2), and one GPU process crash in the whole 2.0.1 run failed 163 pages in 10 minutes; under contention it still slowed 1.65 times at 8 guests with half the machine idle. Changes 73's Done and 73-16's WebGL runner: the whole suite in chunks, each in a new browser, each failed page run once more in a new browser, the chunks in parallel guests of 2 CPUs; Chromium with `--ignore-gpu-blocklist` and its GPU process unsandboxed, stated in the Done.
5. vkd3d-proton's time is one test (about 10 of its 11 minutes; the other 577 tests end in 90 to 95 s), 8 jobs need more than 8 GiB (peak 9.1 GB, out-of-memory kills in 8 GiB), and lavapipe segfaults make its crashes flaky; Wine's Direct3D tests on DXVK stop before their summary in 6 of 13 units at every CPU count. Changes 74-6: vkd3d-proton whole in a 16 GiB guest or at 4 jobs, its crashes matched against the Debian guest as flakes; Wine's tests on Proton's path matched only on where each unit stops, and run whole on WineD3D, where 12 of the 13 units finish and dxgi, which stops making progress, is cut at 120 s (343 s in all, 1,054,450 checks, 9 failures).

Also found, smaller: Xvfb without `-noreset` caused 23 of 50 piglit failures at 4 CPUs (the runners pass it now, as Mesa's CI does); a kept shader cache makes vkd3d-proton 5.3 times and dEQP-VK 2.6 times faster, so Done runs start cold; `GALLIVM_PERF=nopt` saves 16 to 17% on dEQP-VK with the same results.
