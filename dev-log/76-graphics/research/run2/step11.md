Go, for deliverable 2's device assignment on this laptop. Linux 6.12 with QEMU 10.0.13 gave the HD 4400 to a Debian guest through VT-d and vfio-pci with no ROM and no legacy mode; the guest's i915 lit the panel and drew glmark2 at 98.8 percent (GL) and 98.3 percent (GLES) of the host's own score with translation on; the GPU went back to the host's i915 at full speed without a reboot, after a clean power-off and after QEMU was killed under load, by a 1 s FLR; the host logged no DMAR fault and no GPU error in any of three assignments. ## What it changes in the plan

* 79-1 and 79-5, the QEMU options: legacy mode cannot run on this machine (UEFI firmware, no video BIOS in memory, the ROM reads 0 bytes) and is not needed. What works is `-machine q35 -nodefaults -vga none -device vfio-pci,host=0000:00:02.0,bus=pcie.0,addr=02.0,x-igd-opregion=on`, SeaBIOS (which reads `etc/igd-opregion` and `etc/igd-bdsm-size`, writes ASLS and BDSM and reserves 64 MiB), and the guest's kernel booted by QEMU (`-kernel`, `-initrd`), since step 10 found Debian's GRUB resetting under SeaBIOS without a VGA device; the guest's own GRUB with the assigned GPU was not tried. A UEFI guest was not tried either: QEMU's source and SeaBIOS's log show SeaBIOS doing the IGD setup, and Debian's OVMF was not checked for it. 79-5's "QEMU's legacy IGD mode" becomes this; risk 3's fallback of a ROM file from the firmware image is not needed.
* 79-3, VFIO's scope shrinks: QEMU used the BARs, the configuration space (QEMU emulates GGC and BDSM itself), MSI, the OpRegion's device-specific region and the FLR. It did not use the legacy VGA ranges, the ROM or the LPC and host bridge regions; those serve legacy mode only. The OpRegion is optional for the panel (the guest lit it without one) but carries the VBT's ports and panel data, so 79-3 keeps it.
* 79-5 and deliverable 2's device loss: no reboot is needed to take the GPU back. vfio-pci's AF FLR ("ready 1023ms after AF_FLR") and a full driver probe (i915's whole bring-up and mode set, 0.6 s) restored the panel, Crocus, full speed and RC6, even after SIGKILL of the VMM under load. Rach2's Intel driver must do the same: FLR, then its full bring-up and mode set.
* 79's Done ratio and 80-7's comparisons: an assigned GPU runs as the host's GPU with translation on (9b's 15 percent cost), and the VM adds 1 to 2 percent: glmark2 1185 / 1183 against 1199 / 1203 with translation on and 1387 / 1379 off. Name the ratio against the translation-on number, and expect the driver VM to trail Rach2's native path by about 15 percent from VT-d alone.
* 79-2 and 79-3, reserved regions: the GPU is alone on dmar0 and in group 0; its RMRR, 0xab800000-0xaf9fffff, is exactly its 2 MiB GTT and 64 MiB of stolen memory, reported `direct-relaxable`, and VFIO mapped guest memory over those addresses without complaint (the pc run, 3 GiB below 4 GiB). Rach2 should treat the graphics RMRR as Linux does: identity-mapped while its own driver owns the GPU, dropped when the GPU goes to a VM, and not reported to QEMU as a hole. The USB controllers carry the other RMRR, which matters only if they are ever assigned.
* 79-1's premise is wrong for Debian: its text says Linux by default remaps interrupts but not DMA and has no groups. That was the first run's Alpine kernel. Debian 13's 6.12 translates DMA by default (12 groups, the GPU alone in group 0, i915 "VT-d active for gfx access"), with or without `intel_iommu=on`; `intel_iommu=off` removes the groups (9b).
* 80-4: Debian 13's kernel is built without `CONFIG_VFIO_NOIOMMU`, so the VM's backend cannot take its interrupts through vfio-pci in no-IOMMU mode on Debian's own kernel. 80-4 should use 80-5's small kernel module, or give the VM QEMU's emulated intel-iommu so that ordinary vfio-pci works inside it.
* 75-1: Debian loads `iTCO_wdt` by itself (Lynx Point LP TCO, version 2, I/O 0x1860, `nowayout=0`), and the TCO timer stops on a magic close. The safety net's design holds; its expiry test is still 75-1's.
