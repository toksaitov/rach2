## What risk 1 and decisions B and C should say

Risk 1 (Crysis may miss about 24 FPS even under Linux). Cleared under Linux, on WineD3D's GL renderer. On Debian, the island flyby at Low 1366x768 averages 44 FPS (42.7 in its first loop, minimum 30), at Low 800x600 61 FPS, and at the game's own choice, Medium 1024x768, about 30 FPS, which the owner found playable at the machine. The target of 24 FPS is met with margin. Four things for the game work:

* WineD3D on Vulkan is not a route on this machine: it crashes in WineD3D's SPIR-V shader backend as the timedemo starts, with constant updates "Not implemented" beforehand, before anything reaches hasvk. The game's renderer on the Lenovo is WineD3D on GL, and a WineD3D-on-Vulkan comparison has only that crash as its Linux reference.
* The game hides its HUD for good at the suit's reboot on the first level when it has no sound output (7 of 9 tries here, and the owner's game), a race in its own level script; with a working sound device, silent or not, the HUD comes back. So "at least the first level" needs Rach2's audio path to give Wine a working output and the game `s_SoundEnable = 1`; a run with sound off is not a fair test of the HUD.
* Under Weston 14's Xwayland the game's fullscreen window is framed at +32+32 (or cropped under the kiosk shell), so these numbers were taken under cage, which puts it at 0,0. The desktop's compositor must place a fullscreen X client at the origin, or the game's plan names cage.
* First-use hitches are real and small: Mesa's shader cache takes two thirds of them away, and a second pass through the same place is smooth. Mesa's on-disk cache must work on Rach2 for the same result.

Decision B (Chromium's GPU video decoding on the HD 4400). Option 1, as recommended, now measured on the pinned Debian. Chromium 156 and Chrome 157 select VA-API, fail at the first frame and decode on the CPU, with 0 of 1,738 H.264 and 0 of 1,740 VP9 frames dropped at 1080p30 over 58 s, at 4.7 to 4.8 W. Firefox decodes H.264 on the GPU and drops 62 of 1,727 frames, and drops 52 of 1,728 VP9 frames that it decodes on the CPU. So its drops are not the GPU decoder's: it drops as many in software, and its `requestAnimationFrame` runs at 44 Hz. Their cause was not traced. Rach2's acceptance compares Firefox against these Debian numbers, not against zero. For option 2, the fault is now located at one call: Chromium exports a VA surface before decoding into it, and i965 refuses to export a surface that has no buffer yet (`i965_ExportSurfaceHandle`, "invalid VASurfaceID"), which is the case for a surface created without a pixel format. A fix is either i965 allocating the buffer at export or Chromium creating its surfaces with a format; which Chromium call makes that surface was not read. Chromium's `chrome://gpu` says "Video Decode: Hardware accelerated" throughout, so acceptance must read the decoder from Chromium's media log, as 73-17 and 75-14 already say.

Decision C (WebGPU for users on the Lenovo). Option 1, test-only switches, with the switch now measured: `--ignore-gpu-blocklist` alone gives WebGPU on the GPU (Dawn's Intel gen-7 adapter on hasvk), `--enable-unsafe-webgpu` alone gives only SwiftShader on the CPU, and no switch gives no adapter. So the test launcher passes `--ignore-gpu-blocklist`, and the two together give the fastest compute. A WebGPU picture was shown on the panel at 59.9 FPS. Firefox ESR 153 offers only an unnamed fallback adapter here, so WebGPU on the GPU is Chromium's alone. On the sandbox (risk 10): Debian's Chromium and Google's Chrome run their GPU process unsandboxed on Debian itself in every configuration, with the same thread warning as on the night of 2026-11-04, and `--gpu-sandbox-start-early` sandboxes it at the cost of the GPU (the process cannot open `libEGL.so.1` and Chromium falls back to software). So 73-17's "sandboxes on" can only mean what Linux gives, an unsandboxed GPU process with that warning, and the first switch it names to try fails on Linux too. The renderer's sandbox state was not read.
