# The Lenovo under Linux: the night of 2026-11-04

The Lenovo G50-70 ran Alpine Linux from a one-time boot entry between 04:37 and 05:15 (UTC+6), driven over SSH from the Mac with its screen dimmed and its sound driver not loaded. Every path the plan counts on for the Lenovo works under Linux: OpenGL 4.6 and GLES 3.2 on Crocus, Vulkan on hasvk (1.2, or 1.3 with one driver option), WebGL 1 and 2 in both browsers, WebGPU in Chromium behind one switch, VA-API decoding of H.264 in Firefox and ffmpeg, mode setting through legacy and atomic KMS, and Vulkan straight to the display. Chromium's VA-API decoding fails on Haswell's i965 driver and falls back to the CPU. Direct3D 11 runs through WineD3D on OpenGL and on Vulkan; DXVK 2.7.1 refuses the GPU, since it needs Vulkan 1.3 with 64-bit shader integers (shaderInt64), which hasvk does not offer. Venus works over hasvk through virglrenderer's vtest server. VT-x is on and locked by the firmware, and KVM boots a Linux kernel to its first process in 1.1 s against 8.8 s under TCG. The chipset's watchdog is there. Raw outputs are in the Mac's `build/tmp/gfxref-lenovo/out/` (`facts/`, `runs/`, `extra/`, `dxvk/`, `venus-kvm/`); the scripts that made them are in `build/tmp/gfxref-lenovo/probe/`.

## How it ran

* The method of milestone 70: the Alpine 3.24.3 netboot kernel and initramfs (sha256 checked) copied with GRUB's `linux.mod` onto the Lenovo's boot partition, a `boot.cfg` block keyed on `next_entry=recovery` that clears the key before booting Linux, and `bootctl trial` to arm it; the modloop and the SSH key served from the Mac. The original partition image was saved first (sha256 4dc8e9d9...); after the session, back in Rach2, its 21 changed runs (41 MiB) were written back at 05:18 and the partition read back equal to it, with `bootctl status` showing the candidate saved, no next entry and the candidate booted.
* Alpine 3.24.3, Linux 6.18.58-0-lts, Mesa 26.1.6, Chromium 156.0.8078.79, Firefox 151.0.3, libva 2.23 with i965 2.4.1, Vulkan loader 1.4.347, wlroots 0.20.2 and cage 0.3.0 as the compositor, seatd. Packages from Alpine's own mirror; Debian's and Fedora's DXVK packages from their mirrors, each checked against its repository's index.
* Kernel command line: `ip=dhcp ... modprobe.blacklist=ath9k,snd_hda_intel panic=10`; the backlight at 47 of 937. Two things the netboot system needed: the loopback interface brought up by hand (Chromium's debugging port and the local page server bind to 127.0.0.1), and `WLR_LIBINPUT_NO_DEVICES=1` for cage, since the initramfs runs mdev and libinput finds no devices without udev's database.
* Browsers ran as an ordinary user under cage, each with a fresh profile, loading one local page (`probe/probe.html`) that measures WebGL 1 and 2, WebGPU, the media capabilities and the frame rate, and posts its results back; Chromium's own report came over its debugging port (SystemInfo.getInfo and the text of chrome://gpu, through `probe/cdp.py`). The same page serves the QEMU guest, so the numbers compare.

## The machine

* Lenovo G50-70 (product 20351, board Lancer 5A2), firmware 9ACN32WW of 2015-07-20; Core i3-4030U, 4 threads; 16 GB.
* GPU: Haswell-ULT GT2, HD Graphics 4400, `8086:0a16` rev 0b, subsystem `17aa:380c`; graphics version 7; 2 subslices, 20 EUs; clocks 200 MHz (RPn and RP1) to 1000 MHz (RP0 and boost), efficient 500 MHz; CS timestamp 12.5 MHz.
* i915 is `card1` (simpledrm took minor 0 first and was replaced), render node `renderD128`, debugfs `dri/128`. Capabilities: LLC, RC6, RPS, runtime PM, engine reset, L3 parity; aliasing PPGTT (type 1, 31 bits); no 64-bit relocations; legacy ring submission (no logical ring contexts); swizzling bit 9/10 for X tiles and bit 9 for Y tiles; DMA mask 40 bits.
* Display: eDP-1 connected, one mode, 1366x768 at 60.016 Hz (76.3 MHz pixel clock), 340x190 mm, 6 bits per colour (pipe bpp 18), DisplayPort link of 1 lane at 2.7 Gb/s, pipe A, no scalers; CDCLK 450 MHz; panel power up 200 ms, down 50 ms, cycle 500 ms, backlight on 1 ms and off 200 ms (`eDP-1-i915_panel_timings`). Connectors HDMI-A-1, DP-1 and HDMI-A-2 present and disconnected. PSR status unreadable (no device). The 6 KiB VBT and 8 KiB OpRegion are saved raw (`facts/debugfs/i915_vbt`, `i915_opregion`); no decoder was available (IGT's tools and edid-decode are not packaged in Alpine 3.24); the EDID is saved as hex in `facts/connectors.txt`.
* RC6: 9960 ms of 10 s idle in RC6. During WebGL the GPU reached 1000 MHz; the package drew about 4 W with Chromium and 7 W with Firefox (RAPL, `runs/*.samples`).
* Input: AT keyboard and Synaptics touchpad both on PS/2 (i8042), a USB Logitech keyboard and mouse, lid and power buttons, Ideapad hotkeys. No I2C HID.
* VT-x: `IA32_FEATURE_CONTROL` = 0x5, locked with VMX outside SMX enabled; `kvm_intel` loads with EPT, unrestricted guest and nesting, without APICv, and `/dev/kvm` appears. QEMU 10 booting Alpine's own kernel and initramfs (2 CPUs, 512 MiB) reached its first process at 1.11 s under KVM (`-cpu host`) and at 8.84 s under TCG (`-cpu max`); wall times 1.78 s and 11.81 s (`venus-kvm/venus-kvm.log`).
* Watchdog: iTCO_wdt finds a Lynx Point LP TCO, version 2, heartbeat 30 s, nowayout 0, inactive. Not armed tonight.
* IOMMU: the DMAR table has two units and two RMRRs (0xaae11000-0xaae30fff and 0xab800000-0xaf9fffff); interrupt remapping is on, but DMA translation is off in Alpine's kernel by default, so there are no IOMMU groups.

## OpenGL, Vulkan and VA-API without a browser

* EGL 1.5 on GBM, driver `crocus`: OpenGL 4.6 core and compatibility, GLSL 4.60, OpenGL ES 3.2 (`facts/eglinfo-*.txt`).
* Vulkan: hasvk, "Intel open-source Mesa driver", apiVersion 1.2.354, conformance 0.0.0.0, with the warning "Haswell Vulkan support is incomplete". With the driver option `hasvk_report_vk_1_3_version=true` (environment or driconf) it reports 1.3.354. It offers VK_KHR_external_memory_fd, VK_EXT_external_memory_dma_buf, VK_EXT_image_drm_format_modifier, VK_EXT_queue_family_foreign, external semaphore and fence fds, timeline semaphores, synchronization2, dynamic rendering, robustness2, transform feedback, extended dynamic state, depth clip enable, maintenance4 and 5, swapchain; not custom border colour, graphics pipeline library, buffer device address or shaderFloat64 (`extra/hasvk-13-full.txt`).
* kmscube: 600 frames at 60 Hz through legacy KMS and through atomic KMS. vkcube: 600 frames in 10.15 s straight to the display (VK_KHR_display) and in 10.00 s under cage (Wayland).
* The Wayland compositor offers dma-bufs with LINEAR, X-tiled and Y-tiled modifiers for XRGB, ARGB, the 10-bit and 16-bit formats and NV12 (`extra/wayland-info.txt`).
* VA-API: i965 2.4.1 loads (iHD does not): decoding MPEG-2, H.264 (constrained baseline, main, high, multiview, stereo), VC-1, JPEG; encoding MPEG-2 and H.264; video processing. No VP8, VP9, HEVC or AV1 in the hardware.
* ffmpeg 1080p H.264 (testsrc2, 600 frames): VA-API 0.82 s (730 frames/s, 0.9 s of CPU), software on one thread 5.06 s (119 frames/s), on four threads 2.41 s (249 frames/s); 720p: 0.52 s, 2.40 s, 1.21 s. The first 60 frames decoded by VA-API and by software have equal MD5s.

* WebGL 1 and 2 draw correctly (pixels read back) in every run; 1024x1024 canvases; the frame rate is 60 Hz in Chromium (Firefox measured 49 Hz in its first two seconds).
* Chromium's default report: canvas, compositing, rasterization, OpenGL, WebGL and video decode "Hardware accelerated", video encode software only, Vulkan disabled, WebGPU "Hardware accelerated" yet `requestAdapter()` returns null: Chromium lists both Dawn adapters (OpenGL ES compatibility mode and Vulkan) as available, but offers them to pages only with `--enable-unsafe-webgpu`. Problems listed: video encode disabled; WebGPU on Vulkan through GL interop disabled (crbug 442791440, 475935650); `msaa_is_slow` on Intel. The GPU process ran unsandboxed under this setup ("Sandboxed: false"); its cause was not looked into tonight.
* Chromium video (1080p H.264, 12 s): it selects VaapiVideoDecoder, which fails at the first frame (`vaExportSurfaceHandle failed, VA error: invalid VASurfaceID`), then falls back to FFmpegVideoDecoder; 0 of 360 frames dropped on the CPU. The same with the copying GL path and with the driver checks ignored. Chromium's media capability report still calls H.264 "power efficient".
* Firefox video: VA-API through its FFmpeg decoder in the RDD process ("VA-API FFmpeg init successful", VA-API frames logged); 23 of 340 frames dropped in the first 12 s. Firefox reports HEVC as supported and power efficient, which Haswell's decoder cannot do; not checked further.

## Direct3D through Wine

* The program (`probe/d3d11probe.c`, built with Alpine's mingw-w64 GCC 15.2) creates a Direct3D 11 device on the hardware driver, names the adapter, then 1000 times clears a 64x64 render target to green, copies it to a staging texture and maps it, and prints the last pixel and the time. Wine 11.0 from Alpine, a fresh prefix, run under cage (Wine's Wayland driver).
* WineD3D on OpenGL (Crocus): feature level 11_0, adapter "Intel(R) HD Graphics 4000" (Wine's table names the nearest card it knows, device 0x0162), pixel 0 255 0 255, 1000 round trips in 182.3 ms.
* WineD3D on Vulkan (`WINE_D3D_CONFIG=renderer=vulkan`, hasvk at 1.2): feature level 11_0, adapter "Intel(R) HD Graphics 4400 (HSW GT2)" 0x0a16, pixel correct, 1000 round trips in 26.6 ms.
* DXVK: Debian's `dxvk-wine64` 2.6 is a winelib build (`.dll.so` against glibc's Wine) that musl's Wine cannot load, so DXVK 2.7.1's PE DLLs came from Fedora 45's `wine-dxvk` and `wine-dxvk-dxgi`, each checked against the release's repository metadata. Fedora marks both DLLs "Wine builtin DLL" in their DOS stub, so Wine refused them as native (`d3d11.dll ... not found`) until that mark was cleared in copies of them.
* DXVK 2.7.1 then loads and finds the GPU, but takes no adapter: with hasvk at 1.2, "Device does not support Vulkan 1.3"; with hasvk reporting 1.3, "Device does not support required feature 'shaderInt64'". `D3D11CreateDevice` fails with 0x80004005 (`dxvk/dxvk-12p`, `dxvk-13p`). hasvk does not offer shaderInt64, so DXVK 2.7.1 does not run on this GPU with today's Mesa; other 2.x releases were not tried. The DXVK 1.10 series, which needs only Vulkan 1.1, was not tried tonight: its builds are on GitHub, and no distribution's old package of it was looked for.

## Venus over hasvk

* Alpine's virglrenderer 1.11.0 with `virgl_test_server --venus` on hasvk, and Mesa's Venus driver as the client over the vtest socket (`VN_DEBUG=vtest`): vulkaninfo sees "Virtio-GPU Venus (Intel(R) HD Graphics 4400 (HSW GT2))", an integrated GPU, driver ID MESA_VENUS, Vulkan 1.2.354 (1.3.354 with hasvk's 1.3 option), 113 device extensions among them swapchain, external memory fd and dma-buf, DRM format modifiers, timeline semaphores, synchronization2, dynamic rendering, robustness2, transform feedback and maintenance4, and a 1.5 GiB device-local heap (`venus-kvm/venus-*-full.txt`). The vtest socket copies commands through the host and maps memory through shared files, so it proves the Venus renderer on hasvk, not virtio-gpu's blob path.
* vkcube on Wayland under cage, 300 frames at the panel's 60 Hz: 5.07 s through Venus over vtest, 4.99 s on hasvk directly, the same with only hasvk's ICD listed (`venus-kvm/cube3-*.log`). A first try with the arguments in another order (`--wsi wayland --c 600`) ran to its 60 s limit through Venus and directly alike; it was not looked into.

## What it means for the plan

* 77-2 (the reference session) has its first half: vulkaninfo for hasvk, VT-x, the modes, EDID, VBT and OpRegion bytes, debugfs and the Linux numbers above. Still for 77-2: Debian with the pinned Mesa, glmark2, piglit, IGT's tools (register dumps, VBT decode), Crysis.
* Risk 2 (VT-x off in the firmware) is gone: VT-x is enabled and locked on, and KVM runs a guest. The sitting at the end needs no VT-x step. Milestone 78's acceptance can compare Rach2's KVM against these Linux numbers on the same machine: 1.11 s to the first process under KVM, 8.84 s under TCG.
* 75-1: the iTCO watchdog exists and is free (nowayout 0, inactive), so the safety net can use it.
* Decision 8 and "What the plan leaves out": DXVK 2.x cannot run on the HD 4400 (no shaderInt64, even with hasvk reporting 1.3), so Direct3D 9 to 11 on the Lenovo means WineD3D, whose Vulkan backend did the test's 1000 round trips 7 times faster than its OpenGL backend (26.6 ms against 182.3 ms), or DXVK 1.10, untried. Crysis on the Lenovo therefore runs through WineD3D or DXVK 1.10, and vkd3d-proton (Direct3D 12), which asks more of Vulkan than DXVK does, is out on this GPU too. WebGPU on the Lenovo works in Chromium with `--enable-unsafe-webgpu` on hasvk (compute and render read back correctly), so it is no longer left out for lack of an API.
* Risk 5 (Venus on the Lenovo needs hasvk's shared images): hasvk offers every extension Venus asks of its host (external memory fd and dma-buf, DRM format modifiers, foreign queue, sync fds), and virglrenderer's Venus renderer runs on it (above). What is left is virtio-gpu's blob memory mapped into a guest, which only a VM on the Lenovo can show.
* 75-14 and 73-17: on Haswell, Firefox decodes H.264 through VA-API and Chromium does not (its export of VA surfaces fails on i965), so the browser acceptance for GPU video names Firefox, and Chromium's GPU decoding is either a recorded limitation or work on the zero-copy path. Both browsers decode VP9 and AV1 on the CPU; 1080p30 H.264 plays without drops on the CPU in Chromium.
* 75-5 and 75-11 get their ioctl and parameter lists from the tables above instead of from reading Mesa.
* 75-9 and 75-10: the panel's timings, link and clocks above; the scanout tiling list (linear, X, Y) from the compositor.
* 73-10: the touchpad is PS/2, so no I2C HID driver is needed for the Lenovo.
* 79-1: DMA translation is off by default; its session boots with `intel_iommu=on` and reads the groups then.
* The plan's tests can use this page and `probe/cdp.py` for browser numbers; glmark2 and IGT's tools are missing from Alpine 3.24, which is one more reason the reference stays on Debian.
