# Milestone 38: exFAT

The kernel reads and writes exFAT, the file system every flash drive above 32 GB and every SDXC card is sold with, on the same buffer cache and under the same write rules milestone 37 fixed for FAT32. A volume another implementation formatted and filled mounts and reads; a volume this kernel writes passes the host's own reader and the external checker; a machine killed inside a write sequence leaves a volume the next mount checks and repairs. Nothing touched the Lenovo.

## What was built

`sys/fs/fatcommon.c` is what the two FAT drivers both need and neither owns: the UTF-8 to UTF-16 name check and conversion, the UTF-8 rendering, the DOS timestamp bit fields, the mount options, the first-fit allocation hint, and the dirty-volume walk's bitmap with its lock. The bodies came from `fat32.c` and `fat32name.c` unchanged, and the FAT32 tests passing unchanged is what says nothing moved wrongly.

`sys/fs/exfat.c` is the `exfat` type, 3745 lines, written from Microsoft's published specification. It reads: both boot regions and their checksums before any field is trusted, with the two-region rule (main bad and backup good reads the backup's fields with the main's flags, read-only; a disagreement or a bad backup is read-only; both bad is refused); the root's allocation bitmap and up-case table, each through its FAT chain; the up-case table in its three forms, held when its stored form is at most 16 KiB, indexed when it is 128 KiB of explicit units, and streamed otherwise, with its checksum, its runs and its mandatory first 128 mappings checked; entry sets with every invariant the specification gives, passed over as one unit with a record when one fails; names through the volume's own table, hashed and compared up-cased; chains with and without `NoFatChain`, bytes past `ValidDataLength` as zeros; and timestamps with their 10 ms field and their UTC offset.

It writes under FAT32's rules with the bitmap in the FAT's place: a cluster's bit set and its chain written before any entry names it, data synced before a length is published, a set unpublished before its clusters are freed, and a held node's clusters freed at its last put. A set is made or unmade by the in-use bit of its file entry, written last with the checksum over the whole; every set this driver makes has its file and stream entries in one sector, so a length, time or flag change is one sector write, and a set another writer straddled takes two, the stream's sector first. A contiguous file or directory has its run written into the FAT before it grows. `VolumeDirty` is set when a writable mount is constructed and cleared last by the clean unmount; the backup boot region is never written. A directory holding a critical entry this driver does not know is frozen: every change inside it answers `EROFS` and the rest of the volume is unaffected.

A volume whose dirty bit is set is walked before the mount is published, under the bitmap the two drivers share: the system objects seeded, every set checked as the reader checks it, the chains marked against the volume's bitmap both ways. Three states this driver's own order leaves are repaired after the whole walk (a set failing only its checksum, orphan secondaries, a surplus tail); a cluster allocated that nothing reaches is counted and left, since its bytes may be the only copy of a file a rename had unpublished; anything else mounts read-only with the fault named.

`tests/host/mkexfatfixture.c` is the independent implementation: a builder that writes both boot regions with their checksums, the bitmap, the recommended up-case table, a label, a GUID entry, vendor entries and a tree of every shape a reader must take, with the variants each breaking one rule or naming a geometry; and a reader, `--check`, that walks a volume as `fsck_exfat` does and names every fault, with `--list`, `--read` and `--dump`. `tests/run exfat` is the suite.

## What running it found

The rows found, before any review: the recommended up-case table ends with a `0xFFFF` that maps U+FFFF to itself and is not a run marker; a read of a damaged chain gives the sound bytes and then `EIO`; a cross-directory rename computed its new set's checksum over the inactive file entry it wrote first; a write through an unlinked file's description republished its retired set; the filler that keeps a set's two entries in one sector skipped the slot when it held the directory's end marker, which BusyBox's `mv` found; `free_slots` counted free slots past the directory's last allocated cluster, which two hundred files made and remade found; the walk re-read a parent's sets after a subdirectory and marked its chain twice; and the root's chain, which no entry gives a length, was taken for a surplus tail. Each is in `notes.md` with the row that found it.

## What the review found

Six review units read the milestone on Codex, two after phase 3 and four before the sweep, and returned fifty-six findings; every one is fixed or recorded with its reason in `notes.md`. The serious ones: a mount whose fields were validated from the main boot sector before the backup was considered, and a fallback that copied five of them; system objects read as contiguous extents rather than through their FAT chains; a set that could swallow the next file's entries, whose secondaries the repair then wrote inactive; a repair inside a contiguous directory that followed a FAT the directory does not have; two sleeping constructors that could take one mount slot; a rejected set passed over one entry at a time, so an entry inside it could be read as a primary; a vendor allocation's clusters left allocated at unlink and its entry dropped by rename; `ENOSPC` decided after the FAT of a contiguous file had been written; a percentage that was not one; and an odd second lost from a modification time. Ten rows and eight mutations came with the fixes.

## Verification

`tests/run host` runs `exfatcheck_hosttest` over forty-five rows of the builder's volumes, `exfat_hosttest` over the driver, and `fatname_hosttest` over the shared name rules. The driver's rows read every shape the builder writes, in each of the up-case table's three forms; refuse or repair each malformed variant by name; write ten sequences and stop each after every one of its writes, walking the image and reading it back at each of 137 stops with the fixture's own files intact; make and remake two hundred files twice over; and mount each state a crash leaves under the dirty bit, three repaired, a lost chain counted with its bytes intact, nine faults read-only with the image untouched, with a matrix over the check's own repair writes. The rows that phase 3 would not pass without are there too: a volume `newfs_exfat` made and this host's driver filled mounts and lists every file of its manifest with the right bytes and times. `tests/run exfat` is six boots: a program writes a tree and unmounts it, the host's reader and `fsck_exfat` judge the volume, the next boot reads every byte back, a boot killed mid-sequence is followed by the mount's check and repair, BusyBox writes through the shell, and two writers and a sync run under the lag device. Forty-six mutations, each from a restoring script that rebuilt before the verdict, were run: forty-four caught by a named row, and two that no row can tell apart, the order of the two writes of a straddling set under a 4 KiB block cache and a 32-bit narrowing that only a file at the 4 GiB limit reaches; both are recorded in `notes.md`.

A passing run proves that this driver reads what other implementations write and writes what they read, that its order of writes holds at every sector boundary one process can stop at, and that the check repairs what that order leaves and refuses what it does not. It does not prove a torn sector, a volume from Windows, a camera or a phone, or a 4 KiB logical sector, which is refused by name; those are milestone 40's, with the USB disk. The full set ran on the final tree in 9 minutes 6 seconds, all 64 suites, against 9 minutes 0 seconds and 63 suites at the close of milestone 37, and `make test` in 4 minutes 25 seconds against 4 minutes 17 seconds.

`exfat.c` is 3745 lines and `fatcommon.c` 442, against 2900 for `fat32.c`, which lost 321 lines to the move and gained the review round's fixes.
