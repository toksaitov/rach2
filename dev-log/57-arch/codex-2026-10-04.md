# The Codex review of 2026-10-04

Milestone 57's changes, `git diff 5349d3df..HEAD`, were reviewed by Codex on 2026-10-04, one Astra unit at a time at xhigh effort. The diff was cut into 146 units of at most six files, each read for at most ten minutes; unit 18, which ran out its ten minutes, was read again as units 145 and 146. Each unit was asked for wrong behavior, races between processors, overflow and truncation, wrong use of the i386 or x86-64 interface, a contract the prose states and the code breaks, and a test that cannot fail. Thirty-eight units found nothing.

Every finding was read against the code before it was acted on. A fix came with a test that failed before it: a host test row, a probe row or a suite row, made to fail by a fault compiled into what it runs and passing again with the source restored, as AGENTS.md requires. Host tests were proved on the Mac; probes and suites on the testing host, in trials that ran each fix, its fault and its restored source on both architectures where the code is shared. Where no test can be trusted to see a fault, a race whose window is a few instructions or bytes of a kernel stack that differ from run to run, the bullet says so. The findings of units 17 and 46 are left open for the owner.

## Processors, clocks and interrupts

* The debug shell's preemption threads began with the big lock's depth at 0, so the tick that should have preempted one could not take the lock again (unit 1). Every primed kernel thread now begins holding the lock once, and the smp suite runs the test on two processors (`1bc40295`).
* On i686 an NMI or a machine check went through the locked interrupt router, where a processor holding the big lock would wait on itself (unit 5). Both are taken without the lock, stop the machine in any mode, and only the first processor's panic is printed; the smp suite has an NMI row on two processors (`261096d5`). An NMI that came while a processor held the console's lock, or between the panic's claim and its record, could keep the panic from being written (units 11 and 43): a panic is begun before the paranoid handlers write, the panicking processor writes without the console's lock, and the claim takes the processor's number in one compare-and-exchange (`57deadf4`, `5884309d`). The last is a race of a few instructions and has no test.
* The other processors read the place within a tick from their own counters, out of step with the boot processor's, and a sleep could end up to a tick early (units 8 and 13). They read the reference counter's edge, under a sequence count, and smpmusl begins each sleep at another place in the tick; the trial failed three boots of three under the fault and passed them fixed (`d05e19a3`, `d87b49e1`). Another processor's microsecond clock could run back across a tick (unit 42); it is held short of the period the boot processor has not counted and never reads earlier than a reading already given, which `rtc_hosttest` checks with two faults (`332cd83b`).
* The spurious register kept end-of-interrupt suppression set (unit 12), and every processor's spurious vectors were counted in one word (unit 11) (`2310186a`, `27103867`).
* The machine's stop ran with interrupts on, so the SCI that entering ACPI mode raises could keep it from writing SLP_EN, and the keyboard's interrupt could deliver one byte twice (unit 14) (`1c3d37aa`, `5ee409b2`). Both are races the shutdown and keyboard suites cover without being able to force.
* The HPET's comparators were left armed, a stopped HPET hung every wait on it, and the waits rounded down (unit 15). The comparators are quieted, a counter that stands still is refused, and the waits round up with a bound; `refclock_hosttest`, new, fails under five faults (`8ae5144e`).
* A stop's panic output raced a processor the stop had not yet reached (unit 34), and a processor that started after the boot's wait ran uncounted (unit 48). The stop waits a second at most for each processor, and a starting processor is admitted or refused in one compare-and-exchange; `smp_hosttest` has a row for each and `smp_mutate.sh` a fault (`50024686`, `ad969434`).
* An x86-64 thread's FS and GS bases went stale when a program loaded a selector, since the switch saved them from the record and not the MSRs (unit 16). They are saved from the MSRs when the thread switches away; `fsbaseprobe`, new, fails under the old switch (`348f2e60`).
* A core dump copied the registers of sibling threads while they ran on other processors (unit 44). The dumping process's other threads are parked, their state saved, until the core is written; `coredump_hosttest` has three faults and coremusl a two-thread row whose registers fail without the park (`95ff1480`, `ce176b01`).
* A robust futex was released, and a futex word read, a byte at a time (unit 45): one load reads the word and a compare-and-exchange releases it, and `futex_hosttest`'s raced row fails without the retry (`4735b33c`). `strncpy_from_user` read each byte twice, so a store from another processor could leave the copy without its NUL (unit 40) (`315d2c4d`), and a user page was mapped before it was zeroed or copied (unit 39) (`26d0d1ef`). The two are races with no reliable test.
* A new POSIX timer was listed before its id was stored (unit 32) (`d2025b66`); a race, with no test.
* /proc/uptime summed the processors' idle ticks in 32 bits, which 64 processors wrap in under eight days (unit 142). The sums are 64 bits; `procfs_hosttest` has a row past 32 bits that fails when the sum is cut (`edbaefca`).

## Memory and the system calls at x86-64's width

* `smaps`, `mm_resident` and the walks of unmap, protect and move went page by page over a region of terabytes under the big lock (units 29 and 38). They step past the page tables a region lacks, and count in longs; `procprobe` and mapmusl have rows over 64 TiB, and mapmusl's three faults each fail (`4a2a568a`, `ac927a24`, `13993770`).
* mmap's bound underflowed on x86-64, and a shared mapping's page offset was cut past 2^32 pages (units 39 and 41). The offset and length are bounded at a long's width, and at 32 bits for a file or shared memory, as Linux does; mapmusl has EOVERFLOW rows (`f5838973`). A truncation's cut counted pages and its offset in 32 bits (unit 37) (`42a85da5`). A refused frame was not cleared on a refused instruction fetch (unit 39) (`f7ecb36f`).
* Values cut to 32 bits on x86-64: the clocks' seconds and a sleep's bound (unit 49, `f78d1217`); statx's times (unit 51, `0ecd58d2`); `/proc/PID/mem` offsets and seeks, and a writable `/proc` file read at 4 GiB (unit 51, `34136834`); execve's argument pointers, where one with a zero low word ended the vector (unit 53, `63f1d09c`); the id getters, which returned an id past 2^31 as an error (unit 53, `77348cf8`); a pty's TCFLSH selector (unit 54, `070f3a72`); unshare's flags (unit 45, `dc1b1780`); msgrcv's capacity and semop's count (unit 33, `5f1a8851`); recvmmsg's and sendmmsg's count and socket's domain (unit 52, `0f564481`); and a ppoll or pselect6 timeout, narrowed before it was clamped (unit 31, `d94b236a`). Each has a probe row that fails under the old narrowing.
* A read, write or socket transfer over 2 GiB returned a negative count (unit 32); counts are capped at Linux's MAX_RW_COUNT (`25135dd2`). A control message's padding was counted past the receiver's buffer (unit 33, `31eeace6`).
* The x86-64 semid64_ds lacked Linux's reserved words (unit 20, `2559fd05`); an rt signal frame's XSAVE image was not marked UC_FP_XSTATE (unit 5, `4b0aba10`); a queued siginfo lost its errno and the alternate stack's flags were saved wrongly (unit 19, `33367109`); and seccomp's strict mode allowed i386's numbers on x86-64 (unit 47, `71ed27dd`). Each has a probe row.
* HDIO_GETGEO's and sigaltstack's records gave their padding from the kernel's stack (units 28 and 53) (`3db9d575`, `fdc0f258`). What the padding holds differs from run to run, so neither has a test.
* /proc/stat's cpu line summed the processors' ticks in 32 bits, and two cpuN directories could share an inode (units 29 and 30) (`3bb13e0d`, `857623ca`).
* A core's notes could wrap their 32-bit size (unit 44, `80eebbae`); it takes millions of threads, so it has no test.
* A shmget of a size tmpfs refuses put the node back before the open file had it, so the open file's release found no node and the open was never given back (unit 139). The open file takes the node once it is opened and gives both back; ipcmusl makes a thousand such calls with the heap's `HeapUsed` unchanged, and fails under the old order (`81f79bef`).
* A data ping's size was cut to 32 bits before its check (unit 136): pingtest's fourth test sends one of 2^32 + 1 bytes, which the old code sent as one byte (`df570d5f`).
* The x86-64 boot carved its page tables at the break and stopped the machine at the first page the memory map does not offer, though usable pages followed (unit 145, `5c87ab82`); QEMU's maps put no hole there, so it has no test. Without 1 GiB pages each GiB of the direct map takes a page directory in the boot's window below 32 MiB, which a machine of terabytes would fill (unit 145). The direct map stops at 64 GiB on such a processor, and the memory past it is counted unreachable; bigmem boots 72 GiB with 1 GiB pages off and requires 9 GiB unreachable, which a kernel reaching 64 TiB there fails (`b424a1bb`).
* PTRACE_GETREGSET used the tracee it had looked up before the copy of its iovec, which may sleep while the tracee ends (unit 137, `741e3b2d`); a race, with no test. A tracer's write of a data segment selector succeeded without being kept, since the x86-64 kernel keeps none for a thread (unit 146). Only the null selector is taken, and ptracemusl's write of 0x2b must fail with EIO (`7e95db99`).

## Found while proving the fixes

The trials that proved the fixes ran the suites they touch, and these failures came from them, not from the review.

* The debug shell's preemption test failed on two processors, every run on both architectures: the thread that spins in the kernel holds the big lock, so the other processor could not take its own tick, the other thread never ran, and the shell then freed a thread still running on the second processor, which faulted in kernel mode. Unit 1's fix had moved the test onto two processors. The test binds the shell and its threads to the shell's processor, which is the one-processor test it was (`d667cc07`).
* An init that exits with a child spinning in user mode on the other processor never returned to the shell: `proc_end_others` called `schedule`, which returns at once with the big lock held when the processor has nothing else to run, and the child's processor needed the lock to take its SIGKILL. The loop now waits for an interrupt with the lock let go after each turn; survivorprobe hung every run on both architectures before and returns every run after (`26fe6f1c`).
* installer-repair's archive-stop row copied rach2-install, 72 KB, into the recovery system's RAM root, which has 53 KB free on i686 and 32 KB on x86-64, so the copy was cut short and the row failed. Its copies go to `/dev/shm`, as rach2-install's own do, and rach2-install's comment no longer says the root has a few hundred kilobytes.
* The grow suite compiled growroot on the host without `utils/gptdisk.c`, which growroot has needed since milestone 57 gave it GUID tables.
* A doom suite row typed a line of 276 characters, past the 255 the console keeps; `tests/run style` found it, and the row is two lines.
* procprobe failed one run in three on 16 processors: a processor that missed a tick while the big lock held its interrupts off never counted it, so idle and busy fell short of the uptime. Each processor now counts the global ticks since its last count (`70945aa6`), and procprobe counts over every processor (`74e66a6b`); it passed two runs on 16 and one on 30.
* The host suite on Linux, whose linker refuses an undefined symbol the Mac's leaves, did not build `power_hosttest` and `smbios_hosttest` on an x86-64 host, nor `tty_hosttest` and `smp_hosttest`, and LeakSanitizer took the SMP test's idle stacks for lost (`cefb5fef`, `c9689d9e`, `8f9f0890`, `df610138`, `e7b01b85`).
* `build_rules_test.sh`'s check of `make no-recovery-key` read `make -n`, which prints the loop and not the paths since `d0ede385`; it runs the target over planted files (`cdafdec8`).
* The machine grow plan's archive did not fit the recovery system's RAM root, 53 KB free on i686 and 28 KB on x86-64. The root is 5 MiB and an image with less than 512 KiB free is refused (`7c43f929`).
* threadmusl's handler-mask row hung in three boots of forty-eight, on both architectures. The row now prints its threads from /proc before it kills a child that does not end, and the report showed one taker gone and the other asleep in pause with nothing pending or blocked: the taker had looked at the two handlers' marks and then called pause, and its own handler ran between the two, before the other thread's had set its mark. The takers keep both signals blocked between the look and the sleep and wait in sigsuspend, which unblocks them and sleeps in one step (`6669fdca`). Forty-eight boots of the fixed probe, on both architectures and on two and four processors, all passed; with the wake that `signal_set_blocked` gives another thread taken out, forty-three of forty-eight failed, and twelve of twelve passed restored.
* clonemusl's 20 ms futex wait ended early by the monotonic clock once on i686. The futex, semtimedop, a unix socket's time limit, rt_sigtimedwait and a timerfd counted their deadlines from `kernel_ticks`, which takes a tick only when the boot processor's handler runs, while the clocks count a tick whose interrupt is pending; a wait begun then ended a tick early by the clock. They count from the clocks' tick count, as poll and nanosleep did; `futex_hosttest` and `timerfd_hosttest` have a row with a tick pending that fails under the old count (`a4114f2b`). Forty-eight boots of clonemusl after the change, on both architectures, all passed.
* `rehearse_group_mutate.sh` looked for the installer root's recipe as `... $@ 64 16384`, which the 4 TiB install row's change made `64 16384 32768`, the inode ratio added, so the fault was never applied. It sets the group size to mke2fs's default of 32768 in the recipe as it now reads, and the rehearsal's setup fails on both architectures (`c44435c8`).
* `ext2_batch_mutate.sh`'s per-call fault, which drains the batch after every write, passed: since milestone 56's write-back (`228b1047`) a mount without `sync` writes a drained batch into the cache and issues no command before fsync, so the batch saves nothing the probe can count there. The same run at the milestone's base, 5349d3df, passed the fault too. `ext2probe cost` mounts its fixture with `MS_SYNCHRONOUS`, whose ordered write-through is what the batch saves commands on: the fixed kernel costs 4 commands and 2 flushes for the rewrites and 6 and 3 for the appends, the fault 64 and 32 and 12 and 6, on both architectures (`08661f63`). The script builds for the architecture `ARCH` names, where it had booted an i686 kernel whatever it was (`9bf728f2`).

## The fixes after the review

The fixes made after the review's units, from the checks and the machine, were reviewed by Codex on 2026-10-05 in rounds, each over the fixes of the round before, until a round found nothing:
