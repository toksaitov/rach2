# Machine

The Hardware section of `AGENTS.md` says what may be written and when the owner is needed. This skill has the checks, the Lenovo's procedures and the sittings.

## Targets

* This work is the owner's: only the owner's sessions on the Mac mini, the manager and its worker sessions among them, write a disk or work on the Lenovo. A task that needs the Lenovo is still open to every loop, as Handing over a step below says. `tools/flow/release.sh`, which the manager starts, installs on the Lenovo each commit that passed on every machine.
* The owner has authorized writes to two targets: the development flash drive on the Mac mini, and any disk of the Lenovo. Both hold nothing but this work, and both are a sandbox: write them as the work needs, without asking and without naming them to the owner first, and take no backup, restore or hash for their safety. Report afterwards what was written and where. Any other target, such as a second flash drive, another machine or hardware of another kind, needs the owner's authorization.
* The Mac mini's internal SSD, an APPLE SSD AP0512Z with serial `0ba02f1140bcba20`, is never written.
* A check of the target runs before every write, in place of asking. A target on the Mac must be external, removable and on USB, and must report the flash drive's serial and size: a SanDisk 3.2Gen1 of 61,524,148,224 bytes with USB serial `00014101091624031318`. Its device node changes between connections, so the serial and the size identify it. A target that is internal, or that reports the SSD's serial, is refused and the write does not run.
* The system itself needs no host device path: the kernel probes its own disk controller and chooses its root from its boot options, as `rach2/sys/block/Readme.md` describes.

## Reaching the Lenovo

* The Lenovo is on the Mac's network, not the testing host's, so sittings and stick writes run from the Mac. The Mac reaches it over its wired port, `en0`, at 1000baseT; its Wi-Fi port, `en1`, is down. The Lenovo's 192.168.0.106 is wired only on the Lenovo's side, and the testing host's 192.168.0.1 is a separate network.
* The committed development key reaches every machine built from this tree, as `tools/Readme.md` says under Working an installed machine from the host. When Linux runs on the Lenovo and IPv4 cannot reach it, it answers SSH on its link-local address, `fe80::2ad2:44ff:fe7e:e0af%en0`.
* A program built on the Mac and started from a Claude Code session there is charged to Claude Code by macOS's Local Network privacy, and its UDP or TCP to the Lenovo fails with EHOSTUNREACH until Claude Code has that permission (System Settings, Privacy & Security, Local Network). Apple's own `ssh` and `nc` are exempt, so they still work from the same shell. The release's native datagram cases need the permission.
* Its disk stays in its bay, since removing and refitting it is an hour of physical work. A machine left unbootable is reinstalled from the flash drive, which stays plugged into it.
* The machine's own offsets are in `dev-log/43-machine/hardware.md`. Take a path from there or from `tools/Readme.md`; do not derive one.

## Taking the Lenovo

* One task works the Lenovo at a time, since several milestones may be open at once. Before its first boot or write, a task takes the machine with `mkdir "$(git rev-parse --git-common-dir)/../build/flow/lenovo"` and writes its issue number to `issue` in that directory; when the mkdir fails, another holder has it, named in that file, and the task never takes it: a worker's session hands the task back as Handing over a step says and goes on with other work. The holder removes the directory once the release is back in both slots.
* The manager takes the lock in the same way for a checkpoint's trial boot, with `checkpoint NN` in `issue`, and removes it once the release is back in both slots. `release.sh` takes it itself, as `release M`, each time it comes to the Lenovo, hours after it starts, gives it up when it must wait before it has touched the machine, and removes it once the Lenovo runs the committed release; after a failed release the manager removes it only once the release is back in both slots or the release, started again, has passed.
* Several prepared steps that need the same kernel and take neither the screen nor the GPU may share one boot, which the manager runs under the lock: it takes each task with `board.sh take N`, runs the steps in turn, records each result on its own task, and finishes the task or hands it back. A step that takes the screen or the GPU, graphics among them, has a boot to itself.
* The Lenovo is the bottleneck, so a step measures in runs of a few minutes and stops a run the moment its figure is clear. It repeats no clear result and takes one boot unless a second gives what the first cannot. Each game, benchmark or test starts straight in its measured state: a benchmark or timedemo mode, a save copied under a harness name and loaded at start, intros skipped, caches warm or cold on purpose, all prepared and checked in QEMU before the step. Full runs, many loops, every boot and every save, wait for the very end of all phases, once the work is stable; until then a short run that settles the question meets a gate.

## Handing over a step

* A loop of the owner's that reaches a step on the Lenovo runs it itself: it takes the lock as its first act on the machine, runs the step, and finishes the task, with whatever code the step's result needs. A boot of code that has never run on the Lenovo waits for the manager's go on the issue, as Trial boots below says.
* A loop that cannot run the step now, a team member's always, and one of the owner's while another holds the lock or the go is missing, prepares the step whole and writes it on the task as one comment: it pushes `task/N` and names its commit; gives the command that builds the kernel, root or trial root from it on the Mac, or, for what only Linux builds, where a copy is kept until the task closes and the command that fetches it; gives the commands in order, what to read and what passes, and whether the step plays sound or takes the screen, which needs the notice below; and, for code that has never run on the Lenovo, adds the readiness note that Trial boots below asks for. It then hands the task back with `sh tools/flow/board.sh release N owner-only`, or with `trial` in place of `owner-only` when the step boots never-run code and the issue has no go, which puts it in Backlog until the manager says go and sets it Ready.
* `board.sh next` offers an `owner-only` task to the owner's loops only while the lock is free and no other `owner-only` task is In progress. The loop that takes it runs the step as written, as the first bullet says.

## Trial boots

* Over SSH, a worker tries its own kernel whenever QEMU cannot show what it needs: `bootctl kernel FILE` puts it in the candidate slot and arms one boot of it, and the boot after that, or a power cycle after a hang, returns to the fallback slot. Since the candidate slot held the installed release, check first that the fallback holds the same kernel, by the SHA-256 of each slot's bytes against the release record; if it does not, run `bootctl fallback` while the release is the booted, committed candidate. Afterwards put the release back with `bootctl kernel`, a boot, `bootctl commit` and `bootctl fallback`, and check both slots again. The trial kernel must fit the candidate slot, whose size the `candidate:` line of the Lenovo's `bootctl status` gives: a build's link allows its own slot size, which can be larger than the installed disk's until a release moves the disk to it.
* A trial boot of code that has never run on the Lenovo waits for a review that it is ready, since a hang costs the machine until the owner is home. The worker writes on the issue why each new path cannot stop the machine: every register sequence checked against the reference driver line by line, every wait bounded by a timeout, interrupts masked until their handler is ready, and a host test of the worker's own against the chip model, run alone as the Testing section of `AGENTS.md` allows a new test, that runs the very path the trial will run, start to finish. The manager reads that and the diff, and says go on the issue before the boot; a trial with any doubt left runs the new path one step at a time, each step logged over SSH before the next.
* Every trial boot sends the kernel's log to the Mac, the only record of a hang or a panic on a machine with no serial port ([netconsole(4)](../../../man/man4/netconsole.4)). Before the reboot, start `nc -lu 6666 > build/runs/netconsole-N-$(date +%Y%m%dT%H%M).log` on the Mac as the owner's user, with no sudo, in the background with the tool's notice, and stop it once the trial is over. The trial's line carries `netconsole=6665@192.168.0.106/,6666@IP/STATION`, IP being the Mac's address that `ipconfig getifaddr en0` gives and STATION the station address the Lenovo's `/proc/net/arp` holds for it, since of the broadcast datagrams that Linux sends without one, four in five of a boot's never reach the Mac: a trial root among the words of the block that stages it, and a kernel trial, from the release booted as the candidate, through a block that `bootctl config` stages after `bootctl kernel`, which runs when `next_entry` is `candidate`: `set next_entry=`, `save_env next_entry`, `search --no-floppy --fs-uuid --set=root 1ACE-F00D`, `multiboot2 /boot/kernel` with this boot's `/proc/cmdline` and the `netconsole=` word, and `boot`. Afterwards empty the menu with `bootctl config` of a file that holds one comment line. The log is whole when its last line is the last record the machine wrote, a panic's among them.
* A go covers the task's own commits it names, rebased onto the milestone branch as it stands at the build, since another task's code that reaches the device runs first in that task's own trial. While a review round reads the branch, the go names the head the last round read whole instead, since no commit under review is tested. A rerun on fixed code needs a new go when a fix changes a path that reaches the device: a register, an interrupt, DMA, a wait on the hardware, or a mapping that gives a program the device's registers or memory. A rerun whose fixes stay in software that cannot stop the machine, such as a file system, the text of /proc, /sys or debugfs, or bookkeeping, needs none; the worker names each fix on the issue and why it is of that kind before the boot.
* A silent trial runs whenever the work is ready, at any hour; none is held for a time of day. If one leaves the machine off or hung, record it on the issue and go on with other work: the machine waits until the owner is home, the hang is pushed to the owner's phone while the owner is awake, and the owner is never asked to come to the machine for it.
* Debian 13.7 on p5 boots on the Lenovo, through GRUB's one-shot `next_entry`, only for the Linux references and disk work the graphics plan names, each a session and a boot of its own: 80-2 (with 81-1's trace), 76-15c's shrink of p3 and its new data partition, 78-1b's watchdog expiry, 83-6b (trixie-backports' kernel, removed afterwards), 77-10, 78-10d.2's CRC references, 78-13b.4's WebGL retake, 82-4b.2's translation-on reference and, at the sitting, 84-2.1's HDMI dump. Its entry and p5 stay between sessions. Any other Linux is booted for discovery, once, and what it added to the boot partition is deleted afterwards. Linux leaves by `rtcwake -m off -s 120` rather than a warm reboot, which once left the firmware unable to boot.
* A trial root is an unreleased root image on the data partition, booted through the loop driver (`rach2.rootimage`), so that tests of the userland need not wait for a release. It is copied there from the task's build and named with its SHA-256, which is checked after the copy; armed with `bootctl` for one boot, as `bootctl(8)` says, with the task's trial kernel or the installed one; read over SSH, its root image's SHA-256 and the release record checked before the step runs; and left by the boot that follows, or a power cycle after a hang, which returns to the installed root. The installed root and the boot slots keep the release throughout, and the image is deleted when its task closes.
* A step writes every file it reads under `/data/trial` itself, the release kernel among them, and removes what it wrote. It uses a file another step left, such as a trial root, only after checking its SHA-256, and copies it again from its source when it is gone.

## Sound and screen

* The owner works beside the Lenovo, so a step there is silent and does not take the screen unless it must. Run `mixer vol 1` after every boot, since HDA starts at 100, and again once a program that sets the volume, such as Doom, has started. Measure sound by the kernel's counters and `/proc/audio` at that level, not by ear.
* A step that plays sound aloud or shows something on the screen, Doom, `audiotest` or a tone among them, is announced first: comment "Lenovo notice:" and what will play on the task's issue, which the manager passes to the owner, and wait ten minutes before running it. Everything the notice names is part of the step and waits the ten minutes, its reboots and their boot text among them.

## Sittings

* A sitting, which needs someone at the machine, comes last in a task, once every part is written and the machine is built, installed and through its automatic steps. The owner is asked for it only with the time it will be ready, and names the time.
* During a sitting the owner gets one step at a time: the step, its command and what to report, and then nothing more until the owner says it is done and what happened. Results are told apart by who produced them and when. A helper stages its scripts ahead but starts nothing audible, on screen or waiting on the owner until the owner has that step. Everything else, background fixes and merges among them, waits until the sitting ends.

## Updating the installed system

* `make machine`, with `ARCH=x86_64` for the Lenovo and on the testing host and with `GNU_AGENTS=all` as the release gives it, builds everything the machine is given, recovery included; a part built by hand can be older than the rest.
* The installed Lenovo is updated over SSH: a kernel through `bootctl` on the running system; a root through the recovery system with `rach2-install --console term --update`, which keeps the machine's accounts; a partition the installer does not know about through `reflash`. The recovery partition's own key, `boot/recovery_authorized_keys`, holds the development key, so the disk's recovery system answers SSH; its recovery kernel and image are the files `boot/recovery` and `boot/recovery.krffs`, which a release replaces in place, keeping the key and the accounts' archive. `tools/flow/release.sh` runs these steps in order and says why that order; read it before an update by hand.
* A root update rewrites `/opt`. Until Crysis is installed on the data partition (80-6), `sh build/cache/crysis/push-lenovo.sh` puts the copy in `/opt` back after each update; from then on the game, its saves and Mesa's shader cache live on the data partition, which an update keeps, and nothing is put back.
* The manual has the commands: `rach2-install(8)` for an install, `recovery(7)` for the recovery system and the three update paths, `bootctl(8)` and `reflash(8)` for the two writes, and `disk(7)` for the device nodes they write through, each limited to one region of the disk.
