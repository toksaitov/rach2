# Shared buffers and fences

`sys/dmabuf/` shares buffers and completion fences between devices and processes through Linux's dma-buf, sync-file and udmabuf interfaces. `/dev/udmabuf` exports pinned ranges of sealed tmpfs files. A software timeline supplies fences for tests through `sw_sync_open`, which debugfs binds to `sync/sw_sync`. The user structures and ioctl numbers are in [`../drm/uapi.h`](../drm/uapi.h), and the kernel interfaces in [`dmabuf.h`](dmabuf.h) and [`../drm/drm.h`](../drm/drm.h).

## Use

Open `/dev/udmabuf` and call `UDMABUF_CREATE` with a memfd, a page-aligned offset and a page-aligned size, or `UDMABUF_CREATE_LIST` with ranges to concatenate. Each file must have `F_SEAL_SHRINK` and lack `F_SEAL_WRITE`. The resulting read/write descriptor maps the same pages shared, reports its size through `lseek(fd, 0, SEEK_END)` and `fstat`, and has an inode distinct from other exports. `/proc/PID/fd` names it `anon_inode:dmabuf`, and a sync file `anon_inode:sync_file`. [udmabuf(4)](../../../man/man4/udmabuf.4) and [dma-buf(7)](../../../man/man7/dma-buf.7) give the user contracts.

`DMA_BUF_SET_NAME` stores a name of at most 31 bytes and its NUL, accepting both ABI encodings. A bad address returns EFAULT, and a name without a NUL in its first 32 bytes EINVAL, as Linux's `strndup_user`; a failure leaves the previous name. `/proc/PID/fdinfo/N` reports a supplied name, including an empty one, as `name:`.

## Interfaces

* `dma_fence_init` starts a fence with one reference, a context and a sequence number. `dma_fence_signal` records monotonic time, runs each pending callback once and wakes the fence's queue. `dma_fence_set_error` records a negative producer error before completion. `dma_fence_status` returns zero while active, one on success or that error. `dma_fence_wait_until` holds a reference across sleeps, with an absolute monotonic deadline or a negative value for no deadline; it returns zero on completion, `-ETIME` on expiry or `-EINTR` for an interrupted wait. The producer error is read separately. As on Linux, a wait or a callback asks for the signal (`dma_fence_enable_sw_signaling`), which marks the fence `listened` and runs its `enable_signaling` once, with interrupts masked, and a wait then sleeps in the fence's own `wait` when it has one, else in `dma_fence_default_wait`; `dma_fence_is_signaled` asks the fence's `signaled` and enables nothing.
* `struct dma_fence_chain` is a point of a timeline, Linux's dma_fence_chain: `dma_fence_chain_init` puts a fence after the previous point and takes both references; the point signals once its fence and every earlier point's have, without their errors. `dma_fence_chain_walk` steps back to the nearest earlier point whose fence has not signalled, unlinking from the chain each signalled point it passes and dropping the chain's reference to it, as Linux's does, and `dma_fence_chain_find_seqno` finds the point that covers a number. `dma_fence_count` and `dma_fence_at` list the leaves of any fence in Linux's unwrap order, each call afresh, and `dma_fence_unwrap_first` and `dma_fence_unwrap_next` walk them once, as Linux's `dma_fence_unwrap_for_each`, holding the point they are at, so that a leaf that signals meanwhile, which leaves the list, moves no other out of the walk and the leaf returned stays while the caller sleeps. The walk passes a chain's signalled points without unlinking them, where Linux's steps with `dma_fence_chain_walk` and unlinks them; a syncobj's new point unlinks those behind it. Every caller that visits each leaf walks them so, a virtio GPU submission's wait for its dependencies among them, which sleeps between leaves. `dma_fence_unwrap_merge` merges the leaves still active, and `dma_fence_get_stub` gives the shared signalled fence. The syncobjs of [the DRM core](../drm/Readme.md) keep their timelines as chains.
* `dma_fence_merge` builds a flattened all-of fence, keeping the latest point of each context and propagating a producer error after all children complete. A zero-input merge gives a completed stub, and a single leaf keeps that leaf. A merge adds its callbacks to its children only once its own signal is asked for, as Linux's dma_fence_array, and a query of it, or of a chain, reads its children's state; it owns its children and withdraws their callbacks when released. Names come from `dma_fence_ops`; deadline hints reach every leaf.
* A reservation (`struct dma_resv`) is made by `dma_resv_init` and guarded by its sleeping lock, `dma_resv_lock` and `dma_resv_unlock`, as Linux's ww_mutex. `dma_resv_add_fence` adds a read fence or joins outstanding writers on a write, taking a reference of its own. `dma_resv_snapshot` gives a reader the outstanding writers, and a writer the outstanding writers and readers, as one referenced fence. Each takes the lock itself; the `_locked` forms leave it to a caller that must test and add in one step, as a submission orders itself against the work its snapshot names. `dma_resv_get_fences` and `dma_resv_get_write` give a reader references to the fences themselves, taken under the lock, which `dma_resv_put_fences` drops; a reader such as GEM_BUSY waits and tests them after the lock is gone, as Linux's readers take references under RCU. `dma_resv_fini` releases every slot for the last user.
* `dma_buf_export` takes exporter storage on success and returns one reference. Its optional external reservation must outlive the buffer. `dma_buf_fd` installs another reference and leaves the caller's alone; `dma_buf_get` finds a descriptor with a new reference, `dma_buf_ref` takes one directly and `dma_buf_put` releases one. The exporter's `mmap` attaches backing with a buffer reference for the mapping, and `release` returns exporter storage. Its optional `page` hook lends physical frames to device importers, and `map_flags` records their cache type. `read_only` selects read-only descriptors; its default of zero keeps udmabuf read/write. Its optional `cpu_access` hook receives validated DMA_BUF_SYNC flags first, as Linux's `begin_cpu_access` and `end_cpu_access` do, so that it may wait its own way; at the start, the reservation's wait follows only when the hook returns 0.
* `sync_file_create` installs a close-on-exec sync file with its own fence reference. A call that gives the caller a new sync file uses `sync_file_copy_out`, as Linux's ioctls do: it reserves the descriptor, makes the file, copies the caller's record out and only then attaches the file, so that a failed copy leaves no descriptor and no file. A call that cannot fail after its work, such as an atomic commit's OUT_FENCE_PTR, reserves the descriptor and makes the file with `sync_file_new` before the work, attaches it with `sync_file_attach` after, and on failure frees it with `sync_file_drop`. `sync_file_get_fence` returns a reference or NULL. `SYNC_IOC_MERGE` omits completed leaves as Linux does and keeps a completed stub when nothing is pending. `SYNC_IOC_FILE_INFO` counts or copies the leaves, filled before the one copy out, and with rows asked for reports the first leaf's status that is active or failed, as Linux does. Poll watches the fence's own queue and reports POLLIN on completion, including a producer error, which FILE_INFO reports.
* `sw_sync_open` attaches a new timeline to a just-opened descriptor, named by the opener's command, as Linux names it, and puts it on the list of live timelines that `sw_sync_info` writes for debugfs's `sync/info` in Linux 6.12's text; each point keeps a copy of the name, which its fence reports after the timeline has closed. CREATE_FENCE returns a sync file, INC advances the 32-bit timeline with signed wraparound comparisons, and close completes outstanding points with `-ENOENT`. GET_DEADLINE reads a hint supplied through the sync file, or returns `-ENOENT` when none was supplied.

## Design

Fences, reservations and buffer references are safe on any processor without the big kernel lock, which the descriptor operations of buffers, sync files, udmabuf and software timelines still take (`file_ops_lock` in `fs/file.h`). So are the producers that run without it: i915's and virtio-gpu's interrupt handlers, which signal fences, i915's EXECBUFFER2 and buffer calls, an atomic commit on i915, and vgem's ATTACH and SIGNAL, which keep each file's fences under a mutex of their own, as Linux's `fence_mutex`. A wait, a copy to or from user memory and an exporter operation may sleep; a completion callback may only signal, wake or queue work, and may run under the signaller's own spinlocks, as virtio-gpu signals its answers' fences under its device's lock and Linux's under `drv->lock` (virtgpu_fence.c:134-174), so a callback takes no lock that a signaller holds around a signal.

A fence's references and a buffer's change atomically, as Linux's kref. Each fence has a spinlock, taken with interrupts off, over its `signaled` and `listened` marks and its list of callbacks; every fence's lock is of one class, since none is taken under another's. `dma_fence_signal` marks the fence under its lock, then takes each callback off the list in turn and runs it with the lock let go and interrupts still masked, as Linux runs them with the fence's lock held and interrupts off; running them off the lock lets a callback signal another fence or add a callback to it without nesting fence locks. `dma_fence_add_callback` tests the mark and links the callback under the lock, so that a signal on another processor either runs it or finds it, never neither; it never runs the callback itself. `dma_fence_remove_callback` unlinks a callback still waiting its turn, and when that callback is the one running on another processor it spins until the callback returns, as Linux's caller holds the fence's lock that the signal holds through each callback, so that whoever removes a callback may free its owner at once. Asking for the signal marks the fence `listened` under the lock and runs its `enable_signaling` once, after the lock is let go, with interrupts masked, so an `enable_signaling` may not sleep either. A callback runs with interrupts masked wherever the signal comes from, so it may not sleep.

A timeline point (`struct dma_fence_chain`) holds a reference to itself while its callback is armed, as Linux's enabling takes one, and its earlier-point link, which a walk unlinks while others read it, is read with a reference and swapped under one spinlock for every chain, where Linux uses RCU and `cmpxchg`; no fence's lock is taken under it. A reservation's lock is a sleeping mutex, after the big lock, a DRM device's commit lock and the drivers' object locks and before every spinlock; its readers take references under it in place of Linux's RCU restart. The ownership and device-loss rules in [`../drm/drm.h`](../drm/drm.h) apply to every exporter and fence producer.

Udmabuf takes a frame reference for each page before publishing a descriptor. The frame remains pinned after the memfd closes. A shared device mapping holds the dma-buf, and its last region releases that reference at unmap, exec or exit; fork shares the mapping's backing record. Each buffer poll takes a reservation snapshot and adds one callback, to its first leaf not yet signalled, as Linux's `dma_buf_poll_add_cb`, so that a poll asks no other producer for its signal; that leaf's completion wakes the buffer's queue, and a later poll reads the reservation again and listens on the next. Explicit sync-file export takes a snapshot which later imports cannot change.

## Limits

Udmabuf uses Linux's default bounds of 1,024 ranges and 64 MiB per export. It supports tmpfs files; Rach2 has no hugetlbfs files. Shared mappings use cached RAM and need no cache maintenance on x86. An exporter for other memory supplies its own mapping and CPU-access hooks.

## Tests

`tests/host/fence_hosttest.c` drives fences, context ranges, callback removal, an interrupt that frees two points of a chain while another fence's callbacks run, an interrupt raised while an array's signalling is set up, which comes after the setup and finds the array armed once, timed and interrupted waits, flattened merges and errors, reservation snapshots, buffer size and inode identity, sync-file export, merge, info and import, buffer poll, the exporter's CPU-access hook before DMA_BUF_IOCTL_SYNC's wait, software timeline completion, deadline hints and producer close, under the sanitizers. Its descriptor table and scheduler are models; the fence and buffer code is included whole.

`tests/host/syncobj_hosttest.c` drives fence chains through timeline syncobjs: points in and out of order, the leaves sync files list, and long chains freed without recursion.

`tests/fenceprobe.c`, in the same suite, runs a slow producer, eight concurrent producers on one buffer and a failing producer, each behind a software timeline, and consumers that read the fence's state, success or the producer's error, before the buffer.

`tests/udmabufprobe.c`, the `dmabuf` suite, runs the real descriptor operations on both architectures: rejected inputs, distinct inodes and size, shared pages and reversed list ranges, fork, export, merge, info, import, CPU access and poll, then pages retained after all descriptors close. It releases every descriptor and mapping on either exit. The checkpoint and release run the consumer tests named in the milestone plan.
