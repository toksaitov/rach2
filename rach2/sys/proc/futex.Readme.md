# Futexes

A futex is a 32-bit word of user memory on which a thread sleeps until another wakes it, the word having held the value the sleeper expected when it went to sleep. C libraries build their mutexes, condition variables and one-time initialization on it, and enter the kernel only when a lock is contended. `futex.c` has the `futex` call, the futex2 calls `futex_waitv`, `futex_wake`, `futex_wait` and `futex_requeue`, the robust lists through which a thread's end releases the locks it held, and the rest of a thread's end that concerns futexes, `proc_thread_gone`. The contracts are in [futex(2)](../../../man/man2/futex.2); this document describes how the kernel identifies a futex, files its waiters and keeps their deadlines.

* `futex` takes `FUTEX_WAIT`, `FUTEX_WAKE`, their bitset forms, `FUTEX_REQUEUE`, `FUTEX_CMP_REQUEUE`, `FUTEX_WAKE_OP`, `FUTEX_LOCK_PI`, `FUTEX_LOCK_PI2`, `FUTEX_TRYLOCK_PI`, `FUTEX_UNLOCK_PI`, `FUTEX_WAIT_REQUEUE_PI` and `FUTEX_CMP_REQUEUE_PI`, each with `FUTEX_PRIVATE_FLAG`; the bitset wait, requeue wait and `LOCK_PI2` accept `FUTEX_CLOCK_REALTIME`. The word's address must be aligned to four bytes, and a bitset must not be 0; either is `EINVAL`.
* `futex_waitv` waits on 1 to 128 words at once, each named by a `struct futex_waitv` of Linux's layout, the same in both ABIs, with `FUTEX2_SIZE_U32` and optionally `FUTEX2_PRIVATE`, and an absolute timeout of 64-bit fields on `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` or `CLOCK_REALTIME`. It returns the index of the futex a wake ended it on. The compat table of the x86-64 kernel has the same handler at the same number, as it has for the three calls below.
* `futex_wake`, `futex_wait` and `futex_requeue` are Linux 6.7's calls of `FUTEX_WAKE_BITSET`, `FUTEX_WAIT_BITSET` and `FUTEX_CMP_REQUEUE` with futex2 flags: `FUTEX2_SIZE_U32` with `FUTEX2_PRIVATE` or without, any other flag, `FUTEX2_NUMA` among them, being `EINVAL`, as in Linux 6.12. A value or mask must fit 32 bits. `futex_wait` takes an absolute timeout as `futex_waitv` does, and `futex_requeue` two `struct futex_waitv`, the futex to wake with its value and the one to move waiters to, with equal flags in both entries or `EINVAL`. They share the queues of `futex`.
* `set_robust_list` takes the address of the list's head and its size, which must be the size of the head's three words: 12 bytes on i686 and 24 on x86-64.
* `get_robust_list` gives the head of the caller's list or of another thread's, named by its id in the caller's PID namespace: a thread of the caller's process, any thread to a privileged caller, and a thread that is not privileged and whose real or effective user id is the caller's effective one.
* `proc_thread_gone` (`process.h`) runs at a thread's end and at its `execve` ([processes](Readme.md)).

## Design

### Keys and buckets

A futex is identified by a key of two words (`futex_key`): the address space, which is the page directory of the caller's process, and the user address. The threads of a process share a page directory, and so do a `vfork` child and its parent, and processes made with `CLONE_VM`. A word in a `VM_SHARED` region, shared anonymous memory or a file of `/dev/shm` mapped `MAP_SHARED`, is identified instead by its physical address, the page's frame and the offset in it, with a marker in place of the address space, unless the operation has `FUTEX_PRIVATE_FLAG`. So processes that map the same page identify its futexes alike, as a mutex made `PTHREAD_PROCESS_SHARED` needs.

A waiter's record, `struct futex_q`, is filed in one of `FUTEX_BUCKETS`, 64, buckets by a hash of its key, while its thread sleeps on the record's `struct futex_sleeper`, which has a wait queue on which only that thread sleeps. A `futex` wait has one waiter on its kernel stack; a `futex_waitv` call has one waiter for each word, in an array it allocates, all pointing at its one sleeper, so a wake of any of the words wakes the thread. A wake walks one bucket, so it passes over the waiters of other futexes only when their keys hash alike.

Each bucket, `struct futex_hb`, has a spinlock, taken with interrupts off, over its list and over the links, keys and marks of the waiters on it, as Linux's `futex_hash_bucket` (Linux 6.12.115, `kernel/futex/futex.h:115`). A waiter records the bucket it is on (`hb`). A call that names two futexes takes both locks, the one at the lower address first and the other as the second of their one class, or one lock when the keys hash alike, as `double_lock_hb` (`futex.h:322`), so two requeues in opposite directions cannot each wait for the other.

### Locks

The futex calls but `get_robust_list` run without the big lock, on both entries, as Linux's futexes have their bucket locks alone; [the processes' Readme](Readme.md), under Processors and the big lock, lists them. The order is the big lock where it is taken, then the address space's regions' lock (`mmap_lock`), then the bucket locks, then the wait queues' and the scheduler's spinlocks a wake takes ([processes](Readme.md), Locking). A futex word is read or exchanged only under its bucket's lock, with the regions' lock held to read, through `get_user_u32_nofault` and `cmpxchg_user_u32_nofault` (`mm/usercopy.h`): they touch the word only when its page is present and allows the access now, which the regions' lock keeps true until it is let go, and otherwise answer `EFAULT` and bring nothing in. The caller then lets its locks go, brings the page in (`vm_prefault_range`), which may sleep, and starts again, as Linux's callers of `futex_get_value_locked` do (`kernel/futex/core.c:466`). A private futex is named without the regions' lock; a futex that may be shared is named under it, and a shared mapping's page that is not there is brought in the same way.

The PI operations take the big lock before any bucket's lock (`futex_pi_call`), and so does a thread's end (`proc_thread_gone`), so that the PI states, their owners and the donations change one operation at a time; the bucket locks cover the PI waiters' places on the lists beside the others'.

### Wait and wake

`futex_wait` and `sys_futex_waitv` first turn a timeout into a deadline (below). `futex_sleep_on` then holds the regions' lock to read and, for each word in order, names its futex, takes its bucket's lock, reads the word, and files the word's waiter when the word holds its expected value, as Linux's `futex_wait_multiple_setup` does (`kernel/futex/waitwake.c:414`). A wake that comes after the comparison takes the same bucket's lock and finds the waiter. A word that differs takes off the waiters filed for the words before and returns `EAGAIN`, or the index of one a wake marked meanwhile; a word whose page is not there takes them off too, brings the page in with the locks let go, and starts again. The thread then sleeps on its sleeper's queue (`wq_wait_event`) until a waker marks one of its waiters, its deadline comes, or a signal arrives, and takes every waiter off its bucket before it returns; the call returns the index of the last marked one, as Linux's `futex_wait_multiple` does.

A waiter takes itself off as Linux's `futex_unqueue` does (`kernel/futex/core.c:589`): it reads its bucket, takes that bucket's lock and reads the bucket again, since a requeue may have moved it meanwhile, and starts again when it changed. A bucket of NULL means a wake took the waiter off and is done with it.

`futex_wake` marks up to the requested number of matching waiters under the bucket's lock, at least one as Linux's walk does, but none for a `futex_wake` call of 0, a waiter matching when its key is the wake's and its bitset meets the wake's, takes them off the list, and wakes each one's sleeper's queue, so a wake of one makes one thread runnable and the bucket's other sleepers stay asleep. It sets the waiter's bucket to NULL last, since the waiter and its sleeper are in memory the sleeping thread frees once it reads that NULL. Linux stores that NULL first and wakes the thread later through a reference to it (`kernel/futex/waitwake.c:117-123`); here the queue woken is the waiter's own, so the wake comes before the store. A PI waiter, or one of `FUTEX_WAIT_REQUEUE_PI`, on the key ends the walk with `EINVAL`.

`futex_requeue` names both futexes and takes both buckets' locks, under the regions' lock when it compares the first word or names a futex that may be shared, compares the first word with the expected value for `FUTEX_CMP_REQUEUE` and the futex2 call, wakes up to the requested number of the first futex's waiters and moves up to the second count more to the second futex's key and bucket, where a wake of that futex finds them; it returns how many it woke and moved. `futex_wake_op` takes the regions' lock and both buckets' locks, changes the second word by the encoded operation in one compare-and-exchange that does not fault (`futex_op`), as Linux's `futex_atomic_op_inuser` does, wakes the first futex's waiters, and wakes the second's when the comparison of the old value holds; a second word that is not there or not writable is brought in for a write with the locks let go, and the call starts again.

### Deadlines

A deadline is a tick of the kernel's clock, which counts `TICK_HZ` (`timer.h`) a second. A relative timeout, `FUTEX_WAIT`'s, becomes whole ticks, a part of a tick counted as one (`futex_ticks`); an absolute one, `FUTEX_WAIT_BITSET`'s, `futex_waitv`'s and `futex_wait`'s, is measured when the call begins against the monotonic clock, or the real-time clock with `FUTEX_CLOCK_REALTIME` or the futex2 calls' `CLOCK_REALTIME` (`futex_ticks_until`). The futex2 calls refuse negative seconds and nanoseconds outside 0 to 999999999 with `EINVAL`, as Linux's `futex2_setup_timeout` does, a 32-bit caller's nanoseconds having their high word ignored (`futex2_deadline`). The deadline is the clock's current tick plus those ticks plus one, so a wait never ends before the time it asked for. It is fixed before the word's page is brought in, so a fault's sleep counts against it, and a deadline that the fault outlasted ends the call with `ETIMEDOUT` once the word is compared. The tick wakes the waiter through the record's `wakeup_tick` ([processes](Readme.md)). A deadline on the real-time clock keeps its time and the clock's step count (`futex_rt_deadline`), and its ticks are the span to that time, since the tick count is slewed with real time and only a step parts them: after a step of the clock, a setting or a leap second, the tick wakes the waiter, which counts its tick again from the clock as it then is and waits on while the time is still to come (`futex_rt_count`, `futex_sleep`), so a setting past the time ends the wait and a setting back keeps it, as Linux's real-time hrtimer does, and a wait whose tick comes before the clock reaches its time counts again too; a restarted `FUTEX_WAIT_BITSET` keeps the time on that clock.

A signal the process acts on ends a wait with `EINTR`. The dispatcher restarts a `futex` wait without a timeout, as it restarts a read (`ERESTARTSYS`), and every `futex_waitv` and `futex_wait`, whose deadline is absolute. A timed wait files its word, value, bitset and deadline tick in the thread's restart block (`futex_wait_until`) and ends as Linux's `ERESTART_RESTARTBLOCK`: after a handler it returns `EINTR`, and when no handler runs, as after a stop and a continue, `restart_syscall` resumes it (`futex_wait_restart`) and it ends at its first deadline, never one measured again from the restart ([signals](signal.Readme.md)).

### Priority inheritance

The PI operations follow Linux 6.12's `kernel/futex/pi.c` and `requeue.c`. An unlocked PI word is 0. Its low 30 bits name the owning thread in the caller's PID namespace; `FUTEX_WAITERS` forces an unlock through the kernel and `FUTEX_OWNER_DIED` reports an owner's death. A kernel state, `struct futex_pi`, identifies a contended word and its owner. Each waiter holds a reference until its wait returns, including while it repairs a handoff's word; the last reference deletes the state, so an uncontended mutex can again unlock entirely in user space.

The lock reads and exchanges its word under the regions' lock and the key's bucket's lock, and a word that is not there or not writable is brought in for a write with both let go before the lock starts again. An atomic race restarts the acquisition under the bucket's lock. An owner id of 0 lets the caller acquire the word while keeping `OWNER_DIED`; another live user thread is recorded as the owner and the caller queues behind it. A recursive lock and a blocked-owner cycle are `EDEADLK`; a missing owner is `ESRCH`, a kernel thread is `EPERM`, and a busy trylock is `EAGAIN`. A thread releasing its futexes at exit is waited for before the acquisition is retried, with its id looked up anew after each sleep so no process pointer survives a reap. Ordinary and PI waiters on one key cannot be mixed: a PI attachment or ordinary wake that finds the other kind is `EINVAL`.

An owner's effective real-time priority is the highest base or inherited priority of the waiters behind every PI mutex it holds. `futex_pi_recalculate` starts each owner's donation from none and propagates the donations over the acyclic blocked-owner graph until no value changes, building them aside (`pi_next`) and only then giving each owner its own under the run queues' lock (`sched_set_inherited`), so that no selection sees a donation half built. A waiter of an owner that itself waits on another mutex therefore donates along the whole chain. Timeout, signal, handoff and exit remove the old donation and recalculate the remaining ones; an owner that has just let go of a mutex has its donation built with the others and given to it once, so that one another held mutex still boosts never shows a lower priority meanwhile and keeps its place on its queue, as Linux's `rt_mutex_setprio` leaves a task whose priority does not change. The scheduler selects effective real-time priorities before ordinary threads, FIFO at equal priorities for `SCHED_FIFO` until a yield or block, and round-robin for `SCHED_RR`; a donation leaves the base policy and the priority reported by `sched_getparam` unchanged.

An unlock checks the caller's id, atomically writes `WAITERS` and the highest-priority waiter's id, and transfers the state before waking that waiter; equal priorities keep queue order. With no waiter it clears the word. `LOCK_PI` takes an absolute deadline on the real-time clock; `LOCK_PI2` takes one on the monotonic clock unless `FUTEX_CLOCK_REALTIME` selects real time. Signals and deadlines remove a waiter and its donation, but a completed handoff wins over either. PI acquisition restarts after a handler even without `SA_RESTART`, retaining its absolute deadline; the dispatcher and signal delivery keep that restart separate from an ordinary interrupted wait. A requeued waiter interrupted after transfer returns `EAGAIN`, since retrying the condition wait would wait on the wrong word.

`WAIT_REQUEUE_PI` compares the condition word and records the distinct destination key. `CMP_REQUEUE_PI` requires `val` 1, compares the condition word with `val3`, and transfers at most the fourth argument plus one waiters, acquiring a free target for the first waiter or attaching it behind the target's owner. Every transferred waiter must have asked for that destination. The pair has absolute deadlines, and a plain wake before transfer ends the condition wait with `EAGAIN`.

### Robust lists and a thread's end

`proc_thread_gone` runs at a thread's end (`proc_exit_cleanup` in `exit.c`) and at its `execve`. An `execve` calls it before a thread that is not its process's leader takes the leader's id, so the id it compares is the one the thread locked its words with. It works in this order:

1. It walks the thread's robust list, as glibc lays it out (`struct robust_head`): the first entry, the offset from an entry to its futex word, and the entry the thread was taking or giving back. At most `ROBUST_LIST_LIMIT`, 2048, entries are walked, so a list with a loop in it ends. Each word that still has the thread's id gets `FUTEX_OWNER_DIED` in its place, with `FUTEX_WAITERS` kept, and one waiter is woken when the word has waiters, as Linux's `handle_futex_death` does; the next owner learns from `EOWNERDEAD` that the state the lock guarded may be half made.
2. It takes the pending entry last. When its word has no owner and it is not a priority-inheritance futex, the thread ended between an unlock and the unlock's wake, or between a wake and taking the lock; one waiter is woken, and the word is left as it is.
3. It transfers every contended PI mutex still owned by the ending thread to its top waiter. PI list entries are marked dead without an ordinary wake; the new owner repairs the word through its own mapping before returning, including when the former owner no longer has a usable mapping.
4. When another thread shares the address space, it writes 0 to the word given with `CLONE_CHILD_CLEARTID` or `set_tid_address` and wakes one waiter on it, which is how `pthread_join` learns of the end.

A word's page is made present and writable before the word is read. The read, the comparison and the write are then one step under the regions' lock, with no fault between them, and the write is a compare-and-exchange, read again when the word changed, so a thread on another processor that takes the lock or sets `FUTEX_WAITERS` meanwhile in user mode does not have its change written over.

## Limits

* On i686, `futex_time64` holds a count of seconds past 2147483647 there, since the native timespec's seconds are a `long`: an absolute deadline after January 2038 ends in it.
* `FUTEX_CLOCK_REALTIME` is taken with `FUTEX_WAIT_BITSET`, `FUTEX_WAIT_REQUEUE_PI` and `FUTEX_LOCK_PI2`; with `FUTEX_WAIT` or `FUTEX_LOCK_PI` it is `ENOSYS`.
* A deadline is a whole number of the kernel's ticks.
* A thread's end walks at most 2048 entries of its robust list.

## Tests

`tests/run default` runs these probes, and `ARCH=x86_64 tests/run default` runs them on x86-64 ([the tests' Readme](../../../tests/Readme.md)):

* `tests/musl/clonemusl.c` waits and wakes within one address space, times out on both clocks no sooner than asked, refuses a bitset of 0 and `FUTEX_CLOCK_REALTIME` with `FUTEX_WAIT`, and checks that a timed wait a handler with `SA_RESTART` interrupts ends with `EINTR`.
* `tests/musl/threadmusl.c` runs mutexes and condition variables among four threads, and a process-shared robust mutex in shared memory that a child's thread holds into an `execve`, which the probe then locks with `EOWNERDEAD`.
* `tests/futex2probe.c` wakes a `FUTEX_WAIT` waiter with `futex_wake` and a `futex_wait` waiter with `FUTEX_WAKE`, checks masks, `EAGAIN` and deadlines, moves waiters with `FUTEX_REQUEUE`, `futex_requeue` and `FUTEX_CMP_REQUEUE`, changes and wakes with `FUTEX_WAKE_OP`, and checks the refusals of Linux 6.12.
* `tests/futexvprobe.c` waits with `futex_waitv` on several words, private and in shared memory, and checks the index a wake returns, `EAGAIN`, both clocks' deadlines and the arguments Linux refuses; `tests/wow64probe.c` makes the same call through `int $0x80`.
* `tests/musl/capmusl.c` checks that a thread without capabilities may not read the robust list of a process that has them.
* `tests/musl/nsmusl.c` checks that the first process of a new PID namespace reads its own robust list as pid 1.

In `tests/run smp`, two threads on two processors take turns through a futex word (`tests/musl/smpmusl.c`). `tests/run host` runs `tests/host/futex_hosttest.c` ([the host tests](../../../tests/host/Readme.md)), which checks what `futex.c` reads of a thread that may end while it works, the robust futexes a thread's end releases, a wait's deadline, including a restart one tick before it, which interrupted calls are restarted, and the bucket locks: a requeued waiter's unqueue from the bucket it was moved to, two buckets' locks taken in address order, a wake that clears the waiter's bucket last, a word that is not there under the lock, and the big lock the PI operations take and the others do not.

`futex_pi_hosttest.c` and `futex_pi64_hosttest.c` exercise the PI operations and the ownership graph over modeled user pages; `sched_pi_hosttest.c` checks actual ready-queue selection under base and inherited priorities. `tests/musl/pifutexmusl.c` checks raw PI operations and pthread PI and robust PI mutexes, a condition variable over a PI mutex and process-shared owner death. The default family runs it for each native architecture and the ia32 profile. The GNU family's `pi_futex` row compiles the same source against glibc inside its guest.
