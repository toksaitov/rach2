# ext2

`ext2.c` mounts an ext2 volume through the buffer cache, as `vfat` and `exfat` do, reads and writes it, and is what `rach2.root=ext2` boots from in milestone 42's later phases.

## What is read

Revision 0 and revision 1 superblocks. Block sizes 1024, 2048 and 4096; inode sizes 128 and 256. Of the feature words the driver accepts `filetype` as the one incompatible feature and `sparse_super` and `large_file` as the read-only-compatible ones, and it ignores every compatible feature except `dir_index`. An unknown incompatible feature refuses the mount and the record names the bit; an unknown read-only-compatible feature, `dir_index`, and a block size below 4096 each mount the volume read-only with the reason in the record. `dir_index` is read-only because a writer that kept only the linear entries would leave the index stale, and the images this project makes are formatted without it; a small block is read-only because `bcache_write` writes a whole 4 KiB buffer, and two dependent metadata blocks sharing one buffer could not be written in order. A volume whose valid bit is clear or whose error bit is set mounts read-only until the mount-time check of a later phase decides otherwise.

Every field a lookup depends on is checked against the superblock and the group descriptors before it is used. The constructor requires the descriptor table to lie inside the volume, each group's two bitmaps and inode table to lie in that group, past its superblock copy and descriptors where it has them, apart from each other and past block zero, and each group's free counts to be no more than the group holds; a descriptor outside those bounds refuses the mount by name. A block pointer must lie inside the volume and outside the structural blocks: the boot block, every copy of the superblock and the descriptor table, each group's two bitmaps and its inode table; a pointer into one of those fails the read with `EIO` and a record naming the inode and the block. A directory entry must lie within its block and within the directory's size, with a `rec_len` of at least its eight-byte header and a multiple of four, its name within the entry, and its inode number within the count; a malformed entry fails the lookup or the listing with `EIO` and a record naming the directory and the offset. The volume may have at most 128 groups, 16 GiB at 4 KiB blocks; a larger one is refused by name.

A regular file is read through its direct, indirect, double-indirect and triple-indirect blocks, with a hole read as zeros. The kernel's file offset is 32 bits: an inode whose size does not fit answers `EOVERFLOW` to `open` and `stat`, is not written to, and is left exactly as it is on disk, high word included. A symbolic link shorter than sixty bytes is in the inode's pointer words, a longer one, up to a block less one byte, in its first block, and a slow link without that block answers `EIO`; `readlink` answers `ENAMETOOLONG` when the buffer cannot hold the target. A device node reports its numbers in `stat` from the first pointer word in the old encoding, or from the second when the first is zero, and cannot be opened, because devices live in `/dev`; that is what `MS_NODEV` means here, and it is enforced by never opening one. Owners, modes, link counts and the three timestamps are the inode's, in seconds, with the year 2038 limit the format has. The owner's high sixteen bits are read from the inode's second half.

`readdir` lists every entry with an inode, `.` and `..` included, with `is_dir` from the entry's type where `filetype` is on and from the inode otherwise. The directory cursor is a byte offset into the directory. `path` rebuilds a directory's path from its `..` entry and the parent's entry that names it, for a directory descriptor's `*at` calls. `statfs` reports the format's magic, the block size, the block count, the free count summed from the group descriptors at mount, and a name limit of 255.

## Writing

Metadata is written through and data is written back, as [vfs.Readme.md](vfs.Readme.md) describes for the cache. Every inode, bitmap, descriptor, pointer block and directory block goes through `bcache_write` and reaches the disk before the call returns, in call order, so the order in which the driver writes metadata is the order in which it reaches the disk. A file's bytes go through `bcache_dirty` and reach the disk at eviction, at `fsync`, at `sync` and through the periodic sync; where a size must not cover bytes that have not reached the disk, the driver syncs the device before it writes the size. Every operation that changes the volume runs under the mount's namespace lock for its whole length.

The promise is the one the format allows without a journal: at every stop between two completed sector writes, no data is lost or shared outside the range the interrupted operation was itself changing or removing, and `e2fsck` repairs the volume without discarding a reachable file's contents. It is not a promise that every stop is structurally clean; the stops below leave what `e2fsck` reports as a count, a block-map or a topology repair, and the interruption matrix in `tests/run host` allows exactly those kinds and no other.

* An inode is freed by writing it zeroed with its deletion time, then clearing its bit and its group's count; it is allocated by setting its bit and lowering the count, then writing it initialised, then naming it. A stop between the last two leaves an allocated inode with no name, an orphan.
* A block is allocated in its bitmap, with its group's count lowered and the descriptor written through, before a pointer names it; a pointer block is allocated, zeroed and written before the pointer to it; a block given out for part of a write is zeroed before the bytes land, since it holds whatever was freed there; the pointers and `i_blocks` are written, the data synced, and `i_size` written last. A stop between the pointers and the size leaves blocks past the end, a block-map repair; a stop before the pointer leaves a lost block, a bitmap repair.
* An entry is added by writing the new entry into the slack behind an existing name, where the old record still covers it, and then shortening the old record to end where the new one begins; a stop between leaves the volume as it was. A directory grows by a block zeroed with one empty entry spanning it, allocated and chained with `i_blocks`, then covered by the size, then written with the entry.
* An entry is removed before the inode's link count is lowered, the first entry of a block by clearing its inode number and any other by merging it into its predecessor's record. The inode is freed, with its blocks, when the count reaches zero and nothing holds it; a held node keeps its inode and blocks until its last `put`, which is what makes an open unlinked file work, and a crash while it is held leaves an orphan.
* A truncation writes the smaller size first, zeroes the tail of the last block kept, then clears the pointers from the end, each written through before its block is freed, with `i_blocks` following; a stop between leaves blocks past the end. A growth writes the size over a hole.
* `mkdir` allocates the inode's bit, writes the new directory's block with `.` and `..`, then its inode with a link count of two and its size, then the parent's count, and the parent's entry last. `rmdir` is the reverse, on a directory with nothing but `.` and `..`, and refuses one a description or a working directory holds with `EBUSY`.
* `link` raises the count, then writes the name; a stop between is a count one too high. `symlink` writes a target shorter than sixty bytes into the inode's pointer words, a longer one into a block written before the inode, and the name last.
* `rename` of a file within a mount is a `link` followed by an `unlink`: the count is raised, the new entry written, the old entry removed, the count lowered, so the file has a name at every stop. A rename whose source and destination are one inode changes nothing. A rename onto an existing file raises the source's count, rewrites the destination entry to the source's inode, lowers the victim's count and frees it as an unlink does, then removes the old entry and lowers the source's count; the stop after the rewrite leaves the victim an orphan. A rename onto an existing empty directory is refused unless the source is a directory, and then the victim goes as `rmdir`'s does before the move. A directory may not be moved into itself or a descendant, which the walk from the destination's parent to the root decides before anything is written, with `EINVAL`. A directory's rename across parents writes the new entry, rewrites its `..`, raises the new parent's count, removes the old entry and lowers the old parent's count; the stops inside leave the directory with two names or with a `..` naming one parent while an entry names another, which `e2fsck` repairs as topology.

The superblock's free counts are summaries the group descriptors hold exactly, and they are written at `sync` and at unmount; the descriptors are written through with their bitmaps. Mounting for writing clears `s_state`'s valid bit with `bcache_write` before the mount is published, and `prepare_unmount` sets it again after everything has reached the disk and only when the error bit is clear. A mount that went read-only after a failed write leaves the volume as it is, for the next mount's check.

An inode is allocated in the parent's group when it has one free, else in the first group that has one. A block is allocated first-fit from the block after the last one given out, wrapping once through every group. `ENOSPC` is decided under the mount's lock before any bitmap is written, and the root's reserved blocks are refused to a caller who is not root. A file's bytes are bounded by the kernel's offset: a write that would pass 4 GiB less one answers `EFBIG`. A name of 256 bytes answers `ENAMETOOLONG`; a symbolic link's target may be up to 511 bytes.

## Symbolic links

The walker follows a symbolic link when it is not the last component. For the last component the operation decides, as on Linux: `open` follows unless `O_NOFOLLOW` is given, in which case a link answers `ELOOP`, or `O_CREAT|O_EXCL` is given, in which case a link, dangling or not, answers `EEXIST`; `stat`, `access`, `chdir`, `execve`, `truncate`, `chmod`, `chown` and `utimensat` without `AT_SYMLINK_NOFOLLOW` follow; `lstat`, `readlink`, `unlink`, `rename`, `link`, `symlink` and `rmdir` do not. A followed link's target replaces the component, an absolute target restarts at the root, and the walk continues from the directory the link was found in, with a bound of forty links along one path, after which `ELOOP`. `..` is physical, so `link/..` goes to the parent of the target, as it does on Linux, and `..` after a link to a file answers `ENOTDIR`. `symlink`, `symlinkat` and `readlinkat` are real syscalls, and `link` and `linkat` are real on ext2, with `EPERM` for a directory, `EXDEV` across mounts and `EROFS` on a read-only mount; on KRFFS and the synthetic types both answer `EPERM`. [vfs.Readme.md](vfs.Readme.md) has the resolver.

## Limits

| Limit | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Block sizes | 1024, 2048, 4096 read; 4096 written |
| Inode sizes | 128, 256 |
| Groups | 128, which is 16 GiB at 4 KiB blocks |
| Largest file | 4 GiB less one, the kernel's 32-bit offset; a larger one answers `EOVERFLOW` and is left as it is |
| Symbolic links along one path | 40 |
| A symbolic link's target | 511 bytes, the resolver's bound; the format allows a block less one |
| A followed target and the rest of its path | 511 bytes; `ENAMETOOLONG` past that |
| Name | 255 bytes, the format's and the kernel's |
| Timestamps | 32-bit seconds, until 2038 |
| Held nodes per mount | 64, a pool like FAT32's; `ENOMEM` beyond |
| Mounts at once | 4 |

## What stays out

ext3's journal and ext4's extents, 64-bit block numbers, checksums, flexible groups and large inodes: refused or read-only by the feature rules, which is what the three feature words are for. Extended attributes and access lists: ignored on read. Quotas: no. `mknod`: no, devices live in `/dev`.

## Verification

`tests/run host` runs `ext2_hosttest`: the driver over the buffer cache over volumes the host's `mke2fs` made from a staged tree at 4 KiB blocks with 128- and 256-byte inodes, at 2 KiB, and at revision 0 without `filetype`, with `debugfs` adding device nodes in both encodings and a size past the kernel's offset; every file reads back against the tree and, on the profile volume, every node against `setidcheck`'s reader; a 1 KiB volume has a sparse file with a byte behind each pointer level and mounts read-only with the reason recorded; a `dir_index` volume, an unknown read-only feature and a clear valid bit mount read-only with the reason recorded; an unknown incompatible feature, no magic, a free count past the group and tables placed where a group's cannot be refuse the mount by name; a block past the volume, a block inside the descriptors, a block inside the inode table, an entry whose `rec_len` runs past its block, an entry past the directory's size, an entry naming an inode past the count and a slow link without its block fail by name; a slow link of 1021 bytes reads; a size of 4 GiB less one reads and one past it answers `EOVERFLOW`; and the sixty-third node held answers `ENOMEM` until one is put. The writer's rows run thirteen sequences on a fresh volume each, create and write, extension through the pointer levels, a write past the end, truncate, unlink, mkdir and rmdir, rename, rename over a file, a directory renamed across parents and over an empty one, link, symlink, an open unlinked file and three hundred files; each reads back through the driver, unmounts, is called clean by `e2fsck -fn`, is walked by the guard's reader, and reads back the same after a remount; `debugfs` lists what the driver wrote. The refusals decided before anything is written, a name of 256 bytes, a write past the offset and the reserve to a caller who is not root, leave the volume byte for byte as it was; the sixty-fifth node answers `ENOMEM`; the superblock's counts stay as they were through a sequence and are written at `sync`, and the valid bit is cleared by the mount and set by the unmount. The interruption matrix then stops every sequence after each of its writes, on a volume whose free blocks hold junk, and judges the stop three ways: the volume mounts with the base files intact and the sequence's files reading only what was written or zeros, and a renamed or linked file reachable by a name at every stop past the operation's start; `e2fsck -fn` reports nothing but the count, block-map, orphan and topology repairs the order of writes lists; and `e2fsck -fy` on a copy repairs it with the base files intact and the file's name kept. A base file changed under a repaired copy fails the comparison. `tests/run ext2` boots the harness kernel beside a fixture disk `tests/system/mkext2fixture.sh` wrote and runs `ext2probe`'s link rows on the mounted volume ([tests/system/Readme.md](../../../tests/system/Readme.md)).
