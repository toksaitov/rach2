# exFAT

`exfat.c` is the `exfat` type. It reads an exFAT volume in the partition window that `mount(2)` places, as [vfs.Readme.md](vfs.Readme.md) describes, through the buffer cache in 4 KiB blocks, one buffer held at a time. exFAT is the file system every flash drive above 32 GB and every SDXC card is sold with; this driver reads it from Microsoft's published specification, and what it shares with the FAT32 driver (the name check and conversion, the DOS timestamp fields, the allocation hint, the dirty-volume walk's bitmap and lock, the mount options) lives in `fatcommon.c`. It writes as well, under the rules below.

## The volume check

The constructor reads the boot sector and checks it field by field before the mount is published; a volume that fails is refused with `EINVAL` and a record naming the field, `exfat: refused: FIELD`. Both regions' checksums are read first, and the fields that are checked are those of the region the mount trusts (below). In order: `EXFAT   ` at offset 3; the jump `EB 76 90`; fifty-three zero bytes at 11; the boot signature; a bytes-per-sector shift of exactly 9, because every block device here is 512-byte sectors and a volume whose logical sector is larger is a different layout in every offset, so it is a valid exFAT this kernel does not read, refused by name; a sectors-per-cluster shift that keeps a cluster within 32 MiB; one or two FATs; a file-system revision of 1.00; a volume length within the device and of at least 1 MiB; a FAT offset at or past sector 24, so that both boot regions lie before it; a nonzero cluster count at most 2^32 less 11; a FAT length of at least `(ClusterCount + 2) * 4` bytes, so that every cluster has an entry; a cluster heap that follows the FATs; a cluster count that fits the heap; a root cluster within the heap; and an active FAT the volume has. `PartitionOffset` is read by nothing: the window does not know its first sector.

Both boot regions are checked, always, before any field is trusted: the checksum over the eleven sectors before each region's checksum sector, skipping `VolumeFlags` and `PercentInUse`, which a mount changes, every word of the checksum sector repeating it, and the backup boot sector compared with the main in every field but those. Both good and agreeing: the mount proceeds. Main bad and backup good: the mount proceeds read-only from the backup's fields, with the main's flags, which the specification says are the live ones, and with a record, because the main copy is what the next driver will trust. Main good and backup bad, or the two disagreeing: read-only with the disagreement named, because a writer that trusted one of two conflicting descriptions could be wrong. Both bad: refused. A volume with two FATs is TexFAT and mounts read-only; so does one whose flags say `MediaFailure`. The percent in use is read and never trusted. The record is `exfat: mounted read-only: WHY`.

## The root and what it must hold

The root directory is read next, through its FAT chain; a chain that leaves the heap or loops is refused. It must hold the allocation bitmap of the active FAT and one up-case table entry, and a second of either is refused, each with a first cluster in the heap and a length that fits, the bitmap's at least `ceil(ClusterCount / 8)` bytes; a volume missing either is refused with the object named. A volume label is optional and read by nothing. A benign primary entry, such as a volume GUID, is kept byte for byte. A bitmap entry for the other FAT, which only TexFAT has, keeps the mount read-only. A critical entry of a type this driver does not know is passed over by the reader; what it means for a writer is phase 4's.

## The up-case table

The table is the volume's, and every comparison and every name hash goes through it, for every UTF-16 unit, because that is what exFAT's case-insensitivity means; ASCII folding is not enough. The bitmap and the table are read through their FAT chains, since their entries carry no `NoFatChain`. At mount the stored table is read once through the cache and checked: its checksum over the stored bytes must match the entry, and its runs must expand to exactly 65,536 mappings, where a `0xFFFF` followed by a count is that many identity mappings, anything else maps the next unit, a run of zero length or one past the end is a fault, and a `0xFFFF` as the very last unit is the mapping of U+FFFF to itself, which the recommended table ends with; and the first 128 mappings are the mandatory ones, each code point to itself but `a` to `z` to `A` to `Z`. A table whose stored form is at most 16 KiB is kept per mount in that form and up-cased by walking its runs. A larger table is not held: an uncompressed one, which is exactly 128 KiB of explicit units with no run marker, is indexed through the cache at unit times two; a compressed one larger than 16 KiB, whatever its size, is streamed from its beginning through the cache for every unit, which is correct and slow. The record `exfat: up-case table: FORM` says which form the mount has.

## Entry sets, names and chains

A file is a set: the file entry, then exactly one stream extension in the second position, then one name entry per fifteen units, then any benign secondaries. The set is checked before it is trusted: `SecondaryCount` is 2 to 18; the set checksum over every byte of every entry but its own two matches; `NameLength` is at least one and its `ceil(NameLength / 15)` name entries follow the stream at once, with the units past it zero, and only benign secondaries after them, a second stream or a critical secondary this driver does not know being a fault; the reserved flag bits are zero; `ValidDataLength` is at most `DataLength`, and a directory's two lengths are equal and at most 256 MiB; a `NoFatChain` stream has a length; the stream's `AllocationPossible` is set, as the specification requires of a stream; a stream with a nonzero length has a first cluster in the heap and one with no length has none; a `NoFatChain` stream's run fits the heap; and the name hash equals the hash of the up-cased name. A set that fails any of these is passed over as one unit, `SecondaryCount` and all, with a record, `exfat: set skipped: WHY`, and the directory's other sets are read; a set whose only failing check is its checksum is passed over the same way until the dirty-volume check rewrites it. A set may cross a sector and a cluster boundary, and the reader follows the directory's chain across both. Benign secondaries are kept and counted; a vendor allocation entry names clusters the walk marks, and its contents are never interpreted.

A stream with `NoFatChain` set is contiguous and its FAT is never read; one without it follows the FAT, where the end of a chain is `0xFFFFFFFF`, a bad cluster `0xFFFFFFF7`, and a value outside the heap, or a cluster that names itself, ends the chain with `EIO`, under the same bound on steps as FAT32; a longer loop within a file's length repeats its clusters, and the dirty-volume check is what finds it. A chain that ends before the file's length answers `EIO` at the first byte it cannot reach, after the bytes it can. Bytes past `ValidDataLength` and up to `DataLength` read as zeros, whatever the disk holds there, and the file's size is `DataLength`.

Lookup converts the name (`fatcommon.c`: at most 255 bytes of UTF-8 in at most 255 UTF-16 units, none of the characters FAT forbids, which are the control characters below U+0020 and `" * / : < > ? \\ |`, no trailing dot or space), hashes it through the up-case table, and compares by hash first and then by up-cased units. `.` is the directory itself. exFAT directories have no dot entries, so `..` is the directory this one was found in, which its node remembers; when that node has been put, the parent is found again by a bounded walk of the tree from the root. A name of 255 units that does not fit the kernel's 255-byte component is listed by its first bytes and unreachable by path, as on FAT32.

## Writing

Metadata is written through and data is written back, as [vfs.Readme.md](vfs.Readme.md) describes for the cache, and every mutation runs under the mount's namespace lock, which the driver asserts at each. The order of writes is FAT32's with the bitmap in the FAT's place: a cluster's bitmap bit is set and its chain written before any entry refers to it; data is written and synced before a length is published; a set is unpublished before its clusters are freed, and the clusters of a node a description holds are freed at its last `put` and not before; a truncation shortens the stream before it frees the tail; a directory grows by a zeroed, chained cluster before an entry is written into it.

A set is made or unmade by one bit, the in-use bit of its file entry. Creation writes the name entries, then the stream extension, then the file entry last, with its in-use bit and its checksum over the whole; a stop before the last write leaves secondaries with no primary, which every reader passes over and the check frees. When a set's entries lie in two sectors, the sector without the file entry is written first. Every set this driver makes has its file and stream entries in one sector: when the free run in a directory would put the file entry in a sector's last slot, an inactive entry fills that slot and the set begins in the next sector. A change that touches only those two entries, which is every length, time, flag and attribute change, is then one sector write, the unit the guarantee is built on. On a set another writer made whose two entries straddle a sector, the change is two writes, the stream's sector first and the file entry's last, and the stop between them leaves a set whose checksum alone fails, which the check repairs by rewriting it, as `fsck.exfat` does. Removal writes the file entry inactive, the barrier, then the secondaries inactive, and the chain and its bits go at the node's last `put`. A rename within one directory that keeps the entry count rewrites the names and then the file entry's sector; any other rename finds the room at the destination first, so `ENOSPC` destroys nothing, writes the new set there with its file entry inactive, unpublishes a replaced destination (its clusters kept for its holders, as unlink does) and then the source, and publishes the new file entry last. A stop after the source is unpublished leaves a nameless chain with its data intact, which the check counts and a host tool recovers: the one way a crash costs a name.

Every file and directory this driver creates has a FAT chain and `NoFatChain` clear, which every reader accepts. A contiguous file or directory it extends has its run written into the FAT first, its stream entry changed with the flag clear, and only then grows. `ValidDataLength` equals `DataLength` for everything written here; a shrink sets it to the smaller of its old value and the new length; a growth by `truncate` leaves it, so the new range reads as zeros without a write; a write beyond it zero-fills and syncs the gap first, the part inside the last allocated cluster included, and publishes the new lengths after. A cluster is allocated first-fit in the bitmap from the cluster after a file's last, so a file written in one go is contiguous, and `ENOSPC` is decided before anything is written. A name is checked as on FAT32 and hashed through the up-case table; two names that up-case alike in one directory are one name (`EEXIST`). Timestamps are written as UTC with the offset byte valid and zero: creation once, modification at every write with its 10 ms field, access never advanced.

`VolumeDirty` is set in the main boot sector when a writable mount is constructed and stays set for its life, as Linux does; `sync` writes the percent in use beside it, and the clean unmount, after every dirty buffer is written, clears it last, so a failure anywhere before leaves the volume marked dirty. The backup boot region is never written, and the boot checksum skips the bytes that change. A directory found to hold a critical entry this driver does not know, when it is first read to its end before a change, is frozen: every change inside it answers `EROFS` with a record, and it cannot be removed or renamed; the rest of the volume is unaffected. The write-side limits: a file of 4 GiB less one, the kernel's file offset; a directory of 256 MiB, the format's.

## A dirty volume at mount

A volume whose dirty bit is set was written by something that did not unmount it. Before a writable mount is published, and under the walk bitmap the FAT drivers share, one walk at a time, the tree is walked: the root's chain, the bitmap's and the up-case table's are marked first, disjoint and allocated; then every set the tree reaches, in every directory, is checked as the reader checks it and its chains marked, a contiguous stream counted by length, a chained one followed, a vendor allocation's clusters marked. A cluster reached twice, a chain that leaves the heap or loops, a stream shorter than its length, a reached cluster whose bitmap bit is clear, an in-use set that fails any check but its checksum, a critical entry this driver does not know, and two names that up-case alike in one directory are faults: the volume mounts read-only with the first one named, `exfat: check: WHY; read-only`, and nothing is written. A clean volume is trusted as every other driver trusts it.

Three states this driver's own order leaves behind a crash are repaired once the whole tree is walked, each repair a metadata write, in an order a second crash cannot make worse: a set failing only its checksum, which a stop inside a straddling change leaves, has its checksum rewritten; orphan secondaries, which a stop before a file entry's write leaves, are written inactive; and a surplus tail, which a stop between a chain's join and its length's publish leaves, is detached at the last cluster the length justifies and then freed, since those clusters were never published. Then the bitmap is compared with the walk: a bit set that nothing reached is lost, counted, recorded and left allocated, because a nameless chain after a crash may be a file a rename had unpublished and not yet republished, and its bytes are the only copy; a host checker recovers such chains into files, and this driver does not. The free count is the bitmap's after the repairs. The dirty bit stays set through the mount and is cleared by the clean unmount as usual.

## Nodes, times and modes

A node is an entry in a fixed per-mount pool of 32, freed at its last put; a lookup that finds the pool full answers `ENOMEM` and logs it. A directory's identity is its first cluster; a file's is its directory's cluster and the index of its file entry, and the inode number is built from the same fields: a directory's is its cluster, a file's has its directory's cluster plus one in the high word and its index plus one in the low.

Timestamps are decoded from their bit fields, which are FAT32's date and time in one word, with the year based at 1980, the 10 ms increment added where the field exists, and the UTC offset applied when its valid bit is set: the field is local time and the offset is the zone's, in quarter hours as a signed seven-bit count, so UTC is the field less the offset. A timestamp without a valid offset was written by something that did not record its zone; it is reported as if it were UTC, which is the same approximation FAT32 makes, and is not a conversion. Every entry reports the mount's `uid` and `gid` and the mode `0777` less its `umask`, the options `umask=`, `uid=`, `gid=` and `ro` that vfat takes; an entry with the read-only attribute reports no write bits. `statfs` reports the bitmap's zero bits as free, counted once and kept, with `bsize` the cluster size and the magic `0x2011BAB0` Linux reports.

## Limits

Four volumes can be mounted at once. Clusters up to 32 MiB, the format's limit. A logical sector of 512 bytes; any other is refused by name, and the SDXC cards and drives formatted with 4 KiB sectors are the media that means. A file of 2^64 bytes on the format; 4 GiB less one reachable here, because the kernel's file offset is 32 bits. The 2^20-bit walk bitmap shared with FAT32 covers 128 GB at 128 KiB clusters; a dirty volume beyond it mounts read-only and says why.

## Verification

`tests/run exfat` is six boots on a disk whose second partition is an exFAT volume: a program writes a tree of every shape and unmounts it, the host's reader walks the volume and reads its bytes, and `fsck_exfat` or `fsck.exfat` judges it where the host has one; the next boot reads every byte back; a boot killed inside a write sequence leaves the dirty bit set, and the next mount checks and repairs the volume and records it; BusyBox copies, moves and removes through the shell; and two writers and a sync interleave under the lag device. `tests/run host` runs `exfatcheck_hosttest` over `mkexfatfixture`'s volumes ([tests/host/Readme.md](../../../tests/host/Readme.md)) and `exfat_hosttest` over the driver: the well-formed volume must read back what the builder wrote through every shape (the contiguous file, the file chained over clusters that are not adjacent, the tail past `ValidDataLength` as zeros, the empty file, the name of four entries, the set with vendor entries, the set straddling a sector, the set crossing a cluster boundary, the directory of two levels), a name is found by another case through the table, in each of the table's three forms; each malformed variant is refused at mount with the field named, mounts read-only with the reason named, has its set skipped, or fails at the file it damages; and the gate: a volume the host's own tools formatted (`newfs_exfat` through `hdiutil`) and filled through the host's own driver mounts and lists every file in its manifest with the right bytes and times, where the host has those tools, and says so where it does not.
