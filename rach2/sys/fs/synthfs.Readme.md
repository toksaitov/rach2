# Synthetic file systems

The kernel makes `/dev`, `/proc` and `/sys` from its own tables each time a program opens, stats or lists them, and stores none of the three. `/dev` gives the devices, `/proc` the processes and the system's counters as text, and `/sys` the device records that Linux's tools read. They exist so that musl, BusyBox, util-linux and the GNU programs find the files they open on Linux, at the same paths and in the same formats. [synthfs.c](synthfs.c) turns a path into a kind and its coordinates, [devfs.c](devfs.c) implements the devices, [procfs.c](procfs.c) writes the text of `/proc` and `/sys`, and the system-call layer, [../syscall_fs.c](../syscall_fs.c), opens, stats and lists all three.

A dma-buf descriptor's `/proc/PID/fdinfo/N` includes its `name:` once `DMA_BUF_SET_NAME` has supplied one; [dma-buf(7)](../../../man/man7/dma-buf.7) gives the request's contract.

`/proc/interrupts` has one count column per online processor. `/proc/irq/N` exposes each enabled PCI message's requested and effective affinity masks, with the Linux hexadecimal format and a privileged write of `smp_affinity` to move the interrupt. `effective_affinity` is read-only. [proc(5)](../../../man/man5/proc.5) gives the masks, errors and default-mask behavior.

## Interfaces

On x86-64, `/proc/stat` also reports KVM's live VM, vCPU, slot, guest-physical table-page and run-page mapping-object counts, which [the KVM Readme](../kvm/Readme.md) gives.

`install_permanent` in [vfs.c](vfs.c) mounts the three at boot as permanent entries of the mount table; the table in [vfs.Readme.md](vfs.Readme.md) gives their slots and flags. Each has a device number of its own:

| Path | Type | `st_dev` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/dev` | `devfs` | 5 (`DEVFS_FSID`) |
| `/proc` | `proc` | 6 (`PROCFS_FSID`) |
| `/sys` | `sysfs` | 7 (`SYSFS_FSID`) |

A namespace file reached through `/proc/PID/ns` has device 4 (`NSFS_DEV`), as on Linux. `statfs` gives each mount Linux's magic number for its type ([statfs(2)](../../../man/man2/statfs.2)).

A description opened by a synthetic path keeps the mount the walk reached in `synth_mnt`. Both statx with `AT_EMPTY_PATH` and `/proc/PID/fdinfo` report that mount's id, including a bind mount or a mount placed over the permanent one. A relative lookup from a held proc directory uses its mount even after another mount covers it. The copied pathname holds that mount until the walk ends. The fd and fdinfo directories keep the original process lifetime as well.

The manual pages give what each file contains and what each call on it returns:

* [null(4)](../../../man/man4/null.4) (`null`, `zero`, `full`), [random(4)](../../../man/man4/random.4) (`random`, `urandom`) and [mem(4)](../../../man/man4/mem.4).
* [console(4)](../../../man/man4/console.4) (`console`, `tty`, `ptmx`, `pts/N`) and [bootlog(4)](../../../man/man4/bootlog.4) (`bootlog`, `screenlog`, `framefail`).
* [disk(4)](../../../man/man4/disk.4) (`diskN`, `diskNpM`, `bootpart`, `grubcore`), [loop(4)](../../../man/man4/loop.4) (`loop0` to `loop3`, `loop-control`) and [rtc(4)](../../../man/man4/rtc.4) (`rtc0`); their block requests are in [ioctl(2)](../../../man/man2/ioctl.2).
* [input(4)](../../../man/man4/input.4) (`input/eventN`), [mixer(4)](../../../man/man4/mixer.4) (`mixer`) and [dsp(4)](../../../man/man4/dsp.4) (`dsp`), whose operations are those of [`../evdev.h`](../evdev.h), [`../audio/mixer.h`](../audio/mixer.h) and [`../audio/dsp.h`](../audio/dsp.h).
* [wsim(4)](../../../man/man4/wsim.4) (`wsim`), the medium of a software radio, whose operations are those of [`../network/wireless/wsim.h`](../network/wireless/wsim.h).
* [busybox-sysklogd(8)](../../../man/man8/busybox-sysklogd.8): `/dev/log`, a link to `/var/run/log` (`SYNTH_DEV_LOG_TARGET`), where `syslogd` makes its socket.
* [proc(5)](../../../man/man5/proc.5): every file of `/proc`, and `/dev/fd`, `/dev/fd/N`, `/dev/stdin`, `/dev/stdout` and `/dev/stderr`, which are the caller's `/proc/self/fd` and its links.
* [sysfs(5)](../../../man/man5/sysfs.5): every file of `/sys`.

Other parts of the kernel call devfs:

* `disk_register` (`../block/disk.c`) makes a new disk record's nodes with `devfs_disk_nodes`, and each scan of its table renames them (`devfs_disk_part_nodes`). `devfs_disk_publish` publishes the whole-disk window of every registered disk at boot (`publish_disks` in `../../kernel.c`) and of a stick when it arrives (`../block/usbdisk.c`).
* `discover_boot_devices` in `kernel.c` publishes `/dev/bootpart` and `/dev/grubcore`. The disk root's mount withdraws `/dev/bootpart` when the root is on that partition, and the debug shell's `ahci` command withdraws every window before it probes the controller again.
* `mount(2)` (`vfs_do_mount` in `vfs.c`) and the kernel's ext2 root turn a `/dev` path into a sector range with `devfs_window_of` and claim it with `devfs_claim`.
* An open of a device node on a disk file system opens the devfs device of the same kind and number (`open_device` in `syscall_fs.c`, `devfs_name_of`), and fails with `ENXIO` when devfs has none.

Wireless radio attributes and the network interface's `phy80211` and `device/driver` entries are described in [`../network/wireless/Readme.md`](../network/wireless/Readme.md). They are present only while the radio is registered and visible in the caller's network namespace.

## Design

### Classification

`synth_classify` in `synthfs.c` takes a path below the root directory, without its leading slash, and fills a `struct vpath` (`synthfs.h`): a `kind`, one of `enum vnode_kind`, and the coordinates that kind uses, such as a device's leaf, a process (`SYNTH_PID_SELF`, -1, for `self`), a descriptor, or a `/sys` node and its device. `VNODE_KRFFS` means the path is not synthetic; `VNODE_ENOENT` and `VNODE_ENOTDIR` are the two misses.

The classifier reads the characters of the path and no kernel state, so it compiles hosted as well, and `tests/host/synthfs_hosttest.c` runs it under the sanitizers. Whether a device, process, descriptor or PCI function exists is checked afterwards by the caller: a two-component `/dev` path other than `pts`, a directory of the table below, `fd`, a standard stream or `log` is `VNODE_DEV` whatever its leaf, and devfs looks the leaf up; so is `/dev/DIR/NAME` for such a directory, by the leaf `DIR/NAME`, as `/dev/input/event0` is `input/event0` and `/dev/dri/card0` is `dri/card0`.

The directories of `/dev` other than `pts` are rows of a table in `synthfs.c` (`synth_dev_dir_at`, `synth_dev_dir_find`, `synth_dev_dir_child`): `input`, inode 5, present always, as Linux's is, `dri`, inode 6, and `vfio`, inode 7, present only while devfs has a node in it, and `cpu`, inode 0xF00. A row may be in another row: `cpu/0` to `cpu/63`, inodes 0xF01 to 0xF40, are `/dev/cpu`'s, one for each processor, which hold its `msr` ([msr(4)](../../../man/man4/msr.4)); a row is present also while a row in it is, so `/dev/cpu` is there while any processor's is. A row's directory classifies as `VNODE_DEV_SUBDIR` with the row in the `vpath`'s `devdir`; `devfs_subdir_present` says whether it is there, and `synth_rest`, `stat`, `open` and the listing of `/dev` ask it, so an absent `/dev/dri` is `ENOENT` everywhere. A descriptor open on a row's directory keeps the row in its record's `pid`. A directory a later driver needs, such as `vfio`, is a new row. A path that goes on past a file is `VNODE_ENOTDIR`, and its `vpath` keeps that file's process, pty, device leaf or `/sys` device; `synth_rest` gives `ENOENT` instead when that is gone (`synth_past_present`), as Linux does for a path past a missing file. A path is split into at most `SYNTH_MAXC` + 1 = 11 components, one more than the ten a path through drivers' entries may have, such as a connector's file reached through `/sys/dev/char`, so a deeper path misses instead of being taken for a shorter one.

The names are tables in `synthfs.c`: the files of a process (`synth_pid_file`, 20 besides `exe`, `cwd` and `root`, and `children` in a thread's directory under `task` alone, as on Linux), the global files of `/proc` (`synth_global_file`), the files of `/proc/net`, `/proc/sysvipc` and `/proc/PID/ns`, the attribute files of each `/sys` directory (`sysfs_attr`), and `/proc/sys`, the 90 entries of `sysents`. Entry 0 is `/proc/sys` itself, and every other entry has its directory's index and, for a file, its text, which is empty for one whose value procfs reads when the file is opened: the host and domain names, the variables of `sysctl_ints` in `procfs.c` (the limits of inotify, `vm.max_map_count`, `kernel.overflowuid` and `overflowgid`, `fs.nr_open`, `kernel.randomize_va_space`, TCP's `tcp_keepalive_time`, `tcp_keepalive_intvl`, `tcp_keepalive_probes`, `tcp_mtu_probing` and `tcp_retries2`), `user.max_mnt_namespaces`, `fs.file-max`, `fs.file-nr`, `fs.pipe-max-size`, `fs.pipe-user-pages-soft` and `fs.pipe-user-pages-hard`, which are unsigned, and the UUIDs of `kernel/random`. New entries go at the end, so that the indices `SYNTH_SYS_*` name keep their values.

A `/sys` device is classified by its name as a process is by its digits. `sys_pci_key` turns `0000:BB:DD.F` into `BB << 8 | DD << 3 | F`, below 2^16. `sys_usb_key` turns `B-P.P...` into the controller less 1 in bits 28 and 29, the root port in bits 20 to 27 and up to five hub ports in four bits each, at least 2^20, so no USB key equals a PCI one. A backlight is classified by its name too, which `backlight_find` (`../display/backlight.c`) looks up, and its key is its index; a radio switch by its name `rfkillN`, which `rfkill_lookup` (`../rfkill.h`) looks up, and its key is N. Each directory of `/sys` has a node number, `SYSFS_ROOT` (0) to `SYSFS_THP` (59), and `SYSFS_REG` (42) stands for the entries drivers add, keyed by their handles; a keyed node (`SYSFS_KEYED`: a PCI function, a USB device, a network interface, a backlight, a processor, its `topology` or its `cache`, a cache's `indexM`, keyed N << 4 | M, a disk node, a radio, a radio switch or a driver's entry) exists only while its device or entry does.

### Routing

The three mounts cover no node of the root file system. The resolver enters each by its name in the root directory and gives the rest of the path, in one call, to the type's `lookup_rest` (`synth_lookup_rest` in `vfs.c`). `synth_rest` resolves `.` and `..` lexically, since the namespace is a table of names and not a tree of nodes, except that a `..` after a link a driver added to `/sys` climbs from the directory the link leads to (`synth_dotdot`, `synth_sys_link_dir`), as Linux's walk climbs from a link's target, so that libdrm's `/sys/dev/char/226:N/device/../subsystem` names the bus of the card's device's parent; a path that ends on or goes on past such a link whose directory lies outside the walk's root, as below a bind of `/sys/bus/virtio/devices`, returns `VFS_LINK`, and the resolver follows the link's text from its directory through the mounts above, as Linux does, except at the end of a walk that does not follow its last link, as `lstat`'s and `readlink`'s, which stays at the link; it puts in front the path of the mount's root, `dev`, `dev/pts`, `proc` or `sys`, followed for a bind of a place inside one by that place's path, and classifies the result, built in a path page of 4096 bytes and shorter than that (`ENAMETOOLONG`), so that a name of `/dev/virtio-ports` of `NAME_MAX` bytes resolves. A `..` above the mount returns `VFS_CLIMB`, and the resolver takes that `..` at the mount's root as it takes one at the root of any mount. The nameless mounts in `/dev`, `shm` and `mqueue`, are entered by the resolver's ordinary step before devfs is given the path (`named_in_rest`); `synth_rest` returns `VFS_ROOT` for such a name met at the mount's root later in a path, as in `/dev/pts/../shm`, and `VFS_LINK` for a path that goes on past `/proc/PID/fd/N`, `cwd` or `root`, or past `/dev/fd/N` or `/dev/stdin`, `stdout` and `stderr`, which the resolver follows to the file or directory the link refers to ([vfs.Readme.md](vfs.Readme.md)).

A resolved node is a `struct synth_node`, the `vpath` and a reference count, from the heap (`ENOMEM` when it has no room), freed when its last holder puts it: it lasts one call, except a mount's root, which lasts as long as the mount. `synth_getattr` gives the resolver only what a walk needs: a directory, of mode 0755 in `/dev` and 0555 in `/proc` and `/sys`; a link (`exe`, `cwd`, `root` and `/dev/log`); or a file. `/proc/self` and the device links of `/sys` are classified, not resolved, so to the resolver each is the directory it leads to, but for a link above whose directory a bind's root lies, which it follows by its text, while `lstat` and `readlink` report a link. The descriptor and namespace links of `/proc/PID` are files to the resolver at the end of a path, and `stat`, `open` and `execve` follow them themselves. `/proc/PID/exe` is followed to the program's file and not to a path: `walk_exe` goes on from the mount and node the address space keeps (`procfs_exe_file`), so the link reaches the program after a rename and after its name is gone. `cwd`, `root`, and a descriptor's link with more of a path after it, are followed the same way to the directory or file they refer to (`walk_object`, `procfs_link_target`). `/dev/log` is followed by its text (`synth_readlink`).

The system-call layer reads the classification back (`vfs_synth_vpath`) and does the rest. A stat that follows `/proc/PID/fd/N` reports the open object's attributes or the error from its file system's attribute read. A synthetic type has no create, remove, rename or write operation, so the calls that need one fail with `EROFS`, after `EEXIST` for a name that exists (`synth_name_exists`), in Linux's order; the directories and links made under `/dev` are the exception (Made entries, below). `chmod` and `chown` of a pty slave store its mode and owner on the pty (`chown_synth`), of a made entry on the entry, and of a device on devfs (`chmod_dev`, `chown_dev`, `devfs_setattr`), which the next open checks, as udev sets the owner, group and mode of Linux's devtmpfs nodes; a node registered anew, such as a card that comes again, starts as root's with its driver's mode. A `/proc/PID/fd/N` link changes what its descriptor is open on, an `O_PATH` descriptor's object included, as Linux follows the link, and `fchmodat2` with `AT_EMPTY_PATH` changes an `O_PATH` descriptor's: udevd sets a node's mode by either. A `/proc/PID/fd/N` link named without following it, or by an `O_PATH` descriptor of it, has its own owner changed (`chown_proc_link`), which the descriptors open on it keep until a lookup of its path gives it its process's owner again, as Linux's `proc_setattr` and `tid_fd_revalidate` have it. Every other node has a fixed mode and owner, so root's `chmod` succeeds and changes nothing, and `chown` succeeds only for the ids the node's stat gives, or -1, by the rule every chown takes (`chown_rule`, Linux's `chown_ok` and `chgrp_ok`). A walk searches each fd or fdinfo directory before looking up its next component, and access(2) uses its chosen IDs through that search and through any magic link it follows. Failed revalidation of a closed descriptor's link detaches its held metadata. Reuse of the number then creates new metadata, while fstat and chown of an older O_PATH descriptor keep using the detached inode.

### Descriptors

`synth_open` in `syscall_fs.c` makes a descriptor of the kind's own sort:

* A device: `devfs_open` binds the registry entry's `file_ops` to an `FD_TYPE_DEVICE` descriptor whose `dev` points at the entry, so the generic read, write, poll, ioctl and close dispatch through the table and no device code is in the system-call layer. `console`, and `tty` when it is the console, give an `FD_TYPE_CONSOLE` descriptor; the pty ends come from `../tty/pty.c`.
* A directory: an `FD_TYPE_SYNTH_DIR` descriptor with a `struct synth_dir` from the heap, freed at the last close: the directory's kind, its process as the proc mount's PID namespace numbers it, and a thread under `task`, or for `/proc/sys` its entry and for `/sys` its node and key; for a process's `fd` directory it also keeps the directory's owner at the open, by which `fchdir` judges a descriptor on it once the process has ended, as Linux judges the inode it has. `synth_dir_path` makes the path from the record for the `*at` calls, `fchdir` and the `/proc/PID/fd` link. A working directory in a synthetic namespace is kept as its canonical path, with `self` replaced by the caller's number.
* A file of `/proc` or `/sys`: an `FD_TYPE_PROC` descriptor with a `struct procfs_file` (below).
* `/proc/PID/fd/N`: a file or directory of a mounted type is opened again by its node, with an open's checks (`proc_fd_reopen`), and a device by the node it was opened through, that node's mode, owner and group checked and its driver's open run again (`proc_fd_reopen_device`): its entry in `/dev`, or a device node of tmpfs or ext2, which the description keeps (`dev_node` in `fd.h`) as an `O_PATH` one keeps its node, by the type's hold, which keeps the node after an unlink; anything else shares the descriptor's own description, as `dup` does (`proc_fd_share`). `/proc/PID/ns/KIND` gives a descriptor on the namespace (`procfs_open_ns`).
* `O_PATH`: `synth_open_path` opens nothing. A directory gives an `FD_TYPE_SYNTH_DIR` descriptor with `FD_FLAG_PATH`, and anything else an `FD_TYPE_SYNTH_PATH` descriptor with a copy of the `vpath` for `fstat` and `fstatfs`, so no device is opened and no text is made.

`synth_stat` makes every `stat` answer from the classification: the namespace's device, a fixed inode, a block size of 4096, a link count of 1 except for a task directory, and for every time the boot time that `/proc/stat` gives as `btime` (`rtc_boot_epoch`): the clock's reading at boot less the uptime it was read at, moved by every step that setting the clock makes, or the steps alone when the clock was not read. The times change only when the clock is set, so that `make` never finds a synthetic file changed; a pty slave has its own access and modification times.

### devfs

`dev_table` in `devfs.c` is the table of fixed devices: 56 entries, each a leaf name, a major and a minor number, a permission mode, whether a seek goes to offset 0, and the `file_ops` an open binds. Every entry is root's, and `synth_open` checks the access asked for against its mode as for a file (`cred_access_bits`). The memory devices, the terminals, the loop devices, `mixer` (14, 0), the event devices (13, 64 + N) and `rfkill` (10, 242) have Linux's numbers; the others use 240 and 254, which Linux reserves for local and experimental use. A virtio console's nodes, `hvcN` and `vportNpM`, are the registry's ([hvc(4)](../../../man/man4/hvc.4)).

A disk node is a `struct disk_node` whose first member is a `devfs_entry`, so a pointer to the entry is a pointer to the node; `disk_node_of` tells a node from a fixed entry by its operations, `diskwin_ops`. `devfs_disk_nodes` takes 16 of the 128 records (8 disks, `DISK_MAX`, of `DISK_PARTS` + 1 windows) the first time a disk record is registered, one for the whole disk and 15 for partitions, and a released record keeps them. `devfs_disk_part_nodes` names the partition nodes after each scan of the table, from the numbers of its entries.

`bootpart` and `grubcore` exist while their windows are published, a disk node while its disk is present and its window is published, `mixer` while an audio driver offers its volume (`mixer_present`) and `input/eventN` while input device N is registered (`evdev_present`); the loop nodes are always there. Lookup (`devfs_find`), `stat`, `open` and the listing (`devfs_dirent`) apply the same test, so a device is listed exactly when it opens. The listing passes over an absent entry and goes on, since a machine can have a later entry without an earlier one, as the metal kernel has `disk0` and no `bootpart`. A device whose name has a slash is listed in its directory (`devfs_subdir_dirent`) and not in `/dev`.

The registry holds the nodes drivers make as they bind (`devfs_node_register`, `devfs_node_unregister`), as many as the heap has room for, such as `dri/card0` at 226:0, `dri/renderD128` at 226:128 and a virtio console's `vport0p1` at 245:1, with its second name `virtio-ports/NAME`. Each takes a slot, a `struct reg_slot` from the heap whose first member is a `devfs_entry`, with its own copy of the name and numbers, so a descriptor open on the node outlives its registration and a slot is never freed; `reg_slot_of` tells a slot by its address, outside the table and not a disk node's, since its operations are the driver's. The slots form one list in the order they were made, and a new one is linked at its tail once it is filled. A slot is present while its node is registered, and lookup, `stat`, `open`, `devfs_name_of` and the listings find it as they find a fixed entry. A name must be a name of `/dev` itself or one name in a row of the directory table, `cpu/0/msr` in `cpu/0` among them, and neither the name nor the numbers of a present character device may be taken already, except by a node marked `alias`, a second name of a device another node has, as udev's links in `/dev/virtio-ports` are; `devfs_name_of` gives the first name. A name, with its directory, is at most 268 bytes (`DEVFS_NODE_NAME_MAX`), the most a lookup carries: a link of `/dev/virtio-ports` with a name of Linux's `NAME_MAX`, 255 bytes. An unregistered node's slot goes first to the next node of the same name, so that `/dev/dri/card0` keeps its inode across an unbind and a bind, and to a node of another name only once 64 slots are free (`DEVFS_SLOTS_SPARE`), so that the slots number at most 64 more than the most nodes held at once; an open sets the descriptor's `priv` to NULL and calls the node's `open`, which may set it.

Inodes do not depend on what is present, and no two nodes of `/dev` share one: `/dev` is 2, entry `i` of the table is 16 + `i`, and the disk nodes follow the table in the order they were made, below 0xF00; `/dev/pts` is 3, `/dev/log` 4, `/dev/input` 5, `/dev/dri` 6, `/dev/vfio` 7, `/dev/net` 8, `/dev/virtio-ports` 9, `/dev/cpu` 0xF00 and `/dev/cpu/N` 0xF01 + N; pty slave N is 0x1000 + N, and N stays below 0x100000 (`PTY_KERNEL_INDEX`), past which `/dev/ptmx` is `ENFILE`; slot `k` of the registry is 0x200000 + `k` (`DEVFS_INO_SLOT_BASE`); and an entry root made is 0x40000000 + its id (`DEVTREE_INO_BASE`), which bounds the slots' count, past which a register is `ENOSPC`. `ttyname` compares `st_dev` and `st_ino`, so `stat` of the path, `fstat` of a descriptor and the listing must agree. Disk and loop nodes are `S_IFBLK` and every other device `S_IFCHR` (`devfs_type_bits`), since `blkid`, `findfs` and `mount LABEL=` look only at block devices.

`devfs_open` fails `O_CREAT|O_EXCL` with `EEXIST` before it allocates a descriptor or a pty pair, and `O_DIRECTORY` with `ENOTDIR`. `ptmx` goes to `pty_open_master`. An event device's open gives the descriptor its queue (`evdev_open`) before it binds the operations, and frees the descriptor when that fails. `tty` is the console when the caller is in the console's session, else the pty slave of the caller's session (`pty_session_slave`), else `ENXIO`; a job-control shell on a pty would otherwise get the console, never find itself in the foreground, and stop on `SIGTTIN`.

`sys_lseek` in `syscall_fs.c` seeks a device by what it is. The raw windows, `/dev/mem` and the loop nodes (`devfs_fd_is_raw`) seek the description's 64-bit `raw_offset` within the size `devfs_fd_raw_size` reports; the boot log seeks the ordinary offset against the ring's length (`seek_bootlog`); `null`, `zero` and `full` go to offset 0; any other device fails with `ESPIPE`. A read or write of a device whose transfer moves the offset, a raw window, `/dev/mem`, a loop node, the boot log or the screen log (`devfs_fd_moves_offset`), locks the open file description for its length (`ops_offset_held`). The transfer may sleep, in a disk request or in a fault on the caller's buffer, before it moves the offset, and a seek or a second transfer through a description it shares must wait or be lost. `pread64` and `pwrite64` lock nothing, since they work on a copy.

`/dev/mem` (`dev_mem_rw`) moves at most a page at a time through a 256-byte buffer on the stack, so the frame's temporary mapping (`kmap_temp`) is never kept over a copy to or from the caller, which may fault. `/dev/bootlog` reads the kernel log's 16 KiB ring (`KLOG_RING_SIZE`) through `klog_read`. `/dev/rtc0` attaches the clock's alarm interrupt the first time it is used (`rtc_irq_setup`); the handler counts the alarm, wakes the readers and ends a suspend to idle (`suspend_wake`), and its wake alarm is the one `/sys/class/rtc/rtc0/wakealarm` reads and sets (`devfs_rtc_set_wakealarm`).

### Raw disk windows

A window is a `struct disk_window` (`../block/disk.h`): a partition view (`block_partition_t`) cut from a backing device, and whether it is published. A disk record has `whole`, for `/dev/diskN`, and `views`, one for each partition; devfs has two windows of its own, `bootpart` and `grubcore`. Boot-device discovery chooses the sectors of the boot windows ([../block/Readme.md](../block/Readme.md)), and `devfs_bootpart_publish` adds one check: it publishes no partition of 2 GiB or more, so that the 32-bit `lseek` reaches every byte. A descriptor stays bound to its window when the window is withdrawn: a read of a disk node then fails with `EIO`, and a transfer of a boot window with `ENXIO`, until the window is published again.

There are four transfer paths. `raw_window_read` and `raw_window_write` move whole sectors at whole-sector offsets, for the boot windows' reads and every raw write. `block_read_bytes` reads a disk or loop node at any byte range, by reading the covering sectors and copying out the bytes asked for, since `blkid` reads a superblock's structure at whatever offset it has. `loop_node_write` writes whole sectors of a loop node. A disk or loop node opened with O_DIRECT takes only offsets, lengths and buffers of whole sectors (`block_direct_misaligned`), as Linux's `blkdev_dio_invalid` asks, and the vector of a readv or writev is checked whole first (`devfs_fd_direct_misaligned`, `block_vec_misaligned` in `../syscall_fs.c`). All four call `block_request` with a kernel buffer and never with a user pointer, since AHCI and NVMe give the controller the buffer's physical address.

`raw_batch_take` gives a transfer pages of its own from the heap, the least of the call, the device's largest request (`block_max_sectors`) and `DEVFS_RAW_BATCH_SECTORS`, 2048 sectors (1 MiB), while fewer than `RAW_BATCH_POOL` (8) are out, so that transfers on two disks each sleep on their own device. When the heap has no pages, or eight are out, the transfer waits its turn for the static buffer of 64 sectors in `.bss` (`DEVFS_RAW_BATCH_STATIC_SECTORS`), whose virtual address is its physical one. The batch makes a whole-disk write finishable: one sector a call would make a 500 MiB write a million driver calls and as many cache flushes. `raw_calls` counts the driver calls, and `/proc/disk` reports it beside the batch size, since a transfer returns the same bytes however many calls it took.

A write is judged whole before its first sector is sent: one that starts at or crosses the end writes nothing and fails with `ENOSPC`. The caller's privilege is checked at each write and not taken from the open, since a process can open the device while privileged and then drop privilege. A short write counts only the batches that completed; the batch that failed may have reached the disk in part, since the block interface reports a command's success and not its progress, and nothing after it was tried. Whether a kernel writes disk nodes is fixed when it is built: `dev_disk_write` has its body only under `-DRACH2_RECOVERY` and fails with `EPERM` otherwise, and `tools/inspect/check_recovery.sh` reads from the kernel's banner which one it has. The boot windows take root's writes in every build, and the metal kernel's driver policy stops them below devfs ([../block/Readme.md](../block/Readme.md)).

Two writers over one range at once would each write their whole length, each report success, and leave neither image on the disk, so each disk has one writer. A write-capable open of a window `raw_write_window` counts, the whole disk and the two boot windows in every build and a partition in the recovery build, records its range in the disk's `writer_held`, `writer_first` and `writer_last`, and a second such open on that disk fails with `EBUSY` until the first description is closed (`raw_close`). The windows are one disk under several names, so they share the reservation; readers are not counted. `/dev/grubcore` has one open description at a time, readers included (`grubcore_held`): replacing the BIOS core is a read of the tail in use, a write of the other, a read back and a rewrite of the selector, and two of those at once can leave the selector pointing at one image with the other's length, which does not boot. The flag clears at the last close, so a tool that dies does not leave the device locked.

The claim table, `claims` in `devfs.c`, has the sector ranges of the mounted volumes, by the rules of [vfs.Readme.md](vfs.Readme.md). `devfs_window_of` gives a source path's view, backing device and range; a loop device has no backing, since a file has no sectors on a disk. `devfs_claim` takes a range. `devfs_disk_claimed` makes `BLKRRPART` (`disk_rescan`) fail on a disk with a mount, and `devfs_window_alias` makes `/proc/diskstats` count the kernel's own view of an ext2 boot root as the partition node's.

### procfs

On x86-64, `/proc/stat` also reports VT-d's fault counts and VFIO's live resources, as [VFIO](../vfio/Readme.md#interfaces) defines them.

`/proc/stat` includes the scheduler's selection and ready-entry work counters, whose definitions are in [the process Readme](../proc/Readme.md#scheduling).

`procfs_open` writes a file's whole text when it is opened, into a `struct procfs_text`: a heap buffer of 256 bytes (`PROCFS_TEXT_FIRST`) that doubles when an append does not fit. The `ap_*` functions format the numbers, since the kernel has no `snprintf`, and 64-bit numbers go through `diskinfo_u64_dec`, since the i686 kernel has no 64-bit division. A text has every row there is, every mount, connection and vector. When the heap cannot grow the buffer, the text is dropped, the open fails with `ENOMEM`, and the kernel logs `procfs: NAME: no memory for the text`; no text is served short.

The text is kept in a `struct procfs_file`, which an `FD_TYPE_PROC` descriptor's `priv` points at, with its origin: the process, the kind of file, the thread's process for a path under `task`, and for `/sys` the node and attribute as `node << 8 | attribute`. `fstat`'s number (`procfs_fd_ino`), `fstatfs` (`procfs_fd_is_sys`) and the `/proc/PID/fd` link (`procfs_readlink_fd`) come from the origin, so a descriptor reports what its path did without the text being made again; `fstat`'s owner is the one the last lookup of its path gave (`procfs_fd_owner`), as the paragraph on process numbers below has it. A read copies from the text at the description's offset. A read from offset 0 after the text's first read makes the text again (`procfs_regen`), from the path and the process the open resolved, which the record keeps, as Linux's `seq_file` makes a file again when it is read from the start; a reader that seeks back or calls `pread64` at 0 sees the file as it is then, and one whose process is gone gets `ESRCH`. The read copies through a buffer on the stack, a piece at a time, and stops short when a sharer made the text again while the copy slept in a fault, since `pread64` holds no lock and the old text is freed. `dup` and `fork` share the description and with it the text, which is freed at the description's last close, so a process's file stays readable after the process is gone until it is read from the start again. Every read and write of a `/proc` or `/sys` file locks the description, as a device's does.

`/proc/disk` alone has a fixed size, `PROCFS_MAX_DISK`: `DISKINFO_RECORD_BYTES` (1280) for the first record and `DISKINFO_DISKS_BYTES` for 8 disks of 15 partitions (`DISK_MAX`, `PART_MAX`), 8 x (96 + 15 x 112), 15488 bytes in all. `diskinfo_format` cuts a text at the size it is given without saying so, so a text that reaches 15487 bytes fails with `EOVERFLOW`, and the kernel logs it. The file is made from the record taken at boot, and opening it sends no command to a disk. `/proc/cpuinfo` reserves `CPUINFO_TEXT_MAX`, 2048 bytes, for each processor online, and `cpuinfo_text` (`cpuinfo.h`, `../arch/x86/cpuinfo.c`) writes it.

Some descriptors keep no text and read the kernel's value at each read, through operations of their own: `/proc/PID/oom_score_adj`, `/proc/PID/mem` and the files of `/proc/sys` always, and, when opened for writing, a user namespace's `uid_map`, `gid_map` and `setgroups` and the writable files of `/sys` (`class/rtc/rtc0/wakealarm`, `power/state`, each backlight's `brightness` and `bl_power`, each radio switch's `soft` and `state`, each device's `uevent`, and a driver's files that take writes). `/proc/acpi/call`, which only root opens, keeps no text either: a write evaluates a method (`acpi_call_write`, `../acpi/call.h`) and a read gives the result of the last call and its NUL (`acpi_call_result`), as Linux's `acpi_call` module does. A `/proc/PID/mem` descriptor takes `mm_hold` on the record of the address space its open allowed, and keeps none of its memory. A read or write takes a user's count of the address space (`mm_get_live`) only while something still runs in it, and returns 0 after; a program the process executes later is out of reach of a descriptor opened before, and an ended process's pages are freed at its end.

A file of `/proc/sys` is read and written as Linux 6.12.115's handler of it reads and writes it (`procfs_sysctl_write` and `sysctl_num_write` in `procfs.c`, and kernel/sysctl.c on Linux); that stable release, which the reviews read, is the one baseline, and its handlers of `fs/nr_open` and `net/core/netdev_budget_usecs` differ from 6.12.0's, which took a signed `nr_open` and a budget of 0. A string, the host and domain names, `kernel/random`'s UUIDs, IPv6's `stable_secret` and the fixed texts `ostype`, `osrelease` and `core_pattern`, is `proc_dostring`'s and reads on from any offset. `stable_secret` reads as Linux's `%pI6` writes the secret, eight groups of four hexadecimal digits, and a write changes that text as `proc_dostring` does with `sysctl_writes_strict` 1: from an offset within it, the bytes written up to a newline or NUL replace the rest, at most 45 bytes in all, and the offset moves by the count; past it, the text and the offset stay. What the text then begins with up to its first byte that is no hexadecimal digit, colon or dot, as `in6_pton` reads it, becomes the secret (`sysctl_secret_write`). Every other file is a number, of one of three families of handler, and reads nothing past offset 0, so a reader taking a byte at a time gets only the first and a read after a write on one descriptor ends at once (`sysctl_numeric`). An empty write to any file returns 0 and changes nothing, but that it marks `stable_secret` written, and a write to a number at an offset past 0 returns its count, moves the offset by it and changes nothing, as `sysctl_writes_strict` is 1. Otherwise a write is read from its first 4095 bytes: spaces, as `isspace` has them, which must not be all of it; a number as `proc_get_long` reads one, a minus unless it is the last byte, then a digit beginning a number in base 0 of fewer than 21 characters with its sign, then a space, a tab, a newline or the end; and the spaces after it. `proc_dointvec` and its `_minmax` and `_jiffies` forms take a sign and an int's range and return the bytes up to the end of those spaces, and every byte past the 4095, moving the offset by them; `proc_doulongvec_minmax` counts the same way and refuses a minus, `-0` among them; `proc_douintvec`, its `_minmax` and `proc_dou8vec_minmax` refuse a minus and more than UINT_MAX, and return the whole count with the offset left at 0, so that a second write on one descriptor stores its value too. A number out of a file's range is `EINVAL`. The writeback ratios and byte counts clear each other as Linux's handlers do, and on every successful write, an empty one among them, `dirty_background_ratio` clears `dirty_background_bytes` and `dirty_background_bytes` clears `dirty_background_ratio`; a write of a hung-task setting wakes the detector. IPv6's `forwarding` and `disable_ipv6` store the value they had when the write's number is refused, which `all` passes to `default` and every interface, and return the whole count, as Linux's `addrconf_sysctl_forward` and `addrconf_sysctl_disable` do. A file of `/proc/sys` is root's, with mode 0644 when it takes a write (those `synth_sys_writable` names), 0600 for IPv6's `stable_secret`, 0666 for `kernel/ns_last_pid` and 0444 otherwise (`synth_sys_mode`), which stat, fstat and the access check alike read. `sysctl_may` judges the caller as Linux's `sysctl_perm` does, at the open (`procfs_open_sysctl`, `EACCES`, O_TRUNC counted as a write) and again at each read and write (`EPERM`), as `proc_sys_call_handler` does, so a process that opened a file as root and then took another uid can no longer write it: an effective uid of 0 takes the owner's bits, an effective or supplementary gid of 0 the group's, others the rest; a holder of CAP_NET_ADMIN in the first user namespace takes the owner's bits on `net` (`net_ctl_permissions`, whose values are the stack's one set), and a holder of CAP_SYS_RESOURCE on `user` (`set_permissions`), which gives others read alone. A descriptor of `user` holds the user namespace of its open, as Linux's inode keeps the table header its open found, and its checks, reads and writes reach that namespace's limit, even once its process has entered another.

A process number in a path belongs to the proc mount's PID namespace (`procfs_resolve_pid`), and `self` is the calling thread's process. A path under `/proc/PID/task/TID` needs both numbers: `procfs_resolve_id` accepts the thread only when it is one of the process's threads and not a zombie other than the leader. `procfs_may_read` is the test for reading a process's memory, descriptors and links; its shared check, `procfs_may_read_mem`, requires another process to be dumpable unless the caller is privileged. `stat` gives a process's 0555 directories its effective user and group IDs even when its address space is not dumpable, as Linux's `task_dump_owner` does. Other directories, files and links get those IDs while it is dumpable, and root otherwise (`procfs_pid_owner`, `synth_pid_owner`). A stat of a path works the owner out afresh. A descriptor open on a process's file, an fdinfo file, a process's `fd`, `fdinfo` or `task` directory, or one of its `exe`, `cwd`, `root`, descriptor or namespace links, O_PATH ones among them (O_PATH with O_NOFOLLOW for a link), keeps the owner the last lookup of its node gave, its open's or a later stat's, as Linux's inode keeps what `pid_revalidate` and `tid_fd_revalidate` set and no getattr of those nodes works it out again; fstat after a change of the dumpable flag or of the process's IDs reports the old owner until a path reaches the node again, and fchdir into `fd` judges that owner (`procfs_owner_keep`, `procfs_lookup_owner`, `procfs_fd_owner`). Every such node a walk reaches gets its current owner, as Linux's `lookup_fast` revalidates each component with `pid_revalidate` or `tid_fd_revalidate`: one passed on the way, as `fd`, `fdinfo` or `task` is before the next component is looked up, even when that lookup fails (`synth_link_part` in `vfs.c`), and the last (`synth_rest`), so readlink of a link and a stat or open that follows it update the link's owner. A name relative to a directory descriptor or the working directory is joined to that directory's path and walked from the root (`copy_path_at`), so the walk updates the starting directory's owner too, where Linux's `path_init` starts at the directory without revalidating it; leaving it alone would need a walk that starts at the descriptor's node. A listing of `/dev` obtains only the inode numbers of its descriptor entries and changes no retained `/proc` owner (`synth_dev_dirent`). The process's own directory and its `ns` work their owner out at each stat, as Linux's `pid_getattr` does. The namespace directory `ns`, under both a process and a thread, has mode 0511, so its owner follows the dumpable rule, and an open of it for reading checks that mode and owner as Linux's generic_permission does (`synth_open_dir`).

`/proc/PID/syscall` and `/proc/PID/wchan`, and the same files of each thread under `task`, show what a thread does in the kernel, as Linux's `proc_pid_syscall` and `proc_pid_wchan` do. `procfs_gen_syscall` reads the frame the thread's entry to the kernel saved at the top of its kernel stack (`proc_user_frame`): a system call's entry (`FRAME_SYSCALL`, the vector 0x80 on both x86 architectures) with nonnegative `orig_nr` gives the call's number as a tracer left it and its six arguments by the call's ABI (`SC_ARG`). A negative number, including one other than -1 left by a tracer, gives that number without arguments; any other entry, a fault or an interrupt, or a call after a handler's `sigreturn`, gives -1 without arguments. Every form ends with the user stack and instruction pointers. A thread ready or on a processor, other than the caller, reads `running`, as Linux's `task_current_syscall` refuses a thread that is not blocked; a sleeping kernel thread, one with no address space outside an exit, reads call 0 with every other field `0x0`, the zeroed frame Linux's `copy_thread` gives it, and an ended leader `-1 0x0 0x0`, as Linux's with no stack. The open makes the text only for its checks; the first read makes it again, as Linux's seq_file makes the file at its first read, so a thread that reads its own file sees its read. The kernel has no symbol table, so a wait records its own name: each place a thread blocks stores a static string in the record's `wchan` just before it sets the thread PROC_BLOCKED, and `procfs_gen_wchan` gives that name while the thread is blocked, `do_signal_stop` or `ptrace_stop` while it is stopped, and 0 otherwise. A wait that names itself (`wq_wait_event_as`, `wq_wait_event_killable`, `poll_wait`'s `wchan`, the waits of `wait4`, `nanosleep`, `pause`, `sigsuspend` and `rt_sigtimedwait`, and the killable waits of a `vfork` parent, `wait_for_vfork_done`, and of `execve` for the threads it ends, `de_thread`) takes the name of the Linux function that sleeps for the same call, which proc(5) lists: for `pause`, `__do_sys_pause` on either ABI, since Linux's `__x64_sys_pause` and `__ia32_sys_pause` are aliases of it and `nm -n`, from which kallsyms takes its order, lists names at one address in order of their bytes; every other wait gives the name of the wait-queue call it made, `wq_wait_event`, `wq_wait_event_nointr` or `wq_wait_event_idle`, which no Linux function has. The name is the one Linux's wchan shows, which passes over every `__sched` function (`__get_wchan` on x86): a PI futex reads `futex_lock_pi`, a requeue-PI waiter `futex_wait_queue` before and after a requeue moves it to the PI futex, since Linux's requeue moves it without a wake and Rach2's unlock hands it the mutex, so it never blocks again in `rt_mutex_wait_proxy_lock`, `futex_waitv` `futex_wait_multiple`, `nanosleep` and `clock_nanosleep` on a time clock `hrtimer_nanosleep`, on a CPU clock `do_cpu_nanosleep`, a sleep that `restart_syscall` resumes, since `hrtimer_nanosleep_restart` is `__sched` too, the entry Linux's x86-64 kernel tail-calls it from, `do_syscall_64`, or `do_int80_emulation` for an i386 call, and on the i686 kernel `__do_sys_restart_syscall`, which Linux's i386 kernel keeps a frame for since it is built with frame pointers (`sleep_resume_wchan`), and an empty `SYSLOG_ACTION_READ` `syslog_print`; Debian's 6.12.115 shows each x86-64 name. syscall is mode 0400 and wchan 0444, and an open of syscall, environ, auxv or mem, whose modes let only the owner open them (`procfs_owner_mode`), checks the mode against the caller's file-system user ID as Linux's generic_permission does. Past the mode, every file of a process that shows its memory, its calls or its descriptors takes Linux's ptrace check with PTRACE_MODE_FSCREDS (`procfs_may_read_mem`): the caller's file-system user and group IDs each of the process's three, a dumpable process and no privilege the caller lacks. A caller it refuses gets EPERM from a read of syscall, as from Linux's `lock_trace`, and 0 from wchan; READ and ATTACH differ only by Yama, which Rach2 does not have.

`/proc/PID/schedstat`, mode 0444, and the same file of each thread under `task`, give the thread's scheduler statistics as Linux's `proc_pid_schedstat`: the nanoseconds the fair class has charged it (`se.sum_exec_runtime`), the nanoseconds it waited ready on a run queue (`run_delay`) and its switches onto a processor (`pcount`), which `schedule` counts ([proc](../proc/Readme.md)). The selftest `steal_time` reads the second number of its own thread's file.

`/proc/PID/sched`, mode 0644, and the same file of each thread under `task`, print the thread's scheduler entity as Linux 6.12's `proc_sched_show_task` does (`procfs_gen_sched`, copied under the run queues' lock): the header of its name, id and thread count, its clocks, run time and migrations, the 28 schedstats and two averages while `kernel/sched_schedstats` is 1, its switches, weight, load averages, policy, priority and slice, and `clock-delta`, each line in Linux's `__PS` or `__PSN` format; the NUMA balancing lines are left out. Its owner's write of anything clears the thread's schedstats (`procfs_sched_clear`, Linux's `proc_sched_set_task`), with the owner check of `comm`.

`/proc/PID/autogroup`, mode 0644, in the process's directory alone, as on Linux, shows the group of the process's session as `/autogroup-ID nice N` and a newline, and nothing for a process in the root group (`procfs_gen_autogroup`). Its owner's write of a nice value, at most 12 bytes with white space around it, sets the group's (`procfs_autogroup_write`, Linux's `proc_sched_autogroup_set_nice`): text that is no number fails with `EINVAL`, a number past an int with `ERANGE`, and the rest as `autogroup_nice` checks it ([proc](../proc/Readme.md)).

Each global file of `/proc` has a fixed inode (`PROCFS_INO_*`: 0x100 to 0x11F, the files of `/proc/net` from 0x120, the `/proc/sys` entries from 0x140, `/proc/acpi` and its `call` 0x1F0 and 0x1F1, `/proc/fs` and its `lockwait` 0x1F2 and 0x1F3, `/proc/asound` and its files from 0x30000, and `/proc/bus`, its `input` and that directory's `devices` and `handlers` 0x38000, 0x38001, 0x38010 and 0x38011), so `stat` and `fstat` agree and no two files share one. `procfs_mount_dev` gives the device number that `stat` reports for the files of each mount, which `/proc/PID/mountinfo` prints: 0 for a KRFFS root, the numbers of the `/dev` node a disk or loop mount was made from, a synthetic type's own number, and 16 plus the mount's slot for any other.

### Made entries

`/dev`, the directories of its table and the directories made in them take new directories and symbolic links, as Linux's devtmpfs does, so that udev can make `/dev/char/M:m`, `/dev/input/by-path` and the rest of its links. [devtree.c](devtree.c) keeps them in one list in the order they were made, at most 4096 (`DEVTREE_MAX`), each with an id never given twice, its parent (`/dev`, a row of the table as `DEVTREE_ROW(r)`, or a made directory), name, mode, owner and three times. `mkdir`, `symlink`, `unlink`, `rmdir`, `rename`, `chmod`, `chown` and their `*at` and descriptor forms work on them (`synth_dev_make`, `synth_dev_remove`, `synth_dev_rename`, `chmod_made` and `chown_made` in `syscall_fs.c`), with the parent's write permission, so only root makes them in `/dev` and its table's directories, which are root's and 0755. A new name that devfs or the classifier already gives is `EEXIST`, and a device, `log` or a descriptor link cannot be removed or renamed (`EROFS`).

The classifier knows nothing of them. When it gives a path to a device that devfs does not have, or past a file that is not there, `synth_made` in `vfs.c` looks the path up in the list (`devtree_resolve`) and makes the node `VNODE_DEV_MADE` with the entry's id in `syskey`. A made link is a text link: the walk follows it at the end of a path and in the middle of one (`synth_link_part`), and reads a relative target from the link's directory (`walk_rest_link`). A made directory's descriptor records the id, and its path, for `fchdir`, `openat` and `/proc/PID/fd`, is built from the list (`devtree_path`). A made entry's inode is 0x40000000 plus its id, and its times are its own.

### Listings

`synth_getdents` in `syscall_fs.c` writes `linux_dirent64` records from `synth_dirent_at`. The description's offset is the index of the next entry; `.` and `..` are entries 0 and 1, both of inode 1. Each call reads the kernel's tables as they are then, from the saved index. `/proc` lists its 26 fixed entries and then each process once, by its leader, as the reader's PID namespace numbers it, without the idle process (`procfs_nth_pid`). `/dev` lists the nameless mounts in it (`shm`, `mqueue`), then `log`, then `fd`, `stdin`, `stdout` and `stderr`, then the directories of the table present that are in `/dev` itself, then the devices present, then the entries made there; a directory of the table lists the rows in it present, as `/dev/cpu` lists `0` and `1`, then its devices present, as `/dev/input` its event devices, then its made entries, and a made directory its made entries. `lseek` on a synthetic directory takes any index up to 0x7FFFFFFF, and on a `/proc` or `/sys` file only `SEEK_SET` and `SEEK_CUR`, since a text has no size to seek from (`seek_listing`).

### sysfs

`/sys` keeps no table of its own: `synthfs.c` classifies its paths, `procfs.c` writes its files from the kernel's device tables, and `syscall_fs.c` lists it (`synth_sys_dirent`). Every `stat`, open and listing of a keyed directory checks that its device is still there (`sysfs_device_exists`): a PCI function by its vendor register, and a function other than 0 also by the multifunction bit of function 0; a USB device by the USB core's table (`usb_core_row`); a network interface by the interface table; a processor by `cpu_table`; a backlight by the count of those registered (`backlight_count`); a radio switch by `rfkill_present`; and a disk node by devfs's table (`devfs_disk_row`). `/sys/block` and `/sys/dev/block` come from that table, the disk nodes first and then each loop device with a file attached, as a disk with no partitions.

The links, `/sys/bus/pci/devices/NAME`, `/sys/bus/usb/devices/NAME`, `/sys/class/net/IF/device` and `/sys/dev/block/MAJOR:MINOR`, are classified as the directories they lead to; `lstat` reports mode 0120777 and `readlink` a relative target (`synth_sys_link`). A radio's links, `/sys/class/net/IF/phy80211` and a radio switch's `device`, lead to `/sys/class/ieee80211/phyN` and read as that absolute path. An attribute file is a snapshot, as a `/proc` file is, written by `procfs_gen_netattr`, `procfs_gen_sysattr`, `procfs_gen_blockattr`, `procfs_gen_backlight`, `procfs_gen_rfkill` or `procfs_gen_uevent`, and reports a size of 4096, as Linux's do. `sysfs_ino` makes inodes from the node, the key and the attribute's place. The writable files open for writing as `sysfs_file_may` judges the access against the file's mode, a registered attribute's own and 0644 for the others, by the file-system user id and the capabilities that pass a mode, as Linux's `generic_permission` (`procfs_open_sysattr`); an open with O_TRUNC is judged as one for writing as well, as Linux's `build_open_flags` adds MAY_WRITE for it, and DMI's serial numbers and UUID, root's with mode 0400 (`sysfs_attr_mode`), open for reading as the same rule judges them (`sysfs_read_refused`). The writable files take the write: `wakealarm` sets, pushes or clears the alarm of `/dev/rtc0` as Linux's `wakealarm_store` does, its number read as kstrtos64 reads it (`k_strtoll`), `power/state` calls `suspend_to_idle` (`../proc/suspend.c`), a backlight's `brightness` and `bl_power` go to `backlight_store` (`../display/backlight.h`), whose descriptor keeps the backlight's index, and a radio switch's `soft` and `state` to `rfkill_store` (`../rfkill.h`), whose descriptor keeps the switch's. Each is given the first page of a write at most, NUL-terminated, as Linux's kernfs gives a write to a sysfs store, and its store parses it with its own limits (`procfs_sysattr_store`); the description's offset moves past the count the store took, as kernfs moves it; a backlight's and a radio switch's read a number as Linux's kstrtoul with base 0 does (`k_strtoul`), so leading zeros, octal and hexadecimal are taken.

`/sys/kernel/mm/transparent_hugepage` has Linux 6.12's top-level files as a kernel built with `CONFIG_TRANSPARENT_HUGEPAGE_MADVISE` boots with them: `enabled` selects `madvise`, `shmem_enabled` `never` and `defrag` `madvise`, and `use_zero_page` and `shrink_underused` are 1 (`procfs_gen_thp`), each a word of `struct thp_policy` (`mm/thp.h`). With less than 512 MiB managed, `thp_boot` sets `enabled` and `defrag` to `never` and `use_zero_page` to 0 at boot, as Linux's `hugepage_init` clears its flags on such a machine; on x86-64, with AMD's family 0x10 or when the kernel had no memory to count huge pages, `thp_refuse` does the same and the three then take no other value (EINVAL). `hpage_pmd_size`, read-only, gives the page-directory mapping size in bytes, 2097152 on x86-64 and 4194304 on i686; the KVM selftests read it to align an anonymous mapping. The other five take root's write as Linux's stores parse it (`thp_store`), and a written policy reads back and wakes khugepaged. An empty write to any writable /sys attribute returns 0 with no store called, as Linux's `sysfs_kf_write` answers it. Its `khugepaged` directory, `SYSFS_THP_KHUGEPAGED`, has Linux's nine files (`procfs_gen_khugepaged`, `khugepaged_store`): the sleeps, `pages_to_scan`, the three `max_ptes_` limits and `defrag`, which root writes as Linux's `kstrtouint` and `kstrtoul` read them, and the read-only counts `full_scans` and `pages_collapsed`. On x86-64 the policy decides the transparent huge pages of private anonymous memory ([memory](../mm/Readme.md#transparent-huge-pages)); on i686, which has none, it reads back and changes no mapping. There are no per-size `hugepages-NkB` directories.

A PCI function's directory has Linux's identity files, `vendor`, `device`, `subsystem_vendor`, `subsystem_device`, `class`, `revision` and, on a VGA function, `boot_vga`, beside `uevent` and `config`, and a `subsystem` link to `/sys/bus/pci` (`sysfs_pci_attr` names them, `SYSFS_PCI_*` numbers them). `sysfs_pci_ident` in `procfs.c` reads the identity from configuration space, or takes the one a driver reported (`sysfs_pci_virtual_add`), which hides the function's own and gives a function with no device behind it its directory; `procfs_gen_pciattr` writes the files and `procfs_gen_pciconfig` puts a reported identity into the header. The binary `config` file, root's with mode 0644, reads the current registers at every offset, and opens for writing as `sysfs_file_may` judges the access, by the file-system user id and the capabilities that pass a file's mode, as Linux's `generic_permission`. Writes stop at the end of configuration space and use aligned byte, word or dword accesses, including in ECAM, so a short write never rewrites adjacent status bits. A file `resourceN`, for each BAR with a length (`sysfs_pci_attr_present`), root's with mode 0600, opens as `sysfs_file_may` judges it (`procfs_open_pcibar`), and a read or write is clipped to it, as Linux's sysfs clips one (`pcibar_span`): a memory BAR's `mmap` maps the BAR's pages shared and uncached through `vm_device_map`, each page found from the BAR's address at its fault, within the BAR's length, and an I/O BAR's read and write reach one port of 1, 2 or 4 bytes, as Linux's pci-sysfs.c gives them. A write copies its first bytes, up to a page, before it is clipped, as Linux's kernfs copies them, so that a source not readable to its length is EFAULT with no port written. Each prefetchable memory BAR also has a `resourceN_wc` file when the page attribute table provides write-combining: the same length, mode, pages and bounds, mapped with `VM_PROT_WC`. A privileged write of a device's `uevent`, a PCI function's, a USB device's, an interface's, a disk's, a partition's, a radio's or a radio switch's (`sysfs_attr_uevent`), sends that uevent with the device's variables and its subsystem (`procfs_uevent_write`), so that `udevadm trigger` replays every device's add; `sysfs_pci_uevent` sends a PCI function's uevent the same way for a driver, as its `bind` once its probe has succeeded.

[sysfs.c](sysfs.c) keeps the entries drivers add, which [sysfs.h](sysfs.h) declares: directories, attribute files with a driver's `show` and `store`, and links, in a table of 512 slots that doubles when full, up to 65,536, each known by a handle that is its slot and the slot's generation, so that a stale handle finds nothing. Entries hang below an anchor, the handle of a fixed directory and key (`sysfs_anchor`), or below each other. The classifier tries the fixed names first; a name they do not have is looked up among the children of the anchor of the deepest fixed directory the path reaches (`synth_classify_sys_at`), and the walk goes on through registered directories (`synth_sys_reg_walk`). A link to a registered directory is followed in place; one to an anchor with more path after it builds the fixed directory's path with the rest and classifies that, at most `SYNTH_SYS_HOPS` (6) times, and a path that ends on a link is `VNODE_SYS_LINK` with the link in `sysreg`. `readlink` gives the target relative to the link's directory through the target's parent, as kernfs writes it (`sysfs_link_text`). `procfs_gen_regattr` grows the text until `show` fits, up to 64 KiB, and `procfs_sysattr_store` passes the first page of a write at most to `store`.

`sysfs_uevent` sends a registered directory's uevent as Linux's kobject_uevent_env does: `action@DEVPATH`, then ACTION, DEVPATH, SUBSYSTEM from the last name of the directory's `subsystem` link, the caller's variables, the lines of the directory's `uevent` file and SEQNUM, each ended by a NUL, at most 2048 bytes, to `netlink_uevent` (`../network/socket/netlink.h`), which queues it for every NETLINK_KOBJECT_UEVENT socket in group 1. `sysfs_uevent_path` sends one for a fixed directory, and `sysfs_uevent_parse` reads a write of a `uevent` file as kobject_synth_uevent does. The DRM core's entries under `/sys/class/drm`, `/sys/dev/char` and `/sys/devices/platform` are made this way ([../drm/Readme.md](../drm/Readme.md)); `/sys/kernel/debug` is a fixed empty directory, over which the kernel mounts debugfs ([debugfs.Readme.md](debugfs.Readme.md)), and `/sys/kernel/uevent_seqnum` gives the last SEQNUM.

## Limits

* The namespaces are fixed but for the directories and links made under `/dev`. Nothing else in them can be created, removed, renamed or given new times (`EROFS`), nor can a made entry be given new times; nothing can be used as a mount point (`EINVAL`, `target_check` in `vfs.c`) or executed (`EACCES`, `syscall_proc.c`). `/dev/shm` and `/dev/mqueue` are separate mounts, entered by name from `/dev` ([tmpfs.Readme.md](tmpfs.Readme.md), [../ipc/Readme.md](../ipc/Readme.md)).
* No device node, regular file, socket, FIFO or hard link can be made in `/dev`, and at most 4096 directories and links. A device that appears with the name of a made entry hides it. A device node on a disk file system opens the devfs device with its number, and a number devfs does not have fails with `ENXIO`.
* `/dev/pts` opens and lists, but the listing of `/dev` does not show it.
* A listing of the root directory shows `dev`, `proc` and `sys` only where the root file system has directories of those names.
* Every entry of one `/proc/PID`, the directory, its files and its links, reports the one inode 0x200 plus the low 16 bits of the process's kernel id, in whatever PID namespace the reader is, so a program that tells files apart by inode takes them for one file.
* A `/proc` file reports a size of 0, and a `/sys` attribute 4096, whatever the length of its text.
* `/proc/PID/mounts` and `/proc/PID/mountinfo` list the mounts visible in the reader's mount namespace, not in that of process PID.
* `/proc/filesystems` lists `devfs` and `proc` and not `sysfs`.
* A listing is consistent only within one `getdents64` call.
* `fs/nr_open` takes at most 16777216, where Linux takes up to INT_MAX rounded down to a multiple of `BITS_PER_LONG`, since a descriptor table of that many entries takes nearly 1 GiB of heap on x86-64 and the table's growth doubles an int. `net/ipv4/ip_forward` takes 0 alone, since the stack forwards nothing between interfaces.
* Disk nodes exist for at most 8 disks of 15 partitions each.
* A USB device on a fifth controller, on a root port past 255, behind more than five hubs or on a hub port past 15 has no directory in `/sys` (`sys_usb_key`).
* A path below a synthetic mount, as far as a link the walk goes on past, has at most 10 components and, with the mount's name, fewer than 4096 bytes.
* A path goes through at most 6 links to fixed directories, and a link's target path is at most 255 bytes.
* `/sys/dev/char` lists only the DRM nodes and the event devices.
* The framebuffer and the mouse are `/fb` and `/mouse` at the root, outside devfs.
