# ACPI

`sys/acpi/` is ACPI on the PC above the kernel's own reader of the tables, `sys/arch/x86/acpi.c`: [ACPICA](../../../vendor/acpica/Readme.md), Intel's interpreter of ACPI, compiled into the kernel, with the OS layer through which it reaches the kernel's memory, ports, locks, time and interrupts, and its bring-up at boot. Once it is up the kernel has the firmware's namespace and runs its methods: the objects' `_STA` and `_INI`, `\_PIC`, the methods of the general-purpose events, and the system control interrupt, the SCI. On ACPICA stand the kernel's own ACPI devices, as Linux has them: the embedded controller, through which a laptop's firmware reaches its keys; the power and sleep buttons and the video bus, whose events are keys on input devices; the IdeaPad's `VPC2004`, whose keys include the airplane key; and `/proc/acpi/call`, through which root evaluates a method. `acpi.c` still reads the tables first, for the interrupt controllers, the processors and the power off, which work without ACPICA.

## Use

ACPICA comes up on every PC boot that has an RSDP. Two options of the boot line ([cmdline.md](../../cmdline.md)) change that:

* `rach2.noacpica`: ACPICA is left out. No method runs, the SCI's line stays masked, and the power off writes the `\_S5` that `acpi.c` found itself.
* `rach2.nosci`: ACPICA comes up, but its handler of the SCI is not installed and the line stays masked, so no fixed event or GPE is seen, and the embedded controller runs no query.

The buttons and the video bus are the event devices "Power Button", "Sleep Button" and "Video Bus" of [input(4)](../../../man/man4/input.4), and an IdeaPad's keys are "Ideapad extra buttons", with its Wi-Fi switch `ideapad_wlan` in [rfkill(4)](../../../man/man4/rfkill.4), as [ideapad(4)](../../../man/man4/ideapad.4) describes. A method is evaluated through `/proc/acpi/call` ([proc(5)](../../../man/man5/proc.5)), as with Linux's `acpi_call` module:

    # echo '\_SB.PCI0.LPCB.EC0._Q12' > /proc/acpi/call
    # cat /proc/acpi/call
    0x0

The kernel's records of ACPICA and of the devices, `acpi: acpica: N objects, E errors` and `acpi: ec: ...` among them, are listed in [acpi(4)](../../../man/man4/acpi.4), and the IdeaPad's in [ideapad(4)](../../../man/man4/ideapad.4).

## Interfaces

[`acpica.h`](acpica.h) is what the rest of the kernel sees, with no type of ACPICA's in it, so that a file may include it beside the kernel's own `acpi.h`:

* `acpica_boot(rsdp)` brings ACPICA up. `platform_firmware_start` (`sys/arch/x86/platform.c`) calls it once from `kernel.c`, after the heap, PCI, the tick and the scheduler, with the RSDP `acpi.c` found.
* `acpica_thread_start()` makes the kernel thread `kacpid`. `platform_threads_start` calls it from `enter_program` when the first program starts, so that the thread takes its pid after init's 1, which BusyBox's `poweroff` signals.
* `acpica_power_off_prepare()` and `acpica_power_off()` enter the soft-off state. `power_prepare` and `power_stop` (`sys/arch/x86/power.c`) call them from `kernel_shutdown`.
* `acpica_up()`, and the counts `acpica_errors()`, `acpica_warnings()`, `acpica_sci_count()` and `acpica_sci_handled()`, read the state.

[`sys/acpicall.h`](../acpicall.h), a shared header that `call.c` defines, is `/proc/acpi/call` ([proc(5)](../../../man/man5/proc.5)), which `procfs.c` reads and writes. [`devices.h`](devices.h) is what `boot.c` calls of the devices, and [`ecio.h`](ecio.h) the embedded controller's protocol, which names nothing of ACPICA's so that a host test drives it. The other files include ACPICA's `acpi.h` and serve ACPICA alone.

## Design

### Sources

* `sources.mk` lists the kernel's files of this directory, `ACPI_C_SRC`, and ACPICA's 163, `ACPICA_C_SRC`, under `$(ACPICA)`. `sys/arch/x86/sources.mk` includes it, so only the PC compiles them.
* `acrach2.h` is ACPICA's platform header, which `acenv.h` includes when `__RACH2__` is defined. It gives the integer widths, i686's 64-bit division in C, by shifts and subtractions in `clib.c` (the kernel has no libgcc, and shared code no assembly), the use of the kernel's C functions, and the global lock.
* `clib.h` and `clib.c` give the C functions ACPICA calls that the kernel lacks, with `vsnprintf` from ACPICA's own `utprint.c`.
* `mapcache.c` keeps the windows of physical memory ACPICA has mapped.
* `osl.c` is the OS layer, every `AcpiOs` function.
* `boot.c` brings ACPICA up and starts the devices among its steps.
* `ecio.c` is the embedded controller's command protocol over its two ports, and `ec.c` the controller on ACPICA: its address space and its queries.
* `button.c` is the power and sleep buttons, `video.c` the video buses, and `ideapad.c` the IdeaPad's `VPC2004`.
* `pcihp.c` is PCI hot plug through the slots of bus 0's host bridge, as Linux's acpiphp.
* `pnp.c` finds the 8042's keyboard and PS/2 mouse among the present devices with `_CRS`, as Linux's pnpacpi and i8042 PNP drivers do, and gives their `_HID` and `_CID`s to `i8042_pnp_device`, whose firmware ID chooses the touchpad's quirks ([`sys/arch/x86/`](../arch/x86/Readme.md)). `acpi_pnp_reserve` then holds for the host the FADT's fixed register blocks, as Linux's `acpi_reserve_resources`, and the memory and the ports from 0x100 up of each present system device's `_CRS`, `PNP0C01` or `PNP0C02`, as Linux's `pnp/system.c`, so that no PCI BAR over them is claimed (`pci_host_reserve`, [the kernel's PCI](../pci.h)). It reads every descriptor of ports or memory as Linux's `acpi_dev_resource_*` do (`acpi_resource_range`): a 24-bit memory descriptor's address and length shifted up by 8, and the Extended address descriptor, which ACPICA's `AcpiResourceToAddress64` does not take, as well as the Word, DWord and QWord ones.
* `call.c` is `/proc/acpi/call`.
* `sleep.c` is the power off.

### The bring-up

`acpica_boot` returns at once under `rach2.noacpica` or without an RSDP, with a record why. Otherwise it runs ACPICA's steps in ACPICA's order: `AcpiInitializeSubsystem`; `AcpiInitializeTables` with room for 16 tables, which the array outgrows; `AcpiLoadTables`, which builds the namespace from the DSDT and the SSDTs; the embedded controller's probe, which installs its address space before any method of the firmware's may read it, but does not yet run its `_REG`; `AcpiEnableSubsystem`, which puts the machine in ACPI mode and installs the SCI's handler; and `AcpiInitializeObjects`, which runs `_STA` and `_INI`. A step that fails is logged as `acpi: acpica: refused: STEP: EXCEPTION` and leaves ACPICA down. Then `\_PIC` is given 1 when the I/O APIC has the lines and 0 for the 8259, so that the firmware's `_PRT` answers for the controller in use; the embedded controller's `_REG` runs and its GPE is taken, the buttons, the video buses and the IdeaPad's device are registered with their handlers; and `AcpiUpdateAllGpes` enables the GPEs that have methods. The work queued meanwhile runs in line, and the namespace is walked to count its objects for the record.

The bring-up takes about 30 ms on QEMU's machines. It runs after the scheduler is up because ACPICA's methods may wait, which only a process can do; at that point of the boot there is no process yet, so its waits spin.

### The OS layer

* Memory: `AcpiOsAllocate` is `kmalloc`. A mapping is a window of whole pages through `map_mmio`, uncached; `mapcache.c` gives a request inside a window made before that window's address again, so that a table longer than a page is contiguous and a register read many times is mapped once. The kernel's MMIO region has no unmap, so `AcpiOsUnmapMemory` forgets nothing, and 256 windows are the most there are.
* Ports and PCI: `inb` and its kin, and the configuration space through `pciconf.c`, its extended part through the MCFG's window.
* Locks: a spinlock is the kernel's, taken with interrupts off. A semaphore counts units under one spinlock; a mutex is ACPICA's semaphore of one unit. A waiter that is a process of its own sleeps on the semaphore's wait queue, with a deadline in ticks for a timed wait; the boot, an interrupt and an idle task spin a millisecond at a time instead, since they cannot sleep.
* Time: `AcpiOsSleep` sleeps to the tick past the time, or spins where the caller cannot sleep; `AcpiOsStall` is `k_udelay`; `AcpiOsGetTimer` is the monotonic clock in ACPICA's unit of 100 ns.
* The SCI: `board.c` masks the SCI's line at boot whenever `SCI_EN` is set, and the I/O APIC keeps it held ([the PC platform](../arch/x86/Readme.md)). `AcpiOsInstallInterruptHandler` lets it go with `isr_line_release`, which routes it level-triggered with the polarity of the MADT's override, and then registers the handler on the line's vector, with interrupts off between, so that no SCI arrives with no handler. ACPICA's handler reads and clears the fixed events' and GPEs' status. A line above 15 is refused.
* Deferred work: `AcpiOsExecute` queues a notify handler or a GPE's method, which the SCI's handler cannot run since a method may wait, in a ring of 64. `kacpid` runs it, sleeping when there is none in a wait that `/proc/stat` does not count as blocked. Before the thread exists the boot runs the queue in line, and work an interrupt queues waits for the thread. A full ring refuses `AcpiOsExecute`'s work; a driver whose request must not be dropped queues a `struct osl_work` with `osl_queue_work` instead, as Linux's `queue_work`: it is linked into a list beside the ring, never refused, queued once however often it is asked before it runs, and its mark cleared as it begins, so a request during its run queues it again.
* The log: ACPICA's output arrives in pieces and is logged a whole line at a time as `acpi: acpica: TEXT`, with its ordinary prefix `ACPI: ` left out. A line that begins `ACPI Error`, `ACPI Exception` or `ACPI BIOS Error` is logged at `ERR` and counted as an error, and a warning at `WARN`; the boot's record gives the count. Every other line, the table list among them, is `DEBUG`, which the log, the serial port and netconsole keep and the screen does not show, as are the records of the SCI's line, `kacpid`, the embedded controller, the video buses and each key's event. Of ACPICA's start the screen shows only the count `acpi: acpica: N objects, E errors`, a refused step, and the errors and warnings, as Linux's console does under `quiet`.

### The embedded controller

* `acpi_ec_probe` takes the first present device `PNP0C09`, the data port and then the command port from the first two I/O resources of its `_CRS`, which it holds for the host as Linux's `acpi_ec_add` requests them, and its GPE from an integer `_GPE`. It installs the `EmbeddedControl` address space on the device after `AcpiLoadTables` and before the objects' `_INI`, which may read the controller's fields, as the ACPI specification requires of a controller with no ECDT; the Lenovo G50-70 has none. The firmware's `_REG` for it runs later, in `acpi_ec_start` after `_INI`, as Linux runs it: the G50-70's `_REG` copies a value chosen by the `OSYS` that `\_SB.PCI0._INI` sets from `_OSI`, and run before it, it reads an uninitialized local and fails.
* The protocol (`ecio.c`) is the specification's: a read writes `0x80` to the command port and the address to the data port, each once the controller has taken the byte before (IBF clear), and reads the byte once the controller has put it out (OBF set); a write sends `0x81`, the address and the byte, and ends when the controller has taken the last; a query sends `0x84` and reads the number of its event. Each wait reads the status every 10 microseconds for 500 ms at most, Linux's limit, and gives up with `-ETIMEDOUT`, which ACPICA gets as `AE_TIME`. The transfers are polled, not driven by the GPE, so a transfer works at boot before any interrupt and in a method that holds interrupts off.
* The address space reads and writes a field wider than a byte a byte at a time, the lowest first, as Linux does. One transfer runs at a time, under a semaphore of one unit with interrupts on: the handler and the queries both run in a process, a process waiting for the controller sleeps until `kacpid` gives it back, since a system call is not preempted and a spinlock would hold it spinning for good, and the GPE's handler only reads the status.
* The GPE is taken as edge-triggered, as Linux takes it. Its handler, in the SCI, queues the queries on `kacpid` when SCI_EVT is set and none are queued; the controller raises the GPE for every byte of a transfer too, which needs nothing. The queries ask for the event and evaluate `_Qxx` under the controller, while SCI_EVT stays set, 16 at most a time. Then the work clears its flag and looks at SCI_EVT once more, with interrupts off, and queues itself again when it is set, since an edge that came after its last look found the flag set; after a timeout or a query of none it waits for the next GPE. A query found pending when the GPE is taken runs at once.
* The firmware's `ERAM` field on the Lenovo is declared with `Lock`, so each access takes the Global Lock, which `acrach2.h` takes for real.

### The buttons and the video bus

* The FADT's fixed power button, which a clear `PWR_BUTTON` flag says the machine has, takes a fixed event handler in the SCI; each present `PNP0C0C` device takes a notify handler, run on `kacpid`, for which Notify 0x80 is a press. Each is an event device "Power Button" of its own, `LNXPWRBN/button/input0` or `PNP0C0C/button/input0` as `EVIOCGPHYS` gives it, of Linux's product number 1. The sleep button is the same with `SLP_BUTTON`, `PNP0C0E`, "Sleep Button" and product 3. A key is reported pressed and released at once, as Linux's button driver reports it, and restarts the console's countdown to blank and wakes a blank one, as a keystroke does, for the video bus's keys too; and the record `acpi: event: power button` is logged. The Lenovo's firmware reports the power button both as the fixed event and as `_Q32`'s Notify on `PWRB`, so one press may give two `KEY_POWER`.
* A present device with `_DOD` or `_DOS` is a video bus, as Linux's `acpi_video.c` tells one. It is the event device "Video Bus", `LNXVIDEO/video/input0`, product 6, with notify handlers on the bus and on each child device, its outputs. `_DOS` is called as Linux calls it: 4 when the firmware asked ACPICA's `_OSI` for Windows 8 or later, which keeps the firmware from changing the brightness itself, and 0 otherwise. A Notify of 0x85 to 0x89 on an output and 0x80 on the bus become the keys [acpi(4)](../../../man/man4/acpi.4) lists. The backlight is the chipset's PWM ([`sys/display/`](../display/Readme.md)) and user space's to change. The discovery keeps the first output with `_BCM` and a `_BCL` of two levels or more, and once the native display drivers have probed, from `arch_drm_probe` (`acpi_video_register_backlight`), it becomes the backlight `acpi_video0` of type `firmware` only when no backlight is registered by then, as Linux's `acpi_video_register_backlight` waits for the GPU drivers: i915 registers `intel_backlight` at its bind even where the firmware left the PWM off and the boot's probe found none, so two drivers never set one panel. Its levels are the indices of `_BCL`'s list after its first two entries, sorted and without repeats, its level at the start is the one `_BQC` reads or else the highest, and a level set is given to `_BCM` on the `kacpid` thread by an `osl_work`, since the class sets it under a spinlock, so that no full queue drops a blank's or a wake's level. A `_BCL` whose least level is above 0, which the ACPI specification allows, leaves the panel lit at the class's level 0, and the class is told so (`backlight_set_lit_at_zero`). The Lenovo's PWM is native, so its `_BCM`, which needs a graphics driver, is not called.

### The IdeaPad's VPC2004

`ideapad.c` drives the first present `VPC2004`, as Linux's `ideapad-laptop` does; on the Lenovo G50-70 it is `\_SB.PCI0.LPCB.EC0.VPC0`. The device reaches the embedded controller's command and data registers through two methods: `VPCW (1, cmd)` writes a command and `VPCW (0, data)` its data, `VPCR (1)` reads the command register, which the controller clears once it has taken the command, and `VPCR (0)` the data register. A read writes the command, waits for the command register to read 0 and reads the data; a write writes the data, then the command, and waits. A wait reads the command register with a pause of 100 microseconds between reads, at least 200 ms in all, Linux's limit, and gives up with `-ETIMEDOUT`. One command runs at a time, under a semaphore of one unit, since a Notify's reads on `kacpid` and an rfkill write in a process may come together. The commands are Linux's `VPCCMD_` numbers.

* At boot, from `acpica_boot` after the video buses, `_CFG` is evaluated as Linux evaluates it. On the G50-70, under the `OSYS` that ACPICA's `_OSI` answers lead to, it only sets the firmware's `APFG` and returns `_VPC`, 0xFDE104; under Windows 7's it would also set the controller's Wi-Fi and Bluetooth bits. A device whose `_CFG` fails is not taken.
* The event device "Ideapad extra buttons", `ideapad/input0`, on `BUS_HOST`, has the keys of Linux's map for the event bits. `VPCCMD_W_RF` 1 turns the controller's radio switch on, as Linux does on a machine without a switch of its own, Linux's list of the machines with one being by name and Rach2 having none. When `_CFG`'s bit 18 is set the rfkill switch `ideapad_wlan` is registered, of type `wlan` and of no radio, whose soft block is written as `VPCCMD_W_WIFI`; it starts as rfkill starts a switch, and nothing is written for it at boot, so the controller's Wi-Fi bit stays as the firmware left it, 0 on the G50-70 while the card has power. The radio switch is read last.
* A Notify, of any value, reads `VPCCMD_R_VPC1` and `VPCCMD_R_VPC2`, the low and high bytes of the event bits, which the reads clear. Bits 6, 7, 8, 11 and 13 are keys, reported by `acpi_report_key`; bit 13 is `KEY_WLAN`, Linux's airplane key, which the G50-70's F7 sets (`bits 0x2000`). Bit 9 reads `VPCCMD_R_RF` again; it would be `ideapad_wlan`'s hard block on a machine with a switch, and is only logged here. The others, bit 4 of the brightness keys among them, which the video bus reports, are only logged.
* The airplane key blocks no radio itself: BusyBox's acpid runs `/etc/acpi/wifi` for `KEY_WLAN` ([init(8)](../../../man/man8/init.8)), which blocks the Wi-Fi switches, each radio's `phyN` and `ideapad_wlan`, or unblocks them.

The protocol and the events are apart from ACPICA's types in `ideapad.c`, behind `struct ideapad_ops`, and its ACPICA glue is left out under `RACH2_HOSTTEST`, so that a host test drives the driver over a model of the methods.

### PCI hot plug

`pcihp.c` takes the hot plug slots of the first host bridge, `PNP0A03` or a bridge whose `_CID` names it, of bus 0, as Linux's acpiphp does: each child device with `_ADR` and `_EJ0` is a slot, device `_ADR >> 16` of bus 0, with a system notify handler. QEMU's `pc` machine has 30, every slot but the host bridge's and the PIIX's, and its GPE 1 method, `_E01`, sends Notify 1, Device Check, to a slot QMP's `device_add` filled and Notify 3, Eject Request, to one `device_del` empties. Every host bridge's `_CRS` gives its windows, each range of ports and of memory at or above 1 MiB in an address descriptor, as Linux's acpi_pci_root_create reads them, which the PCI layer keeps, as many as there are, joining windows of a kind that overlap as Linux's acpi_pci_root_validate_resources does, for the bridge's root bus (`pci_root_window_add`), the first bus number its `_CRS` gives or else its `_BBN` or else 0, as Linux's acpi_pci_root_add finds it; a BAR the host reaches through that root and outside its windows is not claimed, and bus 0's windows are where a hot plugged card's BARs go. The boot logs `pci: hotplug: N slots on bus 0, M windows`.

* A Bus or Device Check lists the slot's functions the PCI list lacks (`pci_slot_add`), gives each BAR without an address the lowest free one in the windows (`pci_assign_bars`, [the kernel's PCI](../pci.h)), sends the function's `add` uevent and gives it to virtio-pci (`virtio_pci_bind`), the driver that takes a function after boot, as Linux's bus probes its drivers; it logs `pci: hotplug: added: 00:DD.F VVVV:DDDD`, with `, without addresses` for a function whose BARs fit nowhere, which no driver is given.
* An Eject Request has virtio-pci let go of each function of the slot it has (`virtio_pci_unbind`), as Linux's acpiphp removes the drivers; turns each function's decoding off, sends its `remove` uevent and takes the slot off the list (`pci_slot_remove`); then runs the slot's `_EJ0 (1)`, after which QEMU takes the card away and sends `DEVICE_DELETED`. It logs `pci: hotplug: removed: 00:DD.F` for each function and `pci: hotplug: ejected: slot D`.
* Both run on `kacpid`, under the big lock that every search of the PCI list holds.

### /proc/acpi/call

`acpi_call_write` copies the request, splits it into its path and up to seven integer or string arguments, and evaluates the path with `AcpiEvaluateObject` in the writer's process, where a method may sleep. The result is kept as text, under a spinlock, for the next read by anyone: an integer, a string, a buffer, a package of them up to four deep, nothing as `0x0`, or the error, as Linux's `acpi_call` spells them. Each call is logged as `acpi: call: PATH: RESULT`. A Notify the method gives runs after it, on `kacpid`.

### The power off

The power off goes through ACPICA as Linux's `acpi_power_off` does. `acpica_power_off_prepare`, from `kernel_shutdown` while the caller may still sleep, runs `AcpiEnterSleepStatePrep(5)`, which reads `\_S5` and runs the firmware's `\_PTS(5)` and `_SST`; then it disables every GPE and waits for the work queued on `kacpid`. `acpica_power_off`, from `power_stop` with interrupts off and the other processors stopped, runs `AcpiEnterSleepState(5)`: ACPICA clears WAK_STS, disables every GPE, clears the status of every fixed event and GPE and enables the wake GPEs, of which the kernel sets none, then writes the sleep types. A status left set, as a press of the power button leaves PWRBTN_STS, may wake the machine as soon as it is off. `AcpiOsEnterSleep` then writes the sleep types with SLP_EN to the PM1 control registers, or to the sleep control register of a hardware-reduced machine, and returns `AE_CTRL_TERMINATE`, so that ACPICA returns at once instead of waiting ten seconds and spinning for good; `power_stop` waits, and on a machine still running writes `\_S5` itself and then tries the board's power controller ([the PC platform](../arch/x86/Readme.md)). The records are `poweroff: acpica: \_PTS(5) run, gpes disabled`, which netconsole still sends, then `poweroff: acpica: status cleared` and `poweroff: powering off`.

## Building

The host build compiles ACPICA's files from the core that `vendor/acpica/build_acpica.sh` extracts under `build/`, as C89 with the architecture's flags and `-Wall -Werror`, not the kernel's whole set of warnings, under which ACPICA's unused parameters and unnamed unions give thousands, and without a sanitizer the caller asks for. The kernel's own files of this directory include ACPICA's headers as system headers, so that ACPICA's warnings stay out of theirs. On Rach2, `guest.mk` unpacks the core from the archive the image stages and gives every file ACPICA's flags; the kernel's files include no header by those names.

The source checks, `check_sources.sh`, `fmtcheck.sh` and `srclists.sh`, read the files in the tree, and ACPICA's are under `build/`, so they see the kernel's files here and not ACPICA's. `tools/verify/guestcheck.c` holds `guest.mk` to the host's lists, ACPICA's among them, and bounds the depth of the kernel's include chains through ACPICA's headers.

## Limits

* Of the general-purpose events only those the firmware gave a method and the embedded controller's are enabled; thermal events are not handled.
* Only the first embedded controller is driven, and one whose `_GPE` is a package naming a GPE block device takes no events. A controller that keeps SCI_EVT set without end, with a new event each time, is asked 16 times, then again behind the other work queued on `kacpid`, without end. Burst mode is not used.
* The lid, `PNP0C0D`, has no driver; its Notify is dropped. Of the IdeaPad's `VPC2004`, the novo button, the touchpad's switch, the camera's and Bluetooth's switches, the keyboard's Fn lock and the backlight are not driven, and only the first device is taken.
* `/proc/acpi/call` takes no buffer or package arguments, and a result longer than 511 bytes is cut.
* The kernel asks ACPICA for no `_PRT`, and no device is routed by it.
* Work an SCI queues waits for `kacpid`, which starts with the first program and does not run while the debug shell waits at its prompt, since the shell runs in a process's place and lets no thread run: a GPE's method, a PCI hot plug among them, runs once a program does.
* PCI hot plug covers the slots of bus 0's host bridge, not those behind a bridge. A card put in is given to virtio-pci alone: a function of another kind is listed without a driver. A card another driver has, vfio-pci or a driver bound at boot that cannot let go of its card, is not ejected, with the record `pci: hotplug: kept: 00:DD.F: DRIVER cannot let go of it`. An I/O BAR is given ports from 0xC000, where the PC's firmware puts the functions' own, since the fixed ports below, ACPI's and the hot plug controller's, are not read from the namespace as Linux reads them.
* 256 windows of memory; a table or region past them is not mapped.
* ACPICA's debugger and disassembler are not compiled.

## Tests

* `tests/run acpi` boots the test image on QEMU's `pc` and `q35` machines, on each architecture, and requires the records `acpi: acpica: N objects, 0 errors` and `acpi: sci: line N`, and after QMP's `system_powerdown` `acpi: event: power button` and `KEY_POWER` on "Power Button", which `tests/evprobe.c` reads. Its video case gives QEMU the SSDT `tests/cases/machine/acpi_video.asl`, a video bus of one output with `_BCL`, `_BCM` and `_BQC` over the levels 0 to 100 by 5, compiled by the pinned release's `iasl` into the `.aml` beside it, which the screen suite's levels case also gives as its backlight; evprobe calls its methods through `/proc/acpi/call`, with arguments, and must read `KEY_BRIGHTNESSDOWN` and `KEY_BRIGHTNESSUP` from its `\RTST`. A boot with `rach2.noacpica` requires the record that ACPICA was skipped and a power off through `\_S5`. QEMU has no embedded controller, which only the Lenovo shows.
* `tests/run virtionet`'s `machine.virtionet.hotplug` boots the test image with no card and puts a virtio-net card in slot 6 and takes it out through QMP while vnetprobe runs, on each architecture ([network](../network/Readme.md)).
* `tests/host/acpi_ec_hosttest.c` runs `ecio.c` against a model of a controller's ports, its timeouts among them.
