# `kvm/`: the KVM interface

The `kvm/` directory is the kernel's side of Linux's KVM API, version 12, on the x86-64 kernel alone: `/dev/kvm`, the VM and vCPU descriptors its ioctls make, each vCPU's run page, the state ioctls QEMU calls, the run loop, and the interface a processor's backend, AMD's SVM or Intel's VMX, gives the rest. A program such as QEMU uses it as it uses Linux's, through `linux/kvm.h`. The full controller keeps the local APIC, PIC and IOAPIC in the kernel, with an optional PIT; the split controller keeps only the local APIC there. Both have MSI routes, irqfds and ioeventfds. Guest memory is the caller's, through memory slots that follow every change to it, the guest-physical tables EPT and nested paging walk, and a dirty log. A guest whose CPUID names Hyper-V gets Linux's Hyper-V enlightenments: its MSRs, the reference TSC page, SynIC and its timers, and its hypercalls, with the IPI and TLB flush ones that Windows and Linux guests use. Every guest is offered KVM's own paravirtual interface, as Linux's: its two CPUID leaves, the kvmclock with Linux's master clock, the steal time, and the hypercalls that kick a halted vCPU, send IPIs and yield. Both Intel's VMX (`vmx.c`) and AMD's SVM (`svm.c`) run guests. [kvm(4)](../../../man/man4/kvm.4) is the user's contract.

## Interfaces

* `/dev/kvm`, character device 10:232, mode 0666, registered by `kvm_start` (`kernel.c`) when a backend's `probe` succeeds, VMX tried first; the boot logs `kvm: ready: vmx` or `kvm: ready: svm`. Without either there is no node, as Linux loads no KVM module.
* `/sys/module/kvm/parameters`, and `kvm_intel`'s or `kvm_amd`'s beside it, made by `kvm_start` with the node: the read-only parameters Linux's KVM selftests read, with Rach2's values, which [sysfs(5)](../../../man/man5/sysfs.5) lists. Every selftest skips without `force_emulation_prefix`.
* The ioctls of `/dev/kvm`, of a VM and of a vCPU, numbered and laid out as Linux's (`kvm_abi.h`). An ioctl a level does not have is `ENOTTY` on `/dev/kvm` and a VM, and `EINVAL` on a vCPU, as on Linux. The VM and vCPU descriptors are `anon_inode:kvm-vm` and `anon_inode:kvm-vcpu:N`, read-write and close-on-exec.
* A vCPU's mapping: KVM_GET_VCPU_MMAP_SIZE is three pages, the `struct kvm_run` page, the page port I/O data goes through (`KVM_PIO_PAGE_OFFSET` 1) and the VM's ring of coalesced writes (`KVM_COALESCED_MMIO_PAGE_OFFSET` 2), the same page in every vCPU's mapping. A shared mapping maps the pages cached, and a fork's child shares it; a private one copies each page at its first touch, as a private mapping of a file does here ([memory](../mm/Readme.md)). Either holds a reference to the VM, so the pages outlive the descriptors.
* KVM_CREATE_DEVICE and the VFIO bridge's attributes, described under [VFIO assignment](#vfio-assignment).
* A VM belongs to the address space that made it: an ioctl of the VM or of its vCPUs from another, a fork's child's, is `EIO`, as Linux's `kvm->mm` check makes it.
* An ioctl of KVM's type, 0xAE, that ends with `EINTR` is never restarted, whether or not a handler runs, since Linux gives KVM_RUN's `EINTR` to the caller as it is (`ioctl_restartable` in `syscall.c`, [signals](../proc/signal.Readme.md)).
* KVM_SET_USER_MEMORY_REGION, KVM_SET_USER_MEMORY_REGION2, KVM_GET_DIRTY_LOG and KVM_CLEAR_DIRTY_LOG on a VM, and KVM_ENABLE_CAP of KVM_CAP_MANUAL_DIRTY_LOG_PROTECT2, with Linux's checks and errors.
* KVM_SET_TSC_KHZ and KVM_GET_TSC_KHZ on a vCPU, and on a VM for the rate its later vCPUs start at, with Linux's results ([the TSC's rate](#the-tscs-rate)). KVM_CAP_TSC_CONTROL and KVM_CAP_VM_TSC_CONTROL are 1 on a processor that scales a guest's TSC, by SVM's TSC ratio MSR or VMX's TSC multiplier, and 0 on one that does not, as the Lenovo's Haswell and QEMU's TCG.
* KVM_REGISTER_COALESCED_MMIO and KVM_UNREGISTER_COALESCED_MMIO on a VM, for a zone of MMIO addresses or, with `pio` 1, of ports; KVM_CAP_COALESCED_MMIO answers the ring's page in a vCPU's mapping, 2, and KVM_CAP_COALESCED_PIO 1 ([the buses](#interrupts)).
* KVM_GET_STATS_FD on a VM and on a vCPU, with KVM_CAP_BINARY_STATS_FD: a read-only, close-on-exec descriptor, `anon_inode:kvm-vm-stats` or `anon_inode:kvm-vcpu-stats:N`, whose file is Linux's: `struct kvm_stats_header`, the id `kvm-PID` or `kvm-PID/vcpu-N`, the descriptors of Linux 6.12's statistics in its order, 15 of a VM and 45 of a vCPU, and their values, read at any offset. The file holds a reference to the VM, so it reads after the VM's descriptors close. A vCPU's `exits`, `io_exits`, `mmio_exits`, `halt_exits`, `irq_window_exits`, `signal_exits`, `hypercalls`, `insn_emulation`, `insn_emulation_fail`, `preemption_reported` and `preemption_other` count, and a VM's `pages_4k`, `pages_2m`, `remote_tlb_flush` and `remote_tlb_flush_requests`; the rest read 0.
* KVM_PRE_FAULT_MEMORY on a vCPU, with KVM_CAP_PRE_FAULT_MEMORY: the range is mapped in the guest-physical tables as its faults would map it, a leaf at a time, and written back moved past what was mapped. Flags, an unaligned, empty or wrapping range are `EINVAL`, an address in no slot `ENOENT` and a signal `EINTR`, each only when nothing was mapped before it, as on Linux.
* KVM_ENABLE_CAP on a VM of KVM_CAP_DISABLE_QUIRKS and KVM_CAP_DISABLE_QUIRKS2, whose value names the quirks that can be turned off ([capabilities](#capabilities)); of KVM_CAP_MAX_VCPU_ID, once and before the first vCPU, for a bound on the ids below Linux's 4096; of KVM_CAP_EXIT_ON_EMULATION_FAILURE; of KVM_CAP_MSR_PLATFORM_INFO; of KVM_CAP_X86_USER_SPACE_MSR, for the reasons a guest's refused RDMSR or WRMSR goes to the caller ([MSRs](#msrs)); of KVM_CAP_X2APIC_API, whose flags, once set, stay set ([interrupts](#interrupts)); and of KVM_CAP_X86_APIC_BUS_CYCLES_NS, with the local APIC in the kernel and before the first vCPU, for the length of the APIC timer's tick, 1 ns unless it is set.
* KVM_X86_SET_MSR_FILTER on a VM (KVM_CAP_X86_MSR_FILTER), the filter of the guest's MSR accesses ([MSRs](#msrs)); a 32-bit caller's layout is translated in `ia32_ioctl.c`.
* KVM_GET_DEVICE_ATTR and KVM_HAS_DEVICE_ATTR on `/dev/kvm` (KVM_CAP_SYS_ATTRIBUTES) of KVM_X86_XCOMP_GUEST_SUPP, the XCR0 bits a guest may have; any other attribute is `ENXIO`.
* KVM_HAS_DEVICE_ATTR, KVM_GET_DEVICE_ATTR and KVM_SET_DEVICE_ATTR on a vCPU (KVM_CAP_VCPU_ATTRIBUTES) of KVM_VCPU_TSC_OFFSET in group KVM_VCPU_TSC_CTRL, the offset of the vCPU's TSC from the host's, as Linux's `kvm_arch_tsc_set_attr` ([KVM's paravirtual interface](#kvms-paravirtual-interface)); any other group or attribute is `ENXIO`.
* The run page's synchronized registers (KVM_CAP_SYNC_REGS): the registers, the special registers and the events, copied to the page after a run as `kvm_valid_regs` asks and taken from it before one as `kvm_dirty_regs` says, a bit outside the three `EINVAL`.
* KVM_SET_VAPIC_ADDR accepts address zero on a vCPU with a controller in the kernel, which QEMU sends on APIC reset even without KVM_CAP_VAPIC. The optional shared VAPIC page is not offered.
* `/proc/stat` counts live VMs, vCPUs, slots, guest-physical table pages and run-page mapping objects as `kvm_vms`, `kvm_vcpus`, `kvm_slots`, `kvm_tdp_pages` and `kvm_run_mappings`. A fork shares a run-page mapping object. These counts include a VM kept by a mapping after its descriptors close and reach zero only after the last reference goes. Beside them, `kvm_ioeventfd_signals` and `kvm_irqfd_raises` count since boot the guests' writes an ioeventfd took and the MSIs an irqfd raised, the work done for a guest without an exit to its caller.
* `/proc/stat` also reports `kvm_exits`, the cumulative count of completed backend entries, including exits handled inside KVM_RUN, read under the big lock. Failed entries whose backend returns an error are excluded.
* The fdinfo of a VM's descriptor, `/proc/PID/fdinfo/N`, gives the VM's interrupt controller as `kvm_irqchip:` and the steps its interrupts reached, so that an interrupt lost on the way shows at the step after the last one it reached. Each `kvm_ioapic_pin:` line is a pin of the in-kernel I/O APIC: raised, coalesced into an edge still high or an RTC interrupt not yet ended, held by the entry's mask or remote IRR, delivered, and delivered to no APIC (`enum kvm_pin_step`, `ioapic.c`). Each `kvm_vcpu_vector:` line is a vCPU's index and a vector: accepted into IRR, dropped by an APIC disabled in hardware or software, acknowledged into ISR or taken from the PIC, injected at an entry, saved from SVM's EXITINTINFO or VMX's IDT-vectoring information, and ended by an EOI (`enum kvm_vec_step`, `vlapic.c` and the backends). A vector taken from the PIC is ended at the PIC, so it has acknowledgements and injections and no EOIs; Linux gives the PIC's IRQ0 the vector of the I/O APIC's pin 2, 0x30, and its timer check delivers a few through the PIC before the I/O APIC. Only lines with a count appear. The counts wrap at 2^32 and are written without a lock, so a read while the guest runs may lag by the steps under way.
* KVM's paravirtual interface, as Linux 6.12's `x86.c` and `cpuid.c` have it: KVM_GET_SUPPORTED_CPUID's leaves 0x40000000, with the signature "KVMKVMKVM", and 0x40000001, whose features are NOP_IO_DELAY, STEAL_TIME, PV_UNHALT, PV_SEND_IPI, POLL_CONTROL and PV_SCHED_YIELD, and CLOCKSOURCE, CLOCKSOURCE2 and CLOCKSOURCE_STABLE_BIT once the TSC's rate is known; the kvmclock's MSRs, old and new, MSR_KVM_STEAL_TIME with KVM_CAP_STEAL_TIME, and MSR_KVM_POLL_CONTROL; KVM_GET_CLOCK's flags TSC_STABLE, REALTIME and HOST_TSC under the master clock; KVM_KVMCLOCK_CTRL on a vCPU; KVM_ENABLE_CAP of KVM_CAP_ENFORCE_PV_FEATURE_CPUID on a vCPU, and of KVM_CAP_X86_DISABLE_EXITS on a VM, with HLT and PAUSE; and the hypercalls KVM_HC_KICK_CPU, KVM_HC_SEND_IPI, KVM_HC_SCHED_YIELD and KVM_HC_CLOCK_PAIRING.
* Hyper-V's, as Linux's `hyperv.c` has them: KVM_GET_SUPPORTED_HV_CPUID on `/dev/kvm` and on a vCPU, KVM_HYPERV_EVENTFD on a VM, KVM_ENABLE_CAP of KVM_CAP_HYPERV_SYNIC, KVM_CAP_HYPERV_SYNIC2 and KVM_CAP_HYPERV_ENFORCE_CPUID on a vCPU, the routes of type KVM_IRQ_ROUTING_HV_SINT, and the exit KVM_EXIT_HYPERV with its SynIC, hypercall and debugger forms.
* `kvm.h` is the interface of the directory's files to each other: the backend's operations, the VM and vCPU records, and each module's calls, among them `kvm_slot_find`, which the guest-physical tables, VFIO and the transport's buffers call to turn a guest frame into the caller's address, `kvm_tdp_fault`, which a backend calls on a guest-physical fault, and `kvm_tdp_root` with the request KVM_REQ_TLB_FLUSH, by which the backend finds the tables and learns when to drop its translations of them.

## Design

### The records

A VM (`struct kvm_vm`) counts its references in `refs`: its descriptor, each vCPU's descriptor and each mapping of a run page. A vCPU lives as long as its VM, as on Linux, so closing a vCPU's descriptor drops its VM reference and nothing else; the last reference frees each vCPU, the modules' shares, the backend's and the VM, and lets go of the address space it held with `mm_hold`. A vCPU's run pages are two pages of the kernel's own, made at KVM_CREATE_VCPU. The descriptors' objects begin with a `struct anon_obj`, as every anonymous descriptor's does ([io](../io/Readme.md)).

### Capabilities

Each module answers only the capabilities it makes true, so a capability turns on with the task that makes it so. `kvm.c` answers its own, then asks `slots.c`, `irq.c`, `iobus.c` and the backend in turn; the stubs answer 0. KVM_CHECK_EXTENSION on a VM gives the same answers as on `/dev/kvm`, since no capability depends on the VM's type.

The bounds are Linux's: KVM_CAP_MAX_VCPUS is 1024 and KVM_CAP_MAX_VCPU_ID 4096, and KVM_CAP_NR_VCPUS the host's processors.

The quirks of KVM_CAP_DISABLE_QUIRKS2 are Linux's, each on until the caller turns it off, and one turned off stays off, as in Linux 6.12.115, whose capability ORs its argument into the VM's set. LINT0_REENABLED gives the boot vCPU's LINT0 ExtINT at reset. CD_NW_CLEARED, SVM's alone, keeps CR0.CD and CR0.NW from the processor; with it off they reach it as the guest set them. LAPIC_MMIO_HOLE reads the APIC's page as all ones in x2APIC mode or with the APIC disabled. OUT_7E_INC_RIP steps RIP past an OUT to port 0x7E before the caller sees its exit. MISC_ENABLE_NO_MWAIT leaves CPUID's MONITOR bit alone when the guest writes IA32_MISC_ENABLE; with it off the MSR's MWAIT bit is the guest's CPUID bit, and changes only for a guest with SSE3. FIX_HYPERCALL_INSN writes the processor's hypercall instruction over the other vendor's; with it off that instruction is #UD. MWAIT_NEVER_UD_FAULTS runs MONITOR and MWAIT as no-ops; with it off they are #UD for a guest whose CPUID lacks them. STUFF_FEATURE_MSRS starts a vCPU's MSR_PLATFORM_INFO with CPUID faulting offered and its MSR_IA32_UCODE_REV at Linux's 0x100000000 on VMX and 0x01000065 on SVM; with it off a new vCPU's are 0, for the caller to fill. UCODE_REV is on the index list, the caller's to write with KVM_SET_MSRS and the guest's to read, and a guest's write of it changes nothing; KVM_GET_MSRS on `/dev/kvm` reads the host's own revision, as Linux's kvm_get_feature_msr does. IGNORE_GUEST_PAT, VMX's alone and offered only on a processor that snoops itself, maps RAM write-back whatever the guest's PAT says. SLOT_ZAP_ALL and VMCS12_ALLOW_FREEZE_IN_SMM are taken and change nothing: a deleted or moved slot drops its own part of the tables either way, which no guest can tell from dropping them all, and there is no nested VMCS.

### The run loop

KVM_RUN (`vcpu.c`) holds the vCPU for the call, swaps in the signal mask KVM_SET_SIGNAL_MASK gave, and gives the backend the caller's answer to the last exit's read, from the port I/O page or the run page's MMIO data, the news that the caller wrote the last exit's OUT, or the result of a Hyper-V hypercall the caller carried out, before anything else, so the answer is not lost when `immediate_exit` or a signal ends the call. The instruction that read is then finished with no guest entered, as Linux's `complete_userspace_io` finishes it: its registers are written, its fault queued or its next exit given to the caller, before any event is chosen and before the call returns, so a POPF or MOV SS from device memory takes effect before an interrupt is injected, and the caller sees the registers after the instruction. A read that a device in the kernel answers finishes its instruction the same way. With `immediate_exit` set it then returns `EINTR` at once. Otherwise, before each entry, it answers a triple fault with KVM_EXIT_SHUTDOWN and a signal or a request to leave with KVM_EXIT_INTR and `EINTR`, lets `kvm_hv_prepare` write Hyper-V's TSC page or end the run in its exits, injects a waiting event, and enters: with interrupts off it marks the vCPU in the guest, checks the requests once more, lets go of the big lock and calls the backend's `enter`; it takes the lock back after the exit and turns interrupts on, which lets the host's interrupt that ended the guest run. The backend's `exit` then names the exit. A port or MMIO write the bus takes and an exit the kernel handles enter again, and so does a HLT once an interrupt ends it, when the local APIC is in the kernel (`kvm_lapic_halt`); the rest go to the caller through the run page, a port or MMIO read waiting for its answer. Under KVM_CAP_SYNC_REGS the registers `kvm_dirty_regs` names are taken from the run page before the loop, and those `kvm_valid_regs` names are written to it when the call ends. `kvm_vcpu_kick` leaves a request and, when the vCPU is in the guest on another processor, interrupts that processor with `smp_kick`, so the guest exits and the loop sees the request.

### The backend

`struct kvm_backend` (`kvm.h`) is the contract: `probe` at boot; `vm_init`, `vm_free`, `vcpu_init` and `vcpu_free`; `enter`, which runs with interrupts off and without the big lock, takes no lock and does not sleep; `exit`, under the big lock, which fills `v->exit` and the interrupt flags the run page reports, and hands a VMCALL or VMMCALL to `kvm_hv_hypercall` while `kvm_hv_hypercall_enabled` holds; `complete`, `save` and `inject`; `check_extension`; `tdp`, the format of its guest-physical entries, EPT's or the AMD64 format, so that one walker in `tdp.c` builds both; and `tsc_scaling`, the largest ratio the processor scales a guest's TSC by, 0 for none. The flush that KVM_REQ_TLB_FLUSH asks for drops the guest's own translations with the guest-physical ones, since Hyper-V's flush hypercalls rely on it: INVEPT does on VMX, and a flush of the ASID must on SVM.

### VMX

`vmx.c` turns VMX on for each run and off after it: `enter`, with interrupts off, sets CR4.VMXE, locks IA32_FEATURE_CONTROL with VMX on where the firmware left it unlocked, and runs VMXON, VMPTRLD, VMLAUNCH, and at the exit VMCLEAR and VMXOFF. A VMCS so never stays current across the big lock, a vCPU moves between processors with nothing to migrate, and an INIT, a kexec or a panic finds the processor out of VMX operation; the panic hook (`k_panic_hook`), which the panicking processor runs and each processor a panic's or a restart's stop halts runs before it halts (`smp_stop_nmi`, `smp_service`), clears a VMCS left current by a fault or an NMI inside the section before VMXOFF, as Linux's emergency disable does. The cost is a VMXON and a full VMCS load for each entry, which Linux pays once per processor and once per migration. Every VMX instruction and every other touch of the processor, MSRs, control and debug registers, the FPU and the host's handlers, goes through the table `vmx_op`, so the host test runs the same code over a model.

The probe takes the controls from the capability MSRs, the true ones where IA32_VMX_BASIC offers them, and needs EPT of four levels, write-back, with INVEPT, the unrestricted guest, the preemption timer, virtual NMIs, the NMI and interrupt window exits, and the exit and entry controls of EFER, PAT and the debug registers; VPID, RDTSCP, INVPCID, WBINVD exiting, XSAVES and TSC scaling are taken where they are. RDTSCP, INVPCID and XSAVES are turned on for a vCPU only where its CPUID offers them, XSAVES with XSAVE, since each is #UD without its control, and the XSS-exiting bitmap is 0. With TSC scaling the VMCS's TSC multiplier, 48 bits of fraction, is written with the TSC offset: the vCPU's ratio, or 1. The unrestricted guest runs real mode and unpaged protected mode as they are, so no mode is emulated. EPT's pointer is write-back with accessed and dirty bits where the processor has them; each vCPU has a VPID, or 0 when all 65535 are taken, which drops the guest's translations at every entry. KVM_REQ_TLB_FLUSH and an entry on a processor other than the last are INVEPT of the VM's context and INVVPID of the vCPU's. The preemption timer bounds each run at 2^22 TSC ticks, so a guest that never exits still lets the run loop see a signal or a request.

The guest's STAR, LSTAR, CSTAR, SFMASK, KERNEL_GS_BASE and TSC_AUX go in and out through the entry's and exit's MSR lists, and SYSENTER, PAT, EFER and DEBUGCTL through the VMCS; each vCPU's MSR bitmap passes the guest's FS, GS and KERNEL_GS_BASE accesses that the MSR filter allows, and every other RDMSR and WRMSR exits to `msr.c`. CR0's CD and NW never reach the processor, whose memory types EPT gives, and CR3 does not exit. The guest's FPU is loaded under its XCR0 for the run, by XRSTOR or, without XSAVE, FXRSTOR, and saved after it. An NMI or a machine check that ends the run is the host's, which `enter` passes to the host's handler once VMX is off; the host's interrupt waits until the run loop turns interrupts on.

Events go in by Linux's order in `kvm_check_and_inject_events`: an exception, an NMI, then an interrupt, the one the last exit found under delivery first; an NMI or interrupt the guest cannot take arms the matching window exit. An exception in real mode carries no error code, a fault but #DB, #BP, #OF, #DF and #MC sets RF in the RFLAGS its delivery saves, as Linux sets it before injecting (`kvm_exception_rf`, for both backends), and NMI blocking is set by the delivery, never before the entry. KVM_SET_GUEST_DEBUG's single step runs the guest under TF, which the caller's RFLAGS do not show, and reports KVM_EXIT_DEBUG with DR6.BS after each instruction, the kernel's own among them; on VMX the STI or MOV SS shadow ends before each entry under the step, as Linux's `vmx_vcpu_run` ends it, since the caller has taken the step over STI or MOV SS and the entry refuses the shadow under TF without a pending single-step trap; software breakpoints take #BP, hardware ones run in the caller's DR0 to DR3 and DR7, and BLOCKIRQ holds new NMIs and interrupts.

The exception bitmap is Linux's with EPT: #DB, #UD, #AC and #MC always, and #BP while the caller sets software breakpoints. #DB and #AC exit so that a guest cannot hold the processor in an endless loop of either, as Linux has taken them since CVE-2015-8104 and CVE-2015-5307. A #DB that is the guest's own goes back to it (`kvm_queue_db`): its bits, which the exit qualification holds, are folded into the guest's DR6 as Linux's `kvm_deliver_exception_payload` folds them, DR7.GD is cleared, and the exception is injected; INT1 exits on the instruction, so RIP steps past it first; and under an interrupt shadow with TF the entry has the single-step trap pending, which the processor checks. The guest's DR6 is so the record's alone: every MOV DR exits, the processor's DR6 is never loaded, and DR0 to DR3 are loaded only while the DR7 the guest runs under enables a breakpoint. #AC goes back with its error code. #UD goes to the emulator (`kvm_emul_ud`), as Linux's `handle_ud` sends it.

### SVM

`svm.c` requires SVM and nested paging, with at least two ASIDs, and refuses a processor whose firmware set VM_CR.SVMDIS. Each processor enables EFER.SVME and installs its host save area at the first entry. Each vCPU owns its VMCB, a second save page for VMLOAD and VMSAVE, which move FS, GS, TR, LDTR and the system-call MSRs, and an MSR permission map of two pages, which passes the guest's FS, GS and KERNEL_GS_BASE accesses that the MSR filter allows. `svm_run.s` loads the general registers around VMRUN, with GIF clear while guest state is installed, and restores the host before setting GIF. A host interrupt that ends the run waits until the run loop enables interrupts. A machine check reaches the host's handler. The panic hook sets GIF and disables SVM on its processor.

Nested paging uses `tdp.c`'s AMD64 entries. Each processor allocates ASIDs in generations; the first entry and generation rollover flush all translations. A vCPU receives a new ASID after migration. A requested flush uses flush-by-ASID where CPUID offers it, otherwise a new ASID. Where CPUID offers the TSC ratio MSR, 32 bits of fraction, a processor's MSR holds 1 from its first entry and takes the vCPU's ratio before a VMRUN when it holds another; the MSR scales only a guest's TSC, so it is not put back. The I/O and MSR permission maps intercept every port and every MSR except the accesses of FS_BASE, GS_BASE and KERNEL_GS_BASE that the MSR filter allows, whose state VMLOAD and VMSAVE carry. Without next-RIP save, a refused RDMSR or WRMSR is decoded for its length only when KVM_CAP_X86_USER_SPACE_MSR asked for its reason, and is #GP with no decode otherwise, as Linux's `kvm_msr_user_space`. CR0 is intercepted while CD or NW must be hidden, which is until KVM_X86_QUIRK_CD_NW_CLEARED is turned off, CR4 keeps the host's MCE bit, and CR8 writes update the local APIC's TPR. The backend decodes the intercepted system instructions when NRIP save or decode assists are absent, as under the TCG profile.

Exceptions, NMIs and interrupts are injected in that order, with an event interrupted during delivery reinjected first. The interrupt shadow blocks external interrupts and NMIs. A pending interrupt arms the virtual interrupt intercept; NMI blocking ends after an intercepted IRET runs, with a single step where needed to reach the next NMI window. An enabled Hyper-V hypercall goes from VMMCALL to `hyperv.c`, with NextRIP's instruction length or 3 bytes without NRIP save. #DB, #UD, #AC and #MC are intercepted, as on VMX and for the same reasons: the guest's own #DB goes back through `kvm_queue_db` with the bits the processor left in the VMCB's DR6, and #UD goes to the emulator. Nested SVM instructions raise #UD, since guest CPUID does not offer SVM. XSETBV goes through the guest's CPUID checks, and WBINVD flushes for a noncoherent assigned device.

Both backends restore and save the guest's full XSAVE image under the host's XCR0. The guest's XCR0 takes effect only for guest execution, so a saved component that the guest has disabled cannot make XRSTOR fault; the saved host mask is per processor. The host's XCR0 has PKRU where the host's protection keys are on: the host's PKRU is saved with the rest of its state before the guest's image is loaded and loaded again after the run, and a guest is offered no PKU, so its CPUID lacks the feature, CR4.PKE is reserved to it and KVM_SET_XSAVE refuses PKRU, which the guest's XCR0 may not name. KVM_GET_SUPPORTED_CPUID offers XSAVES where the host has it, on VMX only with the control that lets a guest run it and on SVM not on an AMD Zen 1 or Zen 2 without Erratum 1386's fixed microcode (`kcpu_xsaves_usable`), as Linux 6.12, with no supervisor state: leaf 0xD's subleaf 1 names no IA32_XSS component, and IA32_XSS takes only 0 from the caller and from a guest whose CPUID offers XSAVES, which is #GP for any other. It is on neither MSR list, as on Linux. The host's IA32_XSS, which the kernel never writes, is 0 as well, so neither is switched around a run. EBX of leaf 0xD's subleaves 0 and 1 follows the vCPU's XCR0, as Linux's `kvm_update_cpuid_runtime` keeps it: the standard form's size, and with XSAVEC or XSAVES the compacted form's, set whenever XSETBV, KVM_SET_XCRS or a reset changes XCR0, and in the entries KVM_SET_CPUID2 gives before it takes or compares them, so KVM_GET_CPUID2 reads what the guest's CPUID returns.

### The instructions the kernel runs

`emulate.c` carries out what an exit hands over, for either backend, with Linux's results: CPUID from the guest's table, a leaf it lacks read as Linux's `kvm_cpuid` reads one, XSETBV under the guest's CPUID, RDMSR and WRMSR through `msr.c` and the local APIC, with Linux's rules for a guest's own writes (IA32_TSC_ADJUST moves the TSC by its change and is ignored without the CPUID bit, IA32_TSC moves the adjustment, a machine-check bank's STATUS takes only 0 unless the guest's vendor is AMD or Hygon and HWCR's McStatusWrEn is set, and the P6's and K7's performance counters and event selects read 0 and ignore writes while no PMU is offered), and with the registers of particular processors that Linux's `kvm_get_msr_common` reads as zero and those whose writes its `kvm_set_msr_common` drops answered the same way, AMD's SYSCFG and NB_CFG among them, which Linux on an AMD processor reads and writes with no check for a fault, port I/O with INS, OUTS and REP through the run page's port page and the ioeventfd buses, and each instruction `kvm_decode` decodes, in [the emulator](#the-emulator). It walks the guest's page tables in each paging mode, setting their accessed and dirty bits as the processor does, and queues #PF, #GP or #UD where the processor would. A hardware task switch, which VMX hands over as exit 9 from CALL, IRET, JMP or a task gate, is carried out as Linux's `kvm_task_switch` does it: the 32-bit and 16-bit TSS, the busy bits, NT and the back link, CR3 and the LDT, a virtual-8086 task's segments, CR0.TS and DR7's local enables, the error code an exception through a task gate pushes, and an INT n's check of its gate's DPL. Its faults are the processor's: #TS for a TSS too small and #GP for a gate's DPL or a selector past its table come in the old task with nothing changed, and #TS, #NP, #SS or #GP for the new task's segments come in the new task once TR and the registers are its own. Linux 6.12 drops these faults: its `kvm_task_switch` never injects the exception its emulator returns. A VMCALL or VMMCALL that is not Hyper-V's is one of Linux's KVM hypercalls (`kvm_emul_hypercall`), numbered in RAX with its arguments in RBX, RCX, RDX and RSI and its result in RAX, 32 bits of each outside long mode: KVM_HC_VAPIC_POLL_IRQ and KVM_HC_SCHED_YIELD return 0, KVM_HC_KICK_CPU wakes the halted vCPU whose APIC id RCX names, KVM_HC_SEND_IPI, with the local APIC in the kernel, sends the ICR in RSI to the vCPUs that the bitmap in RBX and RCX names, bit 0 being the APIC id in RDX, and returns how many took it, KVM_HC_CLOCK_PAIRING is -KVM_EOPNOTSUPP, any other number -KVM_ENOSYS, and every call above CPL 0 -KVM_EPERM. An instruction the kernel finishes steps RIP and ends the interrupt shadow, so the vCPU that `sti; hlt` halted takes an interrupt at once; under the guest's TF it queues the single-step #DB, and under the caller's single step it ends the run with KVM_EXIT_DEBUG. A string IN or OUT moves at most what lies before the end of a page and before its offset wraps at the address size, steps rSI, rDI and rCX as the processor does, a 32-bit register's upper half cleared, leaves RFLAGS.RF set while it repeats and clear once it ends, as Linux's `x86_emulate_insn`, stores at the caller's next KVM_RUN of an IN the elements rCX and rDI allow then, at most those the exit asked for, so that a caller that changed rCX meanwhile leaves rCX at 0 and not past it, as Linux's `pio_in_emulated` counts them again, and with rCX 0 stores none and ends with rDI as it was, and when its bytes cannot be read from or written to guest memory ends KVM_RUN with KVM_EXIT_INTERNAL_ERROR, or `EFAULT` for a page the caller cannot touch, with the registers as they were. An OUT that is not a string's steps RIP once it is written: at once when a device in the kernel takes it, and at the caller's next KVM_RUN when the caller does, as Linux's `complete_fast_pio_out`, so the caller sees KVM_EXIT_IO with RIP at the instruction, and a caller that moved RIP meanwhile keeps its RIP. Only an OUT to port 0x7E under KVM_X86_QUIRK_OUT_7E_INC_RIP steps before the exit. The caller's single step over an IN or an OUT is reported at that KVM_RUN, with no guest entered. MSR_PLATFORM_INFO is the caller's to write and the guest's to read, until KVM_CAP_MSR_PLATFORM_INFO with 0 makes the guest's read #GP; while its bit 31 offers CPUID faulting the guest may set bit 0 of MSR_MISC_FEATURES_ENABLES, after which CPUID above CPL 0 is #GP, as on Linux.

### Memory

A slot (`slots.c`) is a range of the caller's memory at a range of guest-physical frames, numbered below 256, made, moved, flagged and deleted by KVM_SET_USER_MEMORY_REGION with the checks of Linux's `kvm_set_memory_region` in its order: the flags, the alignment, the range within user space, the number, the wrap and size, then the deletion of a slot that does not exist, a change of a slot's address in the caller, size or READONLY flag, a range past the host's physical width (`kvm_tdp_frames`), and an overlap with another slot, `EEXIST`. Two slots may map the same memory of the caller, as QEMU's aliases do. A slot finds its frame by a walk of the table, with the last slot found tried first.

Nothing is pinned, as on Linux, whose KVM holds no page longer than a fault. Each slot registers its range of the caller's address space on the change notice ([memory](../mm/Readme.md)), which the memory manager calls under the big lock before a page there goes or changes: munmap, mremap, brk, mprotect, a fork's write protection, a copy-on-write break, madvise, the truncation of a file or device object, and the end of the address space. The call drops what the tables map of that part of the slot and gets every vCPU out of the guest before it returns (`kvm_tdp_flush`), so no processor reaches a frame the memory manager reuses; the guest's next touch faults and finds the page as the caller has it then. A move, a change of flags and a deletion drop the slot's part of the tables the same way. A killed QEMU so leaves no page pinned or counted: its address space's end drops every slot's mappings, and the VM's end frees the slots and the tables.

The guest-physical tables (`tdp.c`) are four levels in the backend's entry format (`struct kvm_tdp_format`), a frame each, reached through the direct map; `kvm_tdp_root` gives the top one for EPTP or nCR3. A guest-physical fault comes to `kvm_tdp_fault` in the vCPU's thread, which is in the caller's address space: the slot's page is brought in as the caller's own touch would (`vm_prefault_range`), which may sleep, then read again from the caller's page table with nothing that sleeps, and the leaf set from it, so the change notice cannot come between the read and the leaf. The leaf is writable when the slot is not read-only and the caller's page is writable, so a copy-on-write page stays read-only for the guest until a guest write breaks it in the caller. A device's frame, one the frame allocator does not count, such as a BAR mapped from `/dev/mem` or by VFIO, is uncached with the guest's PAT in charge, so the guest may make it write-combining; RAM is write-back with the guest's PAT ignored, as Linux's `vmx_get_mt_mask` gives a VM with no device that does not snoop; while VFIO assigns one that does not (`kvm_tdp_noncoherent`), RAM is write-back with the guest's PAT in charge, so the guest's uncached buffers for that device stay uncached, and the first assignment and the last release each drop every leaf so that the next faults take the new type. A 2 MiB leaf maps 2 MiB of guest frames in one slot that does not log when their caller's addresses are one leaf table whose 512 pages are present and 512 consecutive frames from a 2 MiB boundary, all RAM or all a device's; user memory here is mapped in 4 KiB pages, so that is the case for a device's BAR or a contiguous object, and a change to any of the 512 pages drops the whole leaf. A fault where no slot is, or a write to a read-only slot, answers 1, which the backend turns into an MMIO exit. A page the caller cannot touch is `EFAULT`, which ends KVM_RUN, as Linux's "Bad address".

A zap clears the leaves of a range and takes out a table once it maps nothing, flushes when it cleared a leaf or took out a table, and frees the tables after the flush, since until then a vCPU in the guest may walk them or hold them in its paging-structure caches. A flush sets KVM_REQ_TLB_FLUSH on every vCPU, kicks those in the guest and waits until none is: a vCPU leaves the guest within a moment of its kick, and cannot enter again until it takes the big lock, which the flush holds. The backend drops its translations of the VM before an entry with the request set, and before an entry on a processor other than the vCPU's last.

A slot with KVM_MEM_LOG_DIRTY_PAGES has a log (`dirty.c`), a bit for each page in 64-bit words, as Linux lays it out. A write fault marks the page and maps it writable; a read maps a page whose bit is clear read-only, so its first write faults and is logged. Such a slot has no 2 MiB leaf, and turning the log on or off drops the slot's mappings. KVM_GET_DIRTY_LOG takes the log into a buffer of the kernel's, clears it and takes the write right from the leaves it named, flushes, and copies the buffer out, so a write after the call is logged anew. Under KVM_CAP_MANUAL_DIRTY_LOG_PROTECT2 with MANUAL_PROTECT_ENABLE, which QEMU turns on when offered, GET leaves the log, and KVM_CLEAR_DIRTY_LOG clears the bits the caller names, from a page that is a multiple of 64, and protects those pages; with INITIALLY_SET as well, a log starts with every page set and no page protected. `kvm_dirty_mark` is for the kernel's own writes to guest memory.

### A guest's pages in a program

A guest's driver may report a buffer of its own as runs of guest-physical pages, as the VM's backend of the shared-memory GPU transport does ([virtio](../virtio/Readme.md)), and a program of the host may then map those pages. `kvm_gpamap_new` resolves each run through the slots into the addresses of the VM maker's memory, cutting a run where a slot ends, and takes each page's frame from that memory's tables (`paging_user_ram_frame`): a page present, writable and of ordinary RAM, of a region that is neither I/O, a guard nor memfd_secret's, and in a private region not shared by copy-on-write. A page in no slot or a read-only one, not brought in, not RAM or secret is refused with `EFAULT`, as Linux's get_user_pages refuses a secret page, and so is every page once the maker has exited and its address space has ended while the VM lives on through another descriptor; nothing is held then. The frames are held by a reference each, not pinned, so that no locked-memory limit is charged, as Linux's KVM charges none for the pages its faults take.

A map registers one range on the change notice of the maker's memory, from its lowest run to the end of its highest, and any change that meets a run, an unmap, a protection change, a copy-on-write break, a drop or a truncation, takes the map back, as does the end of that address space. So does a slot over a run that is deleted or moved (`slot_delete`, `slot_change`) and the VM's end (`kvm_gpamap_vm_end`, from `kvm_vm_put`). Taking a map back calls its owner's revoke first, which removes every program's mapping of the frames, and then lets go of them; the record stays the owner's until `kvm_gpamap_free`. `/proc/stat` counts the maps and their pages as `kvm_gpa_maps` and `kvm_gpa_map_pages`. The transport finds the VM by the KVM ioeventfd on its doorbell (`kvm_vm_by_eventfd`), since only a VM's own QEMU registers it there.

### The emulator

Two exits hand the kernel a whole instruction to carry out, as they hand one to Linux's emulator (`arch/x86/kvm/emulate.c`), whose results are the model: an access to a frame no slot holds, MMIO, which tells the backend the guest-physical address but not the operand (`kvm_emul_mmio`); and a #UD the processor raised (`kvm_emul_ud`). Both fetch the instruction at CS:RIP through the guest's own tables and give it to `kvm_decode` (`decode.h`), which reads it as the processor does, in the mode of the code segment: the last segment override and the last of 0xF2 and 0xF3 count, REX counts only right before the opcode, REX.W outweighs 0x66, and the 16-bit ModRM forms take SS for an address through BP. It gives the operation (`KVM_OP_*`) and its form, the operands, each a register, memory, an immediate or a segment, SSE or MMX register, their size, the address as segment, base, index, scale and displacement, and the prefixes.

The set is the one Linux's emulator carries out that can touch memory, with the register forms: MOV with MOVZX, MOVSX, MOVSXD, MOVNTI and MOVBE; the arithmetic and logic group, TEST, XCHG, INC, DEC, NOT and NEG; the shifts and rotates, SHLD and SHRD; MUL, IMUL, DIV and IDIV; BT, BTS, BTR and BTC, whose register bit number reaches past the operand, BSF and BSR; CMPXCHG, CMPXCHG8B and XADD; CMOVcc, SETcc and BSWAP; LEA and the NOPs, prefetches and CLFLUSH; MOVS, STOS, LODS, CMPS and SCAS with REP, REPE and REPNE; PUSH, POP, PUSHA, POPA, PUSHF, POPF, ENTER and LEAVE; the near and far jumps, calls and returns, Jcc, LOOP and JrCXZ; the moves and the stack forms of the segment registers, LES to LGS, SLDT, STR, LLDT and LTR, SGDT, SIDT, LGDT and LIDT, SMSW and LMSW; the flag instructions, CBW, CWD, SAHF, LAHF, DAS, AAM, AAD and SALC, XLAT; the SSE and MMX moves Linux's has, MOVUPS, MOVAPS, MOVNTPS and their double forms, MOVDQA, MOVDQU, MOVNTDQ and MOVQ; FNSTCW, FNSTSW, FNINIT, and FXSAVE and FXRSTOR outside 64-bit mode; and SYSCALL, SYSENTER, SYSEXIT, RDPID, VMCALL and VMMCALL. A segment register is loaded with the checks of Linux's `__load_segment_descriptor`, the descriptor's accessed or busy bit written back.

One pass (`emul_run`) reads the operands, runs the operation on the record's registers and queues the writes; nothing reaches guest memory until the pass has ended well. A read of device memory ends the pass with the registers, the x87 and SSE state among them, put back as they were: the read goes to the caller as KVM_EXIT_MMIO, in pieces of 8 bytes at most, its answer is kept, and the instruction runs again from its start with the answers so far, up to 40 of them. When a pass ends, its writes are translated, those to RAM are made, and each to device memory leaves in pieces of 8 bytes at most, split where a page ends, each piece one KVM_EXIT_MMIO write, the next at the next KVM_RUN with no guest entered between, so a 16-byte SSE store makes two exits. A fault leaves the guest's exception queued and nothing else changed. A REP string instruction runs one element a pass and leaves RIP on itself while elements remain, so an interrupt is taken between them; RFLAGS.RF is set while it repeats and cleared when any instruction the emulator ran ends, as Linux's `x86_emulate_insn` leaves it, so a breakpoint on the next instruction is taken. One that finds rCX 0 moves nothing and ends; for an Intel guest at address size 4 the processor clears all of RCX and, for MOVS, the upper halves of RSI and RDI, for STOS that of RDI, as Linux's `string_registers_quirk`, and INS and OUTS, from the exit or at the caller's next KVM_RUN, clear RCX alone. Either side of an instruction may be RAM, as a MOVS between a device and memory has it.

After a #UD only the instructions Linux marks EmulateOnUD run: SYSCALL, SYSENTER and SYSEXIT, which a processor of the other vendor refuses in modes the guest's CPUID vendor has them in, so a 32-bit SYSCALL under an AMD CPUID runs on Intel; MOVBE and RDPID, for a guest whose CPUID has them on a processor without, which KVM_GET_EMULATED_CPUID lists as Linux does; and VMCALL or VMMCALL, whichever the processor lacks, which is rewritten in the guest's memory as the processor's own and left to run again, as Linux's `emulator_fix_hypercall` rewrites it, and with KVM_X86_QUIRK_FIX_HYPERCALL_INSN turned off is the guest's #UD instead. Any other instruction's #UD goes back to the guest.

An instruction the decoder does not know, one that would pass 15 bytes, and one the emulator cannot finish get what Linux's `handle_emulation_failure` gives: #UD is queued for the guest, and at CPL 0 KVM_RUN also ends with KVM_EXIT_INTERNAL_ERROR, which `kvm_decode_fail` lays out as Linux's `prepare_emulation_failure_exit` does: suberror KVM_INTERNAL_ERROR_EMULATION, the flag that the instruction's bytes follow, the bytes padded with 0x90, then the exit's five numbers as Linux's `get_exit_info` gives them: VMX's reason, qualification, IDT-vectoring information, interruption information and its error code, or SVM's code, EXITINFO1, EXITINFO2, EXITINTINFO and its error code, an error code being 0 where the event has none. Under KVM_CAP_EXIT_ON_EMULATION_FAILURE the caller gets that error at every privilege level and the guest takes no #UD. An instruction a #UD exit brought that is not one of EmulateOnUD's is no failure: its #UD goes back and nothing is reported.

### MSRs

`msr.c` keeps the MSRs of the vCPU's record, and each access carries Linux's `host_initiated`: KVM_GET_MSRS and KVM_SET_MSRS are the caller's, and RDMSR and WRMSR the guest's. MCG_CTL without MCG_CAP's MCG_CTL_P reads 0 for the caller and takes its 0, and is #GP to the guest, as Linux's `get_msr_mce` and `set_msr_mce`. A bank's STATUS takes any value from the caller. MSR_K7_HWCR is on the index list for every vendor, as Linux keeps it: a write may set McStatusWrEn (bit 18) and TscFreqSel (bit 24), bits 3, 6 and 8 are dropped, and any other bit is refused. MSR_IA32_POWER_CTL is kept as written. An MSR of a bank past MCG_CAP's count, below KVM's 32, is refused as a bad value; one past KVM's 32 banks is an MSR KVM does not have. MCG_CAP is set only by KVM_X86_SETUP_MCE: a write of it, the caller's or the guest's, is an MSR KVM does not have, as Linux's `kvm_set_msr_common` has no case for it and no list advertises it. P5_MC_ADDR and P5_MC_TYPE read 0 and take no write. No MCG_CAP offers MCG_CMCI_P or MCG_LMCE_P, which Linux 6.12's VMX offers and its SVM does not, so a bank's CTL2 (0x280 to 0x29F) holds 0 alone: the caller reads 0 and writes 0, the guest's access is refused as a bad value, and one past MCG_CAP's count is refused for both.

KVM_X86_SET_MSR_FILTER replaces the VM's filter whole: up to 16 ranges, each a bitmap of `nmsrs` MSRs from `base`, at most 0x600 bytes, copied in whole 8-byte words as Linux's `kvm_add_msr_filter` copies it, whose set bit allows the accesses its flags name. The first range that holds the MSR and names the access decides it; an MSR no range decides is allowed unless the filter's flags are KVM_MSR_FILTER_DEFAULT_DENY. The x2APIC's MSRs, 0x800 to 0x8FF, are never filtered. An unknown flag, a range of MSRs with no flag, a range's bitmap past the bound, and DEFAULT_DENY with no range are `EINVAL`. The filter judges the guest's RDMSR and WRMSR, never the caller's. A new filter asks each vCPU, with KVM_REQ_MSR_FILTER, to rewrite its MSR bitmap or permission map before its next entry, since the SDM forbids changing a bitmap under a running vCPU; an exit of FS_BASE, GS_BASE or KERNEL_GS_BASE that the new filter allows came under the old bitmap, and enters the guest again with the instruction not stepped.

KVM_CAP_X86_USER_SPACE_MSR names the reasons a guest's RDMSR or WRMSR goes to the caller instead of #GP: KVM_MSR_EXIT_REASON_INVAL for a value or an access the MSR refuses, UNKNOWN for an MSR KVM does not have, and FILTER for an access the filter refuses. KVM_RUN then ends with KVM_EXIT_X86_RDMSR or KVM_EXIT_X86_WRMSR, the index, the reason and a write's EDX:EAX in `msr`. At the next KVM_RUN a nonzero `error` is the guest's #GP at the instruction; otherwise a read's `data` goes to EDX:EAX and the instruction is stepped, as Linux's `complete_emulated_msr_access`, and the caller's single step over it is reported at that KVM_RUN with no guest entered, as it is over a Hyper-V hypercall the caller answered.

### Performance counters

`pmu.c` offers Intel's architectural PMU up to version 2, with up to eight general counters and three fixed counters, bounded by the host's CPUID; AMD has four legacy counters, six through PerfCtrExtCore, and PerfMonV2's global registers when CPUID 0x80000022 offers them. Intel's CPUID 0xA reports the host's widths and architectural events. AMD's legacy MSRs have no presence bit, so a recoverable read and write probe checks that a counter retains a value before offering the PMU; TCG's ignored writes leave it disabled. A vCPU's CPUID bounds its registers, its counter widths and RDPMC indices. The register permissions and reserved bits follow Linux's `arch/x86/kvm/pmu.c`, `svm/pmu.c` and `vmx/pmu_intel.c`: Intel rejects reserved control bits, AMD ignores them, and Intel's legacy guest counter writes sign-extend 32 bits while its full-width aliases reject bits past the counter width. RDPMC requires CPL 0 or CR4.PCE.

The host's own user of the counters is the hard-lockup detector, which holds general counter 0 while it runs ([the lockup detectors](../watchdog.Readme.md)). At each entry a counter whose selector is enabled, where Intel's global control enables it too, is the host's, since KVM clears its own selectors at every exit; each running guest counter takes, in order, a physical counter the host does not hold, and one left without a counter does not count, as Linux's pinned perf event that cannot be scheduled. No physical counter is written: the count of each one a guest counter runs on is read before the entry and again once the exit has stopped it, and the difference is added to the vCPU's counter. VMX switches IA32_PERF_GLOBAL_CTRL as Linux's `add_atomic_switch_msr` does, by the VMCS's load controls where the processor has them and by the MSR lists otherwise; the selectors and FIXED_CTRL are set before the entry, while the host's global control, which keeps only the host's counters, holds them stopped, and the guest's global control is the host's with the guest's counters added, so that the detector counts in the guest as Linux's does. On a version 1 PMU the lists set and clear the selectors themselves. SVM sets the selectors with GuestOnly, starts them last before VMRUN and stops them first after it, at PerfMonV2's global control when there is one. The physical interrupt bits remain clear. A delta that wraps a virtual counter sets its global status bit and, if its interrupt is enabled, queues one PMI through the virtual APIC's LVTPC at the next entry preparation. The host's tick or VMX preemption timer ends a guest run, so an overflow reaches the guest after an exit; it does not interrupt the host directly. Emulated retired instructions also count, under Linux's selector and privilege rules.

KVM_SET_PMU_EVENT_FILTER replaces a VM's filter under the big lock: up to 300 event words, ALLOW or DENY, a separate fixed-counter bitmap, and optionally Linux's masked unit matches and exclusion entries. A matched exclusion wins over an inclusion before ALLOW or DENY is applied. Each vCPU is kicked when the filter changes. Entry preparation reads the filter under the big lock and keeps only the resulting run masks, so a backend outside that lock never touches a freed filter. KVM_CAP_PMU_EVENT_FILTER and KVM_CAP_PMU_EVENT_MASKED_EVENTS advertise both forms. KVM_ENABLE_CAP of KVM_CAP_PMU_CAPABILITY accepts KVM_PMU_CAP_DISABLE before the VM has any vCPU.

PERF_CAPABILITIES offers full-width writes on Intel and the host's LBR format where the host has a legacy LBR stack of format 4 or later, as Linux's `vmx_get_perf_capabilities` requires the call stack: 16 records on Haswell and Broadwell, and 32 with their INFO registers from Skylake on, each register tried at the probe so that no MSR list fails an entry. The caller can set it before the first KVM_RUN, with a PMU, and with no LBR format but 0 or the host's on the host's CPUID model; the guest can only read it, with CPUID.PDCM. A VMX guest with the format, PDCM and the host's model has the stack, as Linux's `intel_pmu_refresh` gives it: IA32_DEBUGCTL keeps LBR and FREEZE_LBRS_ON_PMI, and drops BTF and, without the stack, LBR, while any other bit is #GP. While DEBUGCTL.LBR is set the entry's MSR list loads LBR_SELECT, LBR_TOS and each record's FROM, TO and INFO, the exit's stores them, and the VMCS loads DEBUGCTL; the guest's RDMSR and WRMSR of them exit, a read answered from what the exit stored and a write tried on the processor's register first, so that it faults where the guest's own would under Linux, whose guest writes the registers themselves. On format 4 without TSX a FROM value is loaded with bits 62:61 copied from 60:59, as Linux's `lbr_from_signext_quirk_wr`. A guest #DB that clears DEBUGCTL.LBR is read back from the VMCS, and a PMI under FREEZE_LBRS_ON_PMI clears it, as Linux's `intel_pmu_legacy_freezing_lbrs_on_pmi`. PEBS is not advertised. `/sys/module/kvm/parameters/enable_pmu` reads `Y` when the host's PMU is usable and `N` otherwise; `force_emulation_prefix` reads `0`, since that debugging interface is absent. Both files are read-only.

### Interrupts

KVM_CREATE_IRQCHIP before the first vCPU makes the two PICs and the 24-pin IOAPIC, and the local APIC of each vCPU. It installs Linux's default routes: GSIs 0 to 15 go to the PICs and the IOAPIC, and 16 to 23 to the IOAPIC alone. KVM_GET_IRQCHIP and KVM_SET_IRQCHIP copy each controller in Linux's layout. A second controller is `EEXIST`; creating the full controller after a vCPU is `EINVAL`.

KVM_ENABLE_CAP of KVM_CAP_SPLIT_IRQCHIP instead keeps only the local APICs in the kernel, with the caller's IOAPIC pin count in `args[0]`, at most 4096. QEMU's default `kernel-irqchip=on` uses the full controller; `split` keeps the PIC, IOAPIC and PIT in QEMU, and `off` keeps the local APIC there too. KVM_INTERRUPT injects the caller's PIC vector with the split controller and is `ENXIO` with the full controller.

`i8259.c` keeps the two cascaded PICs: their initialization, priority, masking, edge and level requests, polling, automatic EOI and rotation, and the ELCR. The master's INT reaches the first local APIC whose LINT0 accepts ExtINT, or a processor with its APIC disabled. Acknowledgement takes the master and, on IRQ2, the slave; an EOI notifies the PIT or resampling irqfds routed to that pin. `ioapic.c` keeps the indirect registers and redirection entries at the saved base address, initially 0xFEC00000. Level delivery holds remote IRR until the local APIC broadcasts an EOI; a still-asserted input is then delivered again. KVM_IRQ_LINE_STATUS on the RTC pin tracks every destination until its EOI, so a second RTC edge coalesces while one waits. Restoring either APIC reconstructs that tracking from the local APIC's pending and in-service vectors.

KVM_CREATE_PIT or KVM_CREATE_PIT2, after the full controller, makes `i8254.c`'s three binary counters at ports 0x40 to 0x43. KVM_PIT_SPEAKER_DUMMY adds a silent speaker port at 0x61. The counters have Linux's 1193181 Hz input clock, count and status latches, byte sequencing, mode-dependent output and gate retriggering. Counter zero pulses GSI 0; periodic timers have Linux's 200 us floor. KVM_GET_PIT2 and KVM_SET_PIT2, and the older PIT ioctls, save and restore the counters; HPET legacy mode stops IRQ0. Reinjection keeps elapsed ticks until an EOI releases each one; KVM_REINJECT_CONTROL can instead coalesce missed ticks. The run loop checks expiry before entry, and a halted vCPU waits for the earliest APIC, PIT or Hyper-V deadline. No timer callback outlives the VM.

`vlapic.c` keeps each APIC's 1 KiB register page and follows Linux's `lapic.c`: the readable registers, the LVT masks, the ID, LDR and DFR rules, SPIV's software disable masking every LVT entry, and the base MSR's moves between disabled, xAPIC and x2APIC modes, which the guest's CPUID allows. Version 0x14 has six LVT entries and directed EOI when the guest's CPUID offers x2APIC. The page is reached through the backend's MMIO exits at the APIC base; in x2APIC mode, or with the APIC disabled, a read of it gives all ones and a write is dropped, as Linux's LAPIC_MMIO_HOLE quirk does. The x2APIC registers are MSRs 0x800 to 0x8FF, whose reserved bits and read-only registers raise #GP. Delivery to a fixed, physical, flat or cluster destination, a broadcast and a shorthand is Linux's `kvm_apic_match_dest`, and lowest priority goes to one vCPU by Linux's vector hashing. The boot vCPU's LINT0 takes ExtINT at reset, as Linux's LINT0_REENABLED quirk sets it, so KVM_INTERRUPT's vector, the caller's PIC's, reaches the guest before it programs its APIC; a second KVM_INTERRUPT while one waits is `EEXIST`. KVM_GET_LAPIC and KVM_SET_LAPIC give the page in Linux's layout, the x2APIC id in bits 31:24 as without KVM_CAP_X2APIC_API, and the timer's TMCCT, from which KVM_SET_LAPIC restarts the count.

The timer counts at Linux's APIC bus of 1 GHz in host nanoseconds, by TDCR's divisor, one-shot, periodic with Linux's floor of 200 us, or against IA32_TSC_DEADLINE in the guest's TSC when the guest's CPUID offers that mode. `kvm_lapic_prepare` checks it before each entry, and the host's 1 ms tick makes the guest exit, so an expiry reaches the guest within a tick. Before each entry it also takes an INIT, which resets the vCPU's registers and the APIC and leaves an application processor waiting for SIPI, a SIPI, which starts that processor at the vector's page in real mode, and the NMIs other vCPUs sent, of which two wait at most, or one while an NMI is under delivery or blocked, as Linux's `process_nmi` limits them. The SIPI's vector is kept in the APIC, as Linux keeps it, where the INIT's reset of the vCPU does not clear it: firmware sends the two one after the other, and a vCPU that finds both waiting takes the INIT and then starts at the SIPI's vector. A HLT stays in the kernel, and does not halt the vCPU while an exception is pending, such as the single-step trap of the HLT itself: the halted vCPU, or one waiting for SIPI, sleeps until an interrupt it can take, its timer's tick or a signal, which ends KVM_RUN with KVM_EXIT_INTR and leaves the vCPU halted. KVM_GET_MP_STATE and KVM_SET_MP_STATE give and take that state; SIPI_RECEIVED is taken as a wait for SIPI with one pending.

`irq.c` holds the routes KVM_SET_GSI_ROUTING replaces whole: one MSI or Hyper-V SINT route per GSI below 4096, or, with the full controller, one pin per controller per GSI. KVM_IRQ_LINE and KVM_IRQ_LINE_STATUS apply a source level to every route of a GSI; KVM_SIGNAL_MSI sends one directly and returns the count of APICs that took it, or `EPERM`, Linux's -1, when none did. An MSI's assert bit is ignored, as Linux's `kvm_set_msi_irq` ignores it. KVM_CAP_X2APIC_API answers its two flags. With KVM_X2APIC_API_USE_32BIT_IDS an MSI's destination takes bits 31:8 from the high address, and an MSI whose high address has any of bits 7:0 set is `EINVAL` in KVM_SIGNAL_MSI and in a route, as Linux's `kvm_msi_route_invalid`; KVM_GET_LAPIC gives an x2APIC's id unshifted, and KVM_SET_LAPIC refuses with `EINVAL` an x2APIC id other than the vCPU's. Without the flag KVM_GET_LAPIC gives the id in bits 31:24 and KVM_SET_LAPIC ignores the caller's, as Linux's `kvm_apic_state_fixup`. A destination of 0xFF from an MSI or an IOAPIC reaches an APIC in x2APIC mode as a broadcast, Linux's quirk, until KVM_X2APIC_API_DISABLE_BROADCAST_QUIRK makes it the x2APIC id 0xFF. With the split controller, the EOI of a vector an APIC took level-triggered through a route of a GSI below the pin count asks its vCPU to exit with KVM_EXIT_IOAPIC_EOI and the vector, so the caller's IOAPIC can lower its line or raise it again.

An irqfd (KVM_IRQFD) is a hook on an eventfd ([io](../io/Readme.md)) that raises its GSI at each rise of the counter and takes the counter, as Linux's irqfd does. The hook runs under the eventfd's spinlock, possibly in another process's write, so it delivers from a copy of the route that KVM_SET_GSI_ROUTING refreshes under `irq.c`'s spinlock. PIC and IOAPIC routes are deferred to the run loop under the big lock, with a kick to every vCPU. A counter above 0 at KVM_IRQFD raises the GSI at once. An eventfd may back one irqfd of a VM (`EBUSY`); KVM_IRQFD_FLAG_RESAMPLE is `EINVAL` with the split controller, as Linux's `kvm_arch_irqfd_allowed` makes it, and QEMU then resamples in its own IOAPIC. With the full controller, a resampling irqfd holds its source high until the controller acknowledges it, then lowers that source and signals its resamplefd. The caller, PIT and resampling irqfds have separate source bits, ORed at each pin, so lowering one leaves the others asserted. The irqfd ends at its deassign, the VM's end, or the eventfd's last close, whose hangup frees it; when the VM's end finds the hook already taken off by a close whose hangup has yet to run, it leaves the record to that hangup.

`iobus.c` keeps the ioeventfds (KVM_IOEVENTFD) on Linux's buses, ports, MMIO addresses and s390's notifications, with no limit of their own, as Linux leaves them out of `NR_IOBUS_DEVS`: a guest's write at an ioeventfd's address, of its length, or of any length for length 0, and of its value when it has a data match, signals its eventfd and enters the guest again without an exit. A string OUT signals once for each element. Two ioeventfds that some write would both match are `EEXIST`, as in Linux's `ioeventfd_check_collision`. A write that the bus does not take goes to the caller, and a read is never the bus's.

The same buses hold the zones of coalesced writes (KVM_REGISTER_COALESCED_MMIO), at most 1000 on each bus; an ioeventfd is refused with `ENOSPC` on a bus that holds 1000, as Linux's `kvm_io_bus_register_dev` refuses any device there. A write of at most 8 bytes that lies whole inside a zone is queued on the VM's ring, a page of Linux's `struct kvm_coalesced_mmio_ring` with 170 entries, each the address or the port, the length, the bytes and `pio`, and the guest runs on with no exit; the caller empties the ring from its mapping at its next exit and moves `first`. An ioeventfd at the address is tried first. A write that finds the ring full, 169 entries waiting, goes to the caller as its own exit, as Linux's `coalesced_mmio_write` refuses it. KVM_UNREGISTER_COALESCED_MMIO removes every zone of the bus that holds its range and succeeds whether or not it found one, as on Linux.

### VFIO assignment

KVM_CAP_DEVICE_CTRL offers KVM_CREATE_DEVICE for KVM_DEV_TYPE_VFIO, one bridge per VM. Its descriptor holds a VM reference. KVM_SET_DEVICE_ATTR of KVM_DEV_VFIO_FILE adds or removes a VFIO group or device descriptor by its open file description, so aliases cannot add the same file twice. The bridge holds each file until removal or its own last close. KVM_HAS_DEVICE_ATTR reports the ADD and DEL attributes; GET is `EPERM`, since neither has a readable value. Every device ioctl requires the VM's address space, as its VM and vCPU ioctls do.

VFIO does not enforce DMA cache coherence, so the first file added calls `kvm_tdp_noncoherent` to drop the existing translations and make new ones observe the guest PAT; the last removal or close releases the files before restoring the ordinary policy. Attribute changes are serialized across a file release that sleeps. The bridge follows Linux's `virt/kvm/vfio.c`.

### Hyper-V

`hyperv.c` follows Linux's `hyperv.c` and the TLFS. A vCPU has Hyper-V once KVM_SET_CPUID2 gives leaf 0x40000001 the interface "Hv#1"; the leaves the caller takes from KVM_GET_SUPPORTED_HV_CPUID are Linux's ten, with no enlightened VMCS and no direct timers or cluster IPI without the APIC in the kernel. The VM's share, the partition-wide MSRs, and each vCPU's, made at its first need, hang off the records' `hv`. A guest's RDMSR or WRMSR of Hyper-V's range reaches `kvm_hv_msr` through `emulate.c`, #GP without Hyper-V in its CPUID or, under KVM_CAP_HYPERV_ENFORCE_CPUID, for an MSR or hypercall the CPUID does not offer; the caller's KVM_GET_MSRS and KVM_SET_MSRS reach it through `msr.c`, held to neither, and the index list names Linux's 28 MSRs of it. A write of the hypercall MSR, once the guest has written its id, puts VMCALL, or VMMCALL on SVM, and RET in the page it names.

The reference counter counts 100 ns of the kvmclock. The reference TSC page is written before the next entry (`kvm_hv_prepare`) once the guest or the caller changes its MSR or KVM_SET_CLOCK or a TSC write moves the clock, as Linux's `kvm_hv_setup_tsc_page`: its sequence 0 first, then the scale, 2^64 * 10^7 / the TSC's rate, and the offset that make its formula the kvmclock's, then the next sequence; a page in no slot or a rate the scale cannot hold leaves sequence 0, so the guest reads the MSR. While the guest emulates the TSC the page is left as it stands. A write of the crash control with its notice bit and of the reset MSR end the next KVM_RUN in KVM_EXIT_SYSTEM_EVENT, of type CRASH or RESET.

SynIC needs a local APIC in the kernel. KVM_CAP_HYPERV_SYNIC turns it on and clears each page the guest enables; KVM_CAP_HYPERV_SYNIC2 leaves them as they are. A guest's write of SCONTROL, SIEFP or SIMP ends the run in KVM_EXIT_HYPERV_SYNIC for the caller's model of the pages. A SINT is raised in the APIC as a self IPI of its vector, by a KVM_IRQ_ROUTING_HV_SINT route, its GSI's irqfd among them, or by a timer; one with auto-EOI is taken without going in service (`kvm_lapic_ack`), and an EOI of a SINT's vector or a write of EOM retries the message a timer could not leave in a full slot. The four synthetic timers of each vCPU count in the reference counter's time, one-shot at an absolute count or periodic, and auto-enabled by their count; armed in the host's ns, they are looked at in `kvm_lapic_prepare` before each entry, and the halted vCPU's wait ends at the next one's time. An expiry leaves a timer message in its SINT's slot of the message page, or in direct mode raises its vector.

A VMCALL from a guest at CPL 0 in protected mode with hypercalls enabled is decoded from RCX, RDX and R8, or EDX:EAX, EBX:ECX and EDI:ESI outside 64-bit mode, with the fast input in XMM0 to XMM5 from the vCPU's FPU image; its status goes back in RAX, or EDX:EAX. HvNotifyLongSpinWait is answered at once, HvSignalEvent signals the eventfd KVM_HYPERV_EVENTFD gave its connection, and HvCallSendSyntheticClusterIpi and its Ex form raise the vector in each vCPU of their set, simple or sparse. HvFlushVirtualAddressSpace and HvFlushVirtualAddressList, and their Ex forms, flush each named vCPU's TLB whole with KVM_REQ_TLB_FLUSH, which the TLFS allows for any list, and wait until none of them is in the guest with the request still set, so no vCPU runs on with old translations when the call returns. HvPostMessage and HvSignalEvent of a connection the kernel does not know, with SynIC on, the extended hypercalls from 0x8001 and the debugger's go to the caller in KVM_EXIT_HYPERV_HCALL, whose result KVM_RUN puts in RAX before the next entry. The synthetic debugger's MSRs are kept, and a write of its control or pending page goes to the caller in KVM_EXIT_HYPERV_SYNDBG.

### KVM's paravirtual interface

`cpuid.c` appends KVM's two leaves to KVM_GET_SUPPORTED_CPUID, and KVM_SET_CPUID2 finds them again at any base from 0x40000000 to 0x4000FF00 in steps of 0x100, as Linux's `kvm_find_kvm_cpuid_features` does, so that a CPUID with Hyper-V's leaves at 0x40000000 may put KVM's at 0x40000100, as QEMU does. The features of that leaf are the vCPU's `pv_features`. Under KVM_CAP_ENFORCE_PV_FEATURE_CPUID a guest's access to an MSR or hypercall of a feature not named there is #GP or KVM_ENOSYS, as Linux's `guest_pv_has`, and the MSR's goes to a caller that asked for KVM_MSR_EXIT_REASON_UNKNOWN, as Linux's KVM_MSR_RET_UNSUPPORTED; the caller's KVM_GET_MSRS reads such an MSR as 0, and its KVM_SET_MSRS takes a write of 0. KVM_SET_CPUID2 drops PV_UNHALT from the leaf while the VM has KVM_X86_DISABLE_EXITS_HLT, as Linux's `kvm_update_cpuid_runtime`, since a guest that halts without an exit cannot be kicked out of it.

The kvmclock follows Linux's `x86.c`. A guest's write of MSR_KVM_SYSTEM_TIME_NEW, or the old MSR_KVM_SYSTEM_TIME, names a `struct pvclock_vcpu_time_info` of 32 bytes in its memory, which `kvm_clock_prepare` writes before an entry once a request asks for it: the version odd, then the guest's TSC and the kvmclock at one moment, the TSC's rate as a 32-bit multiplier and a shift, the flags, then the version even. A copy that crosses a page or lies in no slot is not written. The kvmclock is the host's monotonic clock plus the VM's offset, which KVM_SET_CLOCK sets, so it starts at 0 with the VM.

The master clock is Linux's `pvclock_update_vm_gtod_copy`: one moment, the host's TSC and clock read together from the vDSO's clock (`vclock_read_cycles`), which every vCPU's copy carries, with PVCLOCK_TSC_STABLE_BIT, so the guest's reads agree on every vCPU and never go back. It holds while the vDSO's clock runs from the TSC, every vCPU's TSC is of one generation, and the boot vCPU's guest uses the new MSR. A vCPU made, or a TSC the caller writes within a second of the one the last write would read now, joins the last write's generation with its offset, as Linux's `kvm_synchronize_tsc`; another TSC starts a new generation, which stops the master clock until every vCPU has joined it. The vCPU attribute KVM_VCPU_TSC_OFFSET reads the vCPU's offset and sets it, as Linux's `kvm_arch_tsc_set_attr`: an offset that is the last one set, at the last one's rate, joins its generation, and another starts one. Each change takes a new moment, after every vCPU has left the guest with a request to write its copy again. Without the master clock a copy is written again when the vCPU moves to another processor and every 300 seconds, as Linux's KVMCLOCK_SYNC_PERIOD. The wall clock's MSRs write a `struct pvclock_wall_clock` once, the real time at which the kvmclock read 0. KVM_KVMCLOCK_CTRL sets PVCLOCK_GUEST_STOPPED in the next copy, which the guest's watchdog clears.

The steal time is Linux's `record_steal_time`. A guest's write of MSR_KVM_STEAL_TIME names a `struct kvm_steal_time` of 64 bytes at a 64-byte boundary in its memory; a write with any of bits 1 to 5 is refused. Before an entry, `kvm_steal_prepare` writes it when KVM_RUN begins, as Linux's `vcpu_load` asks, when the thread was switched out and in since the last entry, and while the record says the vCPU was preempted: `preempted` cleared, the version odd, `steal` grown by the nanoseconds the thread waited ready on a run queue since the last write, the scheduler's `run_delay` that `/proc/PID/schedstat` gives, then the version even. Across KVM_RUN the vCPU's thread has a `sched_out`, Linux's preempt notifier, which `schedule` calls in the same hold of its lock as the selection whenever it switches the thread away while it can still run, at a slice's end above all, and never when selection keeps it, so that a real-time vCPU thread that outranks every queued thread is never marked. There `kvm_steal_preempted` sets KVM_VCPU_PREEMPTED in the record, through `kvm_tdp_write_nofault`, which writes only a page that is present and writable, as Linux's `copy_to_user_nofault` does, and also refuses one not yet stored into, since it writes through the direct map, which does not set the page's dirty bit, while Linux writes through the user address, where the processor sets it; and only where the guest last stopped for a host interrupt, which the backends mark at an external-interrupt exit and the run loop clears at its top, after its switch and before the work of the next entry, as Linux's `at_instruction_boundary`; it counts each such preemption in the statistic `preemption_reported` and the others in `preemption_other`. A guest's INIT turns the steal time and the kvmclock off, as Linux's `kvm_vcpu_reset`.

### The TSC's rate

A vCPU's TSC runs at the rate KVM_SET_TSC_KHZ gave it, or its VM, and by default at the host's, as Linux's `kvm_set_tsc_khz` (`pvclock.c`). A rate within 250 parts in a million of the host's is the host's, and nothing is scaled. Another rate is a ratio, the rate over the host's as a fixed-point number of the backend's width, which the backend loads before the next entry; the guest's TSC is then the host's times the ratio plus the offset, and every reader of it scales the same way: the TSC MSR and its writes, the matching of the vCPUs' TSCs, the kvmclock's copy and its multiplier, the clock pairing, the APIC's TSC deadline, and Hyper-V's reference TSC page and TSC frequency MSR. A ratio of 0, or one at or past the backend's largest, is refused, and so is a rate at or past what the largest ratio makes of the host's rate, 2^31 - 1 kHz at most, Linux's `max_guest_tsc_khz`.

On a processor that scales nothing, a rate below the host's is `EINVAL`, and a rate above it is kept as Linux's `tsc_catchup` keeps it: before each entry the TSC's offset moves up to where the rate would have the TSC, counted from the value and the moment its generation began at or the guest last wrote it, and between entries it counts at the host's rate. The VM's KVM_SET_TSC_KHZ only records the rate, with the same bound, also once the VM has vCPUs, as Linux's, and each later vCPU tries it when it is made. KVM_GET_TSC_KHZ gives the rate last tried, also one below the host's that a processor without scaling refused, since Linux records the rate before it refuses it.

KVM_HC_KICK_CPU wakes the vCPU it names even when its guest halted with interrupts off, as Linux's `pv_unhalted`, which a Linux guest's paravirtual spinlocks wait on. KVM_HC_CLOCK_PAIRING writes the real time and the guest's TSC read together, while the vDSO's clock runs from the TSC.

KVM_CAP_X86_DISABLE_EXITS takes HLT and PAUSE, at any time as in Linux 6.12. PAUSE never exits here. With HLT a vCPU made afterwards runs HLT in the guest, its intercept off on VMX and SVM, and an event injected into a halted guest sets its activity state back to active, as Linux's `vmx_clear_hlt`.

### Lock order

1. The big kernel lock, which every ioctl runs under.
2. The vCPU, held for one ioctl (`vcpu_lock`), as Linux's `vcpu->mutex`. A second ioctl of the same vCPU sleeps until the first ends, letting go of the big lock meanwhile; a signal does not end that wait, since the holder is a KVM_RUN its own signal ends or a state ioctl that does not sleep long.
3. Spinlocks of other subsystems, such as an eventfd's, which an irqfd's hook runs under ([io](../io/Readme.md)).
4. `irq.c`'s spinlock, over the routes and the irqfds' copies of them, which the hook takes under the eventfd's.

The run loop lets go of the big lock only inside `enter`, with interrupts off and the vCPU held, so nothing else runs the vCPU meanwhile, and another thread changes it only by `kvm_vcpu_kick`'s requests. Nothing sleeps with interrupts off, and no code takes the big lock while it holds a spinlock. The slots, the tables and the dirty logs change only under the big lock, and the change notice calls a slot under it, so `kvm_tdp_flush` may wait for vCPUs to leave the guest without sleeping.

### State

The registers, special registers, XSAVE image, XCR0, events, debug registers, MP state, CPUID, MSRs, TSC offset and machine-check banks live in the vCPU's record; `dirty` names the parts a set changed, for the backend to load before the next entry, and `save` brings the backend's copy back before a get. The checks follow Linux's: KVM_SET_SREGS refuses EFER, CR0 and CR4 that the processor would and bits the guest's CPUID does not offer, and before the first KVM_SET_CPUID2 takes every CR4 bit the host supports, as Linux does until a CPUID narrows them; KVM_SET_XSAVE refuses components past the guest's XCR0, a reserved MXCSR bit and a compacted image; KVM_SET_CPUID2 after the first KVM_RUN accepts only the same table; KVM_SET_MSRS stops at the first MSR it refuses and returns the count before it. A new vCPU has the power-on state Linux gives one, at the reset vector with the boot processor's APIC base.

AMD's SYSCFG and NB_CFG MSRs read as zero, and NB_CFG writes are ignored, as Linux's `kvm_get_msr_common` and `kvm_set_msr_common` specify for the guest's northbridge probes. SYSCFG writes remain refused.

## Limits

* At most 1024 vCPUs in a VM, with ids below 4096, Linux's bounds; 256 memory slots, in address space 0 alone, with no guest_memfd, so KVM_SET_USER_MEMORY_REGION2 takes what KVM_SET_USER_MEMORY_REGION does; 256 entries for KVM_SET_CPUID2 and KVM_GET_CPUID2, and fewer than 256 MSRs at once for KVM_GET_MSRS and KVM_SET_MSRS, as Linux's bounds.
* Only the machine type 0. There is no dirty ring, and no SMM, SMI or nested virtualization. guest_memfd, SMM and asynchronous page faults are left for a later milestone, with the rest of nested virtualization, by the owner's decision; their rows in the corpus are excluded.
* The emulator runs an instruction that touched device memory or raised #UD, never a run of instructions, which Linux's does for a processor without the unrestricted guest; the backends need that feature or nested paging. So the instructions of Linux's emulator with no memory operand that exit by themselves are not in `decode.h`'s set: port I/O, HLT, CPUID, the control and debug registers, the MSRs, RDTSC, RDPMC, INVLPG, INVD, WBINVD and CLTS, which the backends answer, and INT n, INT3, INTO and real mode's IRET, which the processor delivers. FXSAVE and FXRSTOR on device memory in 64-bit mode, SYSENTER from 64-bit mode, and an instruction that reads device memory more than 40 times are emulation failures here, the first two as in Linux.
* KVM_GET_SUPPORTED_CPUID offers no PKU and no AMX, no architectural LBR and no PEBS with the performance counters, and of KVM's paravirtual features no asynchronous page faults, paravirtual EOI, TLB flush or MSI extended destination; a guest runs without them. Without the TSC's rate, as under `rach2.notsc` or with a TSC whose rate varies, the kvmclock is not offered, and a guest falls back to its HPET or ACPI timer.
* The guest-physical tables have 4 KiB and 2 MiB leaves, no 1 GiB ones, and a 2 MiB leaf only where the caller's 512 pages are consecutive frames, since user memory is mapped in 4 KiB pages here; Linux's KVM maps the caller's transparent huge pages as 2 MiB leaves.
* SVM boots the Debian x86-64 guest under TCG on WSL and the Mac and under nested KVM on WSL. There, at the third level, a four-processor Debian booted from its disk through SeaBIOS ends in systemd's emergency mode once its wait for a partition's device has run out, and one such run's QEMU ended by itself; neither is traced, and the testbed's [forward direction](../../../bed/forward/Readme.md#the-accelerator) points to the record. VMX boots it and Rach2's i686 disk on the Lenovo. The i686 guest's intermittent double fault under SVM is being investigated in 81-2b; the corpus still awaits qualification ([results](../../../tests/kvm/Readme.md#results)).
* Hyper-V's enlightened VMCS and direct TLB flush, which serve a nested guest, are not offered, and neither is the paravirtual EOI of the VP assist page, so the guest always writes its EOI. The reenlightenment MSRs are kept but never notify, since the host's TSC rate does not change under a VM.
* Of KVM_GET_STATS_FD's statistics only those [Interfaces](#interfaces) names count. The rest read 0, the halt polling's times and histograms among them.
* The TSC's rate is not scaled on a processor without the feature, where a rate below the host's is refused, as on Linux.
* No EPT misconfiguration is expected, since the tables use one memory type for each leaf, and one would be an internal error. A task switch whose TSS or tables are in no slot is the internal error Linux reports too, and a TSS's T bit raises no #DB in the new task, as in Linux.
* Each entry turns VMX on and loads the VMCS anew, where Linux keeps VMX on and the VMCS current on a processor between runs.
