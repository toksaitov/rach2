# `driver/ath9k/`: the driver of Atheros' AR9003 Wi-Fi chips

This directory is the driver of Atheros' AR9003 family of Wi-Fi chips, whose one chip now is the Lenovo G50-70's, a Qualcomm Atheros QCA9565 (AR9565, PCI 168c:0036): a 2.4 GHz, one-chain 802.11b/g/n chip. The family's chips have no firmware, so the driver tunes, calibrates and runs the radio itself. It serves one card as a radio of [`../../wireless/radio.h`](../../wireless/radio.h), under the station of [`../../wireless/Readme.md`](../../wireless/Readme.md), and follows Linux's ath9k part by part. A chip is a row of the table in `chips.c`; the rest of the driver is the family's. The registers, the descriptors' fields and the EEPROM's layout are in `reg.h` and `ath9k.h`; the driver's state, the table's types and the calls between its parts are in `ath9k.h`; all register access goes through `io.h`'s indirection, with its shared helpers in `io.c`, so that each part's host test runs the part against the chip model of [`tests/host/ath9k_model.h`](../../../../../tests/host/ath9k_model.h). The kernel builds every part. At boot, `rach2/kernel.c` calls `ath9k_probe` after the station layer's `wlan_init`, so that a card present is a radio from the start, and the timer tick calls `ath9k_poll`.

## Sources and license

The driver follows ath9k of Linux v6.16, commit `038d61fd642278bab63ee8ef722c50d10ab01e8f`, `drivers/net/wireless/ath/ath9k/`, which is the only public description of the chips; Atheros and Qualcomm published no datasheet. Where ath9k is terse about the descriptor rings it also reads FreeBSD's AR9300 HAL and OpenBSD's `ar9003.c`.

ath9k is under the ISC license, which allows its code to be taken if its copyright and permission notice are kept. `reg.h` is ath9k's register headers (`ath/reg.h`, `reg.h`, `ar9003_mac.h`, `mac.h`, `ar9003_phy.h` and `reg_mci.h`) resolved for the chips of the table, the EEPROM's layout in `ath9k.h` is `ar9003_eeprom.h`'s, `ar9003_templates.h` is the five board templates of `ar9003_eeprom.c`, and `ar9565_1p0_initvals.h`, `ar9565_1p1_initvals.h` and `ar9003_initvals.h` are ath9k's initial-value tables of the AR9565 and the two tables of other chips it shares; all keep ath9k's notice, and `reg.h`'s opening comment lists what was changed, none of it a value. A part that takes ath9k code says which functions in its comments and keeps the notice. The rest of the driver is Rach2's, under the GNU General Public License, version 2 only (`COPYING`).

## Design

The driver has ath9k's split, and each part owns a member of `struct ath9k`, named after it, and the calls declared under its file's name in `ath9k.h`. Every call runs with the kernel lock held, either on the wireless worker, which may sleep, or in the card's interrupt handler or the tick that stands in for it, which may not; `ath9k.h` says which for each.

### The table of chips (`chips.c`)

A chip is a row, a `struct ath9k_chip`: its name and PCI device ID, the MAC version its `AR_SREV` reads and the revisions the row knows, what it has as `ATH9K_CHIP_*` bits (the bands, LNA combining, Bluetooth coexistence by MCI, two groupings ath9k tests by chip, the parts made for PC makers and those of the AR9462's design, and the steps ath9k takes for one chip by name: its spur masks, its IQ bound, and three settings of its PLL and `AR_WA` in `hw.c`), its chain mask and GPIO lines, ath9k's per-card flags by subsystem ID, and one ops struct for the two steps a chip does its own way: choosing its initial-value tables by its revision and its gain tables by the EEPROM's gain modes, as ath9k's `ar9003_hw.c` does for it. `ath9k_chip_find`, `ath9k_chip_rev` and `ath9k_chip_flags` look a card up. The AR9565 is the one row: revisions 1.0, 1.0.1 and 1.1, of which 1.1 has its own tables; 2.4 GHz, one chain, LNA combining, MCI, and 14 GPIO lines; and the 39 cards of ath9k's table, the G50-70's 17aa:3026 and 17aa:4026 among them with `ATH9K_PCI_2ANT | ATH9K_PCI_BT_ANT_DIV`, any other card with `ATH9K_PCI_BT_ANT_DIV`.

Adding a chip, the AR9462, AR9485 or AR9580, is a row, its initvals and its IDs, and wherever ath9k tests for the chip in code the family shares, a test of the row's caps. `reg.h`'s registers are resolved for the AR9565; where the new chip's differ, as ath9k's `AR_SREV` test at the macro shows, the macro becomes a test of the row again.

### Register access

`io.h` gives every part ath9k's `REG_READ`, `REG_WRITE`, `REG_RMW`, `REG_SET_BIT`, `REG_CLR_BIT`, `REG_RMW_FIELD` and `REG_READ_FIELD`, and `SM` and `MS`, over a `struct ath9k_io` of read, write and wait functions that the card's state points at. A field is a mask `NAME` and its shift `NAME_S`, as in ath9k. `io.c` has the three helpers every part shares: `ath9k_rmw`, `ath9k_wait`, which polls a register every 10 microseconds up to a bound and returns `-ETIMEDOUT` without logging, and `ath9k_write_ini`, which writes a column of one of ath9k's initial-value tables. In the kernel the functions are `pci.c`'s `ath9k_mmio_io`, MMIO on the card's window and `k_udelay`; in a host test they are the model's.

### The bus (`pci.c`)

`ath9k_probe` finds the first card of vendor 168c and a device ID of the table, maps the whole 512 KiB of its first memory BAR, as ath9k does, since the AR9565 has global registers past 128 KiB, turns on memory decoding and bus mastering, sets the latency timer and, if the firmware left none, the cache line size, as ath9k's `ath_pci_probe` does, and takes the card's flags from the row by its subsystem IDs. ath9k's write of the retry timeout at 0x41 is left out: on a PCIe card that byte is the header of the power management capability. The parts give the card 32-bit DMA addresses. The probe attaches the chip, then power, the antennas, receive, transmit and the interrupt, logs the boot line, puts the chip in full sleep and registers the radio with `ath9k_radio_ops`:

```
ath9k: probe: 02:00.0 AR9565 rev 1.0.1 mac b8:ee:65:32:89:15 subsys 17aa:4026
```

that is the card's bus address, the row's name, the revision `AR_SREV` reads, the EEPROM's MAC address and the subsystem IDs, here the Lenovo's. A step that fails is logged as `ath9k: probe: failed:` with the bus address, the step and its errno. Once the radio is registered, the radio switch is read, and again each second on the worker whether the radio runs or not, as `power.c` asks; a registration refused gives the interrupt back and leaves the chip in full sleep. A probe that fails after the chip attached resets it cold into full sleep and then gives back the transmit and receive pages it took, with `ath9k_tx_free` and `ath9k_rx_free`, so that it keeps no DMA memory. `ath9k_poll` is the timer tick's entry: it calls `intr.c`'s `ath9k_tick` for the registered card.

`start` wakes the chip and sets the link's power save, as `ath9k_start` does, resets the chip onto its last channel, or channel 1, writes the keys back, sets the filter, starts receive, arms the baseband watchdog and enables the interrupts, then the calibration's work and the hang checks; a chip that does not wake fails the start with `-EIO`, a start that fails leaves the chip in full sleep, and a start while `power.c` holds the card suspended is refused with `-EBUSY`. `stop` turns the interrupts off first, so that nothing queues a reset after it, then ends the work and DMA, fails the frames accepted for transmit, drops the received frames not yet read, counting each in `rx_dropped`, ends a scan's hold on BlockAckReqs, since the scan ends with the radio, and leaves the chip in full sleep; `tx_idle` that ends a hold reaches a stopped radio too. `config` to a new channel, as a scan's return to the operating channel and the station's following of its AP's channel switch ask, stops DMA and resets onto it as `start` does, with the channel's calibration, the keys and the received frames not yet read kept, the same channel sets the filter alone, and a stopped radio keeps both for its start. A reset of `config` that fails leaves the chip off and counts as a failed reset of the hang checks, with the reason `channel`: the next check tries it again, and so does the next `config`, the same channel among them, and a `start` of the started radio. `sta_state` sets the BSSID from the first authentication to the return to NONE, marks the station associated from association to the return below it, which narrows the filter to the BSS's beacons and starts the receive window of the hang checks, and gives `ampdu.c` the AP on association and none on the return. The station layer gives no association ID, so the chip's stays 0; `stop` clears the association, since the station layer stops the radio without a return to NONE. While the radio is stopped the chip sleeps: `sta_state` keeps the BSSID for the start, `set_key` refuses an install with `-ENETDOWN`, and a clear forgets the key, which the start's restore leaves out. Scanning is the station layer's.

### Power and reset (`hw.c`)

`ath9k_hw_attach` follows `__ath9k_hw_init`: the revision from `AR_SREV`, which must be of the AR9003 form and a version and revision of the row; a power-on reset; the row's initial-value tables for the revision; the wake; ath9k's test of `AR_STA_ID0` with 260 patterns; the EEPROM; the gain tables for its gain modes; and the radio's capabilities: channels 1 to 14, each at the highest target power the EEPROM gives it, as ath9k's test pass finds it with no conformance limit (18 dBm on the Lenovo's card, as Linux reports); the twelve legacy rates; HT20 MCS 0 to 7 with the short guard interval; and TKIP and CCMP in hardware. A refusal names the register and what it read, as `ath9k: attach: refused: AR_SREV (0x4020) reads 0xffffffff` for a card off the bus. The first reset after attach is a power-on reset whatever is asked, and later ones are warm or cold through `AR_RTC_RC`, as in ath9k; a reset that does not end names the register it waited on. The power states are ath9k's: awake, network sleep and full sleep, in which the RTC is off.

`ath9k_hw_reset` follows ath9k's function of that name with coexistence off and no fast channel change. It reads the noise floor of the channel the chip leaves unless the chip slept, calls `ath9k_cal_set_channel`, keeps the TSF, the default antenna and the LED's mode, calls `ath9k_phy_mark_inactive` and resets the chip, cold when it slept or its DMA ran, and sets the PLL. Then it calls the parts in ath9k's order: `ath9k_phy_process_ini`, `ath9k_phy_set_rfmode`, `ath9k_phy_set_delta_slope`, `ath9k_phy_spur_mitigate`, `ath9k_eeprom_set_board_values`, `ath9k_ant_set`, `ath9k_mac_set_address` and `ath9k_mac_set_bssid`, `ath9k_phy_set_channel`, `ath9k_mac_init_queues`, which sets DMA, the queues and the interrupt masks, `ath9k_ani_reset`, QoS, the PCU's timings for a 44 MHz clock and the short slot, which `radio.h` gives no way to change, `ath9k_phy_init_bb`, `ath9k_cal_init`, `ath9k_nf_load` and `ath9k_nf_start`, and writes the LED's mode back. Interrupts and receive stay off for the caller. `ath9k_hw_disable` wakes the chip, resets it cold and leaves it in full sleep.

### The EEPROM (`eeprom.c`)

The card keeps its calibration in an EEPROM or in OTP as compressed blocks laid over one of ath9k's board templates. `ath9k_eeprom_init` looks for a block's header at the end of 1 KiB, 4 KiB and 512 bytes of the EEPROM, then of 1 KiB and 512 bytes of the OTP, reading as ath9k does, the OTP one word per byte. From there the blocks run downward to an empty header; each whose checksum holds is laid over the template it names, or over the image so far. A store without a header, or an image without a good block, fails the attach with `ath9k: eeprom: failed:` and the reason. The image, in `struct ar9300_eeprom`, whose 1088-byte layout the build checks, gives the MAC address, a random locally administered one when it holds none, and the regulatory domain; the country is 00, the world, since the driver keeps no table of ath9k's codes. The Lenovo's card holds its image in OTP, one block over template 4 (h116), with regulatory domain 0x6a.

`ath9k_eeprom_target_power` gives each rate's target power at a frequency, interpolated between the piers, and `ath9k_eeprom_ctl_limit` the lowest conformance limit of a mode there, as ath9k takes them for the world domains. `ath9k_eeprom_set_board_values` writes the board's settings at each reset: the PA bias, the drive strengths, the attenuation, the regulator, the tuning capacitors, the minimum CCA power, the PA's turn-off delay and the thermometer.

### The radio (`phy.c`)

`ath9k_phy_process_ini` writes, at each reset, the row's initial-value tables for 2.4 GHz HT20 in ath9k's order: each stage's SOC, MAC, baseband and radio tables, then the receive and transmit gain tables and, on channel 14, Japan's CCK filter. It then holds receive off until the MAC enables it, gives a chip of the AR9462's design the regulator mode it shares with Bluetooth, sets HT20 and the chains, notes which transmit calibrations the tables enable, and sets the transmit power. `ath9k_phy_set_channel` programs the synthesizer for channels 1 to 14 in fractional mode from the 40 MHz reference, and refuses any other frequency with `-EINVAL`. `ath9k_phy_spur_mitigate` puts the OFDM spur filter and mask A on the first spur of the EEPROM's list within 10 MHz of the channel, and mask B on the next if it is near too, as ath9k does for the AR9565 (`ATH9K_CHIP_SPUR_AB`). `ath9k_phy_set_rfmode`, `ath9k_phy_set_delta_slope`, `ath9k_phy_init_bb`, `ath9k_phy_mark_inactive` and the radio bus calls are ath9k's.

`ath9k_phy_set_txpower` sets each rate's power in half dB: the EEPROM's target, at most the smaller of the caller's limit and the channel's maximum less the antenna's gain and 3 or 5 dB for a second or third chain, and at most the CCK, OFDM and HT20 conformance limits. It writes the open-loop correction and the temperature of the EEPROM's calibration piers interpolated at the channel and the EEPROM's temperature slope, from which the chip compensates the power for temperature itself, and keeps the piers' noise floor for `calib.c`. The limit stays in `phy.power_limit` for the next reset and channel change.

`ath9k_phy_channel_change` retunes a running chip without a reset when no queue holds a frame: it takes the radio bus, retunes, sets the power, delta slope and spurs, gives the bus back, and loads and restarts the noise floor. A change that fails leaves the channel and its calibration data as they were, and the caller resets the chip.

A reset of `hw.c` calls these parts in ath9k's order, which its section gives, and `pci.c` calls `ath9k_cal_start` once the chip runs.

### Calibration (`calib.c`)

`ath9k_cal_init` runs the reset-time calibrations as ath9k's `ar9003_hw_init_cal_pcoem` does on a chip without RTT and with coexistence off: the AGC's offset calibration, with the carrier leak calibration around it and, when ath9k would hold calibration data, the TX IQ calibration in it; ath9k holds none at the start or off channel, which here is while the chip has no BSSID; every reset calibrates both again, as `ath9k_hw_reset` clears their results before `ath9k_hw_init_cal`, then the manual peak calibration of each chain, and the RX IQ calibration started over. An offset calibration that does not finish in 100 ms is logged and returns `-EIO`. `ath9k_cal_set_channel` forgets the kept results and the noise floor's history when the channel is new. The AR9565 1.1's RTT is not used: the G50-70's chip is a 1.0.1, and without RTT the 1.1 calibrates as the 1.0 does.

The noise floor is measured per chain into a history of five readings: a new history takes its readings whole and then their median, and a reading outside the chip's bounds is corrected. `ath9k_nf_read` takes a finished measurement and reports the floor and the noise level in `cal.noise_floor` and `cal.noise`. `ath9k_nf_load` loads each median back, waits up to 22.2 ms for the chip to take it, and releases the registers to -50 dBm; `ath9k_nf_bstuck` takes stuck beacons for interference and stops capping the floor at its maximum until a reading is normal again.

`ath9k_cal_start` starts the periodic work on the wireless worker, as ath9k's `ath_ani_calibrate` does: ANI every second; the RX IQ calibration every second until it is done, and again 20 minutes after; and the long calibration, which reads, loads and restarts the noise floor, every 30 seconds, or every second while interference keeps the floor uncapped. A load the chip does not take asks `recover.c` for a reset (`ATH9K_RESET_CAL`, as ath9k's 7b8aaead958e does) and ends the work until the reset starts it again: a reset that recovers a started radio calls `ath9k_cal_start`, as ath9k's `ath_complete_reset` restarts its work. While the chip's interrupts are off, in a reset or a retune that has not finished or one that failed, the work touches no register and looks again a second later, as `ath_ani_calibrate` skips a chip that is not awake. On a chip with `ATH9K_CHIP_IQ_BOUND`, the AR9565, an RX IQ coefficient at either bound leaves the correction as it was, since applying it drops the best receive rate to MCS 4 (ath9k's 6f37ff96d3bd). While a scan has the radio away from its channel, with `WLAN_FILTER_OTHER_BSS` set, the work skips its turn, since a noise floor read there belongs to another channel. The thermometer's latest reading is kept in `cal.temp`; the compensation is the chip's, from what `phy.c` writes. The work takes turns between two records, since a record cannot be queued while its callback runs.

### Noise immunity (`ani.c`)

`ath9k_ani_reset`, called in each reset after the tables, reads the tables' settings of the fields it moves and sets the levels: OFDM 3 and CCK 2 the first time, which are the tables' own settings, and after that the higher of those and the levels held. `ath9k_ani_monitor`, which the periodic work calls each second, reads the MIB's cycle and phy error counters, and once the receiver has listened for 300 ms raises OFDM's level for more than 3500 OFDM errors a second (1000 below the default level), raises CCK's for more than 600 CCK errors, or lowers one of the two in turn when both rates are low. A level sets the spur immunity's cycle power threshold, the FIR step and the detection of weak OFDM signals, which stays on while the beacons' RSSI in `ani.beacon_rssi` is 40 or less. `recv.c` keeps that RSSI, over the noise floor, from the associated AP's beacons, as described under Receive; it reads 0 until the first of them, and again from each change of association, so that a station without an AP keeps weak signals detected. `rach2.ath9k_noani` on the boot line sets `ATH9K_OPT_NO_ANI`, which leaves the levels at their defaults, since a rate stuck at 1 Mbit/s has been reported with ANI on.

### Queues and DMA (`mac.c`)

The register-level work of the queues and the PCU, ath9k's `mac.c`, `ar9003_mac.c` and the parts of `hw.c` that set them. `ath9k_mac_init_queues`, which `ath9k_hw_reset` calls at its end, sets 128-byte DMA bursts, the receive buffer's length, the status ring and the four data queues: access category VO is QCU and DCU 3 and BK is 0, as in ath9k, each with mac80211's default contention parameters. It sets the interrupt masks without receive mitigation, and the baseband watchdog in the second mask. The other calls push to the FIFOs, stop a queue, abort transmit DMA, start and stop receive, and set the receive filter, the station's address, the BSSID and association ID, and the interrupts' enable, including the chip's own message engine on a card with MSI. The filter is ath9k's `ath_calcrxfilter` for a station: only its own BSS's beacons once associated, every beacon and every multicast frame while the station looks at other networks (`WLAN_FILTER_OTHER_BSS`), and everything in promiscuous mode. It is kept in `rx.hw_filter` and written only while receive runs, so a filter set while receive is stopped is not lost to the chip (f0b2c30a2029). The station's address also sets the CCMP AAD mask of management frames, so the key cache protects unicast management frames.

`ath9k_mac_get_tsf` reads the 64-bit TSF, and `ath9k_mac_set_beacon_timers` starts the station's TBTT, DTIM and TIM timers as `ath9k_cmn_beacon_config_sta` does, with a beacon-miss interrupt after ten missed beacons. A reset sets them again. DMA memory is a kernel page each from `ath9k_dma_alloc`; the host test gives its own.

### Receive (`recv.c`)

The AR9003's EDMA receive: a high-priority FIFO of 16 buffers and a low-priority one of 128, each buffer a page of which 3904 bytes are used, with the chip's 48-byte status at its head. `ath9k_rx_start`, after a reset, pushes every buffer with its status cleared, but those `ath9k_rx_stop` kept. `ath9k_rx_service` reads at most a FIFO's depth of buffers whose status says done, hands each good frame to `wlan_radio_rx` and gives the buffer back. While `wlan_radio_rx_full` says the station has no room, it leaves the rest in the FIFO, the chip having acknowledged them, and the tick reads on. ath9k gives every buffer back at a reset, losing those frames, a key message among them. Here `ath9k_rx_stop` of a retune or a recovery keeps the completed buffers at the head of their ring, each stamped with its channel's frequency and noise level, and the start pushes the others from the last kept one round the ring, so that the chip fills them in the ring's order; the FIFO is marked held, and the tick reads the kept frames first, once, as frames of the channel they came on, since a retune runs on the worker that empties the station's queue and cannot wait for room there. The stop of the radio and the reset of a fatal error, a bus fault that may have left a buffer unwritten, drop them instead, each counted in `rx_dropped`. A frame with a bad CRC or a phy error is dropped and counted; a phy restart after a good delimiter is not an error. A frame that spans two buffers is dropped whole. The FCS and the chip's padding after the header are removed. A frame the key cache decrypted is marked `WLAN_RX_DECRYPTED` with its entry, its IV and MIC left for the station and TKIP's ICV removed; one it failed to decrypt goes up still protected, and the Michael MIC is always checked by the station. Each frame's status carries the 64-bit TSF, the signal, which is the RSSI over the noise level `cal.noise` as ath9k's `ath9k_process_rssi` reads it, and not over the raw floor `cal.noise_floor`, which a quiet channel's first measurement puts some 22 dB lower, and the rate as an index of the band's table or an MCS, and goes to `antenna.c` for diversity. While the station is associated, a beacon received whole whose BSSID is the station's, with an RSSI the chip could read, updates noise immunity's `ani.beacon_rssi` as ath9k's `ath9k_process_rssi` does: an average in 128ths in which each beacon weighs a tenth, the first taken whole, a reading below the noise floor counted as 0.

### Transmit (`xmit.c`)

One queue per access category, a ring of 64 frames each copied into a 2 KiB buffer, and descriptors with four rate and retry series, their durations, RTS where a series asks for it, the key cache entry and the transmit power. The frame is padded after its header as the chip expects, and a frame for the key cache leaves the space of its ICV or MIC to the chip. Each push to the chip's FIFO is a chain of linked descriptors, one frame or an aggregate's, which `ampdu.c` chooses, and at most 8 chains are in the FIFO; the frames pushed are always the first in the ring. A first series that names no rate the chip sends, which rate control leaves for a peer it cannot read, goes at 1 Mbit/s twice, since a descriptor with no tries is one ath9k never writes. A status ring of 64 entries, which holds every status the four FIFOs can have unread, one per chain, is read by `ath9k_tx_service`, which reports each frame with `wlan_radio_tx_status`: acknowledged or not, the tries of each series, which rate control reads, and an ACK's signal, its RSSI over the noise level as for a received frame. A chain went out, for a frame alone and an aggregate alike, when its status has no error, no underrun and no descriptor the chip refused, and, unless the frame asked for no ACK or went to a group address, `AR_FrmXmitOK`, which says an ACK or a BlockAck came. A chain of a BlockAck session is `ampdu.c`'s to finish; the frames it keeps for another push go after the chains still in the FIFO and before the frames not pushed yet. `ath9k_tx` refuses a frame with `-ENOBUFS` while its queue is full or a reset runs. `ath9k_tx_drain` reports every queued frame failed.

### Keys (`key.c`)

The 128-entry key cache, written as ath's `key.c` writes it. A group key takes the entry of its index, 0 to 3; a pairwise key takes the first free entry from 4 whose TKIP MIC entry, 64 above, is free too, other than the entry a key left last, since the station tells a frame opened under a replaced key by its entry. A TKIP key is written with its first bits inverted until its MIC entry is in place. CCMP and TKIP are in hardware, and unicast management frames with CCMP; WEP, the integrity keys of 802.11w and pairwise keys of another index stay in software. The driver keeps a copy of each entry, and `ath9k_key_restore` writes the cache back after every reset. An entry is cleared only once no frame accepted for transmit names it, so that the frames in the FIFO go out with the key they were accepted with; data not pushed yet is dropped and reported filtered, as mac80211 drops a tainted key's frames, and voice frames stay, since message 4 may be among them. A removal that finds such frames takes the entry out of the chip's address search and leaves its key in place, as `ath9k_set_key` does at `DISABLE_KEY`, and the entry is not free for another key; `ath9k_key_reap` clears it on the completion that ends the last frame. A chip that stops completing is caught by the hang check, whose reset drains the frames, and a reset or a stop leaves no dying entry. A group key that takes the entry of a key in place cannot wait: the status ring is served for up to 20 ms, and the chip is reset if frames that name the entry remain, its drain reporting them failed. A group key installed at the index of the key it replaces takes the entry in place, and since the station removes the old key after it installs the new one, a removal clears an entry only while it holds the key removed.

### Interrupts (`intr.c`)

A message-signalled interrupt where the machine can route one, as the RTL8168's driver takes it, on a vector aligned to the count of messages the card's MSI capability offers, four on the AR9565: the chip writes its message number into the low bits of the data whatever Multiple Message Enable says, and every cause uses message 0, so an unaligned vector arrives rounded down, on another device's vector, unless an interrupt remapper routes the message by its handle. Rach2 routes no INTx line of a PCI card, so a card without messages is served from the timer tick alone, which `pci.c` calls, with the line masked by the command word's Interrupt Disable bit, so that a level interrupt nobody clears cannot hold its pin; the bit is set on each polled return of the attach, when messages are given up, and on the detach. With messages on, the tick still looks at the rings, as the fallback for a message that did not come, and counts in `tick_work` what it found. `ath9k_intr` takes a message as `ath_isr` does: it reads the causes first, as `ath9k_hw_intrpend` and `ar9003_hw_get_isr` do, with the chip's interrupts on, since the asynchronous cause shows the MAC's only while they are, and with the read-and-clear `AR_ISR_RAC`; then it kills the chip's interrupts, serves receive, high priority first, and transmit, counts beacon misses and receive overruns, and enables the interrupts again, which writes the message engine's enable as ath9k's tasklet does after every interrupt; all of it in the handler, which runs with the processor's interrupts off. `mac.irq_on` is clear from every `ath9k_mac_intr_disable` to the `ath9k_mac_intr_enable` that ends a start, a reset or a retune; while it is, the handler, the tick and the periodic calibration touch no register, so a message sent before a disable cannot run into the reset that follows it. While a reset is asked for, a message kills the chip's interrupts and does nothing more. A message that finds no cause is counted in `spurious`. After 1000 of those in a row, or 1000 messages between two ticks, the card's messages are disabled at the bus and the tick serves the causes, as Linux's spurious-interrupt check gives up on a line. `rach2.ath9k_nomsi` on the boot line leaves the card polled from the start. Linux drove the Lenovo's card by its INTx line, so its trace has no message path to compare with. A fatal PCIe error, a card that reads all ones, or a baseband watchdog state that needs it asks for a reset, which leaves the interrupts off until the worker has run it.

### Recovery (`recover.c`)

ath9k's hang checks for the AR9003, run on the worker every second: a MAC hung with a DCU waiting for its frame, read from the AR9003's DMA debug registers (3e56c80931c7); a queue that held a pushed frame across two checks with no status; and, while associated as `sta_state` reports it, a receive path that ran less than once a second over a window of four seconds, the first beginning at the association (b5f871ab4913). ath9k judges the receive path unassociated too, which resets a card on a quiet channel every few seconds; a station that hears its AP's beacons ten times a second needs no such guess. `radio_config` starts the receive window again, so that a scan's time away is not read as a stalled receive path. The baseband watchdog raises an interrupt after 25 ms in one state outside idle; its known harmless states are cleared, and the rest reset the chip. An aggregate that went out and drew no BlockAck resets the chip too, as `ath_tx_complete_aggr` has a station do. The check takes turns between two records, as the calibration's work does, since the worker refuses a record whose callback runs.

`ath9k_reset` turns the interrupts off, stops receive and transmit, keeps the received frames not yet read, as described under Receive, but after a fatal error, reports every queued frame failed, and then runs `ath9k_hw_reset` on the channel, which resets cold while DMA runs, as `ath_reset_internal` leaves it to. It restores the station's address, the BSSID, the keys, the filter, the receive buffers, the beacon timers, the watchdog and the interrupts, and counts the reason in `struct ath9k_stats` and logs it. A reset that fails, the hang checks' or `config`'s, leaves the chip off, and the next check tries again, three times in all.

### Antennas (`antenna.c`)

`ath9k_ant_init` chooses diversity from `ATH9K_CHIP_ANT_COMB`, the EEPROM and the per-card flags. The chip row may override the antenna-control byte: the AR9565 uses `0xc9`, as ath9k does even when its EEPROM byte is zero. A known one- or two-antenna card without `ATH9K_PCI_BT_ANT_DIV` has combining disabled. The Lenovo's two-antenna row enables it. The row also holds the bias for each pair of LNA configurations and the threshold for switching the main LNA, 3 RSSI units for the AR9565.

`ath9k_ant_set` restores the board's switch tables at reset in the order of ath9k's `ar9003_hw_ant_ctrl_apply` with Bluetooth antenna diversity off, ath9k's default: the common table, the SPDT table with WLAN's SPDT enable on a chip with MCI, the second common table and the chains'; then, on a chip with LNA combining, the antenna-control byte's LNA control with LNA diversity, its receive protection and the forced WLAN LNA diversity cleared, the byte's fast CCK diversity, and LNA1 main with LNA2 alternate. The MCI and Bluetooth coexistence registers are left alone, as ath9k leaves them while coexistence is off. A reset starts a fresh antenna scan on LNA1 and LNA2.

`ath9k_ant_rx` collects 512 valid main/alternate RSSI samples per window, ignoring failed frames, absent signals and status from an earlier configuration. It waits for an aggregate's end before changing a pair, with the sample count and sums bounded while it waits. It compares LNA1 and LNA2, their sum and their difference in three windows, chooses the main physical LNA and the best alternate, and scans again after 16 windows. This is a packet-based scan using ath9k's configurations and bias table; it does not implement ath9k's short scans.

### Power and the radio switch (`power.c`)

`ath9k_power_init` leaves the PCIe link as firmware set it. `rach2.ath9k_noaspm`, or `ATH9K_OPT_NO_ASPM`, clears only L0s and L1 in the upstream port's and the card's link control words, the first thing to try if the card drops off the bus. It skips absent, truncated and unreadable capabilities. The saved `power.aspm` is the card's firmware setting; the diagnostic option stays in force after resume. While ASPM is on and the option is off, `ath9k_hw_pcie_powersave` sets the link's power save where ath9k's `ar9003_hw_configpcipowersave` runs: at the end of the attach and at each start, with the chip awake, it allows the core into L1 with `AR_PCIE_PM_CTRL` and writes `AR_WA` again, and at those and at each stop it writes the PCIe PHY's table, which on the AR9565 keeps CLKREQ off in L1. The Lenovo's firmware leaves L0s and L1 on, and Linux writes the same three words there.

When the EEPROM enables RF silence, `ath9k_power_init` makes its GPIO an input. `ath9k_rfkill_poll` wakes a sleeping chip for the read and puts it back, as ath9k's `ath9k_rfkill_poll_state` does around its read, restores the GPIO input direction if a reset changed it, compares the GPIO to the EEPROM's polarity and reports a hard block through `wlan_radio_rfkill`. A GPIO outside the chip's range or an unreadable card is blocked. The first sample is reported after radio registration; a later identical sample emits nothing. The bus part must poll after registration and periodically while the radio is stopped as well as while it runs, so a hard block can clear.

`suspend_to_idle` calls `ath9k_suspend_all` before freezing processes and `ath9k_resume_all` after thawing them, on a wake or a signal. The one fixed card is borrowed by these hooks at attach. `ath9k_suspend` stops the station through `wlan_stop`, clearing association and pending transmission, and remembers whether the station ran or awaited rfkill. Its start operation must refuse a start while `power.suspended` is set. Resume samples the switch again and restarts only a wanted, unblocked station; a block defers restart to the station's rfkill worker. A start error is logged and leaves the restart request set for a later `ath9k_resume` call. Neither step preserves an association.

### Transmit aggregation (`ampdu.c`)

The station sends the ADDBA request and takes the response, as mac80211 does, and `ath9k_ampdu_action` starts or stops the TID's session as `ath_tx_aggr_start` and `ath_tx_aggr_stop` do; the receive actions are `-EOPNOTSUPP`, so the station reorders in software. `rach2.ath9k_noampdu` on the boot line sets `ATH9K_OPT_NO_AMPDU`, under which a start is refused and every frame goes alone. `ath9k_ampdu_sta`, which `sta_state` calls on association, takes the AP's longest A-MPDU and least MPDU spacing from its HT Capabilities; until then, or for an AP without them, the most cautious, 8191 bytes and 16 microseconds. An association to an AP with them also turns on `ATH9K_RX_FILTER_COMP_BAR`, as `ath_calcrxfilter` does for an HT channel, so that the AP's compressed BlockAckReqs reach the station's reordering; each reset writes the filter again.

A session keeps ath9k's BlockAck window: its start, the oldest frame not done, and a bit for each frame in it. A QoS data frame of the session's TID to the AP enters the window on its first push, if it fits; one that does not, waits, and so does the queue behind it. A frame whose number comes before the window goes alone outside it, as frames queued before the session do. A number skipped since the last one taken is done at once, and owes the AP a BlockAckReq past it. As ath_tx_form_aggr does, an aggregate takes the frames that follow the first while they are of its session and start at an MCS, at most half the window, and at most the bytes the slowest of its series sends in the queue's TXOP (4 ms for best effort and background, 3 for video, 1.5 for voice), the chip's 65535 and the AP's limit. The series from the first legacy rate on are cut, since an aggregate cannot fall back to one, and an aggregate whose first series is legacy is a single frame. Each subframe but the last is followed by the delimiters `ath_compute_num_delims` gives an AR9003: enough to make it 256 bytes, and enough to keep the AP's spacing at the first series' rate. While the FIFO holds two chains of sessions (ath9k's `ATH_AGGR_MIN_QDEPTH`), the next frame of one waits, so that the frames behind it gather into an aggregate.

`ath9k_tx_idle` answers the `tx_idle` radio call: whether every queue is empty. While a scan holds it, no BlockAck request goes out, since one would leave on another channel; the hold's end sends what is owed, or on a stopped radio leaves it owed, and the radio's stop ends the hold.

`ath9k_ampdu_status` reads a chain as ath_tx_complete_aggr does. An aggregate that went out carries the BlockAck's bitmap from its starting number; one with no bitmap asks for a reset (`ATH9K_RESET_TX_ERROR`), as ath9k does for a station, unless its session has ended: a renewal's DELBA can reach the AP before an aggregate already in the FIFO, and the AP then answers with a plain ACK. A frame its bit acknowledges is done; a frame of the session that was not stays in the ring, its Retry bit set, until it has had 30 attempts (`ATH_MAX_SW_RETRIES`), and then is given up. The attempts of each push count before the choice, and a push makes no more attempts than the frame of it with the fewest left may have, so that no frame has more than 30; ath9k checks the count before it adds the push's, so that a frame there can pass 30. The first frame finished reports the chain's tries and its MPDUs, sent and acknowledged, to rate control, as `ath_tx_rc_status` does, and the others report none. A chain none of whose frames finishes reports them with `WLAN_TX_NO_FRAME`, where ath9k reports nothing, so that rate control counts an aggregate that failed whole. A frame given up or a number skipped owes the AP a BlockAckReq once the window's start has passed it, so that its reordering buffer releases the frames behind the hole; `ath9k_ampdu_bar` chooses and queues it with interrupts off, compressed, at the band's lowest rate, on the TID's queue behind every frame there, with the window's start, before which nothing remains to be sent. A session has one on its way at a time, and it stays owed until an acknowledged one reaches the number owed. One that failed waits for a frame of the session to be acknowledged, as mac80211's `bar_pending` does. A fourth failure in a row (`ATH9K_BAR_MAX_TRIES`, where mac80211 has no limit) ends the session at once, and the worker's `wlan_radio_ba_stop` sends the DELBA, since the AP's reordering buffer would otherwise wait on a hole nothing can release. A BlockAckReq that a reset drops is no failure. A stop leaves the frames queued to go alone. A reset's drain fails every frame, and the window starts again at the next frame with a BlockAckReq.

## Tests

`ath9k_mac_hosttest.c` runs the DMA, interrupt, key and recovery parts on the chip model, with the station's calls, the worker and the clock recorded. It pushes frames through both receive FIFOs: beacons, a padded QoS frame at an MCS rate, CRC and phy errors, a phy restart, a chain of two buffers, a frame the station refuses, frames held for want of room across a retune there and back, a recovery and a fatal error's reset, each delivered once with the channel it came on or counted, and three FIFOs' worth to wrap the ring. It checks a transmit descriptor's words, the buffer's padding and the status mapped to tries per series, the FIFO's depth of 8, a full ring, bad frames, and statuses that are no ACK: an underrun, a refused descriptor and a missing `AR_FrmXmitOK`. It installs CCMP and TKIP pairwise keys and a TKIP group key, reads each entry and the MIC entry and the order of their writes, decrypts with each and sends with one. It replaces a group key at its index and removes the old one, which leaves the new; and it replaces a pairwise key while nine frames name it, eight finished and one that never finishes, which the removal waits 20 ms for and then resets the chip before it clears the entry. It checks that the associated AP's beacons alone move noise immunity's beacon RSSI, by a tenth each, and that a frame's and an ACK's signal stay the same after a quiet channel's first noise floor result and rise with the noise level of a noisy one. It makes a queue hang, a MAC hang, a receive stall, a fatal PCIe error, a baseband watchdog and a failed reset, and after each reset checks that the queues, the status ring, the filter, the receive buffers, the interrupts, the beacon timers and the keys are back. It also checks the filter kept while receive is stopped, the next beacon past a 32-bit TSF, an allocation that fails, the freeing of every page both allocations took, a card whose messages cannot be enabled, and the INTx line masked on every polled path and on the detach, and left alone with messages on.

`ath9k_ampdu_hosttest.c` runs transmit aggregation on the chip model under the station's real data path, with the test as the AP and rate control at MCS 7, 5 and 3 and then 1 Mbit/s. It checks when the station asks for a session and the request's fields, a wrong token, a refusal, a request unanswered and the wait after each, the AP's DELBA either way, a disconnection, and `rach2.ath9k_noampdu` with the DELBA it brings. It checks an aggregate's descriptors: the links, the aggregate and more-aggregate flags, the length, the delimiters of short frames, the series cut at the legacy rate, the durations and the checksums; a BlockAck with a hole, the hole sent again alone with its Retry bit, and each frame reported once, with one report of tries; the limits of half the window, a window that holds the next frames back behind a hole, the AP's 8191 bytes and video's TXOP; a frame given up after 30 attempts, its last push cut to the six left, and the BlockAckReq past it, whose status nobody is told; a BlockAckReq that fails, stays owed, waits for an acknowledged frame and goes again, a debt that moves past one on its way, one a reset drops, one of an older session, one a scan held across the radio's stop that goes after the restart and a new session, and the DELBA at the fourth failure; Minstrel's counts of one member of two acknowledged and of an aggregate failed whole, by a status with no frame; an aggregate with no BlockAck and the reset it asks for, and a plain ACK after a renewal's DELBA, which asks for none; the filter's bit for an AP with and without HT; a full queue that leaves no hole; and a reset's drain and the BlockAckReq at the next frame, chosen and queued with interrupts off.

`ath9k_ant_hosttest.c` runs the antenna and power parts on the chip model with PCIe and station models, and the real `proc/suspend.c` on a modeled scheduler wait. It checks the full switch fields and preserved bits, WLAN ownership and the coexistence registers left alone, the per-card flags, LNA selection and rescans, stale and invalid receive metadata, a long aggregate, firmware ASPM preserved and disabled at both ends, missing and unreadable capabilities, RF-silence polarity and GPIO bounds, deferred initial rfkill reporting, suspend and repeated calls, a wake during preparation, a signal, a resume error and retry, and blocks raised while asleep.

`ath9k_hw_hosttest.c` runs `pci.c`, `hw.c` and `eeprom.c` on the chip model made the Lenovo's AR9565 as Linux 6.18's ath9k found it under mmiotrace: its `AR_SREV`, the values its reset leaves, and its OTP. The probe's accesses, from the read of `AR_SREV` to the end of the OTP's image, 1,628 of them once the polls of the status registers are left out of both, must be the trace's in order and value, and the probe must log the boot line above with the card's MAC address and give channels 1 to 13 Linux's 18 dBm. `ath9k_trial_hosttest.c` runs every part together on the chip model made the Lenovo's card, twice, with messages and polled, through the path a trial boot on the machine takes: the probe at boot, the radio's start, three seconds of beacons served by message and by the tick, a scan of channels 1 to 13 with a probe request on each, a queue that stops sending until the hang check resets the chip, the join of task 10's trial and its traffic, a compressed BlockAckReq of the AP's that passes the model's filter and releases a frame held behind a hole, and the stop. For the join, the station's data path, rate control and key handling are linked in, the test moving the station's state as `mlme.c` does and installing the keys as nl80211 does, and playing the AP from the transmit FIFOs on a link that carries MCS 0 to 5 and OFDM up to 24 Mbit/s: authentication, association, the 4-way handshake's EAPOL in the clear, the pairwise and group CCMP keys read back from the key cache, then four 1400-byte datagrams a millisecond out and two in for three seconds, each frame out checked for its key entry, cipher, lengths and growing PN and each frame in delivered once. In that traffic the station opens a BlockAck session, aggregates, and Minstrel settles on MCS 5, and noise immunity reads the RSSI of the AP's beacons. A queue that stops under that load is reset with the keys and the BSSID written back and the PNs still growing; a receiver that stops hearing while the AP beacons is reset by the receive check, judged on the association `sta_state` gave; and when the AP goes away for five seconds the connection is dropped, which clears every key entry and the BSSID with no reset, and the station joins again and carries traffic. A message goes out while the chip raises an enabled cause with its interrupts on, and the asynchronous cause shows the MAC's interrupt only then, as on the chip; a cause that asks again at once a thousand times is a storm and fails the test, as do a call that waits more than 250 ms, an access outside the model's window or past the window `pci.c` maps, an access to the MAC, baseband or radio while the chip is not awake, DMA outside the driver's pages, a FIFO overrun, and interrupts on before the message vector has its handler or after the stop. Its worker, like the mac test's, refuses a work whose callback runs, as `work.c` does, so that periodic work that queues itself again stops there as it would on the machine. It passing says the driver follows ath9k's description of the chip, not that the card does.

Each part's host test, `tests/host/ath9k_*_hosttest.c`, runs the part against the chip model of `tests/host/ath9k_model.c`, a chip of a row of the table, which keeps the register file, logs the driver's accesses in order, answers the registers ath9k's sequences poll, and fills receive buffers and the transmit status ring through a DMA hook ([`tests/host/Readme.md`](../../../../../tests/host/Readme.md)). The model is written from ath9k, so a chip that behaves otherwise passes the tests and fails on the Lenovo.
