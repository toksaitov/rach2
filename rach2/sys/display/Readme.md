# Display

`sys/display/` draws the console's text and gives the screen to a program that draws through `/fb`, as a 320x200 picture or at the framebuffer's own size and format. The text console, `vga_text.c` behind `textcon.h`, keeps the screen's cells, a scrollback and the cursor, and writes them to the VGA's text memory or, when the loader left a framebuffer, to `fbcon.c`, which draws them in pixels. `fbdev.c` is the `/fb` device's operations. `vga_gfx.c` puts the VGA in mode 13h for `/fb`, or on a framebuffer console keeps the picture in memory for `fbcon.c` to draw and gives the program the framebuffer itself at the native mode. `vga_font.c` is the console's font. `blank.c` blanks the screen after a time without input, and `backlight.c` is the backlight class of `/sys/class/backlight`, which `intelbl.c` registers the Intel chipset's backlight with. The interpreter of the console's escape sequences is `sys/kio.c`, and what is typed at the console is described in [the terminal Readme](../tty/Readme.md).

## Interfaces

Programs see the screen as [console(4)](../../../man/man4/console.4) describes it: the escape sequences of the `rach2` terminfo entry ([`vendor/gnu/rach2.ti`](../../../vendor/gnu/rach2.ti)), the font and its stand-ins, the answers to queries, and the window size. `/fb` is described in [console(4)](../../../man/man4/console.4), [open(2)](../../../man/man2/open.2), [read(2)](../../../man/man2/read.2), [write(2)](../../../man/man2/write.2), [lseek(2)](../../../man/man2/lseek.2) and [ioctl(2)](../../../man/man2/ioctl.2). A program takes `/fb`'s requests, structures and events from `<rach2/fb.h>`, which is [`rach2/include/rach2/fb.h`](../../include/rach2/fb.h), staged as `/include/rach2/fb.h` beside musl's headers and included by the kernel's `fb.h`. Events `FB_EVENT_LOST` and `FB_EVENT_REGAINED` report a transfer to a descendant and the return.

Inside the kernel:

* `textcon.h`, defined by `vga_text.c`: the grid of `textcon_cols` by `textcon_rows` cells, `textcon_write_cell`, `k_scroll_down`, `k_cls`, the cursor (`textcon_place_cursor`, with `textcon_hold` and `textcon_release` around a write of many bytes), the colors, the scrollback (`k_scrollback_page_up`, `k_scrollback_page_down`, `k_scrollback_home`, `k_scrollback_end`) and the font (`textcon_load_font`).
* `fbcon.h`: `fbcon_init`, which the platform calls with the loader's framebuffer, and `fbcon_window`, which the platform defines in `sys/arch/x86/boot.c`; `fbcon_screen` and `fbcon_native_begin` for the native mode, and `fbcon_gfx_begin`, `fbcon_gfx_palette`, `fbcon_gfx_frame` and `fbcon_gfx_end` for the picture; and `fbcon_detach`, `fbcon_attach`, `fbcon_boot_screen` and `fbcon_scale`, through which the console's DRM client (`sys/drm/drm_client.c`) moves the console between screens.
* `fb.h`, defined by `vga_gfx.c`: the graphics modes of `/fb`, `fb_enter`, `fb_enter_native`, `fb_exit`, `fb_screen`, `fb_set_palette` and `fb_present`, and `gfx_current`, the mode in force; and, defined by `fbdev.c`, `fb_file_ops`, the descriptor's operations, `fb_take`, which attaches an open description in `sys/syscall_fs.c`, `fb_release_if_owner`, which removes an ending process from the stack, `fb_size`, which its seek calls, and `fb_owner_pid`, the owner, which the console's read and the end of a process (`sys/syscall_proc.c`) read; and for the console's DRM client, `fb_hold` and `fb_unhold` around a DRM master's hold of the screen, `fb_is_held`, which the blank reads, and `fb_screen_moved` when the console moves to another screen, with `fb_redraw` in `vga_gfx.c`, which draws the mode in force again there.
* `blank.h`, defined by `blank.c`: `console_poke`, which the key and mouse paths call; `blank_init` and `blank_tick`, which `sys/tty/tty.c` calls at boot and on the tick; `blank_params_start`, which `kernel.c` calls once `/sys` takes entries, for `/sys/module/kernel/parameters/consoleblank`; `console_set_blank_time`, which the escape calls; and `console_blank_now`, `console_unblank_now` and `console_is_blank`, which TIOCLINUX calls (`sys/syscall.c`); `console_unblank_now` also from `fbdev.c`, when a program takes the screen, and from the console's DRM client (`sys/drm/drm_client.c`), when a master takes it, which calls `console_poke` too when the console has the screen back; and `console_panic_unblank`, which `k_panic_begin` calls (`sys/panic.c`). `textcon_blank` in `textcon.h` paints the screen black and back, and `drm_client_blank` in `sys/drm/drm_blank.h` turns the console's DRM display off and on.
* `backlight.h`, defined by `backlight.c`: `backlight_register` for a driver, with its `get` and `set`, and `backlight_set_type` for its `type`; `backlight_rebind`, by which a driver takes a registered backlight over by its name, keeping its `bl_power` and reading its level from the new driver, whose units it is in, or, when the new driver reads none lit, giving it the level the class kept; `backlight_driver_change`, by which a driver that changes the level itself, as i915 at the panel's enable, does so under the class's lock and has the class show it; `backlight_init`, which probes the drivers; `backlight_console_blank`; and the attributes' show and store, which `sys/fs/procfs.c` calls for `/sys/class/backlight`. `intelbl_init` is the PC's driver.
* `intelbl.h`, defined by `intelbl.c`: `struct intelbl`, a PCH's PWM reached through a register read and write of the caller's, and `intelbl_attach`, `intelbl_attach_switch`, `intelbl_get`, `intelbl_set` and `intelbl_power`, which i915 drives the same PWM with once it binds the graphics device; `intelbl_take_back` gives `intel_backlight` back to the boot's mapping when i915's device goes.

The boot log records the console's choices:

* `boot: screen: framebuffer, rgb, 1280x800, 32 bits, pitch 5120, at 0xfd000000`, the mode the loader left, or `boot: screen: text, 80x25 cells, at 0xb8000` when it described the VGA's text mode, or `boot: screen: text; the loader described none`, as QEMU's `-kernel` and GRUB's Multiboot leave it.
* `console: framebuffer: 1366x768, 32 bits a pixel; the font at 2x, 85x24 cells`, or `console: framebuffer: not one this console draws; the screen stays as the loader left it`.
* `vga: font: loaded: code page 437 box drawing with Cyrillic` or `vga: font: kept: the adapter's code page 437`, and on a framebuffer `console: font: drawn: code page 437 box drawing with Cyrillic`.
* `cpu: page attributes: 0x0007040600070106; the framebuffer is mapped write-combining`, or the same line ending in `uncached`, or `cpu: page attributes: none; the framebuffer is mapped uncached`.
* `console: card0: a native display` or `console: card0: the firmware's framebuffer` when the console takes a DRM card; `console: card0: 1280x800, the font at 2x` each time it sets its own mode on a native card's buffer, and `console: card0: screen not set: error N` when it cannot; `console: card0: handed to a master, the keyboard off` when a DRM master takes the screen, and `console: card0: taken back from a master, the keyboard on` when it has the screen again, at the card's last close or a hotplug with no master, followed on the firmware's framebuffer by `console: card0: the firmware's framebuffer again`; and `console: card0: left` when the card goes.
* `console: blank: after 600 seconds without input`, or `console: blank: never` under `consoleblank=0`; then `console: blanked: N, CAUSE` and `console: unblanked: N, CAUSE` at each blank and its end, N the count since boot.
* On a machine with Intel graphics at 0:2.0, `backlight: intel_backlight: lynxpoint-lp PWM, levels 0 to 937, at 937, off by the panel's enable`, with `off by a level of 0` when the firmware did not light the panel by the sequencer, or `backlight: intel: none: REASON`.

## Design

### The screen's terminal

The console terminal's sink and the kernel's console calls (`console_putc` in `sys/klog.c`) write the screen through `k_putc` in `sys/kio.c`. It runs each byte under the console's lock (`console_lock`: interrupts off, and a spinlock taken unless this processor has it already), since a keyboard's echo arrives from an interrupt and other processors write too. It interprets:

* CSI sequences, `ESC [` with up to eight numeric parameters: cursor motion `A` to `G`, `` ` ``, `d`, `H` and `f`; erase `J`, `K` and `X`; color `m`; save and restore `s` and `u`; and the queries `c` and `n`. Of the private sequences, `ESC [ ? 1 h` and `ESC [ ? 1 l` set and reset DECCKM, the cursor keys' mode, alone or among other modes in one sequence. Any other private sequence (`ESC [ ?`, `ESC [ >`), one with an intermediate byte, and any other final byte, `r`, `h` and `l` among them, are read and ignored.
* `ESC 7` and `ESC 8`, which save and restore the cursor; `ESC =` and `ESC >`, DECKPAM and DECKPNM, which set and reset the keypad's application mode; and `ESC c`, which resets the colors and both key modes and clears the screen. `ESC (` and its kin take one byte more. An OSC, DCS, APC, PM or SOS string is read to BEL, ESC or another control byte, or for 256 bytes, and is not drawn.
* UTF-8. A character is drawn as its place in the font's upper half (`vga_font_map` when the font was loaded, code page 437 otherwise); one the font lacks as a stand-in, a Latin letter without its accent, an ASCII likeness or a related box glyph; and one with no stand-in as `?`. It takes the cells `k_wcwidth` gives it, from `sys/wcwidth.c`, the table `term` counts by too: a wide character, CJK and most emoji, is its glyph or stand-in and a blank, and one that would start in the last column begins the next line; a combining mark, a variation selector or a zero-width joiner takes no cell, so the font, which cannot put a mark over a letter, draws the letter alone.
* Control bytes. BS, HT (stops every 8 columns), LF, which also returns the carriage, and CR move the cursor; NUL, BEL, SO and SI draw nothing, the last two since the console has one character set, which G0 and G1 both name; any other is drawn as `?`.

The stand-ins (`vt_near_table`) cover the glyphs coding agents draw their own screens with, from the recordings in [`tests/fixtures/agents/`](../../../tests/fixtures/agents/Readme.md): a bullet, U+25CF and its kin, as the font's bullet; the spinners' stars, U+2722 to U+273D, as `*`, and braille, U+2800 to U+28FF, as `.`; the menus' pointers U+276F and U+203A as `>`; U+23BF, the corner under a tool's call, as a box corner; the blocks and quadrants as the nearest half or full block; check marks as `v` and crosses as `x`; and the arrows and triangles as ASCII's. A stand-in the font lacks is looked up again, so that the bullet is U+2022 in the loaded font and U+2219 in code page 437. A reply's CJK and emoji, which no stand-in covers, are `?` and a blank.

The console keeps two key modes, which `kio_key_modes` gives the keyboard to encode keys under: DECCKM, from `ESC [ ? 1 h` to `ESC [ ? 1 l`, and the keypad's application mode, from `ESC =` to `ESC >`. Both are off at boot and after `ESC c`.

A byte written in the last column leaves the cursor there with the wrap pending, as terminfo's `xenl` says: the next printable byte begins the next line, and a cursor move, an erase, BS, HT, LF or CR cancels the wrap. A program can then fill the last row without the screen scrolling.

SGR takes 0; 1 and 22, bold, which sets the foreground's bright bit; 7 and 27, reverse; 30 to 37, 39 and 90 to 97 for the foreground; and 40 to 47, 49 and 100 to 107 for the background, which has eight colors. ANSI's yellow is the VGA's brown.

The answer to a query (`ESC [ ? 6 c`, `ESC [ 0 n`, or the cursor's position) waits in a buffer of 16 bytes until `tty_write` takes it with `k_take_answer` and gives it to the console's input.

### The text console

`vga_text.c` keeps the text in a history ring of 512 lines (`VGA_TEXT_SCROLLBACK_LINES`), each of up to 240 cells, a character byte and an attribute byte. Lines are numbered by a count that only grows. The live area, where output lands, is the last `textcon_rows` lines; the viewport is the lines on the screen, the live area unless the screen is scrolled back. Once the ring is full each new line replaces the oldest.

The VGA's text memory is 32 KiB at physical 0xB8000, 16384 cells, room for eight 80x25 screens. Each store there is an uncached bus cycle on hardware and an exit to the emulator under a hypervisor, so the driver writes each cell once:

* A byte of live output is one store, to the history and to the one cell that shows it.
* A scroll moves the display's start address (CRTC registers 0x0C and 0x0D, through ports 0x3D4 and 0x3D5) down one line and writes the new bottom line blank. When the window would pass the end of the memory, the rows that stay are copied to its front and the start address returns to 0: at 80x25, once every 180 scrolls.
* The cursor (CRTC registers 0x0E and 0x0F) counts cells from the start of the memory, so the window's start is added to it. Placing it takes four port writes, so a writer of many bytes takes a hold (`textcon_hold`, which nests) and the cursor is placed once, at the release.
* Only a viewport that moves, by a scroll back or a clear, is drawn whole (`textcon_sync`).

While the adapter is in a graphics mode the start address is left alone; leaving graphics mode clears the screen, which sets it again.

### Scrollback

The keys come from `sys/input.c`. While the debug shell reads input, Page Up and Page Down move the viewport a screen at a time, and Home and End go to the oldest line and to the live screen. In a session those keys go to the program as escape sequences, and Shift with Page Up or Page Down moves the viewport. Any other key in a session, and a typed character, Backspace or Enter in the debug shell, returns the screen to the live area. While the screen is scrolled back, output goes to the history alone; a new line that pushes the viewport's top line out of the ring moves the viewport to the oldest line. A clear (`k_cls`, which `ESC c` and the end of graphics mode call) empties the history.

### The font

`vga_font.c` is written by `tools/font/mkfont.sh` from GNU Unifont 18.0.01, under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, and is not edited by hand. Its upper half keeps code page 437's box drawing, shades and blocks, with Cyrillic letters in place of the Latin and Greek ones. At boot `k_font_init` loads its 98 glyphs that differ from code page 437 (`VGA_FONT_GLYPHS`) into the adapter's character memory, plane 2 opened at 0xA0000 with 32 bytes a character, of which an 8x16 glyph uses 16, and reads them back (`vga_font_load` in `vga_gfx.c`). When every byte reads back, `kio.c` maps text through `vga_font_map`; otherwise the adapter's own code page 437 stays. `vga_font_all` is the whole font, 256 glyphs with the control codes blank, for the framebuffer, which has no font of its own.

### The framebuffer console

`k_main` in `sys/arch/x86/boot.c` calls `fbcon_init` before the shared boot when the boot record, Multiboot's or multiboot2's, describes a framebuffer of direct color at 8 to 32 bits a pixel or of 8-bit indexed color. GRUB leaves the firmware's under UEFI, and on a disk booted by BIOS it sets the mode the video BIOS (VBE) prefers for the display, by the display's EDID, unless the menu entry "Start Rach2 in text mode" asks for text or no graphics mode can be set (`tools/image/mkdisk_grub.sh`). QEMU's `-kernel` and an ISO booted by BIOS start the kernel by Multiboot in the text mode. `fbcon_init` returns -1, and the console stays on the VGA, when the screen has fewer than 640x384 pixels (80 columns and 24 rows of the font at scale 1), when its pitch is shorter than a row of pixels, or when `fbcon_window` cannot reach it.

The glyph, 8 pixels by 16, is drawn at the largest whole scale at which 80 columns and 24 rows still fit on the screen, so each pixel of the font is a square of whole pixels, and the console has as many cells as fit at that scale, at most 240 by 64 (`TEXTCON_COLS_MAX`, `TEXTCON_ROWS_MAX`), centered. A 1366x768 screen has scale 2 and 85x24 cells, and a 1280x800 one scale 2 and 80x25. A cell's sixteen colors are the VGA's text palette, converted to the screen's pixel fields, or on an indexed screen the nearest entries of the loader's palette.

`fbcon.c` stands in for the adapter. `fbcon_cells`, 16384 cells, takes the stores `vga_text.c` makes, and `fbcon_set_start` and `fbcon_set_cursor` take the start address and the cursor. `drawn` records what each screen cell shows, and a cell is drawn only when the cell the start address selects for it differs, or the cursor came or went, since each of its pixels is a store to the display's memory. After a scroll a cell is drawn only where the line moving into it differs from the line drawn there, so a blank cell moving onto a blank one is not drawn. The cursor is rows 13 and 14 of its cell, the two lines the VGA's text mode underlines a cell with.

`fbcon_window` gives the console the framebuffer's address:

* Until the kernel's own page tables are in force, the loader's view of memory: the physical address itself on i686, where paging is off, and on x86-64 its address in the direct map of the first 4 GiB that `loader.s` builds.
* After that, a mapping in the kernel's MMIO region (`map_mmio_typed`), made the first time the console draws.
* For a framebuffer that reaches past 4 GiB, no address: `fbcon_init` fails and the screen stays as the loader left it.

The mapping is write-combining where the processor has a page attribute table, and uncached otherwise. `pat_setup` (`sys/arch/x86/pat.c`) makes entry 1 of IA32_PAT write-combining ([the PC platform](../arch/x86/Readme.md)), by the SDM's procedure for a memory type change on a running processor. The boot processor runs it in `platform_cpu_init`, before the kernel's tables map the framebuffer, and each started processor runs it first in `x86_ap_entry`, before it can draw. `PTE_WC`, PWT alone (0x008 on both architectures), selects entry 1, and only `fbcon_window` uses it. The console and the `/fb` picture only write the framebuffer, and write-combining merges their stores into bursts, where uncached memory takes a bus transaction for each.

The console terminal's window size is the cells (`k_console_size`, read once by `tty_console_init`), and the `rach2` entry gives no size, so a program takes the screen's from TIOCGWINSZ.

### `/fb`

One process at a time owns the display, recorded by thread group id in `fb_owner_pid` (`fbdev.c`), so any of its threads may use it. A descriptor of `/fb` has the operations `fb_file_ops`, so write(2), pwrite(2), ioctl(2) and the last close reach `fbdev.c` through the descriptor, as a pipe's do.

* An open of `/fb` (`open_framebuffer` in `sys/syscall_fs.c`) fails with EBUSY unless the caller owns the display or descends from its owner. A new owner saves the old owner's keyboard and terminal state and mode, queues `FB_EVENT_LOST`, and enters mode 13h with the sixteen console colors and the rest black. Same-owner opens share the ownership without changing the mode. O_CLOEXEC and O_NONBLOCK are kept.
* The last close of every description opened by an owner, `FB_MODE_TEXT`, and the process's exit release its stack entry. Fork and dup share a description, so closing one inherited descriptor leaves its ownership in place. A last inherited close releases it even when made by another process. Closing an inherited description cannot release an independently opened child's ownership. Exec keeps ownership unless O_CLOEXEC closes its last description.
* The next owner receives its saved mode, palette, keyboard mode, meta setting, locks, LEDs, repeat and console termios, through `kd_state_restore`, before `FB_EVENT_REGAINED`. It redraws its whole screen. An owner that ends while suspended is removed without disturbing the active owner. The last return restores the state from before the first open, flushes input, cancels a held key's repeat and redraws the console's saved text.
* Event reads return one whole `struct fb_event`. Events alternate between lost and regained and are counted without allocating, shared by the owner's open descriptions. An empty read waits interruptibly, or gives EAGAIN with O_NONBLOCK; a short buffer gives EINVAL, and EFAULT consumes the event as eventfd does. Poll reports POLLIN for events and POLLOUT only while the caller owns the screen. A released description drains its events and returns EOF, with POLLHUP. An inherited handle's reads give EBUSY and its poll gives POLLERR.
* `fb_ioctl` takes the five requests of `<rach2/fb.h>` and returns ENOTTY for any other. A caller other than the owner gets EBUSY, with a descriptor inherited by fork or kept after the display was released, except for `FB_MODE_13H` on an unowned display, which takes it. A request of the mode in force changes nothing; one that changes the mode, or takes the screen back with `FB_MODE_13H`, puts the description's offset back at 0. `FB_MODE_TEXT` returns to the suspended owner or the console. `FB_SETPALETTE` reads 768 bytes, 256 colors of 8-bit red, green and blue, for mode 13h, in either mode. `FB_GETINFO` reports the framebuffer whatever the mode, and is ENODEV on the VGA's text mode. `FB_MODE_NATIVE` is ENODEV there and on a framebuffer that is not RGB at 15, 16, 24 or 32 bits a pixel.
* A write (`fb_write`) is refused with EBUSY to any process but the owner. It copies to the pixels of the mode in force at the description's offset, a byte counted from the top left pixel in rows of the pitch: EFBIG when the offset is past the mode's end, ENOSPC at it, and a write that would pass the end stops there. In the native mode the offset moves past what was written, so lseek(2) and write(2) place rows as pwrite(2) does, which works on a copy of the description (`pio_view`). In mode 13h the offset stays, so that each write(2) from 0 is a frame, as Doom writes them, and `fb_present` shows the picture after the copy. An lseek(2) takes any place from 0, `SEEK_END` counting from `fb_size`, the mode's size, 0 in text mode; write(2) and lseek(2) hold the description's lock (`ops_offset_held`).
* A write copies the caller's bytes 4096 at a time into a buffer of the kernel's, since a fault on the caller's memory may sleep, and stores each piece with the console's lock held, under which it checks again that the caller owns the display in the same mode and no transfer or mode change has intervened. A mode left by another thread of the owner meanwhile ends the write at the piece before, so no pixel of the program lands on the console's text. The mode changes of `fbdev.c` take the same lock, so that a processor drawing a byte of the console cannot draw over a screen just given to the program.

On the VGA, `fb_enter` saves the text font from plane 2, programs mode 13h's registers from their tables, and clears the 64000 bytes at 0xA0000. `fb_exit` programs mode 3, loads the sixteen text colors into the DAC, restores the saved font, and redraws the text screen from its history. A palette goes to the DAC shifted right by 2, since the DAC's values have 6 bits. Both run with interrupts off. The VGA's text mode has no native mode.

On a framebuffer console there may be no VGA, as on a machine booted by UEFI, and the VGA's aperture at 0xA0000 may alias the screen the console draws on, so `vga_gfx.c` leaves the hardware alone and the picture is memory of its own (`gfx_shadow`, 64000 bytes). `fbcon_gfx_begin` paints the screen black and centers the picture as tall as the screen, or as wide on a screen narrower than 16:10, at its own 320:200, so that Doom fills the screen's height in its own shape: 1228x768 from (69, 0) on a 1366x768 screen, 1280x800 on a 1280x800 one, and 1024x640 from (0, 64) on a 1024x768 one. A scaled line must fit the line buffer of 16384 bytes, which narrows the picture only past 4096 pixels across at 32 bits. Each of the picture's pixels covers a whole number of the screen's, which on a screen that is not a whole multiple of 320x200 differs by one from pixel to pixel: three or four on the Lenovo's. Until the first palette the picture's first sixteen colors are the console's and the rest black; `fbcon_gfx_palette` converts a palette to the screen's pixels. Each write is drawn by `fbcon_gfx_frame`, which builds each line of the picture once at the scaled width, its column c showing pixel c * 320 / width, and copies it to every row of the screen that shows that line, its row r showing line r * 200 / height.

The native mode (`fb_enter_native`) gives the program the framebuffer itself, through the console's mapping of it (`fbcon_window`), write-combining where the processor allows: `fbcon_native_begin` paints it black and draws nothing more, not the picture either, and `gfx_current` takes the screen's width, height, pitch and depth, and its size, the pitch times the height, 4,227,072 bytes on the Lenovo's 1366x768 screen at 32 bits. Mode 13h and the native mode go straight from one to the other. No cell is drawn while a program has the screen, in either mode; `fbcon_gfx_end` paints the screen black and draws every cell again, and `fb_exit` synchronizes it with the text history.

### The console on a DRM card

`sys/drm/drm_client.c` makes the console the in-kernel client of the first DRM card that sets modes and makes dumb buffers, as Linux's fbdev emulation is; [the DRM core](../drm/Readme.md) calls it as a card registers, goes and sees a hotplug, on each change of a card's master role, and at a card's last close. The user's view is in [console(4)](../../../man/man4/console.4) under DRM masters.

A master's arrival unblanks the console, as taking `/fb` does, and hands the screen over under the console's lock: `fbcon_detach` stops every store to the screen while the cells, the cursor and `/fb`'s modes go on changing, and `fb_hold` makes `/fb`'s writes wait, refuses a new owner and tells the owner on top `FB_EVENT_LOST`. `kd_hold` (`sys/kd.c`) then saves the keyboard's mode and puts it in K_OFF; a KDSKBMODE meanwhile is kept for the release, and KDGKBMODE and an owner's saved state read the mode kept.

The client takes the screen back when the card's last file closes, as Linux's `drm_lastclose` restores the fbdev mode (`drm_client_dev_lastclose`), or when a hotplug comes while no file holds the role, as Linux's hotplug handler does; DROP_MASTER, or the close of the master's file while another file of the card is open, gives nothing back, so that a program may move the role between its own files, as IGT's `kms_flip_event_leak` does, without the console setting its mode on the CRTCs the program set up. It takes the screen back in process context, since the commit may sleep. On a native card it probes the connectors, makes one dumb buffer of XRGB8888 as large as the largest mode it sets and commits it on as many connected displays as can be lit at once, each through its first encoder, which the commit routes it through, so that of two that share one, as DisplayPort and HDMI share a DDI port's, the first is lit, and each on a CRTC that encoder reaches, found by augmenting paths as Linux's `drm_client_pick_crtcs` searches, turning every other CRTC off, in one atomic commit, as Linux's `drm_client_modeset_commit_atomic`, so that a commit that fails changes no output and leaves fbcon on the buffer the CRTCs still show, and maps it through the driver's `vmap`, or, for a driver that cannot map it as one range, as virtio-gpu's buffers of scattered pages, gives fbcon a shadow of the buffer's size in the kernel's heap; then, under the console's lock, `fbcon_attach` gives fbcon the buffer and `fb_unhold` draws the owner's mode again (`fb_redraw`) and sends `FB_EVENT_REGAINED`; `kd_unhold` restores the keyboard last. Before any of it `console_poke` starts the blank's countdown again, since the tick blanks a console whose screen `fb_unhold` gave back with the count from before the master. On the firmware's framebuffer the client turns the CRTC off and fbcon draws on the loader's screen again. A master that comes while the commit sleeps keeps the screen: the client counts every change of the role, and gives the screen to no one when the count moved. The loader's framebuffer may go while the console is still on it, when the native driver that shows it through its aperture gives its device to another owner ([i915](../drm/i915/Readme.md)): `drm_client_boot_screen_gone` detaches fbcon from it, the console draws nothing, as after the loss of its own buffer, and the next device that registers gets the console's mode at once.

`fbcon_attach` keeps the grid of `textcon_cols` by `textcon_rows` that the boot chose and draws it at the largest whole scale of the font that fits the new screen, at least 1, centered, with the cells past the right edge or the foot left out on a screen too small at scale 1. A console on the VGA's text mode becomes a framebuffer console there and draws its scrollback again. A hotplug while no master holds the card does the same on the new modes and then `fb_screen_moved`, which draws the owner's mode again; at the native mode, whose pixels are gone, the owner reads `FB_EVENT_LOST` and `FB_EVENT_REGAINED` to draw them again.

fbcon records the rows of pixels each of its stores changes, and `/fb`'s native writes add theirs (`fbcon_damage`). On a shadow the client's worker, `kdrmcon`, takes those rows (`fbcon_take_damage`) every 50 ms while the console has the screen, copies them into the buffer's pages through the driver's `page` hook and `kmap_temp`, and commits them as DIRTYFB does (`drm_framebuffer_dirty`), as Linux's fbdev emulation does for a shmem driver with its shadow and damage worker.

A buffer that is gone, with its card or out of video memory where the driver can no longer map it, is forgotten (`fbcon_detach(1)`): fbcon draws nothing and `fbcon_native_begin` gives `/fb` no screen until the next buffer is attached, so `/fb`'s native mode falls back to mode 13h's picture in memory instead of writing where the buffer was.

### Blanking

`blank.c` keeps the time without input after which the screen blanks, 600 seconds unless `consoleblank=` says otherwise and 0 for never, which `/sys/module/kernel/parameters/consoleblank` shows, read-only as Linux's, and the tick of the last key or mouse move, which `console_poke` records from every key event of `input.c`, `mouse_push_packet` and the ACPI keys' `acpi_report_key`. The boot processor's tick calls `blank_tick` through `tty_console_poll`: it blanks the screen once that time has passed since the last poke, and unblanks it when a poke asked for that since. A poke only asks, since it comes from an interrupt handler and a repaint of a framebuffer is a store for each of its million or so pixels; the tick does the work under the console's lock, as a byte written to the screen is, at most a tick later. A blank and an unblank each give every backlight its new state (`backlight_console_blank`), ask the console's DRM client to turn its display off or on (`drm_client_blank`), and write a record to the log with their count. An unblank on request restarts the time, as a poke does. A panic shows the screen and gives the backlights their levels again before it draws, taking neither the console's lock nor the backlights', which the code it stopped may hold, and writing no record (`console_panic_unblank`); each driver lights its backlight through its `panic_on`, which takes its own lock only by a try and leaves the backlight dark when the lock is held, and a driver with none, as `acpi_video0`, whose `_BCM` runs on ACPICA's thread, is left as it is; a native display the blank turned off stays off, since its commit may sleep (Limits).

On the VGA's text mode `textcon_blank` clears bit 5 of the attribute controller's index, the palette address source: the screen shows the overscan color, black, and the adapter's memory, start address and cursor are untouched, so output goes on and shows at the unblank; setting the bit again shows it. QEMU draws a blank screen for this bit and not for the sequencer's screen-off bit. On a framebuffer `fbcon_blank` paints the screen black and draws no cell until the unblank, which draws every cell again, as the end of the `/fb` picture does. The screen does not blank while a DRM master holds it (`fb_is_held`), and no backlight changes then, as Linux leaves a console in graphics mode alone; taking `/fb` or the screen unblanks it first, and the console that has the screen back from a master counts the time again from then. Under a program that owns `/fb`, as `term` does on the Lenovo, the console blanks at its time, as Linux's console in text mode blanks under a program that draws on `/dev/fb0`, which sets no `KD_GRAPHICS`; the program's picture is not painted over, since the console cannot draw it again, and the unblank draws nothing over it: the backlights and the display going off darken it. Where neither can, with no backlight that goes dark (`backlight_can_darken`) and no native display of the console's with a CRTC lit (`drm_client_can_blank`), as bochs before its first mode set under the VGA's picture, the console does not blank, logs `console: blank: refused: nothing darkens the picture on /fb` at the debug level and counts the time again, so that TIOCLINUX's subcode 15 never says blank over a picture that shows; its subcode 14 still returns 0 there, as Linux's does, and `setterm --blank force` reads subcode 15 after it and fails.

The display goes off as Linux's fbdev emulation turns its CRTCs off for a blank (`drm_fb_helper_dpms`): the console's DRM client, on a native device, commits each active CRTC with `active` clear and its mode, planes and connectors kept, so that the connector's DPMS reads off and an eDP panel loses its power and backlight, and at the unblank commits those CRTCs active again. The commits may sleep, so the tick only asks, and the client's worker, `kdrmcon`, which the client starts for every native device, makes them. A commit that fails leaves its CRTC as it was and the blank or the unblank half done; the worker logs the first failure, `console: cardN: crtc K not turned on: error E; tried again`, and tries again every second until the commits succeed or the console asks otherwise, so that a wake that failed once does not leave the display dark under a console that shows itself unblanked. A half-done run leaves work for either ask: a key turns on every CRTC a half-failed blank turned off, and a blank turns off every CRTC a half-failed wake lit. The worker's wait ends at the deadline it armed, whatever a placement changed meanwhile. Nothing is committed on the firmware's framebuffer, whose screen is the loader's, or while a master holds the device; a placement of the console's own mode meanwhile, at a hotplug or the return from a master, lights every output, and wakes the worker, which turns them off again while the blank lasts. On the Lenovo the console stays on the firmware's picture, which i915 adopted on pipe A, until a master first leaves the card, and that pipe goes off and on.

`ESC [ 9 ; N ]` sets the time to N minutes, at most 60, and restarts the count, as on Linux. TIOCLINUX's subcode 14 blanks the screen until subcode 4, and a key does not end that blank, as Linux's `ignore_poke` does; subcode 15 says whether it is blank.

### The backlight

`backlight.c` keeps, for each backlight, the level root set and `bl_power` as root wrote it, and gives the driver the level set and whether the backlight is on: off while `bl_power` is 4 or the console is blank. A driver that takes a backlight over (`backlight_rebind`) and reads a level above 0 sets the class's level, in its own units; one that reads 0, as i915 after its device's reset or the stand-in while the device is a VM's, has no lit level to read, and is given the level the class kept, scaled to its range, through its `set` alone, so that a level root set survives the change; a kept 0 is given only when root wrote it, so that the firmware's dark is raised as below. i915 raises a level at or below the panel's least to the most when it turns the backlight on, as Linux's `__intel_backlight_enable` does, and tells the class in the same region under the class's lock (`backlight_driver_change`), which then shows that level, as Linux's `props.brightness`, so that root's write of 0 meanwhile is never overwritten by the raise; a class level 0, which root or a blank without a switch set, stays dark through the panel's enable, so that the wake after a blank never raises it and init never saves the raise. A driver whose level 0 still lights the panel, as an ACPI `_BCL` whose least level is above 0, says so (`backlight_set_lit_at_zero`): the blank leaves it at its level, as Linux's acpi_video leaves its level at a framebuffer's blank, and does not count it as darkening the screen. Each change of a level is logged: `backlight: NAME: level N by a write` and `backlight: NAME: rebound: level N of MAX` at the debug level, and a driver's raise at the info level. init keeps each level across a reboot and a reset in `/var/lib/backlight` ([init(8)](../../../man/man8/init.8)). A driver gives `get`, which reads the hardware's level for `actual_brightness` and for the level at registration, `set`, `power` when it has a switch for the backlight, and `panic_on` when it can light the panel from a panic; all but `panic_on` run with the class's spinlock held and interrupts off. A backlight with a switch is turned off by it at its level, as Linux's i915 does for `bl_power`; one without is given the level 0.

`intelbl.c` drives the PWM of an Intel PCH as Linux's i915 does in `lpt_setup_backlight`, for the Lynx Point and Wildcat Point that `board.c` identifies, the PCH of Haswell and Broadwell laptops. `BLC_PWM_PCH_CTL2`, at 0xC8254 of the graphics device's BAR 0, has the period in its high half, which is `max_brightness`, and the duty cycle in its low half, which is the level; with the polarity bit of `BLC_PWM_PCH_CTL1` the duty cycle is the dark part, and the driver inverts it. The driver registers `intel_backlight` only when the graphics device at 0:2.0 is Intel's and the firmware left the PCH's PWM on with a period, so on any other machine it writes nothing. When the processor's own PWM still drives the panel, which the PCH's override bit clear and `BLC_PWM_CPU_CTL2`'s enable bit set show, its duty cycle goes to the PCH's, the override is set and the processor's PWM turned off, as i915 does. It maps the three pages of BAR 0 that hold the registers, at 0x48000, 0xC7000 and 0xC8000. A write of the level changes the duty cycle alone; on the Lenovo G50-70 the firmware leaves 0x03A903A9, a period of 937 at full duty. When i915 binds the graphics device it attaches the same code to the PWM through its own register access and takes `intel_backlight` over with `backlight_rebind`, so the name, index and `bl_power` stay, the level is i915's reading of the same duty cycle, and the boot's pages are no longer written; i915 then keeps the enable off while the panel's pipe is off ([`../drm/i915/Readme.md`](../drm/i915/Readme.md)). When i915's device goes, `intelbl_take_back` rebinds the backlight to the boot's pages.

The backlight's switch is the panel power sequencer's backlight enable, bit 2 of `PP_CONTROL` at 0xC7204, which drives the panel's enable line; a write carries the unlock key 0xABCD in the high half, as i915's `intel_pps_backlight_power` writes it. The driver takes the switch when the firmware left the panel's power and its enable on, bits 0 and 2, as it does for an eDP panel it drew on, and without it a blank is a duty cycle of 0. A blank keeps the duty cycle because of the Lenovo G50-70: a brightness key pressed while the duty cycle was 0 left its panel dark at full duty, the embedded controller raising no more brightness queries, until `_Q12` was run by hand.

## Limits

* `/fb` has no mmap(2): a program writes the native mode with pwrite(2), each byte copied through the kernel, a whole frame of the Lenovo's screen 4,227,072 bytes.
* `/fb` is one program's at a time: while one owns it, only its descendants may open it, each suspending the owner until it gives the screen back, and any other program gets EBUSY.
* The native mode needs a framebuffer: the VGA's text mode, as BIOS leaves it without one, has only mode 13h.
* The framebuffer console draws only on a framebuffer below 4 GiB, of at least 640x384 pixels, of direct color at 8 to 32 bits a pixel or 8-bit indexed; with any other the console stays on the VGA's text mode, which a machine booted by UEFI may lack.
* The console has no scroll region and no line or character insertion, so a program redraws where a terminal would scroll.
* The background has eight colors.
* The scrollback is 512 lines, the screen's own included, and a clear empties it. Output written while the screen is scrolled back shows when it returns to the live area.
* The window size is set once at boot. TIOCSWINSZ on the console changes what TIOCGWINSZ reports and leaves the screen as it is. On a DRM card's buffer the console keeps that grid, scaled and centered, and does not fill a larger screen with more cells.
* On a DRM card the console stays on the loader's framebuffer, or the VGA's text mode, until a master first leaves the card; it sets no mode of its own at boot.
* The kernel's debug shell runs with the scheduler stopped, so a shadow, as on virtio-gpu, is not flushed while it waits for a key: what it prints shows when a program runs.
* The native backlight is driven only through an Intel PCH's PWM, Lynx Point or Wildcat Point. The ACPI video bus's `_BCM` is `acpi_video0` only on a machine where no native backlight is registered once the native display drivers have probed ([`sys/acpi/`](../acpi/Readme.md)); on the Lenovo it asks the graphics driver through its OpRegion and is not used.
* The serial port does not blank. A program's picture on `/fb` on a screen with neither a backlight that goes dark nor a DRM display of the console's, such as QEMU's VGA, does not blank, since nothing but painting over it, which the console could not undo, would darken it.
* A panic while the blank has a native display off draws its report on a dark panel: turning the CRTC on again is an atomic commit, which may sleep, and the panic runs with interrupts off and the other processors stopped. Linux has the same limit: its `drm_fb_helper_blank` refuses with `-EBUSY` while `oops_in_progress`, and its DRM panic screen draws only on a visible plane, which a CRTC turned off has not. The report still reaches the log, ramoops and netconsole, and the panic resets the machine as it would otherwise.

## Tests

The host tests run with `tests/run host`:

* `tests/host/vga_text_hosttest.c`: the driver against a model adapter whose stores and port writes are counted: a byte as one cell, a scroll as one line and the start address, the copy once in 180 scrolls, the redraw by a scroll back and a clear, the held cursor placed once, and the pending wrap.
* `tests/host/fbcon_hosttest.c`: the console drawn into a framebuffer in memory: glyphs in their colors, the cursor's two lines, a Cyrillic letter reached through UTF-8, the scale and the cells within 240 by 64, pixels of 32, 24, 16 and 8 bits, the centering, what a scroll and a clear draw, the scrollback, the mode 13h picture's size, place and the rows and columns each of its pixels covers, the native mode given black with no cell drawn until it ends and refused on an indexed screen and one of 8 bits, and the blank screen black until the unblank draws every cell again.
* `tests/host/vga_gfx_hosttest.c`: the mode changes with a keyboard interrupt pending at each port access, the font loaded into the character memory, read back, and not loaded in a graphics mode, and the native mode's entry and exit, from and to mode 13h.
* `tests/host/drmcon_hosttest.c` and `drmcon64_hosttest.c`: the console's DRM client over the real master role and mode setting: the handover of the screen, `/fb` and the keyboard to a master, their return at the card's last close, none at DROP_MASTER or at the master's close while another file is open, `video=`, a hotplug, and one after DROP_MASTER, a master that comes during the commit, a buffer that can no longer be mapped, a shadow and its flush, the blank's CRTC off and on again with its mode and buffer kept, the firmware's card, and a loader's framebuffer that goes with its device.
* `tests/host/fbdev_hosttest.c`: `/fb`'s requests and their refusals, the offsets of a write in each mode and their bounds, a write cut short by a mode left meanwhile, the owner's check across fork, nested ownership and events, separate opens, dead suspended owners, and restoration of the console.
* `tests/host/intelbl_hosttest.c`: the Intel PCH backlight against a model of its PWM registers: the Lenovo's PWM taken without a write, a level written as the duty cycle with the period kept, the polarity bit, a PWM left off or with no period refused, and a PWM the processor drives handed to the PCH's.

The suites, each run with `tests/run NAME`:

* `console`: the screen's answers to ttyprobe's queries.
* `uefi`: a machine booted by OVMF, with the console on its framebuffer. A dump of the screen must show the login prompt in the kernel's font, the page attribute table must read back with entry 1 write-combining, and Doom's picture must be on the screen in its palette's colors while it runs, and the console's colors again after it ends.
* `doom`: the game, which draws through `/fb`, on the VGA; it judges the game's memory and exit status and not the picture.
* `screen`: the screen blanked by TIOCLINUX, by the escape and by `consoleblank=`, each judged by the kernel's records of its blanks and by QMP's screen dumps, black while it is blank and showing the console before and after; a key does not end TIOCLINUX's blank and does end the others. Its case `levels` keeps the backlight `acpi_video0`, from the suite's SSDT, and the volume across a reboot and a reset, and reads the backlight's actual level at 0 while the console is blank under `consoleblank=4` and back at its level after a key.
