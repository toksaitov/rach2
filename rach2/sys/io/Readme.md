# `io/`: `poll`, `select`, `epoll`, `eventfd`, `timerfd`, POSIX timers and `inotify`

Rach2 implements `poll`, `ppoll`, `select`, `_newselect`, and `pselect6` over one readiness scanner, and `epoll` over the same scanner and wait. These calls report which descriptors can make progress without waiting. They do not copy file, terminal, pipe, or socket payloads. `eventfd`, `timerfd` and `inotify` are descriptors whose readiness these calls report, a counter, a timer and a queue of changes to files.

## Readiness

Each descriptor reports a subset of the requested `POLLIN`, `POLLOUT`, and `POLLPRI` bits. `POLLERR`, `POLLHUP`, and `POLLNVAL` are returned without being requested.

An operations-backed descriptor calls its `file_ops.poll` method and supplies the wait queue whose wakeup can change its answer. Pipes, devfs files, procfs snapshots, and pseudoterminals use this path. The console calls the tty poller. Sockets call `socket_poll`. Regular KRFFS files and directories report requested read and write readiness without a wait queue.

The main backing rules are:

* A pipe uses its buffered byte count and peer lifetime. Its complete readiness contract is in the [pipe documentation](../ipc/Readme.md).
* A tty is readable when its line discipline has input ready and is always writable. Console readiness is restricted to the descriptor's access mode: `POLLIN` requires read access and `POLLOUT` requires write access. A read-only console descriptor polled only for output reports no readiness. A pseudoterminal also reports peer hangup.
* Datagram and protocol-bearing raw sockets are readable when a packet is queued and always writable. A listening stream socket is readable when an accepted connection is queued. A connected stream socket is readable with buffered data or a received FIN; it is writable in `ESTABLISHED` or `CLOSE_WAIT` while its send ring has space. Socket errors and a closed stream report `POLLERR` and `POLLHUP` as applicable. See the [network documentation](../network/Readme.md).
* Procfs snapshots report read readiness, including at EOF. Devfs character devices report requested read and write readiness.
* An eventfd is readable while its counter is not zero and writable while another 1 fits; a timerfd is readable while it has expirations to read; an epoll set is readable while a wait on it would report an event, so an edge-triggered event already reported, or a one-shot registration that is off, does not make it readable.

The framebuffer, mouse, and any remaining descriptor type use the fallback that reports requested read and write readiness immediately. Mouse readiness is not tied to packet availability, so `poll` cannot wait for a mouse packet.

For `poll`, a negative descriptor is ignored. A closed or out-of-range nonnegative descriptor receives `POLLNVAL` and contributes to the return count. The return value is the number of `pollfd` entries with a nonzero result.

## Scan and wait

The wait, `poll_wait`, takes a scan function, which `poll` and `select` give their descriptors and `epoll` its set. The core disables interrupts and scans every entry once. If one is ready, a signal that is neither blocked nor ignored is pending, or the deadline has passed, it returns. Otherwise it adds the process to every non-null wait queue, arms its timer deadline when needed, marks it blocked, and schedules another process.

A pipe operation, socket path, terminal path, or signal can wake the process. An epoll set has a queue of its own and one for each descriptor it watches, and those of the sets inside it, so a poll or a select of a set is on all of them (`epoll_poll_set`) and a change in any wakes it; a scan that finds more queues than the wait has records for, with nothing ready, ends the wait, which begins again with a record for each. The 100 Hz timer wakes a finite wait at its deadline. After any wake, the process removes itself from every queue and scans again. The scan and all queue insertions happen while interrupts are disabled, so a readiness change cannot occur between the test and the sleep.

If no descriptor is ready, a pending signal that is neither blocked nor ignored returns `EINTR`; the kernel does not restart the call for `SA_RESTART`, nor an `epoll` wait, as Linux does not. A finite timeout ends at the first 10 ms tick at or after its end, measured from where in the tick in progress the call is (`rtc_tick_phase`), so a timeout never expires before the time asked, up to the limits below, and at most a tick after it. A zero timeout performs one scan without waiting. `poll` with no entries and a finite timeout acts as a sleep. A negative `poll` timeout or a null timeout pointer for `select`, `_newselect`, `ppoll`, or `pselect6` waits indefinitely, until readiness or a pending signal that is neither blocked nor ignored.

Finite waits saturate without an error. `select` and `_newselect` convert any seconds value above 2147482 to 2147483647 milliseconds before tick rounding, about 24.86 days. `ppoll` and `pselect6` cap seconds at 21474836, round nanoseconds up to ticks, and cap the sum at 2147483646 ticks, about 248.55 days. Larger requested intervals are shortened. These kernel limits apply even when the timeout fits the 32-bit ABI; they are separate from musl's time64 fallback described below.

## `select` translation

`select` translates its read, write, and exception bitmaps into a dense `pollfd` array. One selected descriptor becomes one entry whose event bits record every set that contained it. The result is projected back as follows:

* `POLLIN`, `POLLHUP`, or `POLLERR` satisfies a requested read bit.
* `POLLOUT` or `POLLERR` satisfies a requested write bit.
* `POLLPRI` satisfies a requested exception bit.

Projection is limited to sets in which the caller selected the descriptor. A hangup on a write-only selection does not create a read result. The return value counts ready set memberships, so one descriptor ready for both read and write contributes two.

An `fd_set` has 1024 bits, matching the maximum number of descriptors a process may hold. `nfds` may be as large as 1024. Every selected descriptor below `nfds` must be open, or the call returns `EBADF`; `nfds` outside 0 through 1024 returns `EINVAL`.

## System-call ABI and limits

The implemented i386 calls are old packed-argument `select(82)`, `_newselect(142)`, `poll(168)`, `pselect6(308)`, and `ppoll(309)`; `epoll_create(254)`, `epoll_ctl(255)`, `epoll_wait(256)`, `epoll_pwait(319)`, `epoll_create1(329)` and `epoll_pwait2(441)`; `eventfd(323)` and `eventfd2(328)`; `inotify_init(291)`, `inotify_add_watch(292)`, `inotify_rm_watch(293)` and `inotify_init1(332)`; `timerfd_create(322)`, `timerfd_settime(325)`, `timerfd_gettime(326)` and their time64 forms, 411 and 410; and `timer_create(259)`, whose timers count real time or, for a clock of processor time, the ticks `sched_account_tick` charges (`ptimer_cpu_tick`), `timer_settime(260)`, `timer_gettime(261)`, `timer_getoverrun(262)`, `timer_delete(263)` and the time64 forms, 409 and 408. The `pselect6_time64(413)` and `ppoll_time64(414)` calls are absent and return `ENOSYS`.

The ported i386 musl calls `poll(168)` directly. It remaps ordinary `select` to `_newselect(142)`. `pselect` and `ppoll` use calls 308 and 309 while the timeout seconds fit in 32 bits. Only a larger timeout makes musl try call 413 or 414 before falling back to the 32-bit call with a clamped timeout.

`poll` accepts at most 1024 entries, the maximum number of descriptors a process may hold. Its descriptor, wait-queue and waiter arrays are allocated from the heap for each call. `select` reads and writes a complete 128-byte `fd_set` for each non-null set. `timeval` and `timespec` fields must be nonnegative and normalized; an invalid value returns `EINVAL`. This implementation does not write the remaining `select` time back to the caller's `timeval`.

`ppoll`, `pselect6` and `epoll_pwait` install the signal mask they are given for the wait (`poll_mask_set`); the old mask comes back when the wait ends, or, when a signal ended it, after that signal's handler runs. No backing reports `POLLPRI`, so an exception-only `select` cannot become ready.

## `epoll`

An epoll set (`epoll.c`) keeps registrations of a descriptor number and the open file it named when it was added, with the events it is watched for and a 64-bit word of data. A wait scans the set with the poll core's descriptor readiness and reports at most `maxevents` ready registrations with their data; a reported registration moves to the end of the set's list, so waits with room for fewer events than are ready report the ready descriptors in turn, as Linux's do. A set another thread grows while a wait sleeps has more queues than the wait has records for; the scan that finds so, with nothing ready, ends the wait's sleep, and the wait begins again with a record for each queue. The scan takes each event it reports, so a second waiter does not report an edge-triggered or one-shot event again, and the copy to the caller follows the wait; an event that cannot be copied is put back in its registration, unless that has gone or changed, and the wait returns the events copied before it, or `EFAULT` when none were, as Linux's does. A registration is level-triggered, or with `EPOLLET` edge-triggered: it is reported when an event appears in its readiness that was not there at its last report, or has gone since, or when its descriptor's wait queue has been woken since that report for a kind of change among its events, which is how a pipe drained and filled again between two waits is reported again. A wake names the kinds of change it is, data, room or a hangup, and a wait queue counts the wakes of each kind (`wakes` in `proc/wait_queue.h`); the registration keeps the queue and the count of the kinds it watches at its last report, a hangup among them always, as `EPOLLERR` and `EPOLLHUP` are. The wakers of each queue a poll reports both input and output on name the kind of their change: those of AF_UNIX sockets, pipes, eventfds, pseudoterminals and network sockets. So data that comes to a descriptor watched for output alone, or room made in one watched for input alone, is no edge, as Linux's wakeup callback, which compares the events of a wake with the registration's, has it; a close, a shutdown, an error and a change of state name every kind (`wq_wake_all`), as Linux's wakes without events do. A write to a pipe wakes its readers at every transfer, the ring empty before it or not, so each write is an edge of a registration of the read end for input, as Linux has it for a pipe it polls. `EPOLLERR` and `EPOLLHUP` are reported whatever a registration asks, a registration of no events included. `EPOLLONESHOT` disables a registration at its first report, errors and hangups included, until `EPOLL_CTL_MOD`. A registration lasts while its open file is open in any process, as in Linux, whatever became of the number it was added by: a fork's child that closes its copy of the number leaves the registration to the parent, and the scan after the open file's last close drops it, which the list of live descriptions tells (`fd_ofd_live`); the last close of a set takes the registrations of it in other sets at once. A scan looks at a registration only through the caller's descriptor at its number, when that names its open file, since the wait sleeps on the queues of what it looks at and only the caller's own descriptors keep those while it sleeps; Linux reports a registration through its open file whatever became of the number. An open file is told from one made later at the same address by the serial number its description has (`struct open_file`). A regular file or directory cannot be added (`EPERM`). An epoll set inside another is ready while a wait on it would report an event, and a wait on the outer set is on the queues of the sets inside it and of what they watch, so a change in any of them wakes it, as in Linux; an edge-triggered registration of an inner set counts the wakes of all those queues. A scan looks into each set once, which ends a cycle, and four sets deep at most, Linux's `EP_MAX_NESTS`, which bounds the kernel stack a scan takes. `EPOLL_CTL_ADD` of a set that would make a cycle, or a chain of more than five sets, is `ELOOP`, as in Linux, so no set is nested deeper than a scan looks: the check counts the longest chain of sets below the set added and the longest above the set it is added to, through every registration that lasts, whichever process made it, so that a fork's child that closed a number cannot make a chain its parent's scans would not reach the end of; for the chain above, each set keeps the registrations of it in other sets (`holders`), and it looks into each set once, keeping the length found there for any other path to it.

## `eventfd`, `timerfd` and POSIX timers

An eventfd (`eventfd.c`) is a 64-bit counter: a write of eight bytes adds, a read takes the whole counter or one with `EFD_SEMAPHORE`, a read of zero waits, and a write that would carry the counter past `0xfffffffffffffffe` waits. A read takes its value before the copy to the caller, which can sleep in a page fault, so a second reader meanwhile finds it gone; a copy that fails loses the value, as in Linux. A read of an eventfd or a timerfd shorter than eight bytes is `EINVAL`, and `read` sends one of zero bytes to the descriptor's read method for that answer, ahead of the zero-length shortcut every other descriptor takes; `readv` of nothing returns 0, as Linux's does. A timerfd (`timerfd.c`) is armed with a first expiration, relative or absolute on its clock, and an interval; the timer tick (`timerfd_tick`, from the timer interrupt) counts expirations of the armed timers and wakes their readers, and a read of eight bytes returns the count since the last read. The read takes the count with interrupts off, since the tick adds to it, and before the copy to the caller, which can sleep: an expiration during the copy is kept for the next read, and a copy that fails loses the count, as in Linux. Times are 10 ms ticks: an expiration is rounded up and the tick in progress is not counted. A timer keeps its times as whole seconds and ticks below a second, since the kernel has no 64-bit division, up to 4294967295 seconds, about 136 years, taking a longer time as that; it waits a time longer than a span of 10737417 seconds, which keeps the signed comparison of ticks valid, as the part below a span and then whole spans, and `timerfd_gettime` reports the whole time. The descriptors of all three have type `FD_TYPE_ANON`, `/proc/PID/fd` names them `anon_inode:[eventfd]`, `anon_inode:[eventpoll]` and `anon_inode:[timerfd]`, and `fstat` reports mode 0600 with no type bits, as Linux's anonymous inodes.

A POSIX timer (`timerfd.c`, `struct ktimer`, the timer a timerfd has) belongs to its process, by an id from 0 in the process's list (`timers` in `struct tgroup`), at most 64, and is armed and read as a timerfd is. An expiration posts its signal, from the tick, with `SI_TIMER`, its id, its value and its overrun in the three words where a child's signal has the pid, the uid and the status, which is where Linux's siginfo has them, to the process or, for `SIGEV_THREAD_ID`, to one of its threads; while the signal is pending, later expirations add to the pending signal's overrun, which is what its handler is given, as Linux counts them. An exec and the end of the last thread delete a process's timers.

## `inotify`

An inotify instance (`inotify.c`) is an anonymous descriptor with a queue of events and the watches it added. A watch is kept by the mount and the inode number of the file it watches, the key an open file description has (`struct open_file`), on a list of every watch of the machine; `inotify_watches` counts them, and every hook tests it first, so a machine with no watch pays one comparison per call. `inotify_add_watch` is in `syscall_fs.c`, which resolves the path, checks the caller may read what it names, and hands the file's mount, inode number and kind to `inotify_watch`; a second watch of the same file by the same instance changes the first, and `IN_MASK_ADD`, `IN_MASK_CREATE`, `IN_ONESHOT` and `IN_ONLYDIR` are Linux's. The events come from two places. The namespace operations of `vfs.c` report what they did to a directory's names, since they have the directory and the name: `IN_CREATE` from create, mkdir, mknod, symlink and link, `IN_DELETE` from unlink and rmdir, and the `IN_MOVED_FROM` and `IN_MOVED_TO` of a rename with one cookie, `IN_ISDIR` for a directory; they look the entry up first to report `IN_ATTRIB` for a changed count of links, `IN_MOVE_SELF`, and `IN_DELETE_SELF` with the watch's `IN_IGNORED` for a file with no link left, where Linux waits for its last close. `vfs_setattr` reports `IN_ATTRIB`, `IN_MODIFY` or `IN_ACCESS` of the attributes it changed, by Linux's `fsnotify_change` rule. The descriptor calls report the data events through the open file description (`inotify_desc`): `IN_ACCESS` from a read, `IN_MODIFY` from a write and a truncation, `IN_OPEN` from `open` and `IN_CLOSE_WRITE` or `IN_CLOSE_NOWRITE` from the last close of the description, which `open` marks with `OFD_WATCH_OPEN` and whether it may write. A file's events reach the watches of the directory it was opened in, under its name there, when the open recorded them (`open_done`): the open looks the name's directory up again and records it and the name when a watch exists and the name is the file opened, not a link to it. A queued event the same as the last one unread is dropped, as Linux merges it; an instance queues 16384 events at most, and one `IN_Q_OVERFLOW`, kept in the instance so that it needs no memory, stands for what was lost. A read takes whole events off the queue before it copies each, so a copy that sleeps in a fault lets a second reader take the next. `umount` gives each watch of a file on the mount `IN_UNMOUNT` and `IN_IGNORED` (`inotify_unmount`), since a watch holds no reference that would keep the mount. A user has 128 instances and 8192 watches, Linux's defaults, which `/proc/sys/fs/inotify` reports.

## Verification

`make test` runs `tests/pollprobe.c`. It checks a timeout on two empty pipes, `POLLIN`, `POLLHUP`, `_newselect`, a child write waking a blocked poller, invalid selected descriptors, the 1024-bit `nfds` bound, and invalid `timeval` normalization. The same default suite runs the musl-linked `addusermusl`, `passwdmusl`, and `loginmusl` programs, which use libc `poll` while collecting data from child-process pipes.

`tests/run host` runs `tests/host/poll_select_hosttest.c` under AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer. It checks bitmap word boundaries, dense translation, multi-set descriptors, array-capacity failure, error and hangup projection, direction gating, and ready-set counting. `tests/host/eventfd_hosttest.c` and `tests/host/timerfd_hosttest.c` run the eventfd and timerfd reads with a copy to the caller that lets a second read, or the next tick, in first, and the timerfd's settings of 200 days and past the most seconds a setting keeps; `tests/host/wait_queue_hosttest.c` checks the count of each kind of wake the edge-triggered registrations read, and `tests/host/epoll_hosttest.c` runs the epoll code over model descriptors: the kinds of change an edge-triggered registration is told of, alone and through a set inside another, the nesting an add refuses, from above, from below and by the longer of two paths, the life of a registration whose number was closed while its open file stays open, in the same process and in a fork's child, and the lists of holders kept whole across closes.

`tests/musl/inotifymusl.c` checks, on tmpfs, which has links, the events of a watched directory and a watched file through a file's life, from its creation to its removal and the watch's `IN_IGNORED`, with a move's cookie and `IN_ISDIR`; a read too small for an event, an empty nonblocking queue, `FIONREAD` and `poll`; the refusals of `inotify_add_watch` and `inotify_rm_watch`, a second watch of a file and `IN_MASK_CREATE`, a one-shot watch, and that `inotify_init` leaves the caller's EBX, which i386 code keeps its global offset table in, as it was.

`tests/musl/epollmusl.c` checks that `eventfd` and `pipe`, which the kernel serves as `eventfd2` and `pipe2` without flags, leave the caller's ECX as it was, since a compiler keeps a value there across a call that does not name it.

`tests/musl/pollmusl.c` is built and staged at `/musl/pollmusl` for manual use. Its short timeouts exercise musl's direct `poll(168)` and `_newselect(142)` paths over a pipe, including timeout, readiness, and a cross-process wake. It does not exercise the time64 fallback.

`tests/musl/epollmusl.c` checks the eventfd counter and its semaphore form, and an epoll set's level-triggered, edge-triggered and one-shot reports, its data word, deletion, a closed descriptor dropped, the refusals, a child's write waking a wait, a registration of no events told of a hangup, a pipe added to a set while a wait sleeps waking it when written, a set whose edge-triggered event was reported polling unreadable, a poll of a set woken by a child's write to a pipe it watches, a write to a pipe two sets deep waking a wait on the outer set, a cycle of three sets and one of two refused with `ELOOP`, five sets nested with a pipe in the fifth reported by a wait on the first, a sixth refused with `ELOOP` whether the chain is built from the outermost set in or from the innermost out, and a set held by two paths refused under another when the longer path makes six, a fork's child that closed its copy of the second of five nested sets' numbers refused a sixth set under them and them under a sixth, and its wait on the first leaving the registration to the parent, whose wait reports a write to a pipe in the fifth, two ready descriptors reported in turn by waits with room for one, a one-shot event a wait could not store reported by the next, the registration of a closed eventfd not reporting the one made next at its number, and `epoll_pwait2` with a timeout of 2^31 seconds, and with negative seconds, `EINVAL`. Its edge-triggered rows of the kinds of change check that an AF_UNIX stream end watched for output is not reported again for a byte that comes to it, nor for room a read makes in its own full queue, while one watched for input is reported for each byte, and that a shutdown of the end is an edge of its registration; that each write to a pipe is an edge of its read end, with data in the pipe before it or not; that a listener is not reported again when an accept leaves a connection queued; that a datagram, and a TCP segment over the loopback, that come to a socket watched for output are no edge; and that room a VINTR makes in a full pseudoterminal is an edge of its master watched for output. `tests/musl/timerfdmusl.c` checks a blocking read of an expiration, the interval reported back, a disarmed timer, a nonblocking read, an absolute time already past, a timer waking an epoll wait, and a timer and interval of 200 days reported whole.

The specialized guest targets extend socket coverage: `tests/run packet` checks `poll` and `select` over a packet socket, `tests/run raw` polls a raw socket, `tests/run netif` checks immediate `POLLERR` on an ioctl-only socket, and `tests/run dhcp` drives the in-house DHCP client's libc `poll` waits.
