# Audio

The audio subsystem plays PCM samples through one of two sound devices, Intel High Definition Audio (HDA) or the Sound Blaster 16 (SB16), and falls back to the PC speaker, which sounds a single tone and cannot play samples. It gives the kernel one driver interface, `audio_driver_t` in `audio.h`, and gives programs the console's audio ioctls, through which `audiotest` plays sound, and `/dev/dsp`, OSS's audio device, over a driver's streams, which mixes what every program plays, Doom and `audiotest -d` among them.

## Interfaces

### Selection

The boot calls `init_audio` (`audio.c`) once, unless the command line has `rach2.noaudio`. It tries HDA (`hda_driver`), then the platform's own device from `platform_audio_driver` (`sys/platform.h`), which on a PC is the SB16, then the speaker, whose `init` always succeeds. The first driver whose `init` returns 0 becomes `audio_hw`, the device's driver, and `audio`, the driver programs reach. `audio_set_dispatch` can put another driver in front of the device's as `audio`, which reaches the device through `audio_hw`; `audio_set_dispatch(NULL)` takes it away. The boot installs `vchan_driver` over a device with streams, leaving the speaker direct. The driver's name and `audio_has_real_output` are `audio_hw`'s. The boot logs `audio: ready:` followed by `Intel HD Audio, codec 0x` and the codec's vendor ID, `Sound Blaster 16` or `PC Speaker fallback`; under `rach2.noaudio` it logs `audio: skipped: rach2.noaudio` and `audio` stays `NULL`.

### The driver interface

`audio_driver_t` has `init`, `shutdown`, `start`, `stop`, `is_playing`, `set_callback`, `write`, `set_volume` and `get_volume`. `start(rate, format, channels)` takes a rate in samples per second, `AUDIO_U8` (unsigned 8-bit) or `AUDIO_S16LE` (signed 16-bit little-endian), and one or two channels, and returns 0, -EINVAL for parameters the driver refuses, or -EIO when the device fails. Each driver checks its own: HDA refuses a rate of 0 or above 384000, an unknown format, a channel count other than 1 or 2 and a format its codec cannot play; the SB16 refuses only a rate outside 5000 to 44100 Hz, plays any format other than `AUDIO_S16LE` as unsigned 8-bit and any channel count other than 2 as mono; the speaker refuses nothing. HDA checks before it stops a stream already playing, the SB16 after. There is one stream for the machine. A caller feeds it in one of two ways:

* With a callback set by `set_callback` before `start`, the driver calls it to fill a whole DMA period: once for each of its periods during `start`, three on HDA and two on SB16, then once for each period played. Those later calls may not sleep. On HDA they run in the driver's thread, `khda`, with interrupts on, once the thread runs, and before that and while the scheduler is off, as under the debug shell, in the controller's interrupt or the tick with them off, as HDA controller describes; on the SB16 they run in the device's interrupt. A period on HDA is the frame count nearest 23 ms at the programmed rate, in bytes rounded to the nearest 128 and at most 16384, and on SB16 128 frames for each 11025 Hz of the rate or part of it.
* With no callback, `write` copies bytes into the driver's ring, three periods of the caller's frames on HDA and eight periods on SB16, and returns how many it took. That may be fewer than offered, or 0 when the ring is full, and the caller retries the rest; -EINVAL means the stream is not playing or the buffer is empty, and the speaker's `write` is always -EOPNOTSUPP. The driver's service takes only whole frames, and a period it cannot fill is completed with silence.

`hda_delay_frames` (`hda.h`) and `sb16_delay_frames` (`sb16.h`) give the frames a sample written now waits behind, at the rate the stream was started with: the ring's and the DMA buffer's ahead of the position. They are safe from any context, and each driver's `delay` operation below gives them.

`set_volume` takes a percentage from 0 to 100; the speaker ignores it. HDA and the SB16 keep the level, which `get_volume` gives back, 100 until one is set, and HDA applies it again at each `start`, which sets the amplifiers afresh. The speaker's `get_volume` is NULL.

### Streams

After `get_volume` come the stream operations, all seven or none: `stream_open`, `stream_setup`, `stream_start`, `stream_stop`, `stream_close`, `stream_position` and `delay`. HDA has them for both directions, and the SB16 for playback; the speaker has none. HDA's `delay` is `hda_delay_frames` for the playback stream that has the output, or for the `write` path when given NULL while none has it, and 0 for capture. A stream (`audio_stream_t`) is one direction of the device, `AUDIO_PLAYBACK` or `AUDIO_CAPTURE`, owned by one caller from `stream_open`, which fails with -EBUSY while the direction is taken, to `stream_close`.

* Parameters. The caller asks for a rate, a format, one or two channels, a period in bytes and the number of periods of its own buffer; `stream_setup`, called while the stream is stopped, writes back what the driver grants. A period may come back smaller, never larger, and stays a whole number of frames. The driver also sets `device_periods`, the periods its DMA buffer holds. A setup that fails leaves the driver as it was.
* Buffers. The caller's buffer, `periods` times `period_bytes` in the stream's format, is the caller's; the DMA buffer is the driver's, which may convert between the two. Data crosses only through the caller's `transfer`, which the driver calls to fill or to empty a whole number of frames of its own buffer. A playback transfer always fills what it is asked for, with silence for what the caller lacks, so the device runs on when the caller is late.
* Time. `stream_start` transfers the first `device_periods` periods before it returns; after that the driver transfers one period for each period the device finishes and then calls the caller's `period`. On HDA both run in the driver's thread `khda` once it runs, `transfer` with interrupts on and `period` with them off, and before that and while the scheduler is off in the controller's interrupt or the tick, both with them off; on the SB16 both run in its interrupt; and the first transfers run inside `stream_start`. Neither may sleep or call the driver. `stream_position` is the frames played or recorded since the start, silence among them, read from the device so that it moves within a period, and may be called with interrupts off. A capture stream's `lost` counts the frames recorded since the start that an overrun dropped before the driver handed them over, set before the next `transfer`, so that the frames handed over and those lost together reach the period being recorded. `delay(s)` is the frames the stream gave the driver that have not played yet, in the stream's rate; `delay(NULL)` is the same for the `write` path. `stream_stop` stops at once and drops what the DMA buffer holds.

### Programs

`/dev/dsp` ([dsp(4)](../../../man/man4/dsp.4)), in `dsp.c`, is OSS's audio device over playback and capture streams, there while `audio` has stream operations. An open for writing takes a playback stream of `vchan_driver`, a virtual channel of the mix (see Mixing), so that any number of opens play at once; one for reading takes a virtual reader of the shared capture stream; and one for both takes both, each with a buffer of its own. An open for one direction fails with ENODEV when the driver lacks it; one for both takes the direction the driver has when it lacks the other, as Linux's `snd_pcm_oss_open_file` opens only the directions a PCM has, and fails only when it has neither, giving back what it took. A driver whose device goes calls the stream's `gone`, which wakes the open's writer, reader and drain, as Linux's `snd_card_disconnect` does: a write or read returns what it moved, a drain fails with ENODEV, and after it every call but the close fails with ENODEV and `poll` reports POLLERR and POLLNVAL; the close neither waits nor asks the driver to play. An open sets OSS's defaults, unsigned 8-bit mono at 8000 Hz; the format, channels, rate and fragments are each open's own, set by OSS's ioctls, each of which sets both of the open's directions alike and reads back what was granted playback, or capture in an open for reading, and a change stops the open's streams and empties its buffers. `SETFMT` takes unsigned 8-bit, signed 16-bit, and signed 24-bit in the low three bytes of four (`AFMT_S24_LE`), packed in three (`AFMT_S24_PACKED`) and signed 32-bit (`AFMT_S32_LE`), all little-endian, which `GETFMTS` lists; any other value, a mask of several among them, is a query that keeps the format set, as FreeBSD's does. The wider three have OSS 4's numbers, which Linux's header lacks, ALSA's OSS emulation takes and cmus defines for itself on Linux. The unsigned 24 and 32-bit forms FreeBSD has are left out: OSS 4 has no numbers for them, and FreeBSD's are OSS 4's `AFMT_FLOAT` and `AFMT_S24_PACKED`. The open's buffer is a circle of up to 64 KiB, of at least two periods of 128 to 16384 bytes each: by default four periods of the power of two nearest 23 ms. `write` copies into it through a 256-byte bounce buffer, since the caller's pages may fault and the buffer is under a spin lock the driver's `transfer` takes with interrupts off, and starts the stream once `device_periods` periods are queued, so that the driver's first transfers find data and no silence follows them. A playback buffer holds those periods and at least one more, so it may reach 80 KiB: cmus writes only as much as `GETOSPACE`'s free periods less one, and with its period, the power of two below a 25th of a second's bytes, 16 KiB for 32-bit stereo at 96000 Hz, the four periods HDA's start takes would fill 64 KiB and leave it waiting for room until its close. While the buffer is full a write sleeps on the open's wait queue, which `transfer` wakes when its take leaves a whole period free, as Linux's pointer update wakes a writer once `avail_min` frames are free (`snd_pcm_update_state`, sound/core/pcm_lib.c), and `period` wakes as well; `poll` reports POLLOUT for a whole free period. The mix takes a channel's frames as far ahead of play as the device's buffer reaches, three periods of HDA's, and a channel that joins a mix already running, beside a loopback reader, starts that far behind them, so a writer woken only when a period has played would find its buffer emptied and leave silence in the mix. `transfer` takes whole frames only, so a partial frame waits for the write that completes it. `GETODELAY` is the buffer's bytes plus the channel's `delay`, and `GETOPTR` counts from `stream_position`. `SYNC` and the last close wait until the position passes the last frame of data the driver took, recorded at each transfer, so the silence after it is not waited for.

Capture runs the other way through a buffer laid out as playback's, though not raised to the driver's `device_periods`, since nothing is queued before a start. The first `read`, or a `poll` for POLLIN, starts the capture stream; its `transfer` adds each period the driver hands over, and `period` wakes the readers and pollers. A read copies out through the same bounce buffer and waits until it has every byte it asked for, as FreeBSD's does, so `dd` reads whole blocks; it may end within a frame, since the buffer's tail, which only `transfer` moves, stays on a frame's edge. When the buffer is full, `transfer` keeps the oldest frames and drops the newest whole frames that do not fit, so a reader that falls behind reads in order up to a gap. `GETISPACE` is the bytes ready to read, and `GETIPTR` counts from `stream_position`, the frames the device recorded, dropped frames among them. `SETTRIGGER` starts and stops each direction by its own bit, and `RESET` stops both and empties both buffers.

A program also drives audio with `ioctl` on a console descriptor: `KIOCSOUND` and `KDMKTONE` sound the speaker, and `AUDIO_GET_INFO`, `AUDIO_START`, `AUDIO_STOP`, `AUDIO_WRITE` and `AUDIO_SET_VOLUME` call `audio` (`sys_ioctl` in `sys/syscall.c`). [ioctl(2)](../../../man/man2/ioctl.2) has their arguments and errors. `AUDIO_WRITE` is the write path above; no ioctl sets a callback. `/dev/mixer` ([mixer(4)](../../../man/man4/mixer.4)), in `mixer.c`, is OSS's mixer, there while the driver has a `get_volume`. VOLUME is the hardware master volume. PCM scales only the playback channels opened by the process making the mixer request, before summing, and starts at 100. A channel inherited across fork stays with the process that opened it; its child cannot change that channel by setting its own PCM. A new open takes the level of the same process's existing channels, or 100 when it has none. The console's `AUDIO_SET_VOLUME` sets the master volume too. On a driver with the input controls (`input_caps`, `input_select` and `input_level` in `audio.h`, HDA's alone) it has MIC, MONITOR and LINE, the levels of the microphone jack, the internal microphone and the line in, by FreeBSD's names; RECLEV, which scales each source's level for the converter's gain on that source; IGAIN, the boost of every source's pin; and the record source, one at a time. On a driver with a beep generator (`beep` and `beep_level` in `audio.h`, HDA's on a codec with a beep widget) it has SPEAKER, the generator's level, by FreeBSD's name. A channel is in the device mask only when the driver has its amplifier. Each channel is one level, which reads on both sides; a write sets the louder side. `KIOCSOUND` and `KDMKTONE` drive the speaker directly, whichever driver is active and under `rach2.noaudio` too, and the driver's beep generator beside it where there is one, as PC speaker describes.

`/dev/dsp1`, minor 19, is the same device over the HDMI codec's driver, `hdmi_audio_driver` (see HDMI codec), there while the codec is bound to the display. Its opens take the codec's one stream directly, with no mix, so one open plays at a time and a second fails with EBUSY; it has no capture, so an open for reading fails with ENODEV and one for both plays alone. The stream takes every format of `/dev/dsp`, one or two channels and any rate from 1000 to 192000 Hz, OSS's default among them, and the driver converts it to the DMA's, as Linux's OSS plugins do (see HDMI codec).

[audiotest(1)](../../../man/man1/audiotest.1) plays a square wave through these ioctls, or with `-d` a scale through `/dev/dsp`. It opens `/dev/console` itself, so it works from an SSH session, whose standard descriptors are a pty and not the console. Doom's sound is described in [its Readme](../../../ports/doom/Readme.md).

### /proc/audio

`/proc/audio` (`audio_proc_text` in `sys/audio/audio_proc.c`, which procfs serves) has the active driver's name and the report HDA keeps (`hda_get_report`): the path it chose, the stream's last setup, every widget of the codec's function group with its capabilities, configuration and connections, read at discovery, and each pin's sense word, read at the last `start`. It has the output routing mode and the jacks the driver watches, each with its state, which the jacks' poll keeps up to date, and its readings, and on a codec with a beep widget a `beep` line with its node, the divider sounding, whether a tone turned the output pins on and the tones begun. It has the input path discovery chose and the input stream's last setup, with the frames it handed over and its overruns, which its service keeps up to date. It also has the stream's queue, read live when the file is opened (`hda_read_queue`): the ring's fill and its high water, the delay from a write to its sound, the bytes taken and refused, the periods filled, the underruns, the missed revolutions and the position. The open copies the whole report through `hda_copy_report`, which holds the stream mutex, so that no service of `khda` and no operation of the driver runs during the copy, and `cap_lock` and the stream lock with interrupts off, so that no capture service or interrupt does either, as Linux's status read under `/proc/asound` holds the PCM's `open_mutex` and then the stream lock (sound/core/pcm.c:441, sound/core/pcm_native.c:1036, Linux 6.12.115); it renders the text from that copy, so both streams' counters and the jacks are of one moment and the length procfs measures is the length it writes, even when capture's frame count wraps between the two. Opening it sends no codec verb, so a person reads all of this over SSH while sound plays. [proc(5)](../../../man/man5/proc.5) describes the fields.

The `service` line counts khda calls and cycles spent around their services, converted to microseconds, including nested interrupts. It also retains playback's missed-revolution count across stream starts and the latest miss: its TSC timestamp, time since the preceding service and first pending playback request, modular position movement and buffer duration. The request is stamped by a read of WALCLK when the interrupt or tick first asks for playback; it is invalid after a stream restart until a new request. These counters survive idle suspension, unlike `queue`. They do not use the scheduler's tick charge. [proc(5)](../../../man/man5/proc.5) gives their units and validity.

### The debug shell

The kernel debug shell, described in [run.Readme.md](../../run.Readme.md), passes a line whose first word is `audio` to `audio_debug.c`, which prints to the console and the serial port and not to the kernel log:

* `audio` or `audio info`: the active driver, and whether it plays samples.
* `audio test scale [ms]`: the notes 262, 294, 330, 349 and 392 Hz through a callback, as square waves of signed 16-bit stereo at 48000 Hz, or 44100 Hz on SB16. Each note lasts a fifth of the duration, which is 2000 ms by default, at least 100 and at most 600000, and a rest of three of the driver's periods follows it. The command prints `Done.` when the callback has made the last rest, by which time the device has read the last note. When the device stops asking for frames, it gives up after four times the scale's length and a second, by the timer, and prints `audio: stalled:` with the frames made and the frames of the whole scale.
* `audio test write [ms]`: the same notes through `write`, as unsigned 8-bit mono at 11025 Hz.
* `audio test watch [Hz] [ms] [interval-ms]`: on HDA only, a tone, 440 Hz by default, while the stream's position in its buffer is printed every interval.
* `audio hda dump`, `codec`, `pins` and `stats`: the controller's registers, the codec's widgets, the output pins and the stream's counters, read when the command runs. `/proc/audio` has the widgets, the pins' sense and the counters too, without the shell; `dump` alone has the registers, and `pins` the pins' control, EAPD and selection as they are now.
* `audio hda out [auto|primary|hp|both]`: the output routing. `auto`, the default, follows the headphone jack while a stream plays; a change takes effect at the next poll in `auto` and at the next `start` otherwise.

### /proc/asound

`/proc/asound` has Linux's ALSA files, from `asound.c`, which procfs serves: card 0 is the analog HD Audio controller's while `hda.c` drives it (`hda_card`), and the HDMI codec's card follows it, card 0 when there is no analog card; with both, card 1 is the one `/dev/dsp1` plays, as Linux's OSS emulation ties them. Linux numbers its cards in the order their controllers probe, which on Haswell, whose display controller is 00:03.0, puts HDMI first unless `snd_hda_intel`'s `index` option orders them. `cards` lists each in Linux's form, ` 0 [PCH            ]: HDA-Intel - HDA Intel PCH` and a line with the controller's address and interrupt, with the short name `snd_hda_intel` gives the function: `HDA Intel PCH` for the PCH functions of its table, `HDA Intel` for another Intel one, `HDA NVidia` and otherwise `HD-Audio Generic`; it reads `--- no soundcards ---` while there is none. Each `cardK` has `id`, the name's last word, as Linux's `retrieve_id_from_card_name` takes it; the HDMI card's, from `hdmi_audio_proc_text` in `hda_hdmi.c`, also has one `eld#C.N` for each device entry of each HDMI or DisplayPort pin, C the codec's address and N the entry, in Linux's `snd_hdmi_print_eld_info` form: `monitor_present`, `eld_valid`, the pin, entry and converter, and for a valid ELD the monitor's name, connection type, versions, IDs, latency, speakers and each short audio descriptor. An ELD file is mode 0644: root's write, kept up to 16 KiB and applied at the last close by `hdmi_audio_eld_write`, sets the fields Linux's `snd_hdmi_write_eld_info` sets, one `name hexvalue` line each, and keeps the monitor's name, IDs and versions; the next open of `/dev/dsp1` takes the rates and widths it gives. Where a written value lies past a table of names, the file reads `reserved`, and it prints at most the 16 descriptors there are, where Linux's print indexes past its tables. IGT's `kms_hdmi_inject` reads these files.

## Design

`audio.c`, `audio_debug.c`, `dsp.c`, `/dev/dsp` ([`dsp.h`](dsp.h)), `hda.c`, `mixer.c`, `/dev/mixer`'s ioctls ([`mixer.h`](mixer.h)), `resample.c`, the rate converter ([`resample.h`](resample.h)), and `vchan.c`, the mix ([`vchan.h`](vchan.h)), are shared code, listed in `rach2/sources.mk`; `dma.c`, `sb16.c` and `sys/arch/x86/speaker.c` are the PC's, listed in `sys/arch/x86/sources.mk`. The i686 and x86-64 kernels build all of them.

### The test scale

The scale's callback makes each frame from the count of frames before it, so the notes are the same whenever the device asks for them. A refill that comes late lets the device read again what its buffer has: the periods written last, two on the SB16 and three on HDA, or, while a refill is being written, the rest of the one before them. The rest after each note is three periods long, so that buffer never has frames of two notes, and the device replays one note or a rest. The notes then reach the device in their order however late the refills come, which is what lets `tests/run audio` judge a recording made on any accelerator.

### Mixing

`vchan.c` mixes every playback open of `/dev/dsp` into one playback stream of the device, as FreeBSD's virtual channels do. `vchan_driver` has the stream operations and dispatches the console interface and the hardware controls; each stream opened on it is a channel with its own format, channels, rate and period, and the channels share one stream of `audio_hw`, the mix. The first channel's open takes the device's stream and asks for signed 32-bit stereo at 48000 Hz (`VCHAN_RATE`), which HD Audio codecs have and HDA converts to the codec's rate on one that lacks it, and a period of 1024 frames. HDA grants the 32 bits where every DAC it programs has 24-bit samples, and plays their top 24, as Linux's snd-hda-intel plays `S32_LE` with 24 valid bits to such a codec; elsewhere it grants signed 16-bit. The SB16 grants signed 16-bit, 44100 Hz and 512 frames. The mix plays at the width and rate it is granted. The last channel's close gives the stream back. The console's write path plays on a channel of its own, fed by its callback or a ring of three periods, so its start and stop leave every `/dev/dsp` channel alone. The device's stream runs while a channel runs: the first channel to start starts it, and the last to stop stops it, which drops what its DMA buffer holds.

The device's `transfer` makes the mix in blocks of 256 frames under the mix's spin lock: for each channel that runs it takes the frames the block needs from the channel's `transfer` in one call, after one of a single frame when the channel joins, through a staging buffer, converts them to 24-bit stereo and to the mix's rate, scales them by the channel's PCM level and adds them to a 32-bit sum, held within 2^30 so that no channel's sample added overflows it. The sum is clipped once, to 24 bits, and stored at the device's width: in the top 24 bits of 32 over a zero byte, or narrowed to 16 bits, rounded to the nearest, a half up. To 24 bits, unsigned 8-bit is moved to signed and widened, 8 and 16 bits exactly, and a 32-bit sample keeps its top 24 bits, as a codec of 24 bits plays it. The top byte of 24 bits in four is not read. Nothing is dithered, as neither Linux's kernel nor FreeBSD's dithers by default. On the Lenovo, whose codec's PCM size and rates word is 0x000A0560, 16 and 24 bits, the mix plays 24 bits. A channel started while the device runs joins at the next block made, after the periods the device holds. A channel stopped leaves the mix at once, but what the mix took from it stays in the sum the device holds and plays out with the others.

Each channel converts its 24-bit stereo frames to the mix's rate through a resampler of its own, which Rate conversion describes. The frames a block reads are known before it is made (`resample_need`), so one `transfer` takes them; at the mix's own rate the resampler copies, and a channel reaches the device unchanged. A channel's rate is granted from 1000 Hz to four times the mix's, at most 192000 Hz, which bounds the staging buffer at 1026 frames of up to 8 bytes. A converted channel is heard 59 frames of the lower of its rate and the mix's after the mix takes it, the resampler's lag, and its count of frames played leaves those out.

A channel's period is the one asked for, cut to the frames one period of the mix plays at its rate, so that a `period` call is due at each period of the mix; its `device_periods` covers the frames the device's start takes from a channel that starts the mix, up to 64 KiB, the largest buffer `dsp.c` lays out. The device's `period` counts the mix's frames played; at each it moves every channel's count of frames played on to them, the frames of the mix since the channel joined times its rate over R, whole and fraction, and calls the channel's `period` once for each of its periods passed. `stream_position` makes the same count from the device's own position, so that it moves within a period, and holds it to the frames the channel gave, which a channel faster than the mix is ahead of by the fraction of a frame of the mix. `delay` is the frames the channel gave less those played.

Capture also shares one device stream. Its only reader may configure the stopped input directly, asking it for signed 16 bits when the reader's format is wider; while several readers are open the hardware keeps its settings and each reader converts from them to its own format, channels and rate, through 24 bits as the mix does: a 24-bit format has the 24 bits, a 32-bit one has them on top of a zero byte, and an 8 or 16-bit one has them rounded as the mix rounds. So a recording of 16 bits reaches a reader of 16 bits unchanged, and a wider one with the 16 bits on top of zeros; a mix of 24 bits under loopback reaches a 24 or 32-bit reader whole. Each reader converts the rate through a resampler of its own, fed each input frame as it comes; its frames lag the input by the resampler's span, and its position, counted from the device's progress, does not. A reader that starts while the input runs has its resampler's first frame delayed to the next time of a reader that started with the input (`resample_delay`), so that two readers at one rate read the same samples from the frames both took. Each reader has a staging period and its own `/dev/dsp` ring, so a slow reader drops only its own frames. The first running reader starts the input and the last running reader stops it; the last close releases it. A reader that starts while the input runs skips the frames the device recorded before it joined and has not handed over yet: from where the frames handed over and lost end on the device's timeline to the device's position. A loss after it joins counts against what it skips, so it skips no frame recorded after it joined. Position counts the device's progress at each reader's rate, including frames its ring drops.

The boot option `rach2.audio.loopback` selects the clipped playback mix as capture. A silent virtual playback channel keeps the device clock running while any reader runs, even on an SB16 with no input. The capture path gets the same samples as the device's playback transfer, before the hardware master amplifier, at the mix's rate. It uses neither QEMU's input backend nor a physical microphone. The option is for the loopback cases of `machine.audio`.

### Rate conversion

`resample.c` converts stereo frames of 24 bits from one rate to another, for the mix, for each capture reader and for HDA's write path and stream. It is a polyphase windowed-sinc filter in integer arithmetic, written after FreeBSD's `feeder_rate.c` (BSD license; no code taken), since the kernel has no floating point and no 64-bit division. The filter is a Kaiser-windowed sinc whose band is that of the lower of the two rates: it keeps what lies below 0.90 of that rate's Nyquist frequency and is designed for 90 dB from 1.00 of it, so a rate converted down folds nothing into the band and one converted up leaves no images above it. `tools/audio/mkresample.c` writes its coefficients, `resample_coef.h`: the sinc sampled 256 times between two zero crossings, 58 crossings each side, in Q30, as FreeBSD's `feeder_rate_mkfilter.awk` writes its tables; the output is committed, since the kernel also builds on Rach2, and [tools/Readme.md](../../../tools/Readme.md) says how to write it again. Each output frame's coefficients are interpolated linearly between the table's entries at its fractional time, so one table serves every ratio.

A converter (`resampler_t`) holds a window of input frames, two for each crossing at the ratio, doubled in its array so that the window is always one run, and a rational phase in 1/out of an input frame, which never drifts. An input frame is low / in of one of the filter's frames, low being the lower rate, so a conversion down stretches the filter over more input frames and scales its sum by low / in, the product made in two halves since it passes 64 bits. The output frame is the window's centre frame plus the filtered differences from it, so that a constant comes out exactly whatever a phase's coefficients sum to; it is held to 24 bits, since the filter overshoots a step at full scale by about 9%. The window starts as silence, and output frame k lies at input time k * in / out less the span, the frames the filter reaches each side: 59 frames of the lower rate, so each frame put is heard that much later, and `resample_held` gives the frames put that wait in the window past the next output frame's time. Each output frame takes the input frames up to its time, as a converter with no filter would, so a caller takes no frames ahead at its start. At equal rates the converter copies each frame unchanged, so that every path stays a bit-exact copy. A ratio above 8 to 1 filters the band of the input's rate over 8, and so lets what lies between that band and the output's Nyquist frequency alias; the window holds at most 930 frames, and its array, of twice that, takes about 15 KiB of each channel, reader and of HDA's driver.

The host test counts 464 multiplies for each stereo frame of a conversion from 96 kHz to 48 kHz, 231 taps of two products each, since every position of a conversion by a whole ratio down falls on an entry of the table and is read without an interpolation; and 327 from 44.1 kHz and 311 from 8 kHz to 48 kHz, whose taps add an interpolation's product wherever the output time falls between two entries. `tools/audio/resamplebench.sh` times the conversion built as the kernel is, without the optimizer, beside FreeBSD's `feeder_rate` loops over the same filter ([tools/Readme.md](../../../tools/Readme.md)). On a Ryzen 9 7950X3D the x86-64 code takes 15.5 ms for each second of the 96 kHz conversion and 11.6 ms at 44.1 kHz, where `feeder_rate`'s polyphase loop takes 18.9 and 9.7 ms; the i686 code takes 29.8 and 22.1 ms, where it takes 25.5 and 13.3. Code built without the optimizer keeps every local in memory, so each step of a loop waits for the store of the step before; `resample_side` declares its sums and pointers `register`, which GCC keeps in registers even so. The mix makes each block of 256 frames with interrupts off under its own lock, `mix.lock`, and restores the caller's interrupt state between blocks; in `khda` it runs without the big lock. During refills this lets interrupts in only in HDA's thread `khda`; the SB16's interrupt, and HDA's interrupt or tick before `khda` runs or while the scheduler is off, keep them off. The first fills run inside `stream_start`, as Streams describes. Another processor's tick that finds the big lock held returns at once ([the PC platform](../arch/x86/Readme.md)), so the conversion takes time from the processor that makes it alone. Before both changes a 96 kHz channel on the Lenovo's Core i3-4030U, whose four processors are two cores of two threads, took 25 to 31% of a counting loop's turns on each of the four, since every processor's tick waited for the mix; the conversion then took about a quarter of the processor that made it, some nine times the Ryzen's 3% of the time. It has not been measured there since.

### HDA controller

`hda_init` takes the first PCI function of class 0x04, subclass 0x03 that `is_hdmi_controller` does not match (Intel's display-only controllers on Haswell and Broadwell, 0x0A0C, 0x0C0C, 0x0D0C and 0x160C, and every Nvidia and AMD function), or the first such function if it matches all of them, and logs any other as `audio: not driven:` with its IDs and address. It maps 16 KiB of BAR0, refusing a BAR the kernel cannot address, enables bus mastering, moves the function to power state D0, resets the controller and waits up to 500 ms for a codec to appear in `STATESTS`. It sets up the CORB and RIRB rings of 256 entries, but sends every verb through the Immediate Command Interface (`hda_verb`). The stream is the first output stream, whose descriptor follows the input streams `GCAP` counts, with stream tag 1. Its buffer descriptor list has three entries of one period each, which Formats and the write path sizes, each raising an interrupt on completion.

`hda_service_stream` reads the play position from the DMA position buffer, or from the stream's `LPIB` register when the controller refused the buffer or, in the first 20 ms of the first stream after the controller was set up, `LPIB` moved and the buffer did not (`audio hda stats` prints those readings); it refills every period played since its last call, as Formats and the write path describes. It and the input stream's service run in the driver's kernel thread, `khda`, which the first program's start makes (`hda_thread_start`), under `SCHED_FIFO` at priority 50, Linux's for an interrupt's thread (`sched_set_fifo`, kernel/sched/syscalls.c:873, from `irq_thread`, kernel/irq/manage.c:1310), so that a refill waits behind no ordinary program. The thread gives up the big lock at its start and holds the stream mutex, `stream_mutex`, for each service; every operation of `hda_driver` that changes the device takes it too, in process context, so no operation runs in the middle of a service, on this processor or another, as Linux's PCM operations hold the runtime's `buffer_mutex` (sound/core/pcm_native.c:715 of 6.12). It is row 8 of the lock order ([the process subsystem](../proc/Readme.md), Locking). The refill of a stream or from the callback, the mix's `transfer` and the rate conversion among them, runs with interrupts on, as Linux's writer copies into the ring with them on, having dropped the stream's lock (`__snd_pcm_lib_xfer`, sound/core/pcm_lib.c:2380-2387); the write path's refill from its ring, whose delay counts the ring itself, runs under the stream lock. The services take the stream spinlock, `stream_lock`, only around the reads and updates of the position and the period state the delay is computed from, `hw_period_last`, `stream_in`, `stream_taken` and `play_held`, the frames the resampler held at the end of the last fill, and around a stream's `period` calls; capture's service takes `cap_lock` around `cap_next` and its counts, and the mix takes its own lock for each block (Mixing). The same lock keeps the interrupt's marks and the services asked for. The position and the delay, which a caller may read with interrupts off, take only the stream lock, so a reader on another processor sees the state before a fill under way or after it, never part of it. As a kernel thread, `khda` is not switched by an interrupt, which only asks for a switch (`no_tick_switch`, [the process subsystem](../proc/Readme.md)), and it takes the switch asked for at the service point after each service, with the mutex given back (`sched_service_point`). A service asked for during the last one runs without a sleep (`wq_wait_event_idle` finds its condition true at once). `/proc/PID/schedstat` of `khda` gives its time. Three paths ask for the services (`hda_service_kick`): the handler on the controller's interrupt, which runs without the big lock (`ISR_UNLOCKED`) and, under the stream lock, reads `INTSTS`, clears each stream's status, marks the stream and stamps its request with the wall clock, and wakes the thread after the lock, as Linux's `azx_interrupt` acknowledges each stream and calls `snd_pcm_period_elapsed`, which wakes the writer, and leaves the copy into the ring to the writer's own call (sound/pci/hda/hda_controller.c:1056 and 1070, sound/hda/hdac_controller.c:684, sound/core/pcm_lib.c:232 and 2274, Linux 6.12.115); `hda_tick`, which the boot processor's timer handler calls at each tick from the part that takes no big lock (`timer_own_work`, `kernel.c`), and which asks for a stream's service only until the stream's first interrupt after a start, and afterwards only while more than a period and a half has passed since its last interrupt (`hda_irq_late`): half a period after the next was due, and a period and a half before the DMA, three periods long, would play again the period that interrupt's missed service did not refill; and `hda_beep`, which, while `khda` runs, asks for the beep's service for each tone or stop that `speaker_tone` or the tick's `speaker_tick` hands it (PC speaker). The handler reads the stream's position and the wall clock, as Linux's position check does from its interrupt. An interrupt that finds the position in the second half of a period, less than a period and a quarter after the boundary last taken as passed, came early, short of the next boundary (`hda_irq_early`): it leaves the stream pending (`play_irq_pending`, `cap_irq_pending`) with the period that boundary begins (`play_irq_period`, `cap_irq_period`), and the tick asks for the stream at each call until a service finds the position in that period or past it, which clears the flag and takes the boundary as passed. Any other interrupt stands for a boundary the position has passed, which it takes as passed, and clears the flag: one in the first half of a period, and one more than a period and a quarter after the boundary last passed, which came late, in either half. So a service that `khda` runs late, in the second half of the period after the one its interrupt ended, leaves nothing pending when no interrupt came between; when the next interrupt has come early meanwhile, the stream stays pending for the period that interrupt's boundary begins, and refills do not fall a period behind. A start takes the wall clock at the start as the boundary last passed, and a start and a stop clear the flags. So a stream whose interrupts come is asked for by them, and by the tick only after an interrupt that came early, and the tick reads no stream register on a quiet tick. Linux updates a stream the same way: `azx_interrupt` calls the controller's `stream_update` for each stream that raised one (`sound/pci/hda/hda_controller.c:1056-1094` of 6.12.115), whose position check (`azx_position_check` and `azx_position_ok`, `sound/pci/hda/hda_intel.c:612-625` and `643-709`) refuses a position more than half a period into its period while less than a period and a quarter has passed since the last update it accepted (`:689-692`), and takes the wall clock of each one it accepts as the next one's start (`:693`; the stream's start sets the first, `sound/hda/hdac_stream.c:137`). It calls `snd_pcm_period_elapsed` when the position has passed the period's boundary, and otherwise marks the stream `irq_pending` and queues `azx_irq_pending_work`, which checks again every millisecond, and clears the flag and calls `snd_pcm_period_elapsed` once the position has passed it (`:718-757`, the clear and the call at `:742-746`). Linux also ignores an interrupt less than two thirds of a period after the update it last accepted (`:659-661`); here such an interrupt asks for a service, which fills only the periods the position has passed. Intel's ICH and PCH controllers have `bdl_pos_adj` 1 (`:1758-1760`), so the deferred check is on there. For a stream it asks for, the tick wakes the thread only when the stream's position has passed into a period the service has not seen; it reads the marks under the stream lock, and the input stream's position and state under `cap_lock` and the output stream's under the stream lock (`hda_service_due`), as Linux's pending work reads them under the bus's `reg_lock` (`sound/pci/hda/hda_intel.c:735-753`), and does no service itself while the thread runs. Capture's service waits for `cap_lock`, whose other holders, the tick's look among them, hold it only a moment; no second service runs beside it (`hda_serve`). The handler is on the controller's PCI interrupt line, registered when the line is 1 to 15, or on a message-signalled interrupt (MSI), when `isr_msi_ready` reports the local APIC ready, which `rach2.nomsi` prevents, and the controller accepts a vector, logged as `audio: interrupts: messages on vector N` (a controller that does not gives the vector back, with the handler). Before the thread is made, and while the scheduler is off, as under the debug shell, the handler and the tick run the services themselves under the big lock, the tick at each call by the same rule: each tries for the lock and, when another processor holds it, leaves the marks it set to a later tick. The tick also asks for the jacks' poll every `TICK_HZ / 4` ticks, when a jack is watched or capture's route is still to be programmed, and for the beep's service while a divider handed to it by a tone or a stop is still to be sent or the pins a beep opened are due to be turned off (Jacks, PC speaker); the thread runs them after the streams' services, with interrupts on, as Linux polls the jacks from a delayed work (`hda_jackpoll_work`, `sound/pci/hda/hda_codec.c:645-659`) and sends the beep's verbs from a work (`snd_hda_generate_beep`, `sound/pci/hda/hda_beep.c:45-51`, queued at `:118`), and the tick, while the thread runs, sends no verb. The jacks' clock counts the ticks `kernel_ticks` counted since the tick's last call, and the beep's silence is timed from the tick at which the generator last fell silent. The service fills nothing when no period has ended, so an extra call costs a register read, and a machine whose interrupts never arrive still plays from the tick. `/proc/interrupts` lists the handler as `hda`. Shutdown stops playback, disables controller interrupts and asserts reset; the registered handler ignores any later interrupt without reading MMIO. Repeated shutdown does nothing.

Capture uses the first input stream, descriptor 0, with stream tag 2, its own descriptor list of up to 32 entries and its own DMA buffer of up to 64 KiB, so that it runs with or without playback. `hda_capture_setup` grants the rate the ADC has nearest the one asked for, and a period of the frames asked for or fewer, rounded down to the descriptors' 128 bytes in the ADC's 16-bit frames and at most 16384 bytes of them; the buffer is as many periods as fit, at most 32. The ADC records 16-bit samples in its own channels, two on every codec seen so far, and `hda_capture_convert` converts each period to the reader's format and channel count, two channels to one as their mean; no rate is converted. `hda_service_capture` runs where playback's service runs, before it, as the handler acknowledges the input stream's bit in `INTSTS` before the output stream's, under `cap_lock`; the tick asks for it by the output stream's rule, until the input stream's first interrupt and while its next is late. It reads the input stream's position as playback reads its own and hands the reader, by `transfer` and then `period`, each period the position has passed. The device overruns when it comes round to a period before the service hands it over: either the position has passed the period's start again, or the wall clock shows a whole revolution the position cannot. Then the service counts an overrun and hands over the newest revolution, every period but the one the device writes. `stream_position` counts every frame the device recorded, lost ones among them, and `lost` the frames lost. A start follows Linux's order and keeps what the converter's start sends from the reader: it powers the function group and the ADC, programs the route with the ADC's amplifier muted, gives the ADC the stream's tag and, 1 ms later, its format, as Linux's `snd_hda_codec_setup_stream` does, waits `HDA_CAPTURE_SETTLE_US`, 10 ms, unmutes the amplifier, waits 10 ms more and only then runs the descriptor, which a reset has put at position 0, so that the first frame handed over was recorded after the unmute. A start whose codec refuses a verb of the path, a power, format, stream tag or route verb, fails with -EIO and leaves the stream stopped, its descriptor halted, the ADC untagged and the pin's input off, for the next start to program again. A stop clears the descriptor's run bit and its status, mutes the ADC's amplifier and takes the stream's tag from the ADC, and leaves the pin's input on.

`hda_verb` holds `verb_lock` from its wait for an idle interface to its read of the response, and keeps interrupts off only while it reads or writes a register: between polls it waits a microsecond with the caller's interrupts, so a verb from a system call does not hold off the tick. A verb waits at most 1 ms by the wall clock, or 1000 polls when the clock does not run, for the interface and then for the response; then it returns -ETIMEDOUT, and -EIO when the codec completed the command without a response. The tick may stop a system call's verb half-way, and spinning there would never end, so a caller in the tick or an interrupt passes `HDA_VERB_TRY`, which returns -EBUSY at once when the lock is held or a command is still under way. A caller treats a failed verb's answer as unknown; `hda_send_verb`, which the rest of the driver uses outside the tick, gives 0 for it.

### HDMI codec

`hda_hdmi.c` drives Haswell's display audio controller, 8086:0A0C, 0C0C or 0D0C, and its HDMI codec, as Linux's `snd_hda_intel` and `patch_hdmi.c` do; `hda.c` leaves these functions to it. `hdmi_audio_init` finds the function at boot, maps 16 KiB of BAR0 and registers the codec's side of the audio component (`acomp.h`); nothing more happens until the Intel display driver registers its side, since the controller and codec are in the display's power well. At the bind the driver takes the display's power, resets the controller, clears the low bits of `TCSEL` and the no-snoop bit, sets the BCLK in `EM4` and `EM5` from the CD clock, as Linux's `snd_hdac_i915_set_bclk`, and finds the codec on the link. The codec's audio function has digital converters and HDMI or DisplayPort pins, each with three device entries, as `patch_hdmi.c` makes them; the vendor widget's bits show every pin and converter and allow DisplayPort 1.2, and the pins' outputs are enabled and unmuted. Each entry's ELD comes from the display through the component's `get_eld` and is parsed as Linux's `snd_hdmi_parse_eld`; an entry is valid when its ELD parses with at least one audio descriptor. The bind then gives the power back, logs `hdmi-audio: ready:` with the codec's ID, address and pin count, and registers `/dev/dsp1`. The display's notification of a port reads that port's ELD again.

The stream takes the display's power from its open to its close, and the controller and codec are set up again at each first take, since the power well may have gone off between. An open binds the first converter to the first valid entry's pin and limits the rates and widths to what the sink's descriptors allow; a stream on no pin plays to none, and a monitor that comes later is given it, as Linux's `hdmi_present_sense` does. The setup grants the stream's format, one or two channels and its rate held between 1000 and 192000 Hz, as Linux's OSS layer does through its plugins. The DMA plays two channels at the allowed rate nearest the stream's, the higher on a tie, in 16 bits or, for a stream of more than 16 where the converter and sink take 24, in 24 bits in 32; its period covers the stream period's time, in multiples of 128 bytes up to 16 KiB, and the DMA buffer holds 40 ms in 2 to 256 periods, at most 48 KiB, fewer when the stream bytes they take would pass 64 KiB. A stream in the DMA's own form is copied; any other is converted at each refill, unsigned 8-bit moved to signed, each sample taken to 24 bits, mono to both channels, and a rate changed by linear interpolation with a 13-bit weight, as Linux's `rate.c` plugin does, so that OSS's default, unsigned 8-bit mono at 8000 Hz, plays on a sink of 32000 Hz. The start selects the pin's converter, moves another valid entry that selected it to a free one, sets the display's clocks for the rate with `sync_audio_rate`, writes the audio infoframe, channel 1 and 2's slots and the channel count, turns IEC958 on, and sets the converter's stream tag 1 and format. The descriptor's interrupt is off: `hdmi_audio_tick`, after `hda_tick` in the timer, reads `LPIB` and refills each period the controller has played, so the tick runs every 4 ms while a stream plays. A tick late by a whole lap of the ring or more, which `LPIB` alone cannot show, counts the laps from the ticks since the last, as Linux's `snd_pcm_update_hw_ptr0` counts jiffies, so the position and the refill do not lose them; `stream_position` only reads, since a wait's condition calls it, and the refill and `period` run only in the tick. The display's going stops the stream, gives back the display's power it held, and calls the stream's `gone`, so that `/dev/dsp1`'s waiters wake; the stream cannot start again, and its close gives nothing more back.

### Codec discovery

`hda_find_output` takes the first codec address in `STATESTS` that answers the Vendor ID parameter and is not an HDMI codec (Intel device IDs 0x2800 to 0x28FF and 0x29FB, any Nvidia or AMD codec), or the first that answers if none qualifies. It finds the first audio function group (AFG) under the root node, powers it, and sets every codec GPIO as a high output, since some laptops enable the speaker amplifier through one. Every codec starts in the `auto` output mode, which Jacks describes.

Each output-capable pin is classified by the default device in its configuration default; a pin whose port has no physical connection serves only as the last fallback. The primary pin is the first speaker, else line out, else headphone, else the first output-capable pin. Its connection list is searched from the entry the firmware selected, then in order, for a DAC (an audio output widget) or a mixer or selector whose own list has a DAC, again preferring its current selection. A headphone pin, kept for routing, must connect directly to a DAC. Without a path `init` fails; the driver never substitutes an unrelated DAC. Before it queries a node from a list, `hda_connection_caps` requires the NID to be one a direct verb can address (127 or less) and inside the AFG.

`hda_read_connections` reads the list length (seven bits) and entry form from the Connection List Length parameter. A short entry has eight bits with a seven-bit NID, a long entry sixteen bits with a fifteen-bit NID, and the Get Connection List Entry verb is sent with the index of the first entry of each 32-bit response: multiples of four for short entries, of two for long. A range entry stands for every NID from the one after its predecessor through its own. The whole list is refused when a range comes first or does not exceed its predecessor, an NID is zero, or the expansion exceeds 256 entries, the most an eight-bit Connection Select index reaches. Discovery records the chosen entry's index in the expanded list, and `start` sends that index with Connection Select. The verbs and entry forms are defined in the [Intel HDA specification](https://www.intel.com/content/dam/www/public/us/en/documents/product-specifications/high-definition-audio-specification.pdf).

`hda_find_input` then chooses the input path from the widgets the report holds, sending no verb. A pin is an input when its caps say it takes input and its port is connected; the internal mic is the first fixed microphone pin, the jack mic the first microphone in a jack, and capture records the internal mic, else the jack mic, else the first line in. The ADC is the first, in node order, whose connections reach that pin directly or through one mixer or selector, and of those the first that reaches the other microphone too, so that a switch between the two needs only the ADC's Connection Select. On the Lenovo G50-70 this is ADC 19, which reads the internal mic 26 on its connection 1 and the jack mic 25 on its connection 2, as Linux's layout has it. Each source, the jack mic, the internal mic and the line in, has a gain, the ADC's input amplifier on its connection, and a boost, its pin's own input amplifier, which start at their 0 dB steps, the amplifiers' offsets, but for the internal mic's boost, which starts at the step nearest +24 dB (`HDA_INT_MIC_BOOST_DB`). Linux's driver leaves the ADC muted at its lowest step and the boost at 0 dB, `alsactl init` sets the capture volume to 75% and leaves the boost, and PipeWire, at its default source volume of 100%, sets the whole path to its top, +6 and +36 dB on the G50-70. There speech recorded at 0 dB peaks near -34 dBFS, so the top would clip it; at +24 dB it peaks near -10 dBFS. A start sets the gain of the source recorded and mutes the ADC's other connections. The pin gets In Enable, and a microphone pin its bias, VREF 80% where its caps have it, else 50%, else 100%, and the source's boost. The input controls set them for `/dev/mixer`, as a percentage of the amplifier's steps, rounded, with 0 a mute where the amplifier can mute: `input_level` sets a gain or a boost, and `input_select` routes another source the ADC reaches, as `hda_input_route` finds it. `input_select` programs the route at once (`hda_input_program`), turning the input of the pin left off, with the ADC's amplifier muted while the input stream is stopped; `input_level` programs it at once while the stream runs, and is kept for the next start otherwise. The microphone jack moves the source too, as Jacks describes. As Linux's codec init does, `hda_init` makes the path ready with the ADC muted (`hda_input_ready`), so that the source's pin has its input and bias on long before a capture, and a digital mic its clock. A machine whose PCI subsystem is in `hda_inv_dmic_machines`, the G50-70 (17AA:3978) alone, has an internal mic of two digital mics with the right one inverted, which cancel when mixed: as Linux's `CXT_FIXUP_STEREO_DMIC` does, its right channel is muted on the ADC, and the service copies the left channel into the right, so a reader in stereo gets two identical channels.

### Formats and the write path

Each DAC's capabilities are its Supported PCM Size, Rates and Supported Stream Formats parameters, read from the DAC when its Format Override bit is set and from the AFG otherwise. `hda_common_pcm` intersects the primary DAC's mask with the headphone DAC's when the two differ, since `start` programs both converters whichever pin is enabled, and gives none when either lacks PCM. Rate bits 0 to 11 are 8000, 11025, 16000, 22050, 32000, 44100, 48000, 88200, 96000, 176400, 192000 and 384000 Hz, and `hda_rates` has the stream format word of each.

`hda_start` refuses, before it stops the current stream, a codec with no path, a rate of 0 or above 384000, an unknown format, a channel count other than 1 or 2, and a format the DACs do not support. With a callback the stream has the requested rate, format and channels exactly, since nothing converts the samples. The write path always plays signed 16-bit stereo at `hda_select_hw_rate`: the closest supported rate at or below the request, or the lowest supported rate when all are higher. `ring_render_to_hw_buffer` converts unsigned 8-bit or signed 16-bit input, keeps a stereo frame's two channels and writes a mono sample to both, and converts the rate through `hda_rs`, the resampler the write path and a stream share. On an underrun it emits silence and holds the resampler, so the silence consumes none of the frames queued later.

A period is the frame count nearest 23 ms (`HDA_PERIOD_MS`) at the programmed rate, in bytes rounded to the nearest 128, the alignment of a descriptor's address, from 128 to 16384: 4096 bytes, 23.2 ms, at 44100 Hz in 16-bit stereo, 4480 at 48000 Hz, and 16384, 21.3 ms, at 192000 Hz. The buffer is three periods, and `start` fills all three, with silence on the write path. The write path's ring holds three periods of the caller's frames, 768 bytes for 11025 Hz unsigned 8-bit mono, up to its array of 32768 bytes, which caps it below three periods only above 96 kHz stereo. A sample written now therefore waits at most six periods, 139 ms at 44100 Hz. Three periods in the ring take a writer's burst of a period beside the period the service takes at once; a writer of a tic of Doom's, 28.6 ms, at a time needs three, and two would not hold it.

A stream of `audio.h`, such as `/dev/dsp`'s, has the output stream alone from `stream_open` to `stream_close`: while it does, `start` and `write` return -EBUSY and `stop` leaves it playing, and `stream_open` returns -EBUSY while the write path or a callback plays. `hda_play_setup` grants unsigned 8-bit and signed 16-bit as asked, and a wider format `AUDIO_S32LE` when every DAC has 24 bits (bit 19 of their PCM size and rates word, `hda_common_pcm`), signed 16-bit otherwise, the period asked for keeping its frames. A stream of 32 bits plays as 24 bits in a 32-bit container, stream format word bits 6:4 at 3, each sample of the DMA buffer the stream's whole; any other plays as signed 16-bit. The console's write path and its callback stay at 16 bits. It plays in stereo, at its own rate when every DAC has it, so 44.1 and 48 kHz play exactly on a codec that has them, and otherwise at the lowest rate they have above it, or the highest below, through `hda_rs` (`hda_play_rate`), which filters the top 24 bits of a 32-bit sample and gives them back over a zero byte. Its rate is granted up to four times the rate played. The DMA's periods are the write path's, about 23 ms; the stream's period is the one the caller asks for, cut to the frames of the stream one DMA period plays, and `device_periods` covers the frames a start takes for three DMA periods. Each refill takes the frames it needs from `transfer` in chunks of 256 frames of the DMA's, through the 8 KiB `hda_stage`. The driver counts the frames of the stream each DMA period was filled with: `delay` is those of the periods ahead of the one playing, the unplayed part of that one, in proportion, and those the resampler holds past the time of its next frame (`resample_held`), and `stream_position` is the frames taken less that delay, never going back. After its refills the service calls the stream's `period` once for each period of the stream the position has passed, so a small period is signalled in a burst at each DMA period's end. A missed revolution silences the rest of the period playing as on the write path, but the stream's frames are the caller's and play late instead of being dropped.

`hda_service_stream` finds the period playing from the position, and fills, in order, each period from the one playing at its last call up to the one before this one, since those have been played; the periods ahead of the one playing hold samples not yet played. The position cannot show a whole revolution of the buffer between two calls, after which the device has played every other period again. The wall clock, `WALCLK` at 24 MHz, can: when the time since the last call exceeds the time the position moved by a revolution less half a period, the service counts the revolutions in `missed`, silences the rest of the period playing from the position, empties the ring, whose samples were due while the stream went round and count as dropped, and fills the other periods afresh. A controller whose wall clock does not run never shows a missed revolution. `hda_delay_frames` adds the ring's frames and those the resampler holds to the DMA buffer's from the position to the start of the period playing at the last service, converted to the caller's rate by the ratio of the rates (`hda_rate_step`), and `/proc/audio`'s `delay-ms` is that count in milliseconds. A missed revolution starts the write path's resampler again from silence.

`start` then programs the stream format, each DAC's converter format and stream tag, the mixer or selector, the primary and headphone pins' Connection Select, EAPD on every pin that has it, and each amplifier at its maximum gain, and then the level set, when it is below 100. It reads every pin's sense and turns the output pins on for the output mode, as Jacks describes. The report records the requested and programmed rates and the format words read back from the stream descriptor and each DAC; `/proc/audio`'s stream line ends with `bits`, the sample size of the stream descriptor's word. `stop` clears the stream's run bit, mutes the primary DAC, turns the output pins and every pin's EAPD off and empties the ring, and the report keeps the last setup. `set_volume` scales the percentage to each amplifier's steps on the DACs, mixer and pins, and mutes at 0.

### Jacks

HDA watches two jacks: the headphone pin, when it is not the primary pin and has a DAC, and the jack mic `hda_find_input` chose, each when its pin caps have presence detect (bit 2), its port is a jack and its configuration default's Jack Detect Override (bit 8) is clear. On the G50-70 they are pins 22 and 25, the two halves of the headset jack on its left side. The driver reads no unsolicited response; it polls the jacks instead (`hda_poll_jacks`), every `TICK_HZ / 4` ticks, a quarter second, in the service the tick asks for, which `khda` runs, with Get Pin Sense, after Execute Pin Sense on a pin whose caps require a trigger (bit 1). Its verbs go through `hda_verb` with `HDA_VERB_TRY`, so a reading that finds the verb lock held or a command under way, or whose verb fails, is unknown: it counts as failed and changes nothing, and in particular is not taken for an unplug. A reading that differs from a jack's state changes it only when the next reading agrees, so a plug on its way in that touches the contacts and leaves them moves nothing, and a change takes effect a quarter to a half second after the plug moves. A start reads every pin's sense once (`hda_sense_pins`) and takes the jacks' states from it at once.

In the `auto` output mode the headphone pin plays, with Out Enable and HP Enable, and the primary pin is off while the headphone jack has a plug; otherwise the primary pin plays, with Out Enable, and the headphone pin is off (`hda_route_output`). A start routes so, and while a stream plays a poll that finds the routing and the jack's state apart moves the output, with a Set Pin Widget Control verb to each pin. The modes `primary`, `hp` and `both`, which `audio hda out` sets, keep their pins whatever the jack. A stop turns both output pins and EAPD off, so the amplifiers are off between streams, and the next start turns them on again.

A change of the microphone jack moves capture between the internal mic and the jack mic (`hda_follow_mic`): to the jack mic when a plug goes in and back to the internal mic when it comes out, as the mixer's record source would, while the source recorded is one of the two and the ADC reaches the other. The route is programmed at once, the input of the pin left turned off and the other's turned on with its bias, with the ADC's amplifier muted while the input stream is stopped. A source the mixer chose stays until the jack next changes.

Code outside the jacks' and the beep's services that programs the pins, a start or stop of either stream, a tone sent while `khda` does not run and the input controls, holds the jacks meanwhile (`hda_jack_hold`), a count kept under `jack_lock`. The services, which `khda` runs, or the tick while it does not, hold no spinlock while they poll or set pins: they take `jack_lock` with a try only to read the count (`hda_jacks_held`), and set no pin while anything holds the jacks, so a service and a system call never set a pin in turn. While `khda` runs, the operations that hold the jacks also wait for the stream mutex the thread holds through each service. What a failed verb left undone, the next poll does again.

### Sound Blaster 16

`sb16_init` probes ports 0x220, 0x240, 0x260 and 0x280 for a DSP that answers a reset with 0xAA and reports major version 4 or later; a base whose data port reads 0xFF after the reset, as an ISA port nothing decodes does, is given up at once, so a machine without the card spends 100 reads of port 0x80 on each base, 400 in all, where it spent 40,400, then selects IRQ 5 and DMA channels 1 and 5 through mixer registers 0x80 and 0x81. `sb16_start` refuses a rate outside 5000 to 44100 Hz and plays unsigned 8-bit samples on channel 1 or signed 16-bit samples on channel 5, mono or stereo. A double buffer of one page in the kernel's `.bss` runs in auto-initialize DMA. Each half is a period of 128 frames for each 11025 Hz of the rate or part of it: 11.6 ms at 11025, 22050 and 44100 Hz, and at most 25.6 ms, at 5000 Hz. The DSP interrupts at the end of each period, and `sb16_irq_handler` reads the DMA controller's count to find the half the DMA is reading and refills the other from the callback or the ring. Counting interrupts instead would fail when two periods end before the first interrupt is serviced, since they raise it once; from the count, a late interrupt costs a period played twice. `set_volume` sets the mixer's master and voice registers, both channels alike.

The playback stream reserves the device until close; console starts and writes return EBUSY while it is reserved, and console stops and callback changes leave it alone. Capture returns ENODEV. `stream_setup` grants unsigned 8-bit or signed 16-bit samples, and signed 16-bit for a wider format, its period keeping its frames, one or two channels, a rate clamped to 5000 through 44100 Hz, and the requested period rounded down to whole frames and clamped to `SB16_PERIOD_MAX`, 2048 bytes. Both DMA halves are primed by `transfer` at start; each interrupt refills the half not playing and calls `period` after the transfer. `stream_position` accumulates the DMA count in frames, including progress inside a period, and `delay` counts the frames transferred but not yet played. The DMA count cannot show a whole revolution missed between readings; late interrupts can therefore leave that many frames out of the reported position. A repeated refill replaces the half queued, keeping the delay within two periods. Stop masks DMA and keeps the last position; restart resets it.

### ISA DMA

`dma.c` programs the two 8237A controllers: channels 0 to 3 move bytes, 5 to 7 move 16-bit words and take their address and count in words, and channel 4, the cascade, is refused. `dma_address_valid` requires a nonempty buffer below 16 MiB that crosses no 64 KiB boundary, 128 KiB for a word channel, with an even address and length on a word channel. `dma_start` masks the channel, writes the mode, the address, the page register and the count, and unmasks it; a refused buffer logs `dma: refused:` with its address and length. `dma_remaining` reads the count register, a byte at a time while the controller counts down, until two readings agree in their high byte, and returns the transfers left before the end of the buffer.

### PC speaker

`sys/speaker.h` declares `speaker_on(hz)`, `speaker_on_divisor`, `speaker_off()` and `speaker_tone`. `sys/arch/x86/speaker.c` programs channel 2 of the 8254 in mode 3, a square wave, with a divisor of 1193182, the frequency's or the one given, clamped to 0xFFFF (about 18 Hz), and sets bits 0 and 1 of port 0x61 to connect the channel to the speaker; 0 and `speaker_off` clear them.

`speaker_tone(divisor, ms)` answers the console's requests in Linux's encodings: `KIOCSOUND`'s argument is the divisor, sounded until the next request, and `KDMKTONE`'s low 16 bits are the divisor and its high 16 bits the tone's length in milliseconds, 0 for silence. A divisor whose pitch is not between 21 and 32766 Hz silences the speaker, as Linux's console does. Each request ends the tone or sound before it. A tone records the low half of the tick count it ends at, and `speaker_tick`, which the boot processor's tick calls, silences the speaker once the count reaches it, so a late interrupt that counts several ticks ends the tone in time. A spinlock taken with interrupts off keeps the request and the tick apart. The counts of tones and sounds begun, of tones the tick ended and of those a request cut short, with the last divisor and length, are the `speaker-` lines of `/proc/console` ([proc(5)](../../../man/man5/proc.5)). `speaker_driver` in `audio.c` accepts `start` with any arguments and plays nothing: its `write` returns -EOPNOTSUPP and it never calls a callback.

A laptop may have no PC speaker, the Lenovo G50-70 among them, and sound the console's tones through its HD Audio codec's beep generator, as Linux does through `hda_beep.c`. `init_audio` gives the speaker the chosen driver's `beep` with `speaker_set_beep` when `beep_level` finds a generator, and `vchan_driver` passes both operations through. Every request then sounds the generator beside the PIT, with the pitch the divisor gives, and the tick that ends a tone stops it too. While `khda` runs, HDA's `beep` hands the divider of a request's tone or stop, and of the tick's stop, to `khda` under the stream lock and sends no verb, so that no wait for the codec runs under the speaker's lock with interrupts off. The thread sends it in a try, turning the output pins on first for a tone with no stream playing, and the tick asks again at each later tick while the command interface is busy or code outside the service holds the jacks; a request that comes before the divider is sent replaces it, so a stop handed on is never sent after a later tone. Linux's `snd_hda_beep_event` likewise records the tone and leaves the verb to the beep's work (`sound/pci/hda/hda_beep.c:45-51` and `118` of 6.12.115). Before the thread runs, a request sends the divider itself, waiting for the codec with interrupts off, and the tick sends its stop in a try, since it cannot wait, and again at each later tick while the interface is busy. The tick takes the speaker's lock with a try as well, and a request that holds it is ended at the next tick. The `speaker-beeps`, `speaker-beep-divider` and `speaker-beep-retries` lines of `/proc/console` count the generator's tones, which while `khda` runs are those handed to it, sent or replaced by a later request before it sent them, where `/proc/audio` counts those sent; the divider it was last given, which while `khda` runs is the one handed on, sent or not, and the stops the tick sent again, which with `khda` running stay 0.

`hda_find_beep` takes the first widget of type 7 and sets it up as Linux's generic parser does: it powers the widget where it has power control, sets its output amplifier to its 0 dB step, the amplifier's offset, unmutes the input of every mixer that lists the widget at that input's 0 dB step, and sends Set Beep Generation (0x70A) 0. `hda_beep(hz)` gives the divider 12000 / `hz`, held between 1 and 255, for a tone of 48000 / (4 x divider) Hz, which is Linux's standard mode, and 0 to stop. On the G50-70's Conexant CX20751/2 the beep widget is node 18, which no widget lists: the codec mixes it into the output pins itself. A stop turns the output pins off, so with no stream playing a tone first turns them on for the output mode, and EAPD, as a start does (`hda_route_output`); the jacks' poll then moves them as it does while a stream plays, and the beep's service in `khda`, which the tick asks for (`hda_beep_service`), turns them off again once the generator has been silent for a second, so that a run of bells does not switch the pins between bells. A stop of playback during a tone leaves the pins on for that time. `beep_level` sets the output amplifier's step nearest the level, muting at 0 where it can mute; a level of 0 sends the divider 0, and any other reads as its step's level, at least 1. The level starts at the 0 dB step: 100 on the G50-70, whose amplifier has seven steps of 4 dB with 0 dB at the last, and no mute.

## Limits

* The console's stream has no owner. Any process with a console descriptor can start, feed or stop it, and a process that exits without `AUDIO_STOP` leaves it playing silence. It is one channel of the mix, beside every `/dev/dsp` open, and a new `AUDIO_START` replaces it.
* Capture records HDA's input, or the mix under `rach2.audio.loopback`, through `/dev/dsp` alone. While several readers are open the input keeps the settings it started with, and each reader converts from them through its resampler.
* A converted rate is heard 59 frames of the lower rate late, 1.2 ms against 48 kHz and 7.4 ms at 8 kHz, and the passband ends at 0.90 of the lower Nyquist frequency, 19.8 kHz from 44.1 kHz. A ratio above 8 to 1, such as a reader at 4000 Hz of a 48 kHz input, aliases what lies between the input's rate over 8 and the output's band. A stopped channel's frames that the mix already took still play, and a channel that starts while others play waits behind the periods the device holds.
* `/dev/dsp` exists on HDA and the SB16. Any number of opens play, and it plays and records the five formats `SETFMT` takes and no other, and has no mmap. It plays at most 24 bits, and records at 16. On the SB16 an open for reading fails with ENODEV, except under `rach2.audio.loopback`. In an open for both directions the rate the ioctls give back is playback's; capture's, which `/proc/audio` shows, may differ where the codec's ADC has other rates.
* The HDMI codec plays one stream of two channels on the first monitor with a valid ELD, through `/dev/dsp1` alone: no mix, no multichannel layout, no compressed passthrough, no capture, no mixer controls, no jack reported to programs, and no DisplayPort MST entries. `/proc/asound` lists the HDA cards alone, not the SB16, and has only `cards`, each card's `id` and the HDMI card's ELD files, with no `pcm`, `codec#` or per-card links; a card's interrupt is its function's legacy line, which neither driver may use. The infoframes have been checked only by `hdmi_audio_hosttest`'s model; no HDMI monitor has played on the Lenovo.
* HDA drives one controller, one codec, one output stream and one input stream. A path is at most one mixer or selector deep, a headphone path is direct, and an NID above 127 is never used.
* Capture records at a rate the ADC has, and converts none. The pin it records is chosen at discovery, by the mixer's record source among those the chosen ADC reaches, or by the microphone jack between the two microphones.
* Headphone playback, the switch between speaker and headphones at the plug, and the headset mic at the jack are checked only by `hda_hosttest`'s model of the G50-70's codec; none has been heard or recorded on the Lenovo itself.
* HDA plays 24 bits only where every DAC has them. QEMU's codec has 16 bits alone, its PCM word 0x000201FC, so in QEMU the mix plays 16 bits, and the 24-bit path is checked only by `hda_hosttest`'s model codec until it is heard on the Lenovo, whose codec has 24.
* The jacks are polled every quarter second and not told by unsolicited responses, so the sound moves a quarter to a half second after a plug. Only the headphone pin and the first jack mic are watched: a line out or a second jack is not, and a headset's microphone is not told apart from headphones without one.
* `/dev/mixer` keeps one level for both sides of every channel. VOLUME is the device's, and PCM is the level of the calling process's own playback opens, so `mixer` reads its own PCM, 100, and cannot show another process's.
* The SB16 uses IRQ 5 and DMA channels 1 and 5 only.
* `/proc/audio` reports what was programmed. It does not measure the DAC's clock or tell which output is audible.
* `/proc/audio` keeps 32 connections of each widget; a longer list shows as `conn invalid`. A pin's sense word is the one the last `start` read; the `jack` lines have the states the jacks' poll follows.
* On HDA the delay counts the samples queued in memory, not those the controller has fetched into its FIFO or the codec holds.
* The PC speaker has no volume, and the console's BEL character does not sound it (`sys/kio.c`); [term](../../../term/Readme.md) sounds its bell with `KDMKTONE`. A codec's beep generator has the mixer's SPEAKER level, and its tones on the G50-70 are checked only by `hda_hosttest`'s model of the codec until they are heard on the machine.

* `tests/host/dma_hosttest.c` checks the mask port and value for each transfer channel, the count register read across a borrow in either reading, and requires channel 4 and invalid channels to do no I/O. No suite runs `audiotest`'s tone or the probe built from `tests/musl/hdaprobe.c`.

Run a suite with `tests/run NAME`.
