# i915

`sys/drm/i915/` is the driver of Intel's Haswell graphics with GT2, the HD 4400 of the Lenovo G50-70 (PCI 8086:0a16) among them, after Linux 6.12's i915, whose names, register values and device table it takes. It binds the chip at 00:02.0, masks its interrupts, brings up forcewake, the display power well, stolen memory and the global GTT, reads the firmware's OpRegion and video BIOS table for the outputs and the panel, enables its MSI message, turns on RC6 and the GT's frequency control, starts the render, blitter, video and video enhancement rings with their status pages and user interrupts, and registers it as the DRM device `i915` ([`../drm.h`](../drm.h)); every access it makes to the chip goes through one hardware boundary, so that a host model and a fake chip stand in for it; the fake, under `rach2.i915fake`, stands on QEMU's `edu` device as the HD 4400, so that Mesa and libdrm reach the driver's real paths in QEMU. It serves buffer objects: their pages, cache domains and caching, maps of them written back, write-combining, uncached and through the aperture, tiling detiled by the fence registers, the eviction of idle bindings, objects purged under memory pressure, objects over a program's own memory, and sharing with other devices through dma-bufs. It keeps each open file's contexts, with a hardware image on the render ring, runs requests on its four rings with fences that the user interrupt signals, runs programs' batches through EXECBUFFER2 with their relocations and fences, each batch whose length the call gives checked by Linux's Haswell command parser and run privileged from a checked copy, and answers GETPARAM, I915_QUERY, GET_RESET_STATS and REG_READ of the render ring's timestamp as Linux 6.12 does. It adopts the picture the firmware lit, pipe A on the eDP panel on the Lenovo, without a mode set, sets the panel's modes on any pipe, a smaller one through the panel fitter, and drives the planes, cursors, page flips, vblanks, watermarks and pipe CRC as a DRM device with `DRIVER_MODESET` and `DRIVER_ATOMIC`, and takes the panel's backlight over from `intelbl.c` ([`../../display/Readme.md`](../../display/Readme.md)). It drives HDMI and DisplayPort monitors on DDI B to D as the VBT and the straps give them, reading their EDIDs over GMBUS, clocking HDMI from the WRPLLs as Linux computes them, and finds them plugged and unplugged by the PCH's hotplug interrupt. [i915(4)](../../../../man/man4/i915.4) is the interface a program sees.

## Use

The driver binds a chip only when the kernel command line has `rach2.i915` ([cmdline](../../../cmdline.md)). `drm_start` calls `i915_probe` through `arch_drm_probe(ARCH_DRM_NATIVE)`, after bochs and before the firmware framebuffer's driver. A bind on the Lenovo logs:

    i915: stolen: [mem 0xaba00000-0xaf9fffff], N KiB usable, inside the device's reserved region; DMAR active, no buffers in it
    i915: gtt: pipe A's firmware surface at 0x0, 4128 KiB, kept
    i915: gtt: 2048 MiB, 256 MiB of it through the aperture; 523256 entries to the scratch page, 1032 kept
    i915: opregion: version 2.0.0 at 0xaafb4018, mailboxes 0x1f; VBT in mailbox 4
    i915: vbt: "$VBT HASWELL        " from the OpRegion (Mailbox #4), version 177, 3 outputs
    i915: interrupts: messages on vector N
    i915: rps: RPn 200 MHz, RP1 200 MHz, RP0 1000 MHz, RPe 500 MHz, max 1000 MHz
    i915: rings: rcs0, bcs0, vcs0 and vecs0 started: 16 KiB each at 0x7fffb000, 0x7fff6000, 0x7fff1000 and 0x7ffec000, status pages at 0x7ffff000, 0x7fffa000, 0x7fff5000 and 0x7fff0000
    i915: workarounds: applied: 3 of the GT and 6 of rcs0, 0 lost
    i915: display: pipe A adopted: 1366x768@60, 76300 kHz, 18 bpp, 1 lane at 270000 kHz, the firmware's framebuffer at GTT 0x0, stride 5504
    i915: backlight: intel_backlight: levels 0 to 937, at N, off with the pipe
    i915: found: 00:02.0: haswell/ult (device id 0a16), graphics 7.5, display version 7.00, gt2, revision 0b
    i915: registered: i915 1.6.0 for pci:0000:00:02.0 on card 0

A machine without an OpRegion, or without a valid table in it, takes the table from the option ROM's shadow at 0xc0000, `from the PCI ROM`, when the chip is the boot display; without either the bind logs `i915: vbt: no video BIOS table; Linux's defaults for each port`. An OpRegion that cannot be used logs `i915: opregion: at 0xA not used: error N`, and a table decoded only in part `i915: vbt: decoded in part: error N`.

With `rach2.i915.firstlight` as well, the bind has the render ring write a value to memory before it registers the device, as the section on rings says, and logs

    i915: first light: ok: rcs0 wrote 0x1915f157 at 0xA, breadcrumb 1, user interrupt latched

where 0xA is a page the GT takes from its space below the rings and the PPGTT's directory, or `i915: first light:` and the failure: `the render ring is not running`, `no page for the value`, `submission refused` (a fault of the backend), `no room in the ring`, `no room for the breadcrumb`, `no breadcrumb`, `read back 0xV at 0xA, wrote 0x1915f157` or `no user interrupt`, those but the first two and the read back with the ring's head, tail, ACTHD and IPEHR. `latched` says the processor had its interrupts off and the interrupt waits in the GT's identity register, `taken` that the handler ran.

With `rach2.i915fake` the probe also binds each function of QEMU's `edu` device, 1234:11e8, which `-device edu` gives a machine, with the fake chip behind it (the section on the fake chip below). A bind logs, beside the lines above but stolen memory's, which it has none of:

    i915: fake: 00:04.0: edu stands in for 8086:0a16
    i915: found: 00:04.0: haswell/ult (device id 0a16), graphics 7.5, display version 7.00, gt2, revision 0b

and a machine without the device logs `i915: fake: no edu device to stand on; QEMU's -device edu gives one`. The fake's faults are armed through `/sys/kernel/debug/dri/DEVICE/i915_fake`, as [debugfs(5)](../../../../man/man5/debugfs.5) gives it: `hang`, `irq`, `submit` or `bind`, alone, with a count or with `always`; `clear`; and `light`, which runs first light now. Each injection logs `i915: fake: injected: NAME: detail`. Read, the file gives each fault's count, each ring's commands, batches and whether it is hung, the resets of each ring's domain alone and of the whole GPU, `resets rcs0 N bcs0 M full K`, and the messages.

Without `rach2.i915` the probe logs `i915: 00:02.0: 0a16 left to the firmware's framebuffer; rach2.i915 binds it` and touches nothing. Whether or not the option is given, the boot takes the chip's stolen memory out of the memory map before the frame allocator is made, logging `i915: stolen: reserving [mem 0xaba00000-0xaf9fffff], 0 KiB of it usable memory` on the Lenovo, whose firmware's map reserves it already. A wait that reaches its bound logs `i915: NAME: timeout after N us: register 0xR reads 0xV`.

Root gives a bound chip to another driver, `vfio-pci` for a virtual machine ([vfio](../../vfio/Readme.md)), and takes it back, through the driver's directory in /sys, as on Linux:

    echo 0000:00:02.0 > /sys/bus/pci/drivers/i915/unbind
    echo 0000:00:02.0 > /sys/bus/pci/drivers/i915/bind

The first logs `i915: handoff: 0000:00:02.0: released: the display off, the GPU reset, interrupts and bus mastering off`, and the second, after the function's reset and the panel's power cycle, the lines of a bind and `i915: handoff: 0000:00:02.0: bound from a reset`. A release that is refused logs `i915: handoff: 0000:00:02.0: not released: error N` and leaves the chip bound; a bring-up whose reset or bind fails logs `not reset: error N` or `not bound: error N` and leaves the function without a driver. The section on the release below has the rest, and [sysfs(5)](../../../../man/man5/sysfs.5) the files.

An engine that hangs is reset, as the section on hangs says, and logs as Linux does:

    i915: hangcheck: rcs0: no heartbeat in 2500 ms: breadcrumb 3 of 5, ACTHD 0x7fffb188, was 0x7fffb188
    i915: hang: GPU HANG: ecode 7:1:00000000, in glxgears [42]
    i915: reset: rcs0 for stopped heartbeat on rcs0
    i915: reset: glxgears[42] context reset due to GPU hang

An engine whose domain does not reset adds `i915: reset: rcs0: its domain did not reset: GDRST reads 0xV` and `i915: reset: the chip for stopped heartbeat on rcs0`, and a GPU that does not reset either `i915: reset: the chip did not reset: GDRST reads 0xV` and `i915: reset: wedged: the GPU takes no work until a reset succeeds`.

## Interfaces

* [`i915.h`](i915.h) is the contract every file of the directory keeps, in its opening comment, and the driver's device, `struct drm_i915_private`, which embeds its `struct drm_device` and is found from it with `to_i915`, and holds the state of the base, `uncore`, `power`, `stolen` and `ggtt`, of the interrupts, `irq`, and of the rings, `gt`. The base's calls are declared there by file: `i915_forcewake_get` and `put`, `i915_uncore_read` and `write`; `i915_power_get` and `put`, `i915_power_well_on` and `i915_power_well_in_use`; `i915_stolen_reserve_early`, which `platform_memory_reserve` ([`../../platform.h`](../../platform.h)) calls at boot; and `i915_ggtt_insert`, `i915_ggtt_clear_range`, `i915_ggtt_pte`, `i915_ggtt_pte_encode` and `i915_ggtt_invalidate`. The interrupts' calls are `i915_irq_reset`, `i915_irq_install`, `i915_irq_register` and `unregister`, `i915_irq_unmask` and `mask`, and `i915_irq_handle`, the device's interrupt; the rings' are `i915_gt_init` and `fini`, `i915_gt_ggtt_take`, `i915_gt_pages_map` and `unmap`, `i915_ring_begin` and `advance`, `i915_engine_emit_breadcrumb`, `i915_engine_passed`, `i915_engine_irq_get` and `put`, and `i915_engine_wait`; the aliasing PPGTT's are `i915_ppgtt_init` and `fini`, which the rings' bring-up and teardown call, `i915_ppgtt_insert` and `i915_ppgtt_clear_range`, which a buffer's binding calls beside the global GTT's, and `i915_ppgtt_enable_ring`, its hook at ring start; and `i915_firstlight` is first light. RC6 and the frequency are `i915_rc6_init`, `enable`, `disable`, `park`, `unpark` and `residency_ms`; `i915_rps_init`, `enable`, `disable`, `park`, `unpark`, `work`, `boost`, `retire` and `mark_interactive`, `i915_rps_set_locked`, `i915_rps_read_actual_mhz` and `i915_rps_set_max_mhz`, `min_mhz` and `boost_mhz`; the pcode mailbox's `i915_pcode_read` and `write`; and the GT's `i915_gt_pm_enable` and `disable` and `i915_gt_unpark` and `park`, which the code that gives the GT work calls around it. `i915_sysfs_register` adds the card's files in /sys. `struct i915_platform` is what Linux's device table says of Haswell GT2 (`hsw_gt2_info`): graphics 7.5, display 7, the four engines, the LLC, RC6 without RC6p, the engine reset and the aliasing PPGTT. `i915_ids` holds Linux's twenty GT2 ids, each with its subplatform, and `i915_id_lookup` finds one. `i915_driver_bind` binds a PCI function whose registers a given `struct i915_hw` reaches: the probe's way after mapping BAR 0, and any other backend's, the host model's among them.
* [`intel_bios.h`](intel_bios.h) is the firmware's description of the machine. `intel_opregion_setup` maps and checks the OpRegion at ASLS into a `struct intel_opregion`, with its mailboxes and the VBT it holds, and `intel_opregion_fini` unmaps it. `intel_bios_is_valid_vbt` checks a table's headers and sizes, `intel_bios_rom_vbt` copies one out of an option ROM's image, and `intel_bios_init` decodes one, or makes Linux's defaults without one, into a `struct intel_vbt`: the general features and definitions, the driver features, one `struct intel_bios_encoder` an output with its port, AUX channel, DDC pin and level shift, and each block copied and padded to Linux's size, which `intel_bios_block` returns; `intel_bios_fini` frees it. `intel_bios_panel_init` decodes the panel of an encoder into a `struct intel_vbt_panel`: its type, from the table or the EDID's product id, its mode, DRRS, backlight and eDP settings with the power sequence. `intel_bios_encoder_lookup` finds a port's encoder and `intel_bios_encoder_supports_crt`, `dvi`, `hdmi`, `dp`, `edp`, `dsi` and `dp_dual_mode` read its type.
* [`i915_hw.h`](i915_hw.h) is the hardware boundary: `i915_read`, `i915_write`, `i915_posting_read`, `i915_rmw`, `i915_read64_2x32`, `i915_delay_us`, the bounded waits `i915_wait_for` and `i915_wait_until`, `i915_hw_mmio_init`, which makes the backend over BAR 0's registers, the GTT's entries, `i915_hw_gtt_init`, `i915_gtt_read` and `i915_gtt_write`, the chip's configuration words, `i915_hw_config16` and `i915_hw_config32`, and the fault points, `i915_hw_fault` with `I915_FAULT_SUBMIT` and `I915_FAULT_BIND`.
* The buffers' calls are declared at the end of `i915.h`: `i915_ioctls`, the driver's table of `I915_NUM_IOCTLS` rows, each `DRM_RENDER_ALLOW` but `GET_PIPE_FROM_CRTC_ID`, the display's, as Linux's; `i915_gem_object_funcs`, the objects' table for the DRM core; `i915_gem_prime_import`; `i915_gem_init_hw`, which the bind calls to write the fences and the swizzle registers, and `i915_gem_unplug`, which the unplug calls to unmap every program's map of an object. The aperture's calls, `i915_aperture_init`, `phys`, `kmap`, `kunmap`, `sync` and `fini`, are in `i915_hw.h`, with a backend's `aperture_page` and `aperture_sync`.
* The display's calls are declared in `i915.h` after the buffers': `intel_display_init`, which reads the pipes out, makes the mode objects and adopts what the firmware lit, and `intel_display_fini`; `intel_display_readout` and `intel_pipe_readout`; `intel_atomic_commit`, the mode configuration's commit; `intel_fb_ggtt` and `intel_fb_modifier`; the planes' and the cursor's `intel_planes_init`, `intel_cursor_init` and `intel_plane_ctl_crtc`; the watermarks' `intel_wm_init`, `intel_wm_sanitize`, `intel_wm_compute`, `intel_wm_intermediate`, `intel_wm_commit`, `intel_wm_vblank` and `intel_wm_cancel`; the CD clock's `intel_cdclk_init`, `intel_max_dotclock` and `intel_cdclk_check`; the vblanks' `intel_vblank_init` and `fini`, `intel_enable_vblank`, `intel_disable_vblank`, `intel_get_vblank_counter`, `intel_crtc_get_vblank_timestamp`, `intel_crtc_arm_flip_done`, `intel_crtc_arm_event`, `intel_crtc_send_event` and `intel_display_irq_work`; the CRC's `intel_crtc_set_crc_source`, `verify_crc_source`, `get_crc_sources` and `intel_crtc_crc_frame`; the backlight's `intel_backlight_setup`, `pipe` and `fini`; the eDP output's `intel_dp_init`, `intel_dp_connector_init`, `intel_dp_compute_config` and `intel_dp_set_power`, its AUX channel's `intel_dp_aux_transfer`, `intel_dp_dpcd_read`, `write` and `writeb` and `intel_dp_read_edid`, its link's `intel_dp_start_link_train` and `stop_link_train`, DDI A's `intel_ddi_pre_enable`, `enable`, `disable` and `post_disable` with the calls for its buffer and training patterns, the power sequencer's `intel_pps_init`, `init_late`, `on`, `off`, `backlight_on` and `backlight_off` and the VDD's `intel_pps_vdd_on_unlocked`, `vdd_off_unlocked` and `vdd_work`, the panel's `intel_panel_init_fixed_modes`, `fixed_mode`, `mode_valid`, `compute_config` and `get_modes`, and the fitter's `intel_pfit_compute_config`, `intel_pfit_pixel_rate`, `ilk_pfit_enable` and `disable`; DDI B to D's GMBUS, `intel_gmbus_setup`, `intel_gmbus_bus`, `intel_gmbus_is_valid_pin`, `intel_gmbus_force_bit` and `intel_gmbus_is_forced_bit`, with `intel_edid_read` and `intel_i2c_probe_ddc` over any of its buses; the PLLs' `intel_dpll_init`, `intel_dpll_plan_init`, `intel_dpll_compute`, `intel_dpll_enable`, `intel_dpll_disable`, `intel_dpll_readout`, `intel_dpll_sanitize` and `intel_dpll_port_clk_sel`, and the clocks' `intel_dpll_calc_wrpll`, `intel_dpll_wrpll_freq` and `intel_dpll_spll_freq`; HDMI's `intel_hdmi_connector_init`, `intel_hdmi_compute_config`, `intel_hdmi_set_infoframes` and `intel_hdmi_dual_mode_tmds`, with `intel_ddi_prepare_hdmi_buf_trans` and `intel_ddi_hdmi_default_level`; external DisplayPort's `intel_dp_ext_connector_init`, `intel_dp_connected` and `intel_dp_hpd_pulse`; hotplug's `intel_hotplug_init`, `fini`, `work` and `live`, with `intel_dp_active_crtc` and `intel_display_commit_pipe`, which set a pipe again; and the buffers' `i915_gem_dumb_create`, `i915_gem_object_pin_to_display` and `i915_gem_object_flush_range`.
* The contexts' and requests' calls are declared in `i915.h` after the buffers'. `i915_driver_open` and `i915_driver_postclose` make and free a file's `struct i915_file` with its default context; `i915_gem_context_lookup` finds a file's context by id, with a reference that `i915_gem_context_put` drops, and `i915_gem_context_engine` gives the `struct intel_context` of one of its engine slots, the state a request runs in, with a reference that `intel_context_put` drops. `i915_request_create` starts a request on a state, `i915_request_begin` gives room for its commands, `i915_request_add` ends it with its breadcrumb and submits it, and `i915_request_wait`, `i915_request_completed` and `i915_request_put` follow it, and its `hold`, a `struct i915_request_hold`, is what its batch needs until it retires; a request's first member is its `struct i915_fence`, so that it can stand as a dma-fence. `i915_retire_requests` retires what has completed, and `i915_requests_unplug` fails what has not when the device goes. `i915_sseu_init`, `i915_engine_lookup_user` and `i915_engine_uabi_class` serve the parameters. The ioctls are `i915_gem_context_create_ioctl`, `destroy`, `getparam`, `setparam` and `reset_stats`, `i915_getparam_ioctl`, `i915_query_ioctl` and `i915_reg_read_ioctl`.
* The hangs' calls are declared in `i915.h` after the requests'. `i915_hangcheck_unpark` arms the heartbeat when the GT wakes, and `i915_hangcheck` runs a beat when one is due, from the submission worker and from `i915_request_wait`, and hands each hung engine to `i915_handle_error`, which captures the error state with `i915_gpu_error_capture`, resets the engine with `i915_reset_engine` or else the whole GPU with `i915_reset_gt`, which wedges it with `i915_gt_set_wedged` when that fails too. `i915_gpu_error_clear` drops the state kept, and `i915_gpu_coredump` captures one that is not kept. `i915_debugfs_register` adds the debugfs files and the card's `error` in /sys; `i915_gem_shrink_flags` purges the objects with bindings, without, or both. `i915_gem_context_mark_guilty` and `mark_innocent` count a hang against a request's context and say whether it is banned; `i915_request_skip`, `i915_request_set_error_once` and `i915_requests_cancel` settle the requests; `i915_ring_reset_prepare` and `i915_ring_resume` stop a ring and start it again at a given head; and `i915_gem_restore_fences` writes the fences again after the whole GPU's reset.
* The command parser's calls are declared in `i915.h` after EXECBUFFER2's: `i915_engine_using_cmd_parser`, whether a ring parses, and `i915_cmd_parser`, which copies the batch a `struct i915_cmd_parse` names to its shadow, checks it and writes the trampoline; `I915_CMD_PARSER_VERSION` is the version GETPARAM gives and `I915_CMD_PARSER_TRAMPOLINE_SIZE` the bytes the shadow keeps past the batch. `i915_gem_object_prepare_read` readies an object for the processor's reads.
* [`intel_dp.h`](intel_dp.h) has the sink's DPCD registers and the AUX channel's requests and replies by the names of Linux's `include/drm/display/drm_dp.h`.
* `i915_fake_probe` ([`i915_fake.c`](i915_fake.c)) binds the fake chip under `rach2.i915fake`, and `i915_firstlight_run` runs first light whatever the command line says, as the fake's file does.
* The release's calls are declared in `i915.h`: `i915_handoff_bound` and `i915_handoff_unbound` ([`i915_handoff.c`](i915_handoff.c)), which the bind and the unplug call to show the driver on the function in /sys and to take it away; `i915_gt_quiesce`, which stops every ring and resets the GPU; `i915_pci_probe`, the bind of one function through its registers, and `i915_fake_bind`, the fake chip's; and `i915_pci_unmap`, which gives a record's mappings of the registers and the GTT back and cuts it off from the chip with `i915_hw_gone` ([`i915_hw.h`](i915_hw.h)), after which its reads are all ones and its writes dropped.
* [`i915_reg.h`](i915_reg.h) has the registers by Linux's names with Haswell's values, in blocks that each name the Linux file they come from: configuration space, BAR 0's layout, uncore and forcewake, interrupts, engines, the commands and the status page's dwords, reset, fences and swizzling, the GTT's entries, RC6 and frequency, power wells, pipes, planes and cursors, DDI and its PLLs, the panel power sequencer and backlight, AUX and GMBUS.
* [`sources.mk`](sources.mk) lists the driver's files for `sys/arch/x86/sources.mk`, since the driver is PC-only.

## Design

The driver follows Linux 6.12. Where it follows Linux 6.18 instead, the comment or sentence that names 6.18 says why: the fitter's limits, which 6.12 does not check; the power sequencer's power cycle, which 6.18 reads as the hardware counts it and 6.12 waits 100 ms too long for, and its poll every 10 ms; the debugfs text and the parameters, which match what the Lenovo printed under Linux 6.18.58; and GETPARAM's `MMAP_GTT_VERSION`.

### The hardware boundary

The driver reaches its chip only through a `struct i915_hw`: a table of `read32`, `write32`, `delay_us` and `gtt_map` and the backend's own pointer. On the machine the backend is BAR 0's first 2 MiB, the registers, mapped uncached with `map_mmio_typed`, as Linux maps them; the second 2 MiB, the GTT's entries, the backend's `gtt_map` maps write-combining when the global GTT comes up, as Linux does, or uncached on a processor without a page attribute table, so that no page of BAR 0 is mapped with two cache types. A backend without `gtt_map`, such as the model, answers for the entries as registers from 0x200000. In a host test the backend is the model of the HD 4400 (`tests/host/i915_model.c`), and in QEMU it may be the fake chip (below), a table of the same calls. An access past the backend's size reads all ones and writes nothing, as a chip that has gone reads.

A backend that stands in for a chip gives the table five more calls, which the MMIO backend and the model leave empty. `config_read32` answers for the chip's identity in configuration space: the bind then reads the ids, the class and revision, the board's ids, the GMCH control word, BDSM and MSAC from it, and the PCI function's own command word, BARs and MSI capability from the function, and has `/sys` report that identity for the function (`drm_device.pci_ident`). `fault` answers at the driver's fault points: `i915_ring_begin` asks `I915_FAULT_SUBMIT` before it takes room, and fails as a wedged GPU refuses work, and `i915_ggtt_insert` asks `I915_FAULT_BIND` before it writes an entry and fails with its error, `-ENOSPC`; `hw.faults` counts the errors injected. `attach` is told of the device once it is registered, `detach` at its unplug and `release` when its record goes: the device owns the backend from its registration, and a bind that fails leaves it to the caller.

Every wait on the chip has a bound in microseconds and a name. It reads at once, then after waits of 1 us doubling to 1 ms, counted by the backend's `delay_us`: the processor's spin on its reference counter on the machine, and the model's clock in a test, so a test of a stuck bit takes no time. A wait at its bound returns `-ETIMEDOUT`, logs its name and what the register read, and leaves the name in `last_timeout`. A wait spins and never sleeps, so its bound is short and a long wait is the caller's sleep between bounded waits.

### Locks

Everything runs under the big kernel lock, as [`../Readme.md`](../Readme.md) says. Beside it the order, outermost first, is a DRM device's `commit_lock`, then the driver's locks of buffers and contexts, which may sleep, then the hardware's: the frequency's lock, `rps.lock`, over RPS's state and its requests, then the register lock of forcewake and of any register the interrupt handler also writes, then the interrupt mask's, all taken with interrupts off and never held across a sleep. The interrupt handler takes only the hardware's locks. The register lock is `uncore.lock`, over forcewake's count, the GT FIFO's and the GTT's flush; the interrupt mask's is `irq.lock`, over the masks, the enables and the table of handlers, which the handler holds while it runs. Each part that adds a lock adds it in this order.

### Forcewake and the GT FIFO

The GT, the render and media engines and their registers, below 0x40000, sleeps in RC6 whenever it is idle, and a register of a sleeping GT reads stale. Forcewake keeps it awake. `i915_uncore_init`, the first of the bind's parts, clears the unclaimed-access bit of `FPGA_DBG` and the GT FIFO's errors in `GTFIFODBG` that the firmware left, and releases every request of `FORCEWAKE_MT`, a masked register, the firmware's among them (Linux's WaRsClearFWBitsAtReset).

The rules, Linux's for Haswell (`fw_domains_get_with_thread_status`):

* A reference is taken with `i915_forcewake_get` and dropped with `i915_forcewake_put`; the count is under the register lock, with interrupts off. Only the first reference touches the chip: it waits up to 50 ms for `FORCEWAKE_ACK_HSW` to drop the last release's acknowledgement, sets the kernel's bit, 0x00010001 in `FORCEWAKE_MT`, waits up to 50 ms for the acknowledgement, and then up to 5 ms for the GT's threads to leave their sleep (WaRsForcewakeWaitTC0). The last put writes 0x00010000 and waits for nothing.
* An acknowledgement that does not come fails the get with `-ETIMEDOUT`, the wait named `forcewake ack clear` or `forcewake ack` in the log and in `hw.last_timeout`; the request is withdrawn and no reference is held, so no caller reads a GT that did not wake. Linux logs the same timeouts and goes on. The threads' wait only counts and logs, as Linux's warning does.
* A caller that makes several accesses to the GT holds a reference across them. `i915_uncore_read` of a GT register without one takes and drops a reference around the read, where Linux keeps it for a further millisecond; a GT register whose forcewake cannot be had reads all ones.
* `i915_uncore_write` below 0x40000 or from 0x116000, Linux's `NEEDS_FORCE_WAKE`, first waits for room in the GT FIFO, as Linux's `gen6_write`; it takes no forcewake, and neither does a read from 0x116000, which Haswell's one forcewake range does not cover.
* A write to a GT register goes through the GT FIFO, which holds writes while the GT wakes and loses one that finds it full. `i915_uncore_write` counts the FIFO's free entries down from what `GTFIFOCTL` last read, and once the count is down to the 20 the FIFO keeps in reserve, reads them again until there are more, waiting up to 10 ms; a wait that reaches its bound is counted and logged and the write goes ahead, as on Linux. Writes need no forcewake.

### The display power well

Haswell has one display power well, which Linux calls "display": it powers pipes B and C, transcoders A to C, the panel fitters and the DDI lanes of ports B to E. Pipe A, the eDP transcoder and port A are outside it, always on. Four registers request it, the BIOS's, the driver's, the KVMR's and the debug register's, each with a request bit (31) and a state bit (30); the well is on while any request is set.

`i915_power_init` makes the BIOS's request the driver's, as Linux's `hsw_power_well_sync_hw`, and takes the reference Linux's `POWER_DOMAIN_INIT` holds until the display is read out: the driver's request set, then the state waited for, 1 ms at most, a timeout named `power well on`. The reference is held for the life of the bind. `i915_power_get` and `i915_power_put` count references; the last put clears the driver's request and waits up to 1 ms for the well to go off, unless another request holds it on, which is logged with the requesters, as Linux does. The last put leaves the well on, `kept`, while a part of it runs, which `i915_power_well_in_use` reads: transcoder A, B or C enabled, a panel fitter enabled, pipe B's or C's plane enabled, or the eDP transcoder fed other than from pipe A always on, which passes through pipe A's panel fitter or pipe B or C (Linux's `force_thru`). On the Lenovo the firmware drives the panel from pipe A through the eDP transcoder, always on, so the well is not in use, and Linux turns it off at idle.

### Stolen memory

The firmware takes stolen memory from the top of the memory below 4 GiB for the GPU: its base is BDSM (configuration offset 0x5c) and its size the GMCH control word's GMS field in units of 32 MiB, 64 MiB at 0xaba00000 on the Lenovo, with the GTT's 2 MiB below it. The firmware's framebuffer is its first 4128 KiB.

* At boot, after the kernel and the modules are taken out of the memory map and before the frame allocator is made from it, `platform_memory_reserve` calls `i915_stolen_reserve_early`, which reads 00:02.0's configuration space by mechanism #1 and, for a chip of the driver's ids, takes the stolen memory out of the map, as Linux's early quirk does, so that no frame of it is ever handed out even where the firmware's map offers it. The Lenovo's map reserves 0xab000000 to 0xaf9fffff itself.
* At the bind `i915_stolen_init` finds it again from the words the bind read, and checks that the allocator owns no frame of it and that it lies inside a reserved region the DMAR table names for the GPU, its RMRR, 0xab800000 to 0xaf9fffff on the Lenovo, where the table names one; on x86-64 alone, since only it reads the table. Memory that fails either check is logged and left unused. The part at its top that the firmware keeps, from `GEN6_STOLEN_RESERVED` when its enable is set, 1 MiB or 256 KiB, is kept out of `usable_size`; a part outside the stolen memory refuses it whole, as on Linux.
* As Linux on a chip before graphics version 8, no buffer is made in it while VT-d translates for the GPU (`for_objects` clear, logged `DMAR active`).

### The global GTT

The global GTT maps the GPU's global address space a 4 KiB page an entry. Its entries are BAR 0's second half: the GMCH control word's GGMS field gives their bytes, 2 MiB on the Lenovo, so 524288 entries and 2 GiB of address space. The first `mappable_end` bytes of it are what the processor reaches through the aperture, BAR 2, whose size `i915_ggtt_init` reads from MSAC (configuration offset 0x62: 128, 256 or 512 MiB, 256 on the Lenovo) rather than by sizing the BAR, since the console draws through it.

An entry is Linux's `hsw_pte_encode`: bit 0 valid, bits 31:12 the address's bits 31:12, bits 10:4 its bits 38:32, and the cache bits: none for `I915_CACHE_NONE`, `HSW_WB_LLC_AGE3` (bit 2) for every other level, since the HD 4400 has no eDRAM. Every entry that maps nothing maps the scratch page, a zeroed frame of the allocator's, uncached.

At the bind the firmware's surfaces are found from the hardware: each primary plane enabled, pipe A's always and B's and C's while the well is on, gives its surface's GTT offset in `DSPSURF` and its bytes, the stride times its rows with the plane's offset, whole tile rows of 8 for an X-tiled one. Those ranges are `kept`: the bind leaves their entries as the firmware wrote them, since the plane scans them out and the console draws on the same pages through the aperture at 0xb0000000, and `i915_ggtt_insert` and `i915_ggtt_clear_range` refuse to touch them with `-EBUSY`; the display's adoption shows them through a framebuffer of its own and keeps them kept after the plane has moved on, since the console may draw there again. On the Lenovo that is GTT offset 0 to 0x407fff, 1032 entries that map stolen memory from its base. Every other entry is pointed at the scratch page, as Linux's `init_ggtt` clears the holes, and the GTT's cache is flushed. Insert and clear write the entries and then flush the GTT's cache by `GFX_FLSH_CNTL_GEN6` with a posting read, under the register lock; the register write also drains the processor's write-combining buffers, so the chip sees every entry written before it.

### The aliasing PPGTT

All programs share one GPU address space on Haswell, as on Linux: the aliasing PPGTT, Linux's `gen6_ppgtt`, one table of 2 GiB that every context of every program runs in. A buffer's binding (`i915_vma.c`) writes its pages at its offset in the global GTT and, at the same offset, in the PPGTT, as Linux's `aliasing_gtt_bind_vma`; its unbinding clears both. The GT's own objects, the status pages, rings, context images and scratch page, are bound in the global GTT alone, so no batch reaches them. A batch that is not privileged starts with the PPGTT bit and so sees every buffer any program has bound, another program's among them; nothing but the command parser stands between one program's batches and another's buffers. Linux runs Haswell so too: its device data gives graphics version 7 the aliasing PPGTT alone (`INTEL_PPGTT_ALIASING`) and version 8 and later a full PPGTT for each context. CONTEXT_GETPARAM's `VM` answers `ENODEV` for that reason.

The PPGTT is 512 directory entries, each naming a table of 1024 entries of 4 KiB pages. Its directory lies in the global GTT's own entries: `i915_ppgtt_init`, which `i915_gt_init` calls before the rings start, takes 2 MiB of the GT's space at a 64 KiB boundary, below the rings, and writes each of the 512 entries there with the address of a table, bits 39:32 in bits 11:4, as Linux's `gen6_write_pde`, then reads the last back and flushes the GTT's cache. A ring's `PP_DIR_BASE` holds the directory's offset among the entries in 64-byte units in its bits 31:16. A table is made from the heap when a binding first reaches its 4 MiB, filled with the scratch page's entry, and named in the directory, which is then read back and flushed again; until then the entry names the scratch table, whose entries all map the global GTT's scratch page. A table stays until the rings stop, even when nothing in it is bound, as Linux keeps them while the directory is bound, since the chip caches directory entries with no way to invalidate them. The entries are the global GTT's encoding. A table that cannot be made fails the binding with `ENOMEM`, the global GTT's entries cleared again.

Each ring's start points it at the PPGTT, as Linux's `set_pp_dir`: `PP_DIR_DCLV` with every 64 MiB of the 2 GiB valid, `PP_DIR_BASE`, and the PPGTT bit of `RING_MODE_GEN7`, which is the render ring's `GFX_MODE_GEN7`; the render ring's start first sets the PPGTT's caching as Linux's `gen7_ppgtt_enable` does at each hardware init, 64-byte lines in `GAC_ECO_BITS` and write-back in `GAM_ECOCHK`, so that a start after a reset sets them again too. Every request then loads the directory into its ring again, as the next section says.

### Rings and interrupts

The interrupts come in four banks, each an identity, mask and enable register: the GT's, power management's, the display engine's and the south display's, whose events reach the display engine as its PCH event. A bank's bit is a source. `i915_irq_reset` masks and disables every source and forgets every handler; `i915_irq_install` sets the display engine's master enable, without which no source raises the message, once the message is enabled. A source's owner registers a handler for its bits with `i915_irq_register`, which enables them, so that an event latches in the identity register once the bit is unmasked; `i915_irq_unmask` and `i915_irq_mask` then open and close it, and a bit with no handler is never unmasked. A south display source enables the PCH event with it. The master enable and the PCH event belong to no owner and are refused.

`i915_irq_handle` is the device's interrupt, as Linux's `ilk_irq_handler`: it clears the master enable and the south display's enables, so that an event that comes while it runs raises a new message once they are back; reads each identity register, writes the bits back to acknowledge them and hands them to their handlers, each handler once with all its bits, the GT's first, then the display engine's with the south display's behind the PCH event, then power management's; and restores the enables. It counts the message in `irq.messages`, a message that finds no bit set in `irq.spurious`, and a bit that has no handler in `irq.unclaimed`, which it logs, masks and disables, so that a source the firmware left latched raises it once.

The bind starts Haswell's four rings with `i915_gt_init`, under one forcewake reference: the render ring, `rcs0`, the blitter ring, `bcs0`, the video ring, `vcs0`, and the video enhancement ring, `vecs0`, each with the name, class, engine id, reset domain and fault register Linux's `intel_engine_setup` gives it, so that the debugfs files and the error state name an engine as Linux does. Each has a status page, one page, and a ring of 16 KiB, Linux's legacy size, each from the kernel's heap and mapped in the global GTT at the LLC's cache level, as Linux maps its internal objects on an LLC part: the processor writes them through its cache and the chip snoops it. Their GTT space is taken from the top of the GTT down, past the firmware's kept surfaces (`i915_gt_ggtt_take`); on the Lenovo the eight take 80 KiB below 2 GiB, and the PPGTT's directory the 2 MiB below the next 64 KiB boundary under them. Space comes back only when the last taken is given back, and all of it when the rings stop, so a later allocator of the GTT leaves `[gt.ggtt_bottom, total)` alone. Each ring is started as Linux's `xcs_resume`:

* Stopped: `STOP_RING` set in `MI_MODE` and `MODE_IDLE` waited for, 100 ms at most; a timeout is forgiven when the head has reached the tail, as Linux does. Then the head moved to the tail, the ring disabled and the head and tail cleared, twice if the head does not clear.
* Every write of interrupt status into the status page masked (`RING_HWSTAM`), but the render ring's bit 0, which Linux's `set_hwstam` leaves to it, and the ring's interrupts masked (`RING_IMR`); the status page's GTT offset written to its register, `RENDER_HWS_PGA_GEN7`, `BLT_HWS_PGA_GEN7`, `BSD_HWS_PGA_GEN7` or `VEBOX_HWS_PGA_GEN7`, and the command streamer's TLB flushed through `RING_INSTPM`, 2 ms at most, a timeout logged.
* `RING_START`, the PPGTT's registers through `i915_ppgtt_enable_ring`, as the section above says, an empty ring, and `RING_CTL` with the ring's size and `RING_VALID`, waited for 5 ms at most; a ring whose `RING_VALID` does not stay is dead, and the start fails with `-EIO` and its registers logged. Then `STOP_RING` cleared.

Before the rings start, `i915_gt_init` writes what Linux writes to the GT at load. `i915_gt_init_hw` is Haswell's part of `intel_gt_init_hw`: the IDI hash mask when the part has eDRAM, `HSW_MI_PREDICATE_RESULT_2` with the lower slice disabled on a GT2, and the three GT workarounds of `hsw_gt_workarounds_init`, which leave the vertex shader's reference count to the hardware and turn L3's atomics off in `HSW_SCRATCH1` and `HSW_ROW_CHICKEN3`, the two registers the command parser lets a program write to turn them on. With it go the GT's two registers of `hsw_init_clock_gating`, written once: the message channel's interrupt mode in `GEN7_SQ_CHICKEN_MBCUNIT_CONFIG` and stream output's arbitration priority in `GAM_ECOCHK`. The render ring's start, as Linux's `intel_engine_resume`, first writes the six workarounds of `rcs_engine_wa_init`: `MI_MODE`'s asynchronous flip performance mode off, `GFX_MODE`'s explicit TLB invalidation and replay mode, `CACHE_MODE_0_GEN7`'s HiZ raw stall optimization on and RC operational flush off, `CACHE_MODE_1`'s pixel subspan collection off, `GEN7_GT_MODE`'s 16x4 WIZ hashing and `HSW_HALF_SLICE_CHICKEN3`'s sample C performance bit. The other rings have none. Each is read back as Linux's `wa_verify` does, and one the chip did not keep is logged as `workarounds: NAME: lost` with the register and counted in `gt.wa_lost`; the bind logs `workarounds: applied: 3 of the GT and 6 of rcs0, 0 lost`. A reset of the render domain puts the engine's registers back to their power-on values and a reset of the whole GPU the GT's as well, so the engine's are written at every start of the ring and `i915_gt_init_hw` runs again after a reset of the whole GPU, as in Linux.

A ring that fails tears every ring down, and the bind goes on without them, logging `i915: rings: not started; the GPU takes no work`. Each ring's user interrupt is then registered, enabled and left masked: `GT_RENDER_USER_INTERRUPT`, `GT_BLT_USER_INTERRUPT` and `GT_BSD_USER_INTERRUPT` in the GT's bank and the video enhancement ring's `PM_VEBOX_USER_INTERRUPT` in power management's, as Linux's `hsw_irq_enable_vecs` has it, so that after the bind the master enable and the four user interrupts are the only enables set and every mask is set.

Commands go into a ring with `i915_ring_begin`, which returns room for an even number of dwords: a command that would cross the ring's end starts again at 0, the end filled with `MI_NOOP`, and a ring without room, 64 bytes always left between the tail and the head, waits up to 10 ms for the chip's head to move, a wait named `ring space`. `i915_ring_advance` orders the writes before the tail's and writes `RING_TAIL`.

A breadcrumb is `MI_STORE_DWORD_INDEX` of the ring's next sequence number into the status page's dword 0x40, Linux's `I915_GEM_HWS_SEQNO`, and `MI_USER_INTERRUPT`; sequence numbers are 32 bits, skip 0, and compare in the order of numbers that wrap. `i915_engine_wait` waits for one with a timeout: the user interrupt is unmasked for the wait, the ring's `RING_IMR` before its bank's mask as Linux's `gen6_irq_enable` and `hsw_irq_enable_vecs`, and masked again with the last waiter. A process sleeps on the ring's queue until the interrupt's handler wakes it or its deadline in ticks passes; with no process, as at boot, or no message vector, the wait polls the status page in a bounded wait named `breadcrumb`.

First light, `i915_firstlight`, runs at the bind under `rach2.i915.firstlight`, before any display code: the render ring writes 0x1915f157 with `MI_STORE_DWORD_IMM` to a page mapped in the GTT for it, followed by a breadcrumb, with the user interrupt unmasked; the driver polls for the breadcrumb, 50 ms at most, reads the page back, and then waits up to 10 ms for the interrupt, which counts as come when the handler ran or, with the processor's interrupts off at boot, when it is latched in `GTIIR`. The page is given back whatever the outcome.

### RC6 and the frequency

RC6 powers the GT down while it is idle. `i915_rc6_init` turns off whatever RC6 the firmware left, under forcewake; `i915_rc6_enable` writes Linux's values for Haswell: the wake rate limits, an evaluation interval of 125000 and idle hysteresis of 25, each engine's idle limit of 10, and the thresholds, RC6's 50000 units of 1.28 us, 64 ms, among them; and reads the pcode's RC6 voltages, which Linux corrects only on graphics version 6. Unparked, `GEN6_RC_CONTROL` enables RC6 under the hardware's timers; parked, it enables RC6 alone and `GEN6_RC_STATE` asks for it at once. `i915_rc6_residency_ms` reads `GEN6_GT_GFX_RC6`, a 32-bit count of 1.28 us units, under the register lock, adds its difference from the last read modulo 2^32 to a 64-bit total, so a wrap between reads is counted, and turns the total into microseconds rounded up and then milliseconds rounded to the nearest, as Linux.

The pcode is the chip's power controller, reached through a mailbox: `i915_pcode_read` and `write` find `GEN6_PCODE_MAILBOX` free or fail with `-EAGAIN`, write the data and then the command with `GEN6_PCODE_READY`, and wait up to 20.5 ms for the ready bit to clear, a wait named `pcode mailbox`; the low byte's status maps to an errno as Linux's.

The frequency is a multiple of 50 MHz. `i915_rps_init` reads RP0, RP1 and RPn from `GEN6_RP_STATE_CAP`, RPe from the pcode's duty cycle control (`HSW_PCODE_DYNAMIC_DUTY_CYCLE_CONTROL`, bits 15:8), held between RPn and RP0, or RP1 without the pcode, and the overclocking limit from `GEN6_READ_OC_PARAMS` where its bit 31 is set; the soft limits start at RPn and RP0, boost at the highest frequency allowed. `i915_rps_enable` writes the down timeout and idle hysteresis, turns RPS's hardware on with the busy and idle averages, sets RPn, and writes the ring's frequency table to the pcode, a ring frequency for each GPU frequency from the highest down, as Linux's `gen6_update_ring_freq`. Each request writes `GEN6_RPNSWREQ`, then the thresholds of its power mode, low, between or high, whose up interval is 16, 13 or 10 ms at 95% busy and down interval 32 ms at 85%, the interrupt limits and `GEN6_PMINTRMSK`, so that the chip raises no up interrupt at the top and no down at the bottom.

Unparked, RPS registers its handler for the up threshold, down threshold and down timeout in the power management bank and unmasks them. The handler masks what it took and records it; the work runs from `irq.deferred` once the handler has dropped the interrupt mask's lock, since Rach2 has no workqueue, and moves the frequency as Linux's `rps_work`: an up step that doubles while the GT stays busy, to RP0; on the down timeout to RPe and then RPn; a down step that doubles; each within the soft limits, and unmasks the events again. Park masks and disables the events, clears them, asks for RPn under forcewake, so that the pcode takes it before the GT sleeps, and sets the frequency of the next start a step below the last, the steps doubling at each park, never below RPe; unpark asks for that frequency. `i915_gt_unpark` unparks RC6 and then RPS, and `i915_gt_park` parks RPS and then RC6.

Two things raise the clock for a client that waits on the GPU, as on Linux. The waitboost, `i915_rps_boost`, Linux's `intel_rps_boost`, marks a request the chip has not begun and counts it a waiter until it retires (`i915_rps_retire`, from the retire); a boost asked after the retire is not counted. The first waiter, while RPS is active below the boost frequency, runs the work at once, which asks for the boost frequency, up to the overclocking limit past the soft maximum, and holds it while any waiter is left; a new boost frequency runs it again. `i915_request_wait` asked for `I915_WAIT_PRIORITY`, as every wait on a request's fence, GEM_WAIT, SET_DOMAIN and the wait for the GT's idle ask, boosts a request the chip has not begun once its spin ends, before it asks for the signal, as Linux's `i915_request_wait_timeout`. `i915_gem_object_wait`, under GEM_WAIT, SET_DOMAIN and the kernel's own waits for an object, first boosts every request of the object's reservation the chip has not begun, as Linux's `i915_gem_object_boost`, and a page flip boosts at the next vblank the request drawing its framebuffer, if the chip has not begun it by then, as Linux's `intel_display_rps_boost_after_vblank`: the flip's in-fence whenever it has one, else the object's pending write; a fence that is not an i915 request is not boosted, and the pending write is not taken in its place. Each flip keeps its own boost, so a later commit on the same plane does not take the pending flip's away, and a pipe that goes off ends its pending boosts at once, boosting a request the chip has not begun, as Linux's `drm_crtc_vblank_off` wakes their waits; a legacy cursor update is not boosted. A blocking commit's wait for its plane fences boosts through `i915_request_wait`; a nonblocking one that the DRM core defers on them boosts each plane's fence that is a request the chip has not begun when the core's `commit_fence_wait` says Linux's tail would wait for it, the first plane's as the commit is queued and each next one's as the one before signals, as Linux's `intel_atomic_commit_fence_wait`, which Linux's commit queues at once, waits for each through `i915_fence_wait`; a merge or another driver's fence is not boosted there, as Linux waits for those in `dma_fence_default_wait`. A request has begun once its engine has passed the one before it (`i915_request_started`). The interactive mode, `i915_rps_mark_interactive`, counts the page flips pending, from the commit until its flip ends at the vblank, as Linux's `intel_prepare_plane_fb` and `intel_cleanup_plane_fb` mark them; a commit holds one mark, as Linux's `state->rps_interactive`, which the last of its pipes to flip lets go, so a commit on two displays counts once and keeps the mode until the second has flipped; while any is pending the power mode is high power, whose up interval of 10 ms reacts before a frame ends, and the first switches to it at once. The thresholds stay 95% and 85% in every mode, as Linux 6.12's. `i915_rps_boost_info` in debugfs shows the waiters, the flips pending and the boosts counted.

The card's files in /sys are Linux's: `gt_act_freq_mhz`, `gt_cur_freq_mhz`, `gt_boost_freq_mhz`, `gt_max_freq_mhz`, `gt_min_freq_mhz`, `gt_RP0_freq_mhz`, `gt_RP1_freq_mhz` and `gt_RPn_freq_mhz` in cardN, and `rc6_enable` and `rc6_residency_ms` in its `power` directory, made or found; i915(4) gives their values and writes.

### The OpRegion and the video BIOS table

The firmware describes the machine's outputs and panel in the video BIOS table (VBT), which the bind reads after the GTT and before it registers the device, so that the display part finds the ports, the panel and its power sequence already decoded in `i915->vbt`. As Linux's `intel_opregion_setup`, it reads the OpRegion's address from ASLS in the configuration space, maps its 8 KiB uncached, the cache type ACPICA gives the same pages, and refuses it without the signature `IntelGraphicsMem`. It sets CHPD in mailbox 1, since the driver handles hotplug itself, and clears ARDY in mailbox 3, since it answers no ASLE request; mailbox 2, SWSCI, is ignored from version 3. The table is the one RVDA names in mailbox 3, a physical address on version 2.0 and an offset from the OpRegion from 2.1, when it is valid; else mailbox 4's, which runs to mailbox 5, or to the OpRegion's end when there is none. Without either, the option ROM's shadow is searched on 4-byte boundaries for the signature `$VBT`, as Linux's `oprom_get_vbt`, when the chip decoded I/O and memory at boot.

`intel_bios_init` copies the table, checks its header, the BDB's and every block's size against the table's end, and copies each block it decodes into a buffer of the block's size or Linux's structure's, whichever is larger, zero past the block, so that a field a version lacks reads as zero, as Linux's `init_bdb_block`. Fields are read by their byte offsets, not through packed structures, so the same code serves both architectures. The LFP data's pointers, absolute in the table, are made relative to the data block and checked as Linux's `validate_lfp_data_ptrs`: sixteen entries of one size in step, each fp_timing ending in 0xffff; malformed ones are dropped, missing ones from version 155 made up from the data block. Each child device becomes an encoder on the port its DVO port names, and a port's lookup finds its first, as Linux uses the first; a child on DDI A loses DVI, which the port cannot carry. Without a table, or with an invalid one, `init_vbt_missing_defaults` gives each port Linux's defaults: eDP on DDI A, DisplayPort and HDMI on B to D, nothing on E.

The panel is decoded per encoder, as Linux's `intel_bios_init_panel`: its type is the table's when it names one, the EDID's product id matched to an entry's PnP id when the table gives 0xff, and 0 as a last fallback that only the late call allows; the LFP data's mode, the DRRS type, dithering, the backlight's PWM, the eDP block's bits a colour, fast link settings and power sequence, and the power conservation features follow. On the Lenovo the panel is type 2, with a 1024x768 mode at 65 MHz that Linux sets aside for the EDID's 1366x768, static DRRS, 18 bits a pixel and the power sequence 200 ms up, 50 ms down, 500 ms between cycles and 1 and 200 ms around the backlight.

### Contexts and requests

A context, `struct i915_gem_context`, is a file's: the default context 0, made at open, and the others numbered from 1, the lowest free id first, as Linux's xarray gives them. As in Linux a new context is a proto context, whose parameters, its engine map among them, can be set until the first lookup that uses it finalizes it. Each engine slot, the default map's `rcs0` and `bcs0` or the user's map's, holds a `struct intel_context`, the state a request runs in, which holds a reference on its context; closing the context drops its states, which breaks the cycle. CONTEXT_CREATE walks the extension chain as Linux's `i915_user_extensions`, 512 at most, and every refusal of the context calls is Linux's on Haswell with ring submission: no full PPGTT, so `VM` is `ENODEV`; no scheduler or preemption, so `PRIORITY`, `SSEU` set and `PERSISTENCE` cleared are `ENODEV`; no execlists or GuC, so load balancing is `ENODEV`, a bond is checked and ignored, and parallel submission is `EINVAL`.

On the render ring a state has an image of `I915_HSW_CXT_PAGES`, 17 pages, Linux's `HSW_CXT_TOTAL_SIZE`, from the kernel's pages and mapped in the global GTT at the LLC's level through `i915_ggtt_reserve`, made at its first request. The first request of the GT records the default image: a state of the GT's own loads three registers (`INSTPM`'s forced ordering, Linux's one context workaround of graphics version 7, and again `CACHE_MODE_0_GEN7`'s RC op flush off and `CACHE_MODE_1`'s pixel subspan collection off, two of the render engine's) with its restore inhibited, then a request on the kernel's state of the render ring saves it, and its pages become the default that every new image is copied from, so that no context starts from what another left. A recording that does not finish in 200 ms wedges the GPU: `gt.wedged` is set and logged, and every request and CONTEXT_CREATE after it fails with `EIO`. The blitter keeps no image.

A request, `struct i915_request`, runs on one ring in order. Its creation unparks the GT when it was parked, takes the ring's next sequence number, and keeps room for its breadcrumb in the ring (`reserved`), as Linux's `intel_ring_begin`. Each request loads the PPGTT's directory into its ring, as Linux's `switch_mm`: the caches' writes flushed, `PP_DIR_DCLV` and `PP_DIR_BASE` loaded with `MI_LOAD_REGISTER_IMM`, `PP_DIR_BASE` stored to the scratch page as a stall, the TLBs invalidated through `RING_INSTPM`, a flush, and the caches invalidated, so that a table made since the ring last loaded the directory is seen. A render request whose state is not the ring's last switches to it as Linux's `mi_set_context` on Haswell: arbitration off, the sleep messages of the other three rings held through their `RING_PSMI_CTL`, `MI_SET_CONTEXT` with the image's GTT offset, `MI_MM_SPACE_GTT`, `MI_SAVE_EXT_STATE_EN` and `MI_RESTORE_EXT_STATE_EN`, or `MI_RESTORE_INHIBIT` for an image never saved, the messages released and read back, arbitration on. The request keeps the state it switched from until it retires, since the chip saves into that image during the switch. Then the caches are invalidated, the render ring with a stalled `PIPE_CONTROL` and the blitter with `MI_FLUSH_DW`. The breadcrumb is Linux's gen7: on the render ring a `PIPE_CONTROL` that flushes and writes the sequence number into the status page through the global GTT, then `MI_USER_INTERRUPT`, 6 dwords; on the blitter `MI_FLUSH_DW` storing it into the status page, 32 more stores of it, and the interrupt, 104 dwords, as Linux's `gen7_emit_breadcrumb_xcs`. A command the ring refuses fails the request with `EIO` and rewinds the ring's tail and its last state, so the ring holds only whole requests.

A request's fence is signalled from the interrupt: the user interrupt is unmasked while the ring has requests, and the handler, through the ring's `irq_notify`, signals each completed fence under the ring's lock. Requests retire in process context, at each request's creation and wait and at `i915_retire_requests`, in order, each moving the ring's head to its end so that its room is free again, as Linux's `i915_request_retire`, and dropping the interrupt's reference with the last one.

The GT parks when Linux 6.12's would on Haswell. Its ring submission puts every context of an engine on one timeline, and a request holds its engine, and the engine the GT, awake until it retires; Linux retires a timeline at once only when its last request signals to a listener, which `signal_irq_work` hands to the engine's `retire_work`, and otherwise at the retire worker's pass, once a second, or a wait for the GT's idle. Here each new request sets its ring's `unretired`, and the GT parks only when no ring has a request on its list, one being built, or `unretired` set. A request is being built, the ring's `building`, from its creation to its publication on the ring's list, both under the ring's lock, as Linux holds the timeline's mutex from `i915_request_create` to `i915_request_add`; meanwhile neither a signal nor the worker clears `unretired`. The flag is cleared when the ring's last request signals after a fence callback, a sleeper or poller on its fence, or `i915_request_wait` asked for its signal while the request ran, even one that has since left: the request's `signaling`, Linux's `I915_FENCE_FLAG_SIGNAL`, by which `signal_irq_work` hands the timeline to retirement. A request whose breadcrumb is already written when its signal is asked for signals at once and does not count, as Linux's `insert_breadcrumb` signals it without the flag; and a query that reads the breadcrumb signals the fence at once, as Linux's `i915_fence_signaled`, after which the ring's next signal or retirement still applies the rule to a request with `signaling` set. So GEM_WAIT, BUSY and a callback asked for between the breadcrumb and its interrupt find the request done. The wait, as Linux's, first polls a request the chip has begun for 8 us and asks for nothing when it is done by then. Every wait on a request's fence is that wait. GEM_WAIT, SET_DOMAIN and the kernel's waits for an object wait for each leaf of its reservation's fences through it, in one walk that holds the timeline point it is at, so that no merge of them asks for a signal and a leaf that signals meanwhile moves no other out of the walk, as Linux's `i915_gem_object_wait_fence`; any other wait, `dma_fence_wait_until`, asks for the signal and then reaches it through the fence's `wait`, as Linux's `dma_fence_wait_timeout` enables signalling before `i915_fence_wait`. A merge of fences or a timeline point asks its fences for their signals only once it is waited for or given a callback, and a query asks for nothing. A retirement signals every request it takes off by the same rule, one whose breadcrumb the chip wrote after the retirement's own signalling pass among them, as Linux's retirement lets go of a request's hold on its context in every path. The worker, `ki915`, then retires at once, having taken the notice and cleared it in one step under the submission's lock. The flag is also cleared by `i915_gt_retire_requests`, Linux's `intel_gt_retire_requests`, for each ring whose last request is complete, which the worker runs on the first whole second after a second from the unpark, and again on the first whole second after a second from each pass, as Linux's `round_jiffies_up_relative(HZ)` rounds it, 1 to 2 s apart, waking for it as for the heartbeat, and which `i915_gt_wait_for_idle`, the default state's recording and debugfs's `DROP_RETIRE` run. So a client's next frame finds the GT awake, at the clock RPS left it, where a park at each idle moment would ask for RPn and start the frame a step lower, down to RPe; while it is idle and awake, RC6 and RPS's down timeout lower its power, as on Linux. `i915_request_wait` waits in slices of a second, rereading the status page between them, so that a lost interrupt costs a second, not the wait; a wait of 0 is a test, a negative one has no end, and a timeout is `ETIME`. Without a message vector, or with no process, the breadcrumb is polled. The unplug fails every request not done with `EIO`, signals its fence, and frees the GT's states, the scratch page and the default image.

GETPARAM answers from a table of Linux 6.12's fixed answers for a Haswell GT2 with its four rings, with MMAP_GTT_VERSION 6.18's 5, which the Lenovo gave under Linux 6.18.58 and which announces the partial maps from inside an object that 6.12 already makes, then the answers of the device: its id, revision, rings, LLC and eDRAM, and the slices, subslices and EUs, which Linux works out for Haswell from the part, one slice of two subslices, and the EUs a subslice from `HSW_PAVP_FUSE1`, 10, 8 or 6. I915_QUERY answers the topology, the engines, and one memory region of system memory, and its items Linux answers `ENODEV` for without perf or GuC. GET_RESET_STATS gives a context's guilty and active counts and, to a privileged process, the device's resets. REG_READ reads only what Linux's list allows on Haswell, the render ring's timestamp, under forcewake: with `I915_REG_READ_8B_WA`, as Mesa asks, the high half, the low and the high again until the highs agree, and without it the low half and then the high, as 32-bit Linux's `readq` does, so that a carry between them may show.

### Buffers

A buffer object, `struct drm_i915_gem_object`, embeds the DRM core's object, whose anonymous pages ([`../drm_gem.c`](../drm_gem.c)) are its own, written back; an import has none and reaches another device's frames through its dma-buf. Its state is Linux's on an LLC part: `read_domains` and `write_domain` among CPU, GTT and WC, its cache level, and `cache_coherent` and `cache_dirty`, which say whether the processor's caches must be flushed before the GPU or the aperture sees its pages. GEM_CREATE_EXT makes the same object after its extensions, as Linux's: MEMORY_REGIONS may name system memory alone, the one region a Haswell program sees, since Linux keeps stolen memory's region private; PROTECTED_CONTENT and SET_PAT answer `ENODEV`, for want of PXP and of graphics 12.70; and `NEEDS_CPU_ACCESS`, which asks for a second region to spill to, `EINVAL`. The extension chains of this call, of CONTEXT_CREATE and of EXECBUFFER2 are walked by [`i915_user_extensions.c`](i915_user_extensions.c). An object made by GEM_CREATE is cached in the LLC, so it is coherent and the processor never flushes for it; one set uncached stays `cache_dirty`, as in Linux, so that PWRITE still writes it through its pages and the flush comes when it moves to the GTT's domain.

A binding, `struct i915_vma`, maps the object, or a view of it, into the global GTT: space from the bottom up below the rings' at the top, aligned for a fence, its entries written at the object's cache level, and a pin count that keeps it there. A binding inside the aperture is `mappable`, and one whose node holds a fence of the object's layout is `fenceable`. `i915_vma_unbind` refuses a pinned binding with `-EAGAIN` and one whose object the GPU may still use with `-EBUSY`, so that no entry the chip may read changes under it; otherwise it revokes the programs' maps of it, flushes the writes made through the aperture, takes its fence and clears its entries, in Linux's order. Where Linux's unbind waits in `i915_vma_sync`, a caller that may sleep waits with `i915_gem_object_wait_idle` first and looks again: placing, moving or retiling a busy object's binding, and pinning a userptr object's pages again. That wait asks for the signal of every busy fence of the reservation before it waits, as Linux's `i915_active_wait` enables its fences, so that a last request the chip finishes during the wait's spin hands its engine to retirement; GEM_WAIT spins first and asks only if the request is still running, as Linux's `i915_request_wait`. An object stays until the last request that uses it retires, since each request holds a reference on its batch's objects, as Linux's active bindings keep an object; a program may close a handle, or exit, while its batch runs, and the object's bindings and pages go only once the chip is past it. The unplug unmaps every program's map of every object, so that its next touch faults to a device that refuses it.

A program maps an object through one of the node's fake offsets, which MMAP_OFFSET gives from the DRM core's mmap space, one for each kind. The kinds of its pages fault in the object's frames with the cache type they name. The GTT's kind faults in the aperture's page: the fault binds the whole object inside the aperture when it fits without evicting or waiting, or else a view of at least 1 MiB around the page in whole tile rows, which for a smaller object is a partial view of the whole of it, a binding apart, so that a fault never waits for a binding the GPU still uses above the aperture, as Linux's `vm_fault_gtt`; a wait for the GPU to free room takes a signal, and a signal, or no room or fence yet, runs the access again, as Linux's `i915_error_to_vmf_fault` gives `VM_FAULT_NOPAGE`; it takes a fence for a tiled object, and maps the page write-combining. Every kind of map lets ptrace and `/proc/PID/mem` reach the object's pages, a GTT map's as they lie in memory, after the aperture's writes are flushed, as Linux's `vm_access`. The binding is marked as mapped by programs, so that unbinding it, changing its layout or giving its fence to another binding unmaps it, and the next touch faults again. GEM_MMAP, the old CPU map that libdrm_intel's `drm_intel_bo_map` and Intel's i965 VA driver use, maps the object's pages itself, write-back or under `I915_MMAP_WC` write-combining, as Linux's `i915_gem_mmap_ioctl` maps the object's shmem file: the kernel's `mmap_device` puts the region in the caller's address space where mmap would, the region holding the object, without a descriptor or an offset of the node.

The 32 fence registers detile the aperture's accesses to a tiled object. A fence is written as Linux's `gen6_write_fence`, the low word cleared first, the high word, then the low word with the valid bit; a binding takes a free fence, or the least recently used one no fault holds, whose old binding's writes are flushed and maps revoked first. The swizzle is read at bind as Linux's `detect_bit_6_swizzle` for an LLC part: while the firmware's tiled surface is kept, as `DISP_ARB_CTL` says; otherwise 9 and 10 for X tiles and 9 for Y when the two channels' sizes in `MAD_DIMM_C0` and `C1` match, and none when they differ. When it swizzles, the bind sets `DISP_ARB_CTL`, `TILECTL` and `ARB_MODE` to match, as Linux's `i915_gem_init_swizzling`.

The kernel reads and writes the aperture, for PREAD and PWRITE through it, through four windows of 2 MiB that `i915_aperture_kmap` maps write-combining, or uncached without PAT, and moves to the page asked for, the least recently used first, as Linux's `io_mapping`. A backend that stands in for the aperture gives each page through `aperture_page` and takes back what was written through `aperture_sync`, which the driver calls before it reads the aperture and after it writes there, and when an object's writes through the aperture must reach memory.

### Memory

The aperture is the first 256 MiB of the 2 GiB global GTT, and every binding shares the GTT, so a working set larger than either runs by eviction, as Linux's `i915_gem_evict_something`. The bindings are on a list in the order of their last use, a binding moved to its end when it is made or pinned again. A binding that finds no room adds the idle bindings in the range it needs to a scan, the least recently used first, each counted as free, until a hole of its size fits; of those scanned, only the ones inside the hole are undone, so that a binding used after the hole was found stays, and the new binding goes in the hole. A binding is idle when it is not pinned and its object has no unsignalled fence. When the idle ones make no room, the GPU is idled, so that its requests retire and their bindings fall idle, and the scan runs once more, as Linux's `ggtt_flush`; a call that may not block, EXECBUFFER2's first pass among them, fails with ENOSPC instead. Placing a binding at a given offset waits for each busy binding in its way and undoes it. The GTT fault tries the whole object first without evicting, then a view that evicts, as Linux's does; PREAD and PWRITE through the aperture take a page without evicting and fall back to the object's pages. GET_APERTURE gives the GTT's size, and as what is available that size less what pinned bindings, the firmware's kept surfaces and the GT's own pages hold.

MADVISE marks an object's pages as needed or not. An object marked DONTNEED that has no pages is purged at once; one that has them is purged by the driver's shrinker ([`../../mm/pressure.h`](../../mm/pressure.h)) when reclaim asks for memory, if the GPU has finished with it, none of its bindings is pinned and no dma-buf of it exists, since another device's import maps its frames without a pin where Linux's attachment pins the pages: its bindings are undone, the programs' maps of it revoked, and its pages freed. A purged object keeps its handle and answers EFAULT to a use of its pages, as Linux's. An allocation of an object's pages that finds no memory runs reclaim once and tries again before it fails with ENOMEM.

USERPTR makes an object over a page-aligned range of the caller's memory. Its pages are pinned when it is first used, by the address space that made it alone, and a notifier on the range ([`../../mm/notify.h`](../../mm/notify.h)) lets them go before what is mapped there changes: an unmap, a change of protection, a fork, a copy-on-write break, a page dropped, or the address space's end. The notifier undoes the unpinned bindings of an idle object and points a pinned one at the scratch page; a busy object's unpinned bindings keep the old frames until the GPU is done, as Linux keeps a busy object's pinned pages. The frames go then, or with the last pin while one holds them, and the next use waits for the GPU and pins the range again, EFAULT once it is unmapped. Such an object refuses PREAD, PWRITE, maps, tiling and every caching but the LLC's, and PRIME does not export it, as Linux refuses each.

### Submission

EXECBUFFER2 runs a program's batch on a ring as Linux 6.12's `i915_gem_execbuffer.c` does with ring submission. The call checks its flags and counts, refuses `I915_EXEC_SECURE` to all but a privileged master with EPERM; it reads the fence array or the extension chain, whose one extension, Linux's timeline fences, names syncobjs at points, and the in-fence, and reserves the out-fence's descriptor first, so that the sync file is installed only once the request is added. It finds the context from `rsvd1`'s low word, the ring from the legacy ring flags, `I915_EXEC_BSD` with any of its ring flags naming Haswell's one video ring as Linux's `eb_select_legacy_ring`, or from the context's engine map, and the objects by handle or, under `I915_EXEC_HANDLE_LUT`, by index; an object may be named twice, as Linux 6.x allows.

The objects are placed as Linux's `eb_pin_vma` and `eb_reserve` place them. Each first tries its binding's offset, or the offset the program presumed, without evicting, then, unless it is `EXEC_OBJECT_PINNED`, any place it fits without evicting. The rest are placed in up to three passes, the pinned objects first in each: the first without waiting for the GPU, the second after unpinning all but the pinned objects, the third after unpinning all and evicting the whole GTT once the GPU is idle, `i915_gem_evict_vm`. A batch with relocations that is not pinned keeps above 256 KiB. An object that moved has its offset marked and its relocations made, and the offsets are then written back to the program's entries.

A relocation follows `eb_relocate_entry`: one whose presumed offset is the target's is skipped; a bad write domain, offset or alignment is EINVAL; the rest write the target's offset plus the delta into the object through the processor, or for an import through a page of the aperture, and write the new presumed offset back into the program's entry. A batch whose length the call gives goes through the command parser, whoever the caller is, as the next section says; a batch without a length runs as it is, unprivileged.

The request holds a reference to each of the call's objects and the parser's shadow until it retires, as Linux's request keeps its bindings active, so that an object closed while its batch runs keeps its pages and binding until the chip is past it. The request then waits for its fences: the in-fence, or under `I915_EXEC_FENCE_SUBMIT` only that request's submission, and the fence of every syncobj the fence array marks `I915_EXEC_FENCE_WAIT`, or, of the timeline fences, the fence of each point named that has not signalled and gone, whatever its flags, as on Linux; a point to wait for that the timeline has not reached is refused with EINVAL. Each object, the last first, has its processor caches flushed, waits for its last writer unless `EXEC_OBJECT_ASYNC`, and takes the request's fence in its reservation, as the writer under `EXEC_OBJECT_WRITE`, which moves it to the render domain. The ring runs `MI_BATCH_BUFFER_START` with the flags Linux gives Haswell, `MI_BATCH_PPGTT_HSW` and `MI_BATCH_NON_SECURE_HSW` on the render ring, `MI_BATCH_NON_SECURE_I965` on the blitter and none for a privileged batch, or for a parsed batch first its shadow, privileged, and then the shadow's trampoline, unprivileged, after the four streamout write offsets are cleared under `I915_EXEC_GEN7_SOL_RESET`, which only the render ring takes. The request is added even when its commands fail, with the error on its fence; the `I915_EXEC_FENCE_SIGNAL` syncobjs and the out-fence take its fence, a timeline's as a new point, whose chain record the call made before the request so that the add cannot fail. A request with fences to wait for reaches the ring from `ki915`, the submission worker, which `i915_request_create` starts, since its start may sleep, and which hands the chip each request whose last fence has signalled. A fence of another driver is waited for 10 s at most, Linux's `CONFIG_DRM_I915_FENCE_TIMEOUT`: the worker wakes at the deadline, takes the callback off and hands the request to the chip with `ETIMEDOUT` on its fence, an error that still lets it run, as Linux's `timer_i915_sw_fence_wake`.

### The command parser

Haswell's command streamer turns some commands of an unprivileged batch into no-ops, `MI_LOAD_REGISTER_IMM` among them, which transform feedback, conditional rendering and queries need; Linux's parser lets through what it finds safe and runs it privileged, and hasvk refuses a Haswell whose GETPARAM gives no parser version. `i915_cmd_parser.c` is Linux 6.12's `i915_cmd_parser.c` on graphics 7. EXECBUFFER2 makes a shadow object of the batch's length and the 8 bytes of the trampoline, readies the batch for the processor's reads as Linux's `i915_gem_object_prepare_read`, and `i915_cmd_parser` copies the batch into the shadow a page at a time, zeroing the rest, and reads its commands from there, so that the program cannot change what was checked. Each command is looked up in the ring's tables, Linux's `gen7_common_cmds`, `gen7_render_cmds` and `hsw_render_cmds` on the render ring `gen7_common_cmds`, `gen7_blt_cmds` and `hsw_blt_cmds` on the blitter, `gen7_common_cmds` and `gen7_video_cmds` on the video ring and `gen7_common_cmds` and `gen7_vecs_cmds` on the video enhancement ring, the last matching entry first as Linux's hash table gives them; a command no table holds is passed over by the length field its client's mask gives, and one of a client the ring does not run fails the call with EINVAL. A command is refused when its entry says so, when it names a register outside the ring's allowlist, `gen7_render_regs` and `hsw_render_regs` or `gen7_blt_regs`, the video rings having none, or writes a masked register other than by `MI_LOAD_REGISTER_IMM` with an allowed value, or when one of its masked dwords is set: the global GTT bit, `PIPE_CONTROL`'s interrupt, register write and status page store, `MI_FLUSH_DW`'s interrupt and stores, `MEDIA_VFE_STATE`'s register access. Every `MI_BATCH_BUFFER_START` is refused, since on graphics 7 Linux never makes the jump allowlist that would follow one. A command past the batch, or a batch without `MI_BATCH_BUFFER_END`, fails the call with EINVAL.

The shadow is bound in the global GTT and started twice: privileged from its start, then unprivileged from its end, where the trampoline is. A batch that passes runs privileged from the copy and the trampoline is `MI_BATCH_BUFFER_END`. A refused batch has its copy's first dword made `MI_BATCH_BUFFER_END` and the trampoline `MI_BATCH_BUFFER_START` with `MI_BATCH_NON_SECURE_HSW` to the original, which runs unprivileged, the chip turning what it must into no-ops, and the call returns 0: version 8's rule that a refused command is the chip's to drop, not EINVAL. GETPARAM's `CMD_PARSER_VERSION` is 10, since every ring parses. The aliasing PPGTT, once made, maps the shadow where the global GTT does, so its one binding serves both starts.

### Binding

`i915_probe` walks the ids and binds function 0 of each match whose BAR 0 is set. The bind reads the board's ids, the GMCH control word and BDSM, which give the GTT's size and the stolen memory; turns on memory decoding and bus mastering; and then, as Linux's `ilk_irq_reset` on Haswell, masks every source, clears every enable, the display engine's master enable among them, and clears every identity register twice: the display engine's, the GT's, power management's and the south display's, with the error, panel self refresh and south error registers. Then it brings up the base, in Linux's order: forcewake, the display power well, stolen memory and the global GTT, as the sections above say; a well that does not come on and stolen memory that fails a check are logged and the bind goes on, and a GTT that cannot come up fails the bind. It then reads the OpRegion and the VBT, as the section on them says. Only then does it take a vector, register its handler and enable the MSI message, so no source the firmware left enabled can raise one, and turn the master enable on; then it turns off the firmware's RC6, reads the frequencies, clearing power management's identity twice more as Linux's `gen6_rps_reset_interrupts`, starts the rings and, under its option, runs first light, and enables RC6 and RPS with the GT parked. Once the device is registered it adds its files in /sys. The vector's handler counts the messages in `irqs` and hands each to `i915_irq_handle`. Before it registers the DRM device it removes the firmware's framebuffer device over the aperture, as Linux's `i915_driver_probe` does, and makes the display, as the next section says. Under `rach2.i915.disable_display`, Linux's `i915.disable_display`, it does neither and logs `i915: display: disabled by rach2.i915.disable_display; the firmware's framebuffer stays`: the device is registered without `DRIVER_MODESET` and `DRIVER_ATOMIC`, and the display, the backlight and the firmware's framebuffer device, which the console keeps drawing on, are as the firmware left them, unlike Linux, which removes that device whatever the option says, so that a trial of the GPU leaves the screen alone; a display that cannot be made is logged, `i915: display: not made: error N`, and the device is registered without `DRIVER_MODESET` and `DRIVER_ATOMIC`, as Linux's i915 on a device without display. A failed registration lets the display go, gives the vector back and restores the command word. The unplug hook turns RPS and RC6 off, lets the display go, masks every source again, gives back the message and its vector, and stops the rings and frees their pages; the release hook frees the mode objects, gives the scratch page back and frees the record.

### Release and bring-up

`i915_handoff.c` adds Linux's directory of the driver, `/sys/bus/pci/drivers/i915`, with the first device bound: `unbind` and `bind`, and, while a device is registered, what Linux's driver core shows of a binding, through `sysfs_pci_bind`: a link to the bound function, the function's `driver` link and `DRIVER=i915` in its uevent, since libudev, and through it IGT's device scan, reads a device's driver from that link (sysfs(5)). Both files take a function's name, `0000:BB:DD.F`, and one write of them runs at a time.

`unbind` releases the device so that nothing of the driver is left on it, in the order of Linux's `i915_driver_remove`. One commit turns the display off, `drm_atomic_helper_shutdown`, which takes the planes, the pipe, the port, its clock and the panel's power down as a mode set does. `i915_gt_quiesce` then stops every ring by force and resets the GPU through `GEN6_GDRST`, as Linux's `intel_engines_release` does before the rings' pages go back, so that the chip reads none of them again. `drm_dev_unplug` then runs the unplug hook of the section above, which fails the queued requests, masks every source, gives the message and its vector back and frees the rings. Last, bus mastering goes off and `pci_unclaim` gives up the kernel's claim on the function, which is what vfio-pci's `bind` waits for. The release is refused, with the device left bound, while a program sleeps with a window of the aperture, `EBUSY`, and when the GPU does not reset, `ETIMEDOUT`, which wedges it; the display is then off until the console or a master sets a mode, and the console is told to look.

A program that still has the card open keeps its record until it closes it, as after any unplug, and the record must not reach a device that is now another owner's. `i915_pci_unmap` gives the record's mappings of the registers and of the GTT back and sets the boundary's sizes to zero, so every read through it is all ones and every write is dropped, and the aperture's size goes to zero with it. The record's slot of the DRM core stays taken until then, so a chip bound again meanwhile is `card1`, as on Linux.

Two users outside the driver drew through the device. The console may still be on the loader's framebuffer, which the Lenovo's firmware puts in the aperture; `drm_client_boot_screen_gone` ([`../drm.h`](../drm.h)) takes it off that screen before the unplug, and it draws nothing until a device gives it a buffer. The boot's backlight driver ([`../../display/intelbl.c`](../../display/intelbl.c)) takes `intel_backlight` back when the display goes and would write the PCH's PWM through a mapping of its own; the release puts the class on a stand-in that keeps the level it is given and writes nothing, until the driver binds again.

`bind` brings up a function that no driver has and whose `driver_override` is clear or names `i915`. It starts from a reset whatever the last owner left: `pci_reset_function`, the function's Advanced Features reset on the HD 4400, then a wait of the panel's power cycle, since the reset drops the panel's power without the sequencer's count of it and `intel_pps_init` takes the last power off as long past; the wait is the released device's `power_cycle` of the VBT, and the eDP specification's T11 and T12, 510 ms, before any device has given one. Then `i915_pci_probe` binds as at boot. It finds no pipe lit and no plane to keep in the GTT, so every entry goes to the scratch page, the guest's among them, which a reset leaves since they are memory; the PWM has no period, so the backlight's levels come from the VBT's frequency, 938 on the Lenovo where the firmware's period gave 937, as Linux reads after the same reset. A reset that fails leaves the function without a driver, and so does a failed bind. Under `rach2.i915fake` the two files release and bind the fake chip's `edu` function the same way, without the reset, which the fake has no state to need; its bind logs `i915: handoff: 0000:BB:DD.F: bound`.

### The display

The display is made at the bind from what the firmware left, as Linux's `intel_modeset_readout_hw_state` reads it. The bind writes only the display's chicken registers of Linux's `hsw_init_clock_gating` and `lpt_init_clock_gating`, which Linux writes once at load (the FBC queue off for asynchronous flips, the vblank masks of panel self refresh, the low-power PCH's partition level gating off and the transcoder's DP0 unit gating off), the panel power sequencer's delays, the AUX channel's messages to the panel and the watermarks: it turns the low-power ones off as Linux's `ilk_wm_get_hw_state` does, then computes every pipe's for the adopted state and writes them, as Linux's `ilk_wm_sanitize` does, so that the first commit's intermediate does not keep the firmware's values; the pipes are written only when a commit asks. The readout, `intel_display_readout`, finds for each pipe the transcoder that feeds it, the eDP transcoder when its input names the pipe and else the pipe's own, and reads from an enabled one the DDI port, the bits a colour, dithering and the lanes, the timings, each register field plus one, the syncs, the link's and the data's M/N and the port's clock from `PORT_CLK_SEL`'s LCPLL; the dot clock of a DisplayPort link is the port's clock times the link's M over N, rounded up, as Linux's `intel_dotclock_calculate`. It reads the pipe's source size, the panel fitter, and an enabled primary plane's format, tiling, surface, stride and offset, and the panel power sequencer's registers. On the Lenovo pipe A is "1366x768": 60 76300 1366 1374 1384 1558 768 771 772 816, 6 bits a colour dithered on one lane of DDI A at 270 MHz, which is what Linux's `i915_display_info` reports.

The device has three CRTCs, each with a primary plane, a sprite and a cursor, Linux's planes for Haswell. When DDI A's strap detects a display and the panel answers on its AUX channel with its capabilities and a fixed mode, as Linux's `intel_edp_init_connector` asks, it also has a TMDS encoder on DDI A, which any pipe may feed, and the eDP connector, always connected; otherwise it has neither, as on the fake chip. A pipe the firmware lit is adopted into the atomic state, as Linux's `intel_find_initial_plane_obj`: on, with the panel's fixed mode and the readout's source size, routed to the eDP connector, its vblanks on, and its primary plane showing a framebuffer made over the firmware's surface, an object of its own over the GTT range the bind kept, which stays kept, since the console may draw through the aperture there again. A commit that keeps a lit pipe's mode, connector and properties programs the planes alone, a fastset; one that changes the mode, the connector's `scaling mode` or `max bpc`, or turns a pipe on or off, is a full mode set, as the next paragraphs say. A pipe turned off has its planes, backlight, transcoder, link and panel power off.

The eDP output is made at the bind as Linux's eDP init makes it. The panel power sequencer takes for each delay the larger of the firmware's registers and the VBT's, or eDP 1.3's limit where both are 0, and keeps T1+T3, T8, T9, T10 and the power cycle from the times it last switched the panel. An AUX message to a panel without power forces its VDD on, waiting out the power cycle and the power up delay, and lets it go five power cycles after the last message, which the connector's next probe does, or at once at the end of the bind when the firmware left the panel dark. The AUX channel's messages follow `hsw_get_aux_clock_divider` and `intel_dp_aux_xfer`, with Linux's bounded retries after timeouts, receive errors, DEFER replies and empty replies. The bind reads the receiver's capabilities, the eDP DPCD and the sink's rates once, writes Intel's OUI as the source's, and reads the EDID over I2C in pieces of 16 bytes. The fixed modes are the EDID's preferred mode and its other modes of that size, or the VBT's mode when the EDID gives none; the connector lists the EDID's modes and the fixed ones, and takes a mode of a fixed mode's size and refresh rate.

A mode set drives the fixed mode whose refresh rate is nearest the mode's, a hertz either way; a mode smaller than the panel is scaled by the pipe's fitter as the connector's `scaling mode` says, Full aspect by default, Full screen or Center, as Linux's `intel_pfit_compute_config`, within its limits: a source of 4096 pixels on pipe A and 2048 on B and C, and a scale down of 1.125 at most, which raises the pipe's pixel rate by its ratio. The link takes the most bits a pixel that the panel's EDID and `max bpc` allow, in steps of 6 down to 18, then the slowest rate and the fewest lanes that carry the mode, or the panel's most rate and lanes for an eDP before 1.4, Linux's `use_max_params`; 18 bits are dithered, and the data and link M/N have the fixed N of Linux and the firmware. The CD clock is read at the bind and fixed, Haswell's way, and a mode whose dot clock or fitter's pixel rate passes it is refused. On the Lenovo the panel's 1366x768 at 60 Hz takes 18 bits on one lane at 2.7 Gb/s, with data M 5333756 over 0x800000 and link M 148159 over 0x80000, which is Linux's own result; the firmware's data M is 5333777.

The mode set runs Linux's `hsw_crtc_disable` and `hsw_crtc_enable` in their orders. The pipe goes off with its planes, the pipe CRC, the backlight, the transcoder, its DDI function, the fitter, the sink told D3, the transcoder's clock, DDI A's buffer, the panel's power and the port's clock; it comes on with DDI A's buffer and the panel's power, the port's clock, the buffer's translations and levels, the sink told D0, the link trained, the pipe's source size, the transcoder's M/N and timings, the fitter, the 8-bit gamma, the line times and watermarks, the transcoder's DDI function and the transcoder, the link on its normal pattern and the backlight, then, when it comes on beside a pipe already on and kept, two of that pipe's vblanks, as Linux's `hsw_mode_set_planes_workaround`, and the pipe CRC again. Training runs clock recovery on pattern 1 and equalization on pattern 2 or 3 with the levels the sink asks for, in Linux's register and DPCD order. A link that fails twice falls back as Linux's `intel_dp_get_link_train_fallback_values` for eDP: first to the panel's most rate and lanes, then to the next lower rate or half the lanes while the mode still fits at 18 bits; the connector's `link-status` goes bad and a hotplug event asks the console or the compositor to set the mode again, which trains the link chosen and sets the status good. When no lower link is left the pipe stays on with its link untrained, as on Linux.

`intel_atomic_commit` pins every framebuffer the commit shows before the DRM core's commit runs, as Linux's `intel_plane_pin_fb`: the object uncached, bound at a multiple of 256 KiB, Linux's alignment behind VT-d, and its processor cache flushed. Since a plane of a chip without a GMCH reads anywhere in the GTT, the whole object is bound inside the aperture when room there needs no wait and anywhere else otherwise, and a remapped view anywhere, as Linux's `intel_fb_pin_to_ggtt` asks for the aperture only on a GMCH. A plane holds the framebuffer it shows and the one before it, which the stall of the next commit has let the plane leave, so the older is unpinned and let go when a commit shows a third. A legacy cursor update has no stall and its write latches only at the next vblank, so the cursor keeps the one before until a vblank has passed since the last write, waiting for one when a vblank fell during that write, and lets go at once an image set and replaced within one frame, which the pipe never latched, as Linux unpins the old cursor from a vblank work. A dumb buffer is linear with its pitch aligned to 64 bytes and maps write-combined, as Linux's; the rows DIRTYFB's damage clips name are flushed from the processor's cache before the plane is written, for the console's shadow and DIRTYFB. The planes have no `FB_DAMAGE_CLIPS` property, which Linux gives only to display version 12 and later, so a commit takes damage from DIRTYFB alone.

The planes are Linux's `i9xx_plane` and `ivb_sprite`. The primary plane takes C8, RGB565, XRGB8888, XBGR8888, XRGB2101010, XBGR2101010 and XBGR16161616F, the sprite XRGB8888, XBGR8888, the two 2101010 formats, the two FP16 formats and the four packed YUV 4:2:2 ones, each linear or X-tiled. ADDFB2 checks a framebuffer against its object's layout as Linux's `intel_framebuffer_init` does: one added without modifiers of an object X-tiled by SET_TILING is X-tiled and of a Y-tiled one is refused, a modifier must agree with the object's tiling and the pitch with its stride, and X tiles must fit the object in whole rows of tiles; while a framebuffer is made of an object, SET_TILING of another layout gives EBUSY. A plane's check clips it to the CRTC without scaling, the primary covering the CRTC, and refuses C8, which needs a LUT the CRTCs do not have, a stride that is not a multiple of 64 bytes or, X-tiled, of 512, a YUV source on an odd pixel, and an offset past 4095. A plane reads a stride of at most 8192 pixels, and at most 32 KiB on the primary plane and 16 KiB on the sprite, Linux's `hsw_primary_max_stride` and `hsw_sprite_max_stride`. A visible plane whose framebuffer's stride passes that, X-tiled or linear at a stride of whole pages, reads a remapped view of the GTT instead, as Linux's `intel_plane_remap_gtt` makes it: the tiles its source rectangle covers, X tiles or a page of each row, from the one that holds the source's corner, laid row after row so that the view's stride is the rectangle's width in tiles. The commit pins that view in place of the whole object (`i915_vma_pin_remapped`), keeps it until the frame after the commit that replaced it, as it keeps the other scanouts, and unpins it on every path. A stride past the limit that cannot be remapped, or a view whose stride still passes it, is refused. So a framebuffer up to 16384 pixels wide, three 3840-pixel monitors side by side among them, shows at four bytes a pixel on the primary plane, and X-tiled on the sprite. A view's stride can still pass the limit, and the plane is then refused, as on Linux: a linear view's rows are whole pages from the page that holds the source's corner, so a 3840-pixel source of four bytes a pixel fits the sprite only from the first 1 KiB of a page, and an FP16 source wider than 2048 pixels never fits the sprite. The source's corner goes into the surface address, page-aligned or a whole tile row, and the rest into the offset register, as Linux's `intel_plane_compute_aligned_offset`; in a remapped view the corner lies in its first tile. The update writes the stride, offset and control, then the surface, whose write arms the rest for the next vblank; the VGA plane is turned off before the primary plane is first shown. The cursor takes ARGB8888, linear with a stride of its width, 64, 128 or 256 pixels wide and, as Linux's `i9xx_cursor_size_ok` lets Haswell, from 8 lines to its width high, its height in `CUR_FBC_CTL` when it is lower than it is wide, or square when turned; it shows a framebuffer's top left corner, which may be larger, and `SIZE_HINTS` lists the squares. It goes anywhere on the CRTC, with its place in `CURPOS` as a sign and a magnitude.

Vblanks are counted from the pipe's frame counter, `PIPE_FRMCOUNT`, through the CRTC's `get_vblank_counter`, so the frames that pass while the interrupt is off are counted when it comes on again, and stamped through its `get_vblank_timestamp`, Linux's `i915_get_crtc_scanoutpos`, with the time the vertical blank ends: the clock when `PIPEDSL` is read, less the lines scanned since that end at the pipe's pixel clock, or plus those still to come inside the blank, so the stamp does not move with the time the deferred work takes to run. The display engine's vblank and flip done events are taken by the interrupt handler under the mask's lock and left for `deferred_display`, which `i915_irq_handle` runs once the lock is dropped, since the DRM core's vblank code may mask the interrupt again. A commit's event is held from `atomic_flush` with a vblank reference and the frame counter read after the planes were written, and sent at the first vblank that finds the counter moved on, when the writes have latched; an async flip, which only the primary plane takes, between X-tiled framebuffers of the same format, stride and place, writes the surface alone and sends its event at flip done, which is unmasked in `atomic_begin`, before the surface is written, since the chip latches only an unmasked event. An event on a pipe that is off is sent at once.

A FIFO underrun is reported as Linux's `intel_fifo_underrun.c` reports it on Haswell. A pipe the firmware lit starts with its underruns unreported, and an idle one with them reported; a pipe reports them from the start of its mode set, before its DDI comes on, or from the first commit of the firmware's picture that sets no mode. Turning a pipe's reporting on clears its underrun in `GEN7_ERR_INT` and unmasks the display engine's error interrupt once every pipe reports. The interrupt clears `GEN7_ERR_INT` of what it holds, and each underrun of a pipe that reports turns its reporting off and masks the interrupt; the deferred work logs it once, as `display: CPU pipe A FIFO underrun`. Each pipe CRC's done bit in the same register reads that frame's CRC, as below.

The backlight is the PCH's PWM that `intelbl.c` attached at boot, now through the device's registers: the bind takes `intel_backlight` over with `backlight_rebind`, keeping its `bl_power`, or registers it when the boot did not. Its levels 0 to the PWM's period map onto the PWM's levels from the VBT's least to the period, rounded to the closest, as Linux's `scale_user_to_hw` and `scale_hw_to_user`: 18 to 937 on the Lenovo, whose VBT gives 5 of 255; level 0 turns the backlight off, as Linux's `intel_backlight_device_update_status`, and `actual_brightness` is 0 while the PWM is off. Its `bl_power` sets the sequencer's backlight enable only while the panel's pipe is on, as Linux's `intel_backlight_enable` runs from the encoder's enable, and is kept for when the pipe comes on; a level at or below the VBT's least comes on at the most, as Linux's. When the device goes the backlight goes back to `intelbl.c`, or to a stand-in that does nothing when the boot registered none.

The pipe CRC is Linux's `ivb_pipe_crc_ctl_reg`, through the DRM core's `crc/control` and `crc/data` ([`../Readme.md`](../Readme.md)): `plane1` the primary plane, `plane2` the sprite, and `pipe`, `pf` and `auto` the panel fitter's output. Pipe A feeding the eDP transcoder past its fitter's power well gives no CRC there, so, as Linux's `hsw_pipe_A_crc_wa`, a source on pipe A sets the pipe again with its input through the well, a full mode set when the fitter is off, and the source's end sets it back. A running source holds the pipe's vblanks. The chip latches a frame's CRC in `PIPE_CRC_RES` at the pipe's CRC done, after the vblank, and signals it in `GEN7_ERR_INT`; the error interrupt reads it then, as Linux's `hsw_pipe_crc_irq_handler`, and the deferred work enters it with the vblank's count, as five values, the CRC and four zeros, as Linux's i915 gives on every chip. The first frame after the source is set, or after a mode set, is dropped, since the chip computes it over a frame it did not see whole; a CRC read at the vblank would still be that frame's.

The watermarks are Linux's for Haswell (`i9xx_wm.c`'s ILK path). The memory latencies come from `MCH_SSKPD` at the bind, level 0's in tenths of a microsecond and the rest in halves: 2, 2, 18, 45 and 80 us on the Lenovo. The firmware's level 0 is replaced at the bind: the Lenovo's leaves a sprite watermark of 56 with no sprite on, which no intermediate can keep, so a compositor's first commit would be refused. A pipe's check computes its watermarks at the five levels from the planes it shows, its mode and its pixel rate, with no low-power level under a scaled sprite, and its intermediate ones, the larger of the old and the new at each level, and refuses a level 0 that does not fit the pipe's share of the FIFO. A commit programs the intermediate watermarks before the planes and the new ones at the vblank that latches them; a mode set programs the new ones at once. The registers merge the levels of every pipe that is on, each low-power level on only while every pipe has it and the merge fits the registers' limits for that many pipes, with the sprite's 5:6 share of the FIFO when one pipe runs a sprite and it keeps more levels on, and the compressor's watermarks on while each enabled level's fits.

### DDI B to D

`intel_display_init` makes DDI B, C and D as Linux's `intel_ddi_init` does on Haswell: each port the VBT names whose bit in `SFUSE_STRAP` says it is wired, with one TMDS encoder that reaches every pipe, a DisplayPort connector when the VBT says the port carries DisplayPort and an HDMI connector when it carries HDMI or DVI, both for a DP++ port. On the Lenovo DDI B is HDMI-A-1 and DDI C DP-1 and HDMI-A-2. A pipe the firmware lit on one of them is not adopted: it is turned off at the bind, with its port and PLL, as Linux's `intel_sanitize_crtc` turns off a pipe it cannot take.

An HDMI connector reads its sink's EDID over GMBUS on the VBT's DDC pin, or the port's default, at 100 kHz: an offset and a read make one indexed transfer, a NAK of the first message is tried once more, and a transfer that does not finish in its time falls back to bit-banging the pin's GPIO lines, as Linux's i2c-algo-bit does, which the bus keeps until a bit-banged transfer fails. A read that fails over GMBUS is tried once more by bit-banging, as Linux's `intel_hdmi_set_edid`. The connector is connected when the EDID is digital, since Haswell's live status is not trusted for HDMI; a DP++ adaptor that answers at 0x40 gives its type, and on a port the VBT calls DP++ its most TMDS clock. A mode is valid when some depth of 12 or 8 bits a colour gives a TMDS clock that the port, at most 300 MHz or the VBT's limit, the adaptor and the sink carry, 165 MHz for a DVI sink without a limit of its own. A mode set takes the deepest such depth within the connector's `max bpc`, limited range for a CEA mode but 640x480 on an HDMI sink, and the AVI, SPD and GCP infoframes, and the vendor one for an HDMI VIC, as Linux packs them; the transcoder runs in HDMI or DVI mode, the port's buffer takes the VBT's translation as its entry 9, and the pipe's colour space conversion compresses limited range.

The PLLs are Linux's shared PLLs for Haswell. An HDMI pipe takes a WRPLL, the one already running its value or else a free one, with the dividers Linux's `hsw_ddi_calculate_wrpll` finds from the LCPLL's 2.7 GHz; a DisplayPort pipe takes the LCPLL output of its link rate, 810, 1350 or 2700 MHz. A commit plans every pipe's PLL before it runs, and refuses with EBUSY a pipe for which no WRPLL is free. A WRPLL is set when its first pipe comes on and turned off after its last goes. The bind reads out the WRPLLs and the SPLL, and turns off one that runs with no pipe.

An external DisplayPort connector is connected while its hotplug pin's live status in `SDEISR` is high and the sink's DPCD reads, and its AUX channel refuses a transfer with ENXIO while the status is low, as Linux's `intel_dp_aux_xfer` does; its EDID gives its modes, with a branch's downstream limits on the dot and TMDS clocks. The link is chosen and trained as for the panel, with the slowest rate and fewest lanes that carry the mode at the most bits a pixel, but from the sink's own rates and lanes, and falls back as Linux does for an external sink.

The PCH's hotplug interrupt, Linux's `ibx_hpd_irq_handler`, reads and clears `PCH_PORT_HOTPLUG`. A DisplayPort port keeps its long and short pulses for `intel_dp_hpd_pulse`: a long pulse probes the connector again, a short one only when the sink's count changed or its DPCD no longer reads, and a sink whose link lost its training sets its pipe again. An HDMI port asks for a probe at once. The hotplug work runs in the DRM worker, probes each connector of a pin that pulsed, forced ones too though their status stays forced, and sends an event for each that changed its status or its EDID; a connector that did not change is probed once more 1 s later, as Linux's `intel_ddi_hotplug`. More than five long pulses on a pin within a second are a storm: the pin is masked, its connectors are polled every 10 s, and the pin is unmasked 2 minutes later.

Pipes B and C, transcoders A to C, the panel fitters and DDI B to D's lanes are in the display power well, so a pipe that uses any of them holds a reference to it while on, as Linux's power domains of a pipe; pipe A driving the eDP transcoder on DDI A without its fitter holds none. Watermarks merge every pipe that is on, as the paragraph above says.

### The fake chip

The fake chip, [`i915_fake.c`](i915_fake.c), is a test backend that lets a program in QEMU reach the driver's real paths where no Intel GPU is: Mesa's loader picks Crocus on it, and the driver's interfaces are proved on it; it draws nothing. A function of QEMU's `edu` device stands in for the chip's. Its configuration space answers with the Lenovo's identity, 8086:0a16 revision 0b on a board of 17aa:380c, class 0x030000, a GMCH control word of 2 MiB of GTT and no stolen memory, and an aperture of 256 MiB, and `/sys` reports that identity for the function. The aperture is the fake's memory, a page for each page of it the driver touches: the fake fills a page from the frame its GTT entry maps, detiled through the fence that covers it, when the driver asks for the page and the entry or fence has changed, and writes it back when the driver syncs it, so a program's GTT map and the kernel's windows both see what the chip would show. Its command word, BARs and MSI capability stay edu's, so the device's message is a real MSI of edu's, which the fake sends by a write to edu's interrupt register at BAR 0 + 0x60, mapped at the attach, while the driver has its message on.

BAR 0's registers are 2 MiB of memory and the GTT's entries the 2 MiB the GMCH control word gives. A register holds what was last written, except where the chip does more, which is what the bind's bounded waits wait for: the masked registers, `FORCEWAKE_MT` and each ring's `MI_MODE`, `INSTPM` and `RING_MODE`, take the bits their high half enables; forcewake's acknowledgement follows the request at once; the GT FIFO always has 64 free entries; a power well's state follows its requests; `INSTPM`'s flush is done when asked; the identity and error registers clear the bits written and the status registers take nothing; `GEN6_GDRST` resets the rings of the domains it names and reads 0 again; `MCH_SSKPD` gives the Lenovo's memory latencies, without which no commit would pass the watermarks' check, the release's among them; `GEN6_RP_STATE_CAP` gives the Lenovo's limits, RPn 200 MHz and RP0 1000 MHz, and a request in `GEN6_RPNSWREQ` is run within them at once and shown in `GEN6_RPSTAT1`; and the pcode mailbox finishes each command as it is posted, with the Lenovo's RPe of 500 MHz for the duty cycle, 0 for the overclocking parameters and the RC6 voltages, the ring's frequency table taken, and any other command refused as illegal. So a bind on the fake reaches its end with no wait at its bound.

A ring runs whenever the driver writes its tail, its control or its `MI_MODE`, while it is valid, not stopped and not hung, and its head reaches its tail before the write returns. Each command is read through the GTT's entries from the memory they map: `MI_STORE_DWORD_IMM`, `MI_STORE_DWORD_INDEX` and the post-sync writes of `MI_FLUSH_DW` and `PIPE_CONTROL` store their values, which is how sequence numbers reach the status page, whichever command a later part's breadcrumb uses; `MI_USER_INTERRUPT` and `PIPE_CONTROL`'s notify raise the ring's user interrupt; `MI_BATCH_BUFFER_START` completes its batch without reading it; every other command of the MI, 2D and 3D types is passed over by its length, `MI_LOAD_REGISTER_IMM`'s by 8 bits and the rest's MI commands by 6; and a command of no known type, or one the GTT maps no memory for, hangs the ring. An event latches in its identity register when its mask lets it through, a user interrupt only when the ring's `RING_IMR` does too, and the device's line, the master enable and any identity bit enabled, raises one message on each rise, as the model's does.

The faults, armed for a count or for always through `dri/DEVICE/i915_fake` and each counted and logged when injected: `submit` and `bind` at the driver's fault points; `hang`, which hangs the ring at the next command it would run with its head there, `ACTHD` at it and `IPEHR` its first dword, until a reset of its domain through `GEN6_GDRST`; and `irq`, which loses the next user interrupt, whose breadcrumb is still written. The fake's lock is taken with interrupts off around each access the driver makes, innermost in the driver's order, and the message is sent after it is dropped, so a handler on any processor sees the registers between accesses.

### Hangs and resets

A hang is found as Linux's heartbeat finds one on a legacy ring. While the GT is awake, a beat every 2500 ms looks at each running ring. When the request the last beat took as its heartbeat has not written its breadcrumb, the engine is hung; otherwise the beat takes the last request the chip has been given and not finished as the next heartbeat. Linux sends a request of its own down the ring at each beat and waits for that; a ring runs its requests in order, so the last request already on it completes exactly when such a request would, and none is sent. A request is thus reset between 5 and 7.5 s after it reaches the chip, whether its breadcrumb and `ACTHD` stand still or a batch that chains to itself keeps them moving. The kernel has no timers for drivers, so `ki915`, the submission worker, sleeps until the next beat of any awake device, and `i915_request_wait` runs a beat at each of its one-second slices as well, which holds the beat when the worker cannot run.

`i915_handle_error` follows Linux's `intel_gt_handle_error`. It captures the error state first, then clears the error registers under forcewake: `PGTBL_ER`, `IPEIR_I965` and `EIR`, a bit of `EIR` that stays set masked in `EMR`, and each hung engine's fault register. The engine reset follows `__intel_engine_reset_bh`: under forcewake the ring is stopped, a stop that times out forgiven, its reset counted for its class, and its domain of `GEN6_GDRST` written and waited for 2 ms until it reads clear, twice, then 50 us more. The first request on the ring that has not completed is guilty: its fence takes `EIO` and its commands from its payload to its breadcrumb become `MI_NOOP`, so that its context switch and breadcrumb still run, and the ring starts again at its head with the requests behind it, its status page, its user interrupt unmasked when requests wait for it, and the breadcrumbs read again in case the reset lost an interrupt, as `xcs_resume` does. An engine whose domain does not reset is reset with the whole GPU, up to four rounds of three tries each, the rounds 10, 20 and 30 ms apart, after the programs' aperture maps through fences are revoked; there the first incomplete request of a hung engine is guilty and that of every other engine innocent, its fence taking `EAGAIN` while its commands run again, and the fences and the GT's workarounds are written back, the render engine's with its ring's start, as after an engine's reset. The whole GPU's reset counts in `reset_count`, which GET_RESET_STATS gives a privileged caller; an engine's does not, as in Linux. A GPU that does not reset is wedged: every request fails with `EIO`, the commands and switches of those the chip was never given taken back first, so that each ring's tail and last state are again what the chip has and the reset that ends the wedge runs none of them, the GT parks, and requests and CONTEXT_CREATE are refused until a later reset succeeds, except after a failed initialization or the unplug, whose wedge is for good.

A guilty context counts the hang in `guilty_count` and an innocent one in `active_count`, which GET_RESET_STATS gives as `batch_active` and `batch_pending`. As Linux's `mark_guilty` a context is banned at its hang when it is not recoverable, at its third hang within 120 s, or at once when it is closed, and never when it is not bannable, which only a privileged program sets. A banned context's requests behind the guilty one fail with `EIO`, its later requests are failed as they reach the chip, and EXECBUFFER2 refuses it with `EIO`. A file scores 3 for a ban and 1 for a hang within 60 s of its last, and at a score of 9 CONTEXT_CREATE refuses it with `EIO`, as Linux's `client_mark_guilty` and `i915_gem_context_create_ioctl`.

The error state, `struct i915_gpu_error`, holds what Linux's `i915_gpu_coredump` holds for Haswell's rings: the time, the reason, the message Linux logs, whether the GT was awake, the device's reset count, `EIR`, `PGTBL_ER`, `ERROR_GEN6`, `DONE_REG`, `GTIER`, `FORCEWAKE_MT`, `GEN7_ERR_INT`, `DERRMR` and the 32 fences; for each running engine its ring's registers, `RING_MODE_GEN7`, `PP_DIR_BASE`, `ACTHD`, `IPEHR`, `IPEIR`, `INSTDONE` and on the render engine the slice, sampler and row INSTDONEs, `INSTPS`, `INSTPM`, `ESR`, the fault address and register, the batch's address and state, `CCID`, `RING_PSMI_CTL` and the status page's address, its breadcrumb, sequence number and reset count, copies of the whole status page and ring, the first incomplete request's head, postfix and tail with its context's process, name and hang counts, and up to 8 requests on the chip with their context, process, head, tail and batch. The first state is kept until it is cleared, as Linux keeps the first error, and the unplug frees it. `i915_gpu_coredump` captures one without keeping it, for `i915_gpu_info`.

### The debugfs files

`i915_debugfs_register`, at the end of the bind, adds Linux's files that IGT opens to the device's directory of 76-18's debugfs, `/sys/kernel/debug/dri/DEVICE`, each CRTC's `i915_pipe` and `i915_current_bpc` beside its `crc` directory, and the card's `error` to /sys; [`debugfs(5)`](../../../../man/man5/debugfs.5) lists them. Their text follows Linux's `i915_debugfs.c` and `i915_gpu_error.c`, line for line where IGT parses it, in the order and with the values Linux 6.18 printed on the Lenovo ([`../../../../tests/fixtures/lenovo-gpu/debugfs`](../../../../tests/fixtures/lenovo-gpu/debugfs)). A text file's text is made at each read from offset 0 without sleeping; `i915_error_state` and `i915_gpu_info` make theirs at the open and hold the device until the close, as Linux holds its coredump. An open of `i915_forcewake_user` holds the GT's forcewake and the device until its last close, as Linux's `intel_gt_pm_debugfs_forcewake_user_open`, so that IGT reads and writes registers through BAR 0's `resource0` file of /sys. The error state's text is `__err_print_to_sgl`'s: the header with the times, platform, PCI ids and global registers, an engine block for each engine with an incomplete request, its status page and ring in Linux's ascii85, then the GT's information, the device's flags, the driver's capabilities, the parameters and the display's. A write to `i915_wedged` hands the engines it names by Linux's engine ids to `i915_handle_error`, by the driver's indices, as a hang does. `i915_current_bpc` reads the pipe's depth without the commit lock, which Linux takes, since a show does not sleep. A write to `i915_gem_drop_caches` follows `i915_drop_caches_set`: `DROP_RESET_ACTIVE` wedges a GPU that is not idle within 200 ms and resets it, `DROP_RETIRE` retires, `DROP_ACTIVE` and `DROP_IDLE` wait for the GT to park, and the bound and unbound flags purge the `DONTNEED` objects with or without bindings through `i915_gem_shrink_flags`, since without swap those are the only pages Linux's shrinker gives back. The directory `i915_params` has a file for each of Linux 6.18's driver and display parameters that Linux makes one for, except `request_timeout_ms`, since the driver has no request timeout. `reset` and `enable_hangcheck`, mode 0600, live in `struct i915_params`, as Linux's `i915_debugfs_params.c` writes them: `reset` 0 makes a hang wedge the GPU, 1 resets the whole GPU alone, and 2 or more an engine first, as `intel_has_gpu_reset` and `intel_has_reset_engine` read it; `enable_hangcheck` false stops the heartbeat and makes new contexts not persistent, refusing persistence with `EINVAL`. GET_PARAM's `HAS_GPU_RESET` follows both. The rest are read-only at Linux's defaults.

## Limits

* A chip is bound only under `rach2.i915`, until the driver answers Mesa's queries; without it a program would find a card of `i915` whose own ioctls all fail.
* Of the driver's own ioctls the buffers', the old CPU map GEM_MMAP among them, the contexts', EXECBUFFER2, THROTTLE, GETPARAM, I915_QUERY, GET_RESET_STATS and REG_READ are served; the rest, SETPARAM and the perf calls among them, answer `EINVAL`, as the DRM core does for a number a driver lacks. Mesa maps through MMAP_OFFSET, since MMAP_GTT_VERSION is above 3, and takes the refusal of PERF_REMOVE_CONFIG as no dynamic perf configurations (`i915_has_dynamic_config_support`, `src/intel/perf/i915/intel_perf.c` of Mesa 25.0.7).
* No fault on a GTT map yet says whether it writes, so every faulted binding counts as written and its writes are flushed before it moves.
* A new image is the recorded default, without the null render state's batch Linux runs into it first, and no residuals are cleared between contexts. The GT does not switch to the kernel's state when it parks, as Linux does, which takes a request of the kernel's context that retires microseconds later. Eviction that must wait for the GPU waits for it until a signal, or in a GTT fault, which takes none, until the GPU finishes or a hang's reset ends the request. The global GTT's space is the buffers' from its bottom and the GT's own from its top, and no part yet turns the display well off, so the VGA register touch Linux makes after turning it on again is not made.
* The error state holds no batch, context image or other buffer of the guilty request, which Linux's adds after the ring, and no engine's `Latency` or `Heartbeat` lines; `i915_engine_info` has no timelines section, and its requests show priority 0. The parameters the debugfs files print are Linux's defaults but `reset` and `enable_hangcheck`, and only those two can be written, where Linux lets root write `mmio_debug`, `error_capture` and most of the display's too; with hangcheck off, a closed context's requests are not cancelled, as Linux's are, since that needs preemption. `i915_gem_drop_caches` drops no freed objects, sequence numbers or RCU, since objects go at their last reference and none of the others exists.
* All programs share one GPU address space, the aliasing PPGTT, as Linux runs Haswell: a program's batches can read and write every buffer bound in the GTT, another program's among them. Linux gives each context an address space of its own only from graphics version 8.
* The boot reserves stolen memory only for the Haswell GT2 ids the driver binds; on another Intel GPU the firmware's memory map alone keeps it from the allocator, which every firmware Linux has met does but the early quirks Linux keeps for older ones.
* Haswell GT1 and GT3 chips, which Linux binds with other device data, are not bound.
* Only the eDP output uses the decoded table, for DDI A's port, the panel's mode, bits a colour, link rate and power sequence; the rest goes to the log. The blocks for panel self refresh, SDVO, MIPI DSI and its sequences, compression and the generic DTDs are not decoded, nor the second panel's mode. The OpRegion's ASLE requests, backlight and lid among them, are not answered, its SWSCI calls not made, and so the panel type Linux asks the OpRegion for on a few listed machines is not asked for.
* DDI B to D drive HDMI, DVI and single-stream DisplayPort: no DisplayPort MST, no LSPCON, no YCbCr output, 4:2:0 among it, no HDMI or DisplayPort audio and so no ELD, and no `Broadcast RGB` or `audio` property, so the range is always Linux's automatic choice. DDI E, the VGA output through FDI that the SPLL clocks on Linux, is not driven. GMBUS polls its status where Linux sleeps on its interrupt. A pipe the firmware lit on DDI B to D is turned off at the bind, where Linux adopts what it can read out. The panel has no IPS, PSR, DRRS or backlight over AUX, and the VDD that an AUX message forces on is let go at the connector's next probe, at the end of the bind for a dark panel, or with the panel's power, where Linux's timer lets it go five power cycles after the last message.
* No colour management: no gamma or degamma LUT and no CTM on the CRTCs, so C8 is refused, and no COLOR_ENCODING or COLOR_RANGE on the sprite, whose YUV is shown with the chip's default BT.601 limited range; the sprite's FP16 formats are shown without a gamma ramp of their own.
* An event is sent at the first vblank after the frame the commit's writes were made in, which may be one frame later than Linux's, whose `intel_pipe_update_start` waits out the vblank evasion window first.
* `unbind` and `bind` send no uevent, and `/sys/bus/pci/drivers_probe` binds only vfio-pci, so a function goes back to the driver only by a write to `bind`. The driver's directory has no `new_id` or `remove_id`.
* What the HD 4400 holds after its reset is known from one run of Linux on the Lenovo, which lit the panel from it: the model's reset in `i915_handoff_hosttest.c` clears DDI A's four-lane bit and keeps the raw clock and the PWM's granularity, and the chip has not been read after a reset under this driver.
* A release that fails after the display is off does not light it again; the console does at its next look, and a master must set its mode again.
* The fake chip reads no batch and draws nothing, loads no register for `MI_LOAD_REGISTER_IMM`, and has no stolen memory, no picture the firmware lit and no panel on DDI A, so it has no connector and no pipe can be lit; a program that needs pixels from it gets none.
