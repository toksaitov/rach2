# DRM

`sys/drm/` is the kernel's Direct Rendering Manager, Linux's interface to display and rendering devices: the card and render nodes under `/dev/dri`, their ioctls, buffer objects, mode setting and the firmware framebuffer's handoff to a native driver. `sys/dmabuf/` beside it shares buffers and fences between devices and processes: dma-bufs, fences, sync files, `/dev/udmabuf` and sw_sync. Programs reach both through libdrm, Mesa and the compositors as they do on Linux 6.12, whose numbers and layouts [`uapi.h`](uapi.h) copies. [`drm.h`](drm.h) is the contract every file of both directories keeps. The core is in place: [`drm.c`](drm.c) the devices, their nodes, open files, events and the ioctl dispatcher with Linux's table, [`drm_auth.c`](drm_auth.c) the master role and magic, [`drm_lease.c`](drm_lease.c) leases of CRTCs, connectors and planes to other masters, and [`drm_gem.c`](drm_gem.c) buffer handles, flink names, mmap offsets, the shmem helper and dumb buffers, [`drm_prime.c`](drm_prime.c) PRIME buffer sharing, [`drm_sysfs.c`](drm_sysfs.c) each device's entries in `/sys` and its uevents, and [`drm_debugfs.c`](drm_debugfs.c) its files in debugfs. Mode setting is Linux's KMS: [`drm_mode.c`](drm_mode.c) the mode objects, properties, blobs, framebuffers and the probe of a connector's modes, [`drm_atomic.c`](drm_atomic.c) the atomic check and commit, with its fences and its worker, [`drm_vblank.c`](drm_vblank.c) the vblank counts, waits and events and the CRC entries, [`drm_legacy.c`](drm_legacy.c) the calls before atomic made commits, and [`drm_edid.c`](drm_edid.c) a monitor's EDID and its modes. Four drivers set modes: [`fbdrm.c`](fbdrm.c) drives the framebuffer the loader left as Linux's simpledrm, [`bochs.c`](bochs.c) QEMU's standard VGA as Linux's bochs-drm, [`virtio_gpu.c`](virtio_gpu.c) the virtio GPU display and virgl 3D, as Linux's virtio_gpu, and [`vkms.c`](vkms.c) is Linux's vkms, a display in software for IGT's tests, with planes composed in memory, vblanks from the tick and a CRC of each frame. [`vgem.c`](vgem.c) is Linux's vgem, which sets no modes: buffers in memory that IGT's tests export to the GPU and hold busy with fences of their own. `drm.4` is the interface a program sees, `drm-kms.7` mode setting, and `bochs.4`, `fbdrm.4`, `virtio-gpu.4`, `vkms.4` and `vgem.4` each driver.

## Use

`drm_start()` runs once from `kernel.c`, after the network has started, and logs `drm: start`. It starts `/dev/udmabuf`, then probes the display devices in the order the handoff needs: the PC's native drivers (bochs and [i915](i915/Readme.md), through `arch_drm_probe(ARCH_DRM_NATIVE)`), virtio-gpu, then the driver of the firmware's framebuffer (fbdrm, through `arch_drm_probe(ARCH_DRM_FIRMWARE)`), then vkms, the display in software. A probe that finds no device answers `ENODEV` and is not logged; any other error is logged as `drm: NAME: error N`. In QEMU with its standard VGA, bochs binds the device and logs `bochs: registered`, and fbdrm then finds the framebuffer taken. With `-device virtio-gpu-pci` or `-device virtio-vga`, virtio-gpu binds it and logs `virtio_gpu: pci: virtio-vga detected at ...` and each scanout's size; under virtio-vga fbdrm then finds the framebuffer taken. Booted by UEFI, or by BIOS with a `gfxpayload` mode, on a machine whose display no native driver drives, as the Lenovo's without `rach2.i915`, fbdrm binds the loader's framebuffer and logs `fbdrm: registered`. A BIOS boot in text mode on such a machine has no card. `nomodeset` keeps bochs, i915 and virtio-gpu off, as on Linux, unless the driver's `modeset` parameter gives another value than -1, so that fbdrm drives the loader's framebuffer ([cmdline](../../cmdline.md)). With `rach2.vkms` on the command line, vkms binds after them and logs `vkms: registered` with its planes and card; vgem always binds last and logs `vgem: registered` with its card. Linux loads vgem on demand; Rach2 builds it in and registers it at startup, so IGT can find it without a boot option. The old `rach2.vgem` word is no longer needed. Right after virtio-gpu's probe, `drm_start` makes `/dev/vgpu-shm`, the node through which a region shared with a VM is attached; virtio-gpu binds over that transport later, with `virtio_gpu_bind`, each time the VM's device answers, and unbinds when it goes ([virtio](../virtio/Readme.md)).

## Interfaces

[`uapi.h`](uapi.h) is the user ABI, Linux 6.12's `drm.h`, `drm_mode.h`, `drm_fourcc.h` (the formats in use), `dma-buf.h`, `sync_file.h`, the sw_sync ioctls and `udmabuf.h`, in C89: every structure the 6.12 ioctl table names, with Linux's names, members and sizes on i386 and x86-64, and every `DRM_IOCTL_*`, `DMA_BUF_*`, `SYNC_IOC_*`, `SW_SYNC_IOC_*` and `UDMABUF_*` number. `UAPI_IO`, `UAPI_IOR`, `UAPI_IOW` and `UAPI_IOWR` encode a number as Linux's `_IOC` does (direction in bits 30 and 31, size in 16 to 29, type in 8 to 15, number in 0 to 7), and `UAPI_IOC_DIR`, `UAPI_IOC_SIZE`, `UAPI_IOC_TYPE` and `UAPI_IOC_NR` take one apart. Linux's `long` is `intptr_t` and `unsigned long` `uintptr_t`, so a member keeps its width on each ABI; a pointer member carries `__user`. The DRI1 calls that 6.12 no longer has are left out, and the one anonymous union, in `hdr_output_metadata`, is flattened, since C89 has none.

[`drm.h`](drm.h) holds the rules every file keeps, in its opening comment, and the types and functions the files share:

* Drivers. `struct drm_ident` is what a driver says of itself: DRM_IOCTL_VERSION's name, description, date and version, its `DRIVER_*` features and its bus, `DRM_BUS_PCI` or `DRM_BUS_PLATFORM`. `DRM_IDENT_I915`, `DRM_IDENT_VIRTIO_GPU`, `DRM_IDENT_BOCHS`, `DRM_IDENT_SIMPLEDRM`, `DRM_IDENT_VKMS` and `DRM_IDENT_VGEM` are the identities Linux 6.12's i915, virtio_gpu, bochs-drm, simpledrm, vkms and vgem report; fbdrm reports itself as simpledrm. `struct drm_driver` holds the identity, the hooks `open`, `postclose`, `dumb_create`, `gem_prime_import` and `unplug`, and the driver's own ioctls as `struct drm_ioctl_desc` rows, each with its full number, its `DRM_AUTH`, `DRM_MASTER`, `DRM_ROOT_ONLY`, `DRM_RENDER_ALLOW`, `DRM_UNLOCKED`, `DRM_MODESET_LOCK` and `DRM_KMS` flags and its `drm_ioctl_t` handler, which takes the kernel's copy of the argument.
* Devices. `struct drm_device` is a bound device: its driver, features (`drm_core_check_feature`), card index, references, `unique` name, PCI function, master file, open files, buffer objects, mode configuration and commit lock. `drm_dev_init`, `drm_dev_register`, `drm_dev_unplug`, `drm_dev_get`, `drm_dev_put` and `drm_dev_at` make, publish, lose, hold and find one. `drm_dev_register` takes the lowest free index, and its `unique` name, unless the driver set one, is Linux's `pci:0000:BB:DD.F` for a PCI device and the driver's name otherwise. `drm_dev_unplug` removes the nodes and the `/sys` entries, zaps every mapping of the device's buffers, wakes the files and calls the driver's `unplug` hook; the last `drm_dev_put` frees its index and calls the driver's `release` hook, which frees the record. Card N is `/dev/dri/cardN`, major `DRM_MAJOR` (226) and minor N, and its render node `/dev/dri/renderD(128 + N)`, minor `DRM_MINOR_RENDER_BASE` + N, up to `DRM_MAX_DEVICES` (64); they are nodes of the devfs registry ([`../fs/devfs.h`](../fs/devfs.h)), whose `/dev/dri` directory is present only while it has one.
* Open files. `struct drm_file` is one open of a node (`DRM_MINOR_PRIMARY` or `DRM_MINOR_RENDER`), shared by every descriptor of its open file description, with its handles (`struct drm_handles`, a table indexed by id, as Linux's idr, whose lookup costs one load), its place in the master records and its queue of `struct drm_pending_event`. The node's `file_ops` read, poll, ioctl, mmap and close it; `drm_ioctl` copies the argument by the direction the number and the table's row share, checks the row's flags in Linux's order and calls the handler under the locks the row names: the big kernel lock unless the row is `DRM_UNLOCKED`, and for a `DRM_MASTER` row that is, only while its permission is checked; the device's commit lock, taken interruptibly with the unplug checked under it, for a `DRM_MODESET_LOCK` row; and a `DRM_KMS` row runs without the big lock only on a device whose driver sets `DRIVER_KMS_UNLOCKED`, as i915 does. Every KMS row that holds the commit mutex is `DRM_UNLOCKED`, `DRM_KMS` and `DRM_MODESET_LOCK`, but for the four lease rows, which walk the device's files and open one under the big lock that keeps that list; WAIT_VBLANK is `DRM_UNLOCKED` and `DRM_KMS`. Drivers without `DRIVER_KMS_UNLOCKED` keep the big lock around these handlers. The syncobj rows use their handle-table and object spinlocks, atomic references, fence locks and wait queues; their shared wake counter is atomic because callbacks on different fences may run together. VERSION, GET_STATS, GET_CAP and the stateless compatibility handlers run unlocked too. Master state, GEM handle operations whose final put may call a legacy driver, PRIME imports and dumb-buffer hooks keep the big lock. The release of an exported buffer, which an importer's unlocked call or retirement may reach, takes the big lock for the exporter's put when the exporter's device lacks `DRIVER_KMS_UNLOCKED`. The i386 calls of an x86-64 kernel reach the same dispatcher, so they take the same locks. For an unlocked row the dispatcher drops the whole big-lock depth inherited from its caller, across the argument copies and the handler, and restores it on every return. A descriptor opened through a file-system device node keeps that node in its description; the native and i386 ioctl paths use the operations flag alone (`file_ioctl_unlocked`), so they reach the dispatcher without taking the big lock for the inode. The generic file-operation guard still locks its last release. A traced entry or another kernel caller may retain a big-lock depth, which the dispatcher drops and restores for an unlocked row. `drm_event_reserve` takes room in the file's 4096 bytes of events, `drm_send_event` signals and drops its fence, queues a reserved event and wakes the readers, and `drm_event_cancel_free` drops its fence and gives the room back; an event reserved for a file that closes is freed when it is sent.
* Master. `drm_is_current_master` says whether a file is its device's master, or a lessee of the master's record. `drm_master_open` makes the first opener of a card with no master master, `drm_master_release` drops the role of a file that closes, and each change calls every `struct drm_master_notifier` registered with `drm_master_notifier_register`.
* Leases. CREATE_LEASE, LIST_LESSEES, GET_LEASE and REVOKE_LEASE are Linux 6.12's. A lessee is a file of the card that CREATE_LEASE opens through the devfs registry, as Linux's `file_clone_open` reopens the lessor's file, with the lessor's access mode and status flags; `drm_node_open` makes it a lessee when the thread that opens it is the one the device's `lease_open` names. It has a master record of its own, names its lessor's in `lessor_id`, and keeps the IDs it leases, ascending, in `leases`. `drm_lease_held` says whether a file may use an object, and `drm_lease_filter_crtcs` renumbers a CRTC mask over the CRTCs a lessee holds; `drm_mode_object_find` gives a lessee none of the connectors, CRTCs and planes it does not lease. `drm_lease_file_release`, which a card file's release calls after `drm_master_release`, takes every object from the lessees of a record whose maker closes, and ends a closing lessee's lease with the card's uevent `change` with LEASE=1, as Linux's `drm_sysfs_lease_event`.
* Buffer objects. `struct drm_gem_object` is a buffer of whole pages, with its flink name, its mmap offset, its `struct dma_resv` of implicit fences and its driver's `struct drm_gem_object_funcs`: `free`, `mmap` into a `vma_t`, `pin`, `unpin`, the borrowed physical `page` for PRIME, which the console's flush of its shadow also copies through, and `vmap`, the kernel's address of a buffer in video memory, which the console draws through; bochs gives it, and virtio-gpu, whose buffers are scattered pages, does not; and `open`, which sees each handle a file gets to the object, by creation, GEM_OPEN or PRIME import, as Linux's hook of that name, and may refuse it. `drm_gem_object_init`, `drm_gem_object_get` and `drm_gem_object_put` make, hold and drop one, releasing its own reservation's fences before its storage; `drm_gem_object_next` walks a device's objects, holding each it visits and passing over one whose last reference is going. `drm_gem_handle_create`, `drm_gem_object_lookup` and `drm_gem_handle_delete` a file's handles to it, the lowest free from 1, the id reserved and hidden from lookups while the `open` hook runs, as Linux's `drm_gem_handle_create_tail`; `drm_gem_release` drops a closing file's handles. `drm_gem_create_mmap_offset` places the object first-fit in its device's mmap space, from `DRM_FILE_PAGE_OFFSET_START`, and `drm_gem_mmap` finds the object whose pages in that space hold a mapping's offset, as Linux's `drm_vma_offset_lookup_locked`, refuses with `EACCES` a file that holds no handle to it, found on the object's own list of handles as Linux's `drm_vma_node_is_allowed` finds it, and calls its `mmap` hook with the page within it, which passes the object to `vm_device_map` ([`../mm/vm.h`](../mm/vm.h)). `struct drm_gem_shmem_object` is the shmem helper's object, whose pages a `struct shmem` holds, mapped cached or, with `map_wc`, write-combining; `drm_gem_shmem_create` makes one, `drm_gem_shmem_page` gives a page, and `drm_gem_shmem_dumb_create` is the `dumb_create` hook of a driver that keeps its dumb buffers in memory.
* PRIME. `PRIME_HANDLE_TO_FD` exports a handle as a dma-buf; `PRIME_FD_TO_HANDLE` imports one. A handle caches its dma-buf with a reference until the handle closes, so a repeated import into the same file returns the same handle; the table hashes its cached handles by dma-buf (`drm_handle_set_prime`, `drm_handle_prime_find`), as Linux's per-file tree of dma-bufs, so an import finds its handle without a walk over the file's handles. A self-import on the same device returns the exporting object, and an imported object reexports the original dma-buf. A dma-buf holds its exporting object, whose `dma_buf` pointer is weak; an imported object holds the dma-buf and uses its reservation. The default import is a shmem object whose `drm_gem_shmem_page` reads the exporter's physical pages, with its cache type. A driver's `gem_prime_import` may replace that helper; it returns one object reference, borrows the dma-buf, and the core attaches its buffer reference and reservation. An export needs an object's `page` hook for import by another device; udmabuf provides its pinned pages. An object whose driver set `no_export` is refused with `EINVAL`, as i915 refuses its userptr objects. Device loss zaps the importing devices' mappings too (`drm_gem_object_zap`) where an importer maps its object under the object's own address, as the shmem helper's import does, and that importer's next touch asks the exporter's `page` again, which alone decides whether it serves after the device has gone. i915 maps an import under its mmap-offset records and keeps the exporter's frames in its GTT and page tables until the import is freed, as Linux's attachment keeps them mapped, so an exporter keeps every frame it gave until its object is freed, its device unplugged or not.
* Fences. `struct dma_fence` is a point on a timeline that signals once, with an error or none; `struct dma_fence_ops` its driver's names and hooks; `struct dma_fence_cb` a callback. `dma_fence_context_alloc`, `dma_fence_init`, `dma_fence_get`, `dma_fence_put`, `dma_fence_set_error`, `dma_fence_signal`, `dma_fence_is_signaled`, `dma_fence_add_callback`, `dma_fence_remove_callback` and `dma_fence_wait_until` are their life. The [shared-buffer Readme](../dmabuf/Readme.md) gives the buffer, reservation, sync-file and software timeline interfaces.
* Display state. `struct drm_mode_object` (with up to `DRM_OBJECT_MAX_PROPERTY` properties), `drm_property`, `drm_property_blob`, `drm_framebuffer`, `drm_plane` (`DRM_PLANE_TYPE_*`), `drm_crtc` with its `drm_vblank` and `drm_crtc_crc_entry` ring, `drm_encoder`, `drm_connector` (`DRM_CONNECTOR_*` status, `DRM_FORCE_*`, its EDID and override) and `drm_display_mode` are the mode objects; `drm_plane_state`, `drm_crtc_state`, `drm_connector_state` and `drm_atomic_state` (up to `DRM_MAX_CRTCS`, `DRM_MAX_PLANES` and `DRM_MAX_CONNECTORS`) a commit's states; `drm_plane_funcs`, `drm_crtc_funcs` (with the CRC hooks `set_crc_source`, `verify_crc_source` and `get_crc_sources`) and `drm_connector_funcs` the drivers' hooks; and `drm_mode_config` a device's limits, objects and `atomic_check` and `atomic_commit`.
* Making a display. A driver calls `drm_mode_config_init`, which makes Linux's standard properties in Linux's order, then `drm_universal_plane_init` for each plane (its formats, modifiers and IN_FORMATS blob), `drm_crtc_init_with_planes`, `drm_encoder_init` and `drm_connector_init`, and `drm_connector_attach_encoder`; `drm_mode_crtc_set_gamma_size`, `drm_crtc_enable_color_mgmt`, `drm_plane_enable_fb_damage_clips`, `drm_plane_create_rotation_property`, `drm_plane_create_zpos_property`, `drm_plane_create_alpha_property`, `drm_plane_create_color_properties` (COLOR_ENCODING and COLOR_RANGE) and `drm_connector_attach_edid_property` add what it has. Each object takes the lowest free ID, as Linux's idr gives them, so the IDs a program sees are Linux's for the same driver. `drm_mode_config_cleanup` frees it all. The `drm_property_create*` calls, `drm_object_attach_property` and `drm_object_property_set_value` and `_get_value` make and use properties; `drm_property_create_blob`, `drm_property_lookup_blob`, `drm_property_blob_get` and `_put` blobs; `drm_framebuffer_lookup`, `_get` and `_put` framebuffers. A driver's `fb_create` in its mode configuration checks each framebuffer ADDFB or ADDFB2 makes before it gets an ID, as Linux's driver hook does, and `fb_destroy` lets go what it took when the last put frees it.
* Commits. `drm_modeset_lock` takes the device's commit lock, `mode_lock`, a mutex its holder may take again, interruptibly; `drm_modeset_lock_nointr` takes it with signals ignored, and `drm_modeset_unlock` lets it go once. `drm_atomic_state_alloc` makes a state, `drm_atomic_get_crtc_state`, `_plane_state` and `_connector_state` add an object's copy of its current state, and the `drm_atomic_set_*` calls change it as the properties do; `drm_atomic_check_only`, `drm_atomic_commit` and `drm_atomic_nonblocking_commit` check it and make it the display's, through the driver's `atomic_check` and `atomic_commit` or the core's `drm_atomic_helper_check` and `drm_atomic_helper_commit`, which run the plane and CRTC hooks in Linux's order. Nonblocking mode sets leave their hardware programming to the commit worker even when no in-fence waits, so that their enable or disable runs after the ioctl releases the commit lock. `drm_atomic_helper_plane_pending` lets a driver's independent cursor update check for a queued write of its plane or its CRTC's mode or colour; such an update may swap only the plane state. `drm_atomic_commit_work` advances each deferred commit's plane waits in order, arming the next plane's fence callback and calling the driver's `commit_fence_wait`, and runs the commits whose waits have ended or timed out, as the `kdrm` thread does. `drm_atomic_helper_check_plane_state` clips a plane to its CRTC for a driver's check, its sides' differences wrapping to 32 bits as Linux's do, so that a plane at INT_MIN is not visible, and `drm_atomic_helper_set_config` is SETCRTC's commit. `drm_helper_probe_single_connector_modes` probes a connector as Linux's helper does: its state, its EDID and modes through the driver's `get_modes`, which calls `drm_connector_update_edid`, or `drm_connector_add_edid_modes` when the driver's detect published the EDID with `drm_connector_update_edid_property`, the DMT modes up to 1024x768 for a monitor without one (`drm_add_modes_noedid`), each mode checked against the connector's and each CRTC's `mode_valid`, 640x480 at 60 Hz tried on a DisplayPort connector the checks left without a mode, and the list sorted.
* The handoff. `drm_aperture_acquire_firmware` records the range of the firmware's framebuffer a firmware driver drives and the hook that removes it, in a table of `DRM_APERTURE_MAX` (4), and `drm_aperture_release_firmware` forgets a device's ranges when its bind fails after it acquired them. `drm_aperture_remove_conflicting` removes each firmware device whose range overlaps a native driver's, and from then on `drm_aperture_acquire_firmware` answers `EBUSY`, as Linux's sysfb_disable leaves no firmware device to bind once a native driver took the screen.
* Vblanks. A CRTC has vblanks when its driver gives `enable_vblank` and `disable_vblank`, which start and stop its interrupt; `drm_dev_has_vblank` says whether any CRTC of a device has. A driver whose CRTC has a frame counter gives `get_vblank_counter`, which reads its 32 bits; the count then moves by the counter's difference at each vblank and when the interrupt goes off and on again, so frames that passed while it was off are counted, as Linux's `drm_update_vblank_count`; without a counter, a driver that gives `get_vblank_timestamp` has the count move by the frames between that time and the one stored, rounded to the nearest, in the mode's frame, `framedur_ns`, which `drm_crtc_vblank_on` sets, as Linux's does, and any other by one a vblank. A driver that can read where its CRTC scans gives `get_vblank_timestamp`, through `drm_crtc_vblank_helper_get_vblank_timestamp_internal` and its scanout position, and the vblanks are stamped with the time the vertical blank ends, as Linux's `drm_get_last_vbltimestamp`, at each vblank, when the interrupt comes on after missed frames and when the CRTC is turned on; without it a vblank is stamped with the clock when its handler runs, and a CRTC turned on has no time until its next. With a counter, the counter and the time are read again while the counter moves between them, and an interrupt whose frame the count already took in keeps the count's time, as Linux's `drm_update_vblank_count`, since a second reading of a frame from i915's scanline counter may lie microseconds before the first and Weston aborts on a time that goes back; `backwards` counts each time stored before an earlier one. `drm_crtc_init_with_planes` calls `drm_crtc_vblank_init`, which leaves the vblanks off and held off. `drm_crtc_vblank_get` and `drm_crtc_vblank_put` hold the interrupt on, `drm_crtc_vblank_count_and_time` reads the 64-bit count and the `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` time of the last vblank as one pair, again while a vblank's store of them moves the CRTC's sequence count, as Linux's seqlock, so that no reply pairs a count with another vblank's time or takes half of a time i686 stores in two moves, `drm_crtc_arm_vblank_event` queues an event for the next vblank and `drm_crtc_send_vblank_event` sends one now. `drm_crtc_vblank_off` and `drm_crtc_vblank_on` are the commit's around a CRTC's disable and enable; off sends every queued event and holds the vblanks off, on adds one to the count. `drm_crtc_wait_flip` waits for a CRTC's `flip_pending` (`DRM_FLIP_QUEUED` while the commit worker has yet to program the flip, `DRM_FLIP_ARMED` until its vblank) to fall below a stage. `drm_vblank_unplug` turns every CRTC off, sends what is queued with `-ENODEV` on its fences, and `drm_crtc_vblank_cleanup` frees a CRTC's queue and CRC ring.
* Debug hooks. `drm_connector_force_write` takes a write of a connector's `force` file, one of `on`, `digital`, `off` or `unspecified` with one newline at most, as Linux's `sysfs_streq` takes it, and the next probe reports that state, calling the driver's `force` hook; `drm_edid_override_set` and `drm_edid_override_reset` set and clear the EDID that can stand in for the sink's. The core uses it for modes when the connector reports connected. `drm_crtc_crc_set_source` takes a write of `crc/control`, checked by the driver's `verify_crc_source`; `drm_crtc_crc_open` starts the source through `set_crc_source` and makes the ring of `DRM_CRC_ENTRIES_NR` (128) entries, `drm_crtc_crc_read` gives an entry as Linux's line, the frame or ten `X`s and each value as `0x%08x`, and `drm_crtc_crc_release` stops it.
* debugfs. `drm_start` calls `drm_debugfs_init`, which makes `dri`; `drm_dev_register` calls `drm_debugfs_register`, which makes the device's directory, named by its PCI function or its platform device as Linux names it, with Linux 6.12's files: `name`, `clients`, `gem_names`, and with mode setting `framebuffer`, `state` (atomic drivers), a directory `crtc-N` for each CRTC, with Rach2's own `vblank_backwards` in it when the CRTC has vblanks, `crc/control` and `crc/data` in it when its driver gives `set_crc_source` and `verify_crc_source`, `encoder-N/bridges` and each connector's `force`, `edid_override`, `vrr_range` and `output_bpc`, the last two from the refresh range and bits a colour that `drm_connector_update_edid` keeps from the sink's EDID, as Linux's display_info, and ENODEV while the connector is not connected; and the links `dri/N` and, with a render node, `dri/(128 + N)`. The directory holds the device for each node below it ([`../fs/debugfs.Readme.md`](../fs/debugfs.Readme.md)), so a file open across an unplug keeps the device to its last close. `drm_dev_unplug` calls `drm_debugfs_unregister`, which takes the links and the directory out first. A failure to make them is logged and leaves the device without them. `drm_debugfs_path` gives a driver the path of a file of its own in the directory, as virtio-gpu's `virtio_gpu_reset`, since a path through the `dri/N` link makes no file. [debugfs(5)](../../../man/man5/debugfs.5) gives each file's text.
* Helpers. `drm_crtc_handle_vblank`, `drm_crtc_add_crc_entry`, `drm_connector_update_edid`, `drm_connector_update_edid_property`, which sets a connector's EDID property from a driver's detect without adding modes, as Linux's `drm_edid_connector_update`, and with it the connector's ELD, which `drm_edid_to_eld` makes as Linux's from the monitor's name and the audio descriptors, speaker allocation and HDMI latencies of the CTA data blocks, those of the CTA extensions and then those of the CTA blocks of DisplayID extensions, as Linux's `cea_db_iter` walks them, and clears with the EDID, and `drm_kms_hotplug_event` are what a driver calls from its interrupts and probes; `drm_client_dev_register` and `drm_client_dev_unregister` tell the console of a device that comes and goes, `drm_client_dev_lastclose`, which the release of a device's last open file calls as Linux's `drm_release` calls `drm_lastclose`, that it may take the screen back, `drm_client_boot_screen_gone` that the loader's framebuffer it may still draw on has gone with the device behind it, and `drm_kms_hotplug_event` calls `drm_client_dev_hotplug`; `drm_framebuffer_dirty` flushes a rectangle of a framebuffer as DIRTYFB does, for the console's shadow; `drm_aperture_is_firmware` says whether a device drives the firmware's framebuffer. The console, `drm_client.c`, listens to the master role through a `struct drm_master_notifier`, and is described with the [display](../display/Readme.md).
* `/sys`. `drm_dev_register` calls `drm_sysfs_register`, which makes the device's entries through the registry of [`../fs/sysfs.h`](../fs/sysfs.h), as Linux's drm_sysfs.c does: below the directory the driver names (`sysfs_parent`), the PCI function's, or `/sys/devices/platform/UNIQUE` for a platform device, a `drm` directory with `cardN` and, with DRIVER_RENDER, `renderD(128 + N)`, each with `dev`, `uevent`, a `subsystem` link to `/sys/class/drm` and a `device` link to the device; below the card, a `cardN-NAME` directory for each connector with `status`, `enabled`, `dpms`, `modes`, `edid`, `connector_id` and `uevent`; and links in `/sys/class/drm` and `/sys/dev/char/226:M`. It sends `add` for each, the card first; `drm_sysfs_connector_add` and `drm_sysfs_connector_remove` add and remove a connector later, `drm_dev_unplug` calls `drm_sysfs_unregister`, which sends `remove` for each before it removes them, and `drm_kms_hotplug_event` and `drm_sysfs_connector_hotplug_event` send the card's `change` with `HOTPLUG=1`. A write of `status` sets the connector's force (`DRM_FORCE_*`), as Linux's `status_store` does, under the commit lock: when the force changed, or the write is `detect`, it probes the connector at once, and when the force changed it sends a hotplug, so a `GETCONNECTOR` after the write sees the new state. A device whose `pci_ident` is set has its PCI function show that identity in place of its own while it is registered, as a driver standing in for another chip needs. A PCI function is shown bound to the driver Linux binds to it, a `driver` link to `/sys/bus/pci/drivers/NAME` and `DRIVER=NAME` in its uevent (`sysfs_pci_bind`), before its card appears, and sends its `bind` uevent once the driver is up (`sysfs_pci_uevent`), as Linux's driver core does around a probe: `bochs-drm` by bochs.c, which fails the bind when the binding cannot be shown, as Linux's really_probe does, and `i915` by its handoff ([`i915/Readme.md`](i915/Readme.md)). virtio-gpu's function is bound to `virtio-pci` by the [virtio transport](../virtio/Readme.md), which adds the virtio device `virtioN` below it on `/sys/bus/virtio`; virtio-gpu shows that device bound to `virtio_gpu` and sets it as its `sysfs_parent`, so that its card is `.../0000:00:02.0/virtio0/drm/card0`, as on Linux, and libdrm and libudev find the PCI function above it. IGT's device scan asserts that the card's parent names a driver.
* Probes. `bochs_probe`, `fbdrm_probe`, `i915_probe`, `virtio_gpu_probe`, `vkms_probe` and `vgem_probe` bind their devices and return how many, or `-errno`. bochs, i915 and fbdrm reach PC-only code, so `drm_start` calls them through `arch_drm_probe` ([`../arch/arch.h`](../arch/arch.h)), which `drm_pc.c` defines on the PC. `vkms_tick` is vkms's vblank timer, which the timer interrupt calls each tick.
* Sync objects. `struct drm_syncobj` holds a fence: none, a binary fence, or the newest point of a timeline, a `struct dma_fence_chain` ([dmabuf.h](../dmabuf/dmabuf.h)). `drm_syncobj_create`, `drm_syncobj_get`, `drm_syncobj_put` and `drm_syncobj_find` make, hold, drop and look one up by a file's handle; `drm_syncobj_replace_fence` and `drm_syncobj_add_point` give it a fence or a point and wake its waiters and eventfds; `drm_syncobj_find_fence` gives a driver the fence of a point, waiting up to five seconds for its submission when asked; `drm_syncobj_release` drops a closing file's handles. The twelve SYNCOBJ ioctls are Linux 6.12's, and each answers `EOPNOTSUPP` on a device without `DRIVER_SYNCOBJ`, or without `DRIVER_SYNCOBJ_TIMELINE` for TRANSFER, TIMELINE_WAIT, EVENTFD, TIMELINE_SIGNAL and QUERY, the features GET_CAP reports. [drm-syncobj(7)](../../../man/man7/drm-syncobj.7) is the user contract.
* Ioctl handlers. Every handler the core's table names is declared, grouped by the file that defines it: `drm.c` the identity, capabilities, `drm_noop` and `drm_invalid_op`; `drm_auth.c` magic and the master role; `drm_gem.c` handles, flink and dumb buffers; `drm_prime.c` PRIME; `drm_mode.c` the mode objects, properties, blobs and framebuffers; `drm_lease.c` leases; `drm_legacy.c` the calls before atomic; `drm_atomic.c` the atomic commit; `drm_vblank.c` vblank waits and CRTC sequences; and `drm_syncobj.c` sync objects.

## Design

The opening comment of `drm.h` is the design every file keeps: locking under the big kernel lock, except the rows and interrupt handlers marked to run without it; a device's commit lock, `mode_lock`, over the mode objects, the framebuffers' and blobs' references, the committed states, the probes, modes, EDIDs and leases, held by every KMS row, the commit worker, the console client, the probes of hotplug, `/sys` and debugfs and a file's release, and taken before an address space's `mmap_lock`, since user memory is copied under it, while WAIT_VBLANK holds none of it, as Linux's takes no modeset lock; spinlocks over what other processors reach without the big lock: a file's handle table under its own lock, as Linux's `table_lock`, a device's buffer objects, their flink names, their handles and its mmap space under its `object_lock`, its files' events and each CRTC's vblank events under its `event_lock`, each CRTC's vblank counts and holds under `vbl_lock` after it, a CRTC's CRC entries under their own lock, and the commit worker's queues under `work_lock`; buffer object references counted atomically, a walk over a device's objects taking one only while the count is not 0, as Linux's `kref_get_unless_zero`; who holds a reference to a device, an open file, a buffer object, a framebuffer and a fence, and what close, `dup`, `fork`, `exec` and exit drop; what an unplugged device answers (`ENODEV` from its ioctls and `mmap`, `SIGBUS` on a revoked mapping, fences signalled with `-ENODEV`, and `read` and `poll` as before); and discovery by identity, never by minor number, since card numbers follow the order of registration.

A sync object follows Linux's drm_syncobj.c call for call. A binary object holds the producer's fence itself, so a sync file exported from it reports the producer's error. A timeline is a chain of points, each holding its predecessor and the merged leaves of the fence it was given, never another chain; a point above the newest continues its context, and one at or below it takes the newest's number in a context of its own, which is how Linux numbers points given out of order. A wait puts a callback on each fence and, for a point not yet submitted, a record on the object's list, which every change of the object's fence checks; the waiter sleeps on a queue of its own until a callback or a submission counts a change, or its deadline. An eventfd's record leaves the list when its point is submitted and frees itself when it signals; it never holds the object, whose release frees the records still waiting. Adding a point drops the signalled points just behind it, so a timeline signalled in order keeps one point.

Mode setting keeps Linux's split between state and commit. Every CRTC, plane and connector has a current state; a commit, whether `ATOMIC` or one of the calls before it, copies the states it changes into a `struct drm_atomic_state`, sets them, checks them whole and swaps them in under the commit lock, so a refused commit changes nothing. Each CRTC the commit leaves on or turns on or off gets an event, the caller's or one of the core's, and its flip is pending until that event is sent at the vblank that shows the state, which also signals its out-fence; on a device without vblanks the event is sent as the commit ends, as Linux's helpers do. A plane given no `IN_FENCE_FD` takes as its in-fence the pending writes of its framebuffer's objects, the write fence of each object's reservation, as Linux's `drm_gem_plane_helper_prepare_fb` does for every GEM driver, virtio-gpu's among them, so that a flip waits for the render job that draws the buffer, which a compositor such as Weston relies on; an `IN_FENCE_FD` overrules them. A blocking commit waits for its in-fences before the swap and for its flips after it. A driver that sets `commit_fence_timeout_ms`, as i915 sets Linux's 10 s `i915_fence_timeout`, bounds the wait for each plane's fence in turn, uninterruptible, from the moment the wait reaches it, and once one times out the commit waits for no other and programs the planes, as Linux's `intel_atomic_commit_fence_wait`; a deferred commit's work then runs at that deadline, which the worker sleeps to, with its current fence callback taken back, and a blocking commit stalled on it runs it then. A nonblocking commit whose in-fences have not signalled swaps its state in at once, moves it to a work item, and returns; the worker arms a callback only on the first pending plane's fence, enabling its signalling. The callback records completion and wakes the worker, which advances to the next pending plane in process context without the signaller's interrupt or engine locks. A later plane's fence is neither listened to nor handed to the driver's `commit_fence_wait` until its turn, so that i915's listening and wait boost follow Linux's sequential waits. A fence already complete when reached needs neither a callback nor a boost. Completion after the current wait's deadline starts no later wait, even if the worker has yet to run; completion before it lets the worker advance even if the worker resumes after that deadline. The `kdrm` kernel thread, started at the first such commit, programs the hardware once the waits end. It gives up the big lock it starts with, as Linux's commit work runs the tail on a workqueue under no global lock (`drm_atomic_helper.c:1876-1882` and `2099` of 6.12.115), and takes it back only for a driver's delayed work, a hotplug event, and a tail on a device without `DRIVER_KMS_UNLOCKED`, as the ATOMIC ioctl's dispatch takes it for such a device; so on i915 a nonblocking commit's tail runs under the commit lock alone, as a blocking one does in the ioctl. A commit that changes a CRTC, plane or connector of a commit still queued, or a CRTC whose flip is pending, is `EBUSY` with `NONBLOCK`, as Linux's stall checks have it; a blocking one waits for the queued commit's fences and programs it itself, since the worker's other works may want the commit lock the caller holds, then waits for the flip. A commit's work stays on the queue until its tail ends, so that no commit frees the states a tail reads. The vblank interrupt goes off at the first vblank 5 s after its last holder, which the driver's own vblank checks, so no timer is needed. That interrupt sends and frees events, so the vblank holds, each CRTC's queue of events and each file's reserved and sent events change with interrupts masked, and a read takes an event off its file's queue before it copies it out, putting it back at the head on a fault, as Linux's `drm_read` does under its event lock. GETRESOURCES and GETCONNECTOR gather the IDs and modes they report before they copy them out, the modes under the commit lock that a probe takes, since a copy may sleep while another thread removes a framebuffer or a probe replaces the modes. A connector's modes come from its EDID as Linux 6.12 reads it, table for table, and the host tests compare the lists with those Linux made of the same EDIDs.

Leases follow Linux's drm_lease.c, where a master lends part of its display to another master, as a VR compositor takes a headset's output. The master names CRTCs, connectors and planes, at least one CRTC and one connector, and a plane when it has the universal-planes cap; without that cap each CRTC brings its primary and cursor planes. Any other object is EINVAL, an ID of none ENOENT, an ID given twice or a plane a CRTC brought ENOSPC, Linux's idr answer, and an object another lessee of the same master holds EBUSY; a lessee may not lease further (EINVAL). The lessee holds the master role exactly while its lessor's record does, as Linux's drm_lease_owner gives it, and cannot set or drop it (EINVAL). It sees only what it leases: GETRESOURCES and GETPLANERESOURCES list its CRTCs, connectors and planes alone; a lookup of another connector, CRTC or plane is ENOENT, and an atomic CRTC_ID of one EACCES; GETPLANE and GETENCODER give a CRTC it does not lease as 0 and their CRTC masks renumbered over its own CRTCs; SETCRTC with a mode, PAGE_FLIP and CURSOR are EACCES when it does not lease the primary or cursor plane; and WAIT_VBLANK counts its CRTCs alone. A revoke empties the lease and leaves the lessee and its ID; the lessee's close frees both, and its ID, the lowest free from 1 in its lessor's record, may be given again. The close of the master's file empties every lease it made, as Linux's revoke of the owner. The leases change only under the commit lock and the big lock; a lessee's own calls read its array without them, since a revoke sets its count to 0 and the array goes only with the file.

The handoff follows Linux's aperture helpers. fbdrm drives the framebuffer the loader left and records its range. A native driver, before it touches its hardware, removes every firmware device in the ranges its device decodes; the console leaves the removed device and moves to the native one when that registers. The native drivers probe first, so on a machine with one fbdrm finds the table closed and never registers. So a machine shows one card for its screen, the native one when there is one. Each other card with mode setting and dumb buffers has its connectors probed once when it registers, as Linux's fbdev client of each device probes them, so that its `status` in sysfs names a connected display before a compositor opens the card.

fbdrm has one mode, the loader's, and copies each framebuffer's damage onto the screen, converting from XRGB8888 to the screen's format, as simpledrm does; its framebuffers are shmem buffers. bochs keeps its buffers as Linux's VRAM helper does: a dumb buffer goes in video memory when there is room, placed from the top so that the firmware's screen at the bottom is used last, and otherwise in system memory; the console's own buffer goes from the bottom, made or moved back, as Linux's fbdev emulation has its buffer at offset 0, so that while it is pinned on the screen the rest of video memory stays in one piece for a master's first commit, which pins its buffer before it unpins the console's. A master's buffers may still lie in video memory when the console makes its buffer, and from the top the console's would land between them and split what they leave: std-vga's 16 MiB would then hold no 1920x1080 frame of 8.3 MB; a commit pins each buffer it shows, moving it into video memory first and moving out unpinned buffers, the least recently shown first, to make room. A mapping follows its buffer: a move zaps it, and the next touch faults to where the buffer is now. A mode set enables the DISPI with `VBE_DISPI_NOCLEARMEM`, since without it QEMU zeroes the mode's size of video memory from offset 0, where another buffer may lie; Linux's `bochs_hw_setmode` leaves the bit clear, a fault [the index of upstream faults](../../../dev-log/upstream.md) records.

virtio-gpu sends each command in DMA pages with room for the answer, the memory entries of `ATTACH_BACKING` going on into extra pages, and every control command but `SET_SCANOUT`, `SET_SCANOUT_BLOB`, `RESOURCE_MAP_BLOB`, `RESOURCE_UNMAP_BLOB`, `RESOURCE_FLUSH` and an `EXECBUFFER` with no reader of its fence carries a fence whose id the device's timeline gives in queue order, so a buffer's reservation and `VIRTGPU_WAIT` see the device's progress. A command that finds the queue full waits on a list the answers drain, in order. Every command holds its pages of the DMA pool, the 64 MiB below `HARD_MEMORY_LIMIT`, until it is freed, so a sender that may sleep, an object's last reference, a context's close and the ioctls that make, fill and submit, reaps and sleeps on the answers while the commands started and not yet freed hold 256 pages or more (`vgpu_room`), as Linux's sender sleeps while the queue has no room, and stops waiting while another thread's reap sleeps, since that reap holds the answers it took and may wait for the mode lock the sender holds, as an unplug does; without that bound a program's exit, which frees every buffer it made with a DETACH and an UNREF each, filled the pool with thousands of commands behind the full queue. Its fence goes on the device's list of fences in flight once the command enters the queue, as Linux emits a fence only once the queue has room, and an answer signals its own fence and every earlier one of its fence context on that list, taking each off, as Linux's `virtio_gpu_fence_event_process` does, so the queue's interrupt walks no more fences than the queue holds. The device is notified once the queue's lock is let go, as Linux's `virtqueue_kick_prepare` and `virtqueue_notify` split it. QEMU's virtio-gpu takes notifies without an ioeventfd, so the write stops the processor in QEMU while QEMU runs the commands queued, virgl's decoding and a flush's redraw of the display among them, some 0.8 ms for a submission on the testing PC; on virtio-pci, whose notify is one register write, a sender that may sleep, `EXECBUFFER`, a commit's plane updates and every waiter, makes it without the big lock, which a sleep there would let go too, so that the other processors' kernel work goes on meanwhile, and the device's removal waits for such a notify before it frees the queues. The shared-memory transport's notify signals an eventfd, whose wakes and irqfd delivery need the big lock, so it is made with the lock held. Commands are answered in the queue's interrupt or by a reap, and the reaper, in process context, frees those nobody waits for; a waiter polls the queue in steps of 10 ms while it sleeps on the queue's answers, or on the fence before probe has set up that wait queue, so a device without working interrupts is still served, and gives up after five seconds, but for `RESOURCE_MAP_BLOB`, whose answer a device may give only once it has pinned the blob's pages, and which, as on Linux, is waited for until it comes or the device dies, or until SIGKILL, which fails the blob's make with EINTR and leaves the command to the reaper. `RESOURCE_MAP_BLOB` goes unfenced, as Linux's `virtio_gpu_cmd_map` sends it: QEMU and the test bed's backend answer it at once, and a fenced answer would signal every earlier fence of the device's timeline while the renderer still works on them, and a program would read back a texture or a framebuffer before it is drawn. The sleeps of an ioctl for the device, for room in the DMA pool and for a transport's memory, end at SIGKILL with EINTR too, as Linux's waits there are killable, so a device that has stopped answering leaves no process in the state D that only a reboot ends; the sleeps of a free and of a close are not, since they must finish. Its buffers are shmem objects whose pages are all given at creation, by the transport where it places them (`virtio_backing_fill`), and handed to the host as the backing of a 2D or virgl 3D resource. A transport whose memory is full while UNREFs are in flight, such as the shared region's heap of a few MiB, which a program that frees and makes buffers faster than the device answers fills with pages already let go, is waited on until an UNREF is answered and asked again; the buffer is refused with ENOMEM only when no UNREF is in flight. The resource's create and its `ATTACH_BACKING` are queued without a wait, as Linux's `virtio_gpu_object_create` queues them, and the attach's fence of a resource that `VIRTGPU_RESOURCE_CREATE` makes, 2D or virgl 3D, goes in the buffer's reservation, as Linux fences that ioctl's create, so a slow host delays only what waits on the buffer; a dumb buffer's attach puts none there, as Linux's `virtio_gpu_mode_dumb_create` passes none, so the console's first commit of a card bound over the shared region, which runs in the transport's thread, waits on no answer that only that thread would reap. An error answer is logged; a buffer's last reference sends `RESOURCE_UNREF` and frees the pages when the host answers, since until then it may read them. A commit copies the damaged part of a dumb buffer to the host, sets the scanout when the buffer, its source or the mode changed, and queues the flush without a fence and without waiting, as Linux does for every framebuffer but a dumb guest blob, which Rach2 never makes; the queue's order puts the flush after the commands that drew. The copy and the scanout go in without a notify, and the flush's notify carries them, made even when a full queue holds the flush back, as Linux's plane update leaves them for its `virtio_gpu_notify`, so QEMU takes the three in one exit. QEMU answers a fenced command only at its next poll of virgl's fences, at the guest's next notify or on a 10 ms timer, so a commit that waited for a fenced flush would hold back its event by up to 10 ms on every frame. `RESOURCE_UNMAP_BLOB` goes unfenced, as Linux sends it, since QEMU holds the queue behind an unfenced command that waits for a mapping's removal but runs the commands behind a fenced one, and an UNREF that meets its resource still mapped fails. A command of an ioctl or of an object's free, and the pages of its payload and memory entries, whose memory earlier commands hold, waits up to five seconds for their answers to free it (`vgpu_cmd_new_wait`, `vgpu_dma_alloc_wait`), as Linux allocates its commands with `__GFP_NOFAIL`: on the shared region the pool is the heap that also holds the buffers' backing, which piglit's streaming-texture-leak fills with pages whose UNREFs are not yet answered, and a SUBMIT_3D that failed at once there returned ENOMEM to Mesa. A commit's commands, whose sender holds the mode lock that a reap may wait for, take only what the pool has. An UNREF that fails, but for an answer of an invalid resource id, or an UNREF, CTX_DETACH_RESOURCE or CTX_DESTROY that cannot be allocated even so, logs the command and unplugs and resets the device before backing is freed; an invalid resource id says the host has no resource of that id and so holds none of the buffer's pages, as after a `RESOURCE_CREATE_BLOB` that failed when the host's render server died, and the buffer is freed with the device left running, as Linux frees it whatever the answer. The configuration interrupt only marks a display change; the thread rereads the EDIDs and displays and sends the hotplug, since both need commands answered. It holds a reference on the device through each pass, since a query sleeps and an unplug may meanwhile drop every other one, as Linux flushes the work before the device goes, and asks a device that a query found dead nothing more.

The virgl path is in `virtio_gpu_3d.c`, `virtio_gpu_submit.c` and `virtio_gpu_fence.c`. Probe negotiates `VIRTIO_GPU_F_VIRGL` and reads each capability set's id, maximum version and size; GET_CAPS reads the requested version, copying at most the host's size, with scatter lists for responses that span pages, and keeps it, as Linux's capset cache does: the host is asked once for each set and version, and every later call, from any process, gets the same bytes. virglrenderer fills a set when it is asked, so a host that answers differently from one request to the next would otherwise give each process a different set. Each open file gets its own context, created lazily and destroyed at close. Its creation, lazy or by CONTEXT_INIT, runs under the context's mutex, held across the allocation of the CTX_CREATE, which may sleep, so that two threads of one file send one CTX_CREATE, as Linux's `context_lock` makes them. Resources share the 2D path's shmem backing and lifetime; an ordinary virgl transfer carries the box, offset and level, and refuses nonzero stride or layer stride as Linux does for a resource without blobs. A context attaches each buffer once, when its file gets a handle to it, by creation, GEM_OPEN or PRIME import, as Linux's `virtio_gpu_gem_object_open` does, and before its first use, and detaches it before the resource's last UNREF. A detach's allocation may sleep, and meanwhile another file's close may destroy its context, so the detach holds the context across the sleep, and the close leaves the record to the last such hold to free; a context closed meanwhile has sent its CTX_DESTROY, which drops its attachments on the host, and is sent no detach; Venus imports another context's memory by its resource id, which the host refuses unless the importing context holds it.

Blobs and context initialization are in `virtio_gpu_blob.c`, as Linux 6.12 has them. Probe asks for `VIRTIO_GPU_F_RESOURCE_BLOB`, `VIRTIO_GPU_F_CONTEXT_INIT` and `VIRTIO_GPU_F_RESOURCE_UUID`, and finds the host-visible region, shared memory region 1, which QEMU's `hostmem` puts in a 64-bit BAR, 4 GiB at 14 TiB; the region is never mapped whole. RESOURCE_CREATE_BLOB checks the blob as Linux's `verify_blob` does, sends the commands that make a blob of host memory as a SUBMIT_3D in the file's context, then CREATE_BLOB, queued without a wait with its fence in the blob's reservation, with the entries of the blob's pages when it has guest memory. A mappable blob of host memory alone takes the first piece of the region it fits in, and MAP_BLOB, waited for, has the host map it there; the answer's cache type makes a program's mapping of the whole blob write-combining, uncached or cached, and the kernel's mapping, `vmap` for the console's client, is a window of the MMIO region onto that piece alone, kept until the blob is freed. A shareable blob asks the device for its UUID when the device gives them. A blob's last reference sends UNMAP_BLOB before its UNREF, and the piece goes back when the host has answered; a MAP_BLOB the host refuses gives the piece back at once. RESOURCE_INFO calls a blob with any host memory HOST3D. A transfer refuses a blob of guest memory alone, and takes a stride only for a blob with host memory. A blob framebuffer is shown with SET_SCANOUT_BLOB, without the copy a dumb buffer takes.

CONTEXT_INIT takes at most four parameters: an offered capability set up to 63, at most 64 rings, a poll mask within them, and a name. It checks them all before any takes effect, refuses a parameter given twice, and refuses once the file's context exists, with EEXIST, which a second thread whose call raced the first's also gets, since the parameters go in and the context is created under the context's mutex; the context's CTX_CREATE then carries the capability set and the name given, an empty one included, or, with none given, the calling thread's name, as Linux's `virtio_gpu_create_context_locked` sends it. Each ring is a fence context of its own. A submission with `VIRTGPU_EXECBUF_RING_IDX` takes its ring's fence context and sends the ring's index to the device, an answer retires only the earlier fences of its own ring, and a ring in the poll mask reserves a bare `VIRTGPU_EVENT_FENCE_SIGNALED` event at submission, which the reaper sends the file at the answer.

EXECBUFFER copies the command and holds every buffer in its handle list until the host answers. In-fence sync files and input syncobjs wait only for fence leaves of another fence context: the device keeps the order of one context's commands, the control queue's or one ring's, and of nothing across them. The submit path never waits for its own earlier fences. The submission carries a fence only when something reads it, an out-fence sync file, an output syncobj, a buffer in the handle list or a polled ring's event, as Linux's `virtio_gpu_init_submit` gives one, since QEMU answers a fenced command only at its next poll of virgl's fences and keeps each fence on its list until then. Its out-fence joins each buffer's reservation and may be exported as a sync file or an output syncobj, including a timeline point. All fallible allocations precede publication; an abandoned prepared fence is signalled with its error. A later fence response retires earlier points on that timeline, including when responses arrive out of order. WAIT with NOWAIT returns EBUSY for outstanding work; a blocking WAIT allows 15 seconds. The worker frees completed commands in process context as well as serving display changes.

Root can write `1` to `/sys/kernel/debug/dri/N/virtio_gpu_reset` to unplug that GPU for a device-loss test. The core revokes every mapping and removes the nodes, the transport stops DMA, every pending fence signals with ENODEV, every context is invalidated, and every resource's backing is detached from the host. Open descriptors keep their records until close, and subsequent ioctls return ENODEV. An object of guest memory keeps its frames until it is freed, and an old mapping of it, its own or an importer's through its dma-buf, takes them again at the next touch, as on Linux, whose shmem helper serves a fault without asking for the device, so a program that drew through the GPU goes on and draws nothing. An object whose frames a transport gave from its own memory, such as the shared region's heap, gives them back at the reset, after zapping its mappings, and an old mapping of it takes new pages at the next touch; one whose dma-buf exists at the reset keeps them until it is freed, since an importer, i915 among them, may hold them in its GTT and page tables, and its old mappings and its importers' take the same frames again; an old mapping of a blob of host memory faults. Reusing the GPU requires another boot. A device that goes for good, as when the VM that serves the shared region dies, is stopped and its pending fences fail before the core unplugs it, since a commit that waits for one of them holds the commit lock, which the unplug and the display's shutdown take, and a device that has gone answers nothing.

vkms has no hardware. Its vblank timer, Linux's hrtimer in `vkms_vblank_simulate`, is a deadline a frame of the mode apart, which `vkms_tick` serves: once the deadline has passed it moves it on past the clock by whole frames, as `hrtimer_forward_now` does, handles one vblank as an interrupt would, and publishes the next deadline through `timer_service_wake_at`, so that the timer pass runs it near its time and not at the next 4 ms tick. The boot processor's tick runs it without the big lock, among its services with locks of their own, as Linux's hrtimer callback runs in an interrupt, so that a holder of the big lock on another processor, whose virtual processor the host may have stopped, does not hold the vblank back: the device's `timer_lock` guards the timer and is taken after `vbl_lock`, its `lock`, Linux's `vkms_output.lock`, guards the scene and the composer's request, and the tick holds neither while the core counts the vblank. `get_vblank_timestamp` stamps the vblank with the latest deadline passed, as Linux's `vkms_get_vblank_timestamp` does, so that vblanks are a frame apart however late the pass runs, and the core counts the frames a late pass skipped from the stamps, so that no event asked for meanwhile carries a time before it was asked for; IGT's kms_flip fails when the times vary by more than 0.5% of a frame, and the times of the passes, on a 4 ms tick, came 16 or 20 ms apart for a frame of 16.67 ms. Each commit's flush copies the planes it shows into a scene; while a CRC source runs, each vblank asks the `kvkms` kernel thread, started at the first source, to compose the newest scene in 16 bits a channel as Linux's vkms does and add its CRC, Linux's `crc32_le` eight bytes at a time ([`lib/crc32.c`](../lib/crc32.h)), for the frames since the last it composed. A commit ends the replaced scene's compositions before it returns, as Linux's `vkms_atomic_commit_tail` flushes the old state's composer work: its flush waits for a composition the thread began to end, then composes the frames already asked for from the replaced scene while the thread takes no frame, so that the CRCs are entered in frame order, as Linux's ordered composer workqueue runs one work at a time, and no CRC of the old scene is entered after the commit returns. A frame of 1024x768 takes most of a vblank, some 11 ms of the testing PC's processor, and is composed without the big lock, as Linux's composer holds no global lock, so that the composition and the system calls of other threads, every ioctl of vkms among them, do not wait for each other: under the lock the composition allocates its rows and looks up and pins the frames each plane's framebuffer covers, as Linux's `prepare_fb` maps them; on i686 it maps each plane's pinned frames, in order, into one window of the MMIO region (`mmio_map_frames`), as Linux maps the buffer once with `vmap`, and takes the window away when it unpins them; it composes the rows without the lock, reading each plane through its window, or, on x86-64, through the direct map, and a plane the region has no room for, or with a frame without a pin, a page at a time through the processor's own mapping slot, `kmap_cpu`, which takes no lock and has no other processor drop a translation; it gives way after each row to a thread queued on its processor, and takes the lock again to enter the CRCs and unpin the frames. The scene's references keep each framebuffer, and so its buffer objects, while the pins keep the frames, so a framebuffer removed during a composition stays whole to its end. A frame that takes no pin is a device's, as a virtio-gpu blob in the host's memory gives, which a reset of the device takes back: a scene with one is composed under the lock, its frames looked up at each read, as before the lock was let go.

vgem has no hardware either. Its buffers are dumb buffers of the shmem helper, mapped write-combined as Linux's vgem_gem_create_object asks. VGEM_FENCE_ATTACH puts a new fence, on a timeline of its own, on a buffer's reservation, which every importer of its dma-buf shares, so that EXECBUFFER2 of an importing i915 holds its request until the fence signals, and the request's commands stay in the ring meanwhile with RING_TAIL behind them, as on Linux. VGEM_FENCE_SIGNAL signals it; the commit worker's delayed work signals one left 10 s, as Linux's timer does, and the close of its file signals every one it still holds.

The files of `sys/drm/` that every architecture compiles are listed in [`sources.mk`](sources.mk), and those that reach PC-only code, bochs, fbdrm, the console's client and the PC's probes, in [`pc.mk`](pc.mk), which `sys/arch/x86/sources.mk` includes, as it does the Intel driver's [`i915/sources.mk`](i915/sources.mk); `sys/dmabuf/sources.mk` and `sys/virtio/sources.mk`, the [virtio transport](../virtio/Readme.md)'s, are included by `rach2/sources.mk`.

## Limits

* On real hardware fbdrm keeps the loader's mode and sets none of its own. i915, which binds only under `rach2.i915`, sets modes on Haswell: on the eDP panel, the panel's fixed mode or a smaller one through the pipe's fitter, and on DDI B to D, HDMI, DVI and single-stream DisplayPort, with HDMI and DisplayPort audio but no DisplayPort MST, no colour management and no VGA output ([`i915/Readme.md`](i915/Readme.md)).
* virtio-gpu has virgl 3D, blobs and context initialization when offered. On i686 a host-visible region past 4 GiB, where QEMU puts it, is refused with a record, since a page table entry there holds no address past 4 GiB; blobs of guest memory still work, and a mappable blob of host memory alone returns EINVAL. A display change sends its hotplug uevent from the `virtio_gpu` thread, which the first open of the device's nodes makes, since a kernel thread made before init would take pid 1; a change before that open is served by the open.
* A sync object's wait ends with `EINTR` on a signal, where Linux restarts it with `ERESTARTSYS`; libdrm's `drmIoctl` repeats the call either way.
* A page flip to a target vblank is `EINVAL`, Linux's answer for a driver without `page_flip_target`.
* vkms has no writeback connector, which Linux's has by default.
* The EDID's quirks, the DisplayID blocks other than CTA blocks, tiles and the non-desktop flag are not read.
* debugfs has no `internal_clients`, and none of the DRM core's HDMI and DisplayPort files of a connector, which IGT's CRC and forcing tests do not open; `bridges` is empty, since no driver has bridges.
