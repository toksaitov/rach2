# DRM

`sys/drm/` is the kernel's Direct Rendering Manager, Linux's interface to display and rendering devices: the card and render nodes under `/dev/dri`, their ioctls, buffer objects, mode setting and the firmware framebuffer's handoff to a native driver. `sys/dmabuf/` beside it shares buffers and fences between devices and processes: dma-bufs, fences, sync files, `/dev/udmabuf` and sw_sync. Programs reach both through libdrm, Mesa and the compositors as they do on Linux 6.12, whose numbers and layouts [`uapi.h`](uapi.h) copies. [`drm.h`](drm.h) is the contract every file of both directories keeps. Most files are still stubs: their ioctl handlers answer `EOPNOTSUPP`, as Linux does for a driver without the feature, and their probes find no device.

## Use

`drm_start()` runs once from `kernel.c`, after the network has started, and logs `drm: start`. It starts `/dev/udmabuf`, then probes the display devices in the order the handoff needs: the PC's native drivers (bochs, through `arch_drm_probe(ARCH_DRM_NATIVE)`), virtio-gpu, then the driver of the firmware's framebuffer (fbdrm, through `arch_drm_probe(ARCH_DRM_FIRMWARE)`), then vkms, the display in software. A probe that finds no device answers `ENODEV` and is not logged; any other error is logged as `drm: NAME: error N`. While every driver is a stub, the kernel logs `drm: start` and `/dev/dri` is absent.

## Interfaces

[`uapi.h`](uapi.h) is the user ABI, Linux 6.12's `drm.h`, `drm_mode.h`, `drm_fourcc.h` (the formats in use), `dma-buf.h`, `sync_file.h`, the sw_sync ioctls and `udmabuf.h`, in C89: every structure the 6.12 ioctl table names, with Linux's names, members and sizes on i386 and x86-64, and every `DRM_IOCTL_*`, `DMA_BUF_*`, `SYNC_IOC_*`, `SW_SYNC_IOC_*` and `UDMABUF_*` number. `UAPI_IO`, `UAPI_IOR`, `UAPI_IOW` and `UAPI_IOWR` encode a number as Linux's `_IOC` does (direction in bits 30 and 31, size in 16 to 29, type in 8 to 15, number in 0 to 7), and `UAPI_IOC_DIR`, `UAPI_IOC_SIZE`, `UAPI_IOC_TYPE` and `UAPI_IOC_NR` take one apart. Linux's `long` is `intptr_t` and `unsigned long` `uintptr_t`, so a member keeps its width on each ABI; a pointer member carries `__user`. The DRI1 calls that 6.12 no longer has are left out, and the one anonymous union, in `hdr_output_metadata`, is flattened, since C89 has none.

[`drm.h`](drm.h) holds the rules every file keeps, in its opening comment, and the types and functions the files share:

* Drivers. `struct drm_ident` is what a driver says of itself: DRM_IOCTL_VERSION's name, description, date and version, its `DRIVER_*` features and its bus, `DRM_BUS_PCI` or `DRM_BUS_PLATFORM`. `DRM_IDENT_I915`, `DRM_IDENT_VIRTIO_GPU`, `DRM_IDENT_BOCHS`, `DRM_IDENT_SIMPLEDRM` and `DRM_IDENT_VKMS` are the identities Linux 6.12's i915, virtio_gpu, bochs-drm, simpledrm and vkms report; fbdrm reports itself as simpledrm. `struct drm_driver` holds the identity, the hooks `open`, `postclose`, `dumb_create`, `gem_prime_import` and `unplug`, and the driver's own ioctls as `struct drm_ioctl_desc` rows, each with its full number, its `DRM_AUTH`, `DRM_MASTER`, `DRM_ROOT_ONLY` and `DRM_RENDER_ALLOW` flags and its `drm_ioctl_t` handler, which takes the kernel's copy of the argument.
* Devices. `struct drm_device` is a bound device: its driver, features (`drm_core_check_feature`), card index, references, `unique` name, PCI function, master file, mode configuration and commit lock. `drm_dev_init`, `drm_dev_register`, `drm_dev_unplug`, `drm_dev_get`, `drm_dev_put` and `drm_dev_at` make, publish, lose, hold and find one. Card N is `/dev/dri/cardN`, major `DRM_MAJOR` (226) and minor N, and its render node `/dev/dri/renderD(128 + N)`, minor `DRM_MINOR_RENDER_BASE` + N, up to `DRM_MAX_DEVICES` (64); they are nodes of the devfs registry ([`../fs/devfs.h`](../fs/devfs.h)), whose `/dev/dri` directory is present only while it has one.
* Open files. `struct drm_file` is one open of a node (`DRM_MINOR_PRIMARY` or `DRM_MINOR_RENDER`), shared by every descriptor of its open file description, with its handles (`struct drm_handle`) and its queue of `struct drm_pending_event`.
* Buffer objects. `struct drm_gem_object` is a buffer of whole pages, with its flink name, its `struct dma_resv` of implicit fences and its driver's `struct drm_gem_object_funcs`: `free`, `mmap` into a `vma_t`, `pin` and `unpin`.
* Fences. `struct dma_fence` is a point on a timeline that signals once, with an error or none; `struct dma_fence_ops` its driver's names and hooks; `struct dma_fence_cb` a callback. `dma_fence_context_alloc`, `dma_fence_init`, `dma_fence_get`, `dma_fence_put`, `dma_fence_set_error`, `dma_fence_signal`, `dma_fence_is_signaled`, `dma_fence_add_callback`, `dma_fence_remove_callback` and `dma_fence_wait_until` are their life. `is_dma_buf_file`, `sync_file_get_fence`, `udmabuf_start` and `sw_sync_open` are what `sys/dmabuf/` offers the rest of the kernel.
* Display state. `struct drm_mode_object` (with up to `DRM_OBJECT_MAX_PROPERTY` properties), `drm_property`, `drm_property_blob`, `drm_framebuffer`, `drm_plane` (`DRM_PLANE_TYPE_*`), `drm_crtc` with its `drm_vblank` and `drm_crtc_crc_entry` ring, `drm_encoder`, `drm_connector` (`DRM_CONNECTOR_*` status, `DRM_FORCE_*`, its EDID and override) and `drm_display_mode` are the mode objects; `drm_plane_state`, `drm_crtc_state`, `drm_connector_state` and `drm_atomic_state` (up to `DRM_MAX_CRTCS`, `DRM_MAX_PLANES` and `DRM_MAX_CONNECTORS`) a commit's states; `drm_plane_funcs`, `drm_crtc_funcs` (with the CRC hooks `set_crc_source`, `verify_crc_source` and `get_crc_sources`) and `drm_connector_funcs` the drivers' hooks; and `drm_mode_config` a device's limits, objects and `atomic_check` and `atomic_commit`.
* The handoff. `drm_aperture_acquire_firmware` records the range of the firmware's framebuffer a firmware driver drives and the hook that removes it, in a table of `DRM_APERTURE_MAX` (4); `drm_aperture_remove_conflicting` removes each firmware device whose range overlaps a native driver's.
* Helpers. `drm_crtc_handle_vblank`, `drm_crtc_add_crc_entry`, `drm_connector_update_edid` and `drm_kms_hotplug_event` are what a driver calls from its interrupts and probes; `drm_client_dev_register` and `drm_client_dev_unregister` tell the console of a device that comes and goes.
* Probes. `bochs_probe`, `fbdrm_probe`, `virtio_gpu_probe` and `vkms_probe` bind their devices and return how many, or `-errno`. bochs and fbdrm reach PC-only code, so `drm_start` calls them through `arch_drm_probe` ([`../arch/arch.h`](../arch/arch.h)), which `drm_pc.c` defines on the PC.
* Ioctl handlers. Every handler the core's table names is declared, grouped by the file that defines it: `drm.c` the identity, capabilities, `drm_noop` and `drm_invalid_op`; `drm_auth.c` magic and the master role; `drm_gem.c` handles, flink and dumb buffers; `drm_prime.c` PRIME; `drm_mode.c` the mode objects, properties, blobs, framebuffers and leases; `drm_legacy.c` the calls before atomic; `drm_atomic.c` the atomic commit; `drm_vblank.c` vblank waits and CRTC sequences; and `drm_syncobj.c` sync objects.

## Sources and license

`uapi.h` is transcribed from Linux 6.12's `include/uapi/drm/drm.h`, `drm_mode.h` and `drm_fourcc.h`, which carry the MIT license's permission notice, and `include/uapi/linux/dma-buf.h` and `udmabuf.h` (GPL-2.0 WITH Linux-syscall-note) and `sync_file.h` (GPL-1.0+ WITH Linux-syscall-note), whose note leaves a program that uses the interface free of the GPL. The two sw_sync structures and their numbers are those of `drivers/dma-buf/sw_sync.c`, which has no uapi header; IGT and Android's libsync copy them in the same way. The identities in `drm.h` are copied from the drivers named beside them. The rest is Rach2's own.

## Design

The opening comment of `drm.h` is the design every file keeps: locking under the big kernel lock with a device's commit lock for commits; who holds a reference to a device, an open file, a buffer object, a framebuffer and a fence, and what close, `dup`, `fork`, `exec` and exit drop; what an unplugged device answers (`ENODEV`, `POLLERR | POLLHUP`, `SIGBUS` on a revoked mapping, fences signalled with `-ENODEV`); and discovery by identity, never by minor number, since card numbers follow the order of registration.

The handoff follows Linux's aperture helpers. fbdrm drives the framebuffer the loader left and records its range. A native driver, before it touches its hardware, removes every firmware device in the ranges its device decodes; the console leaves the removed device and moves to the native one when that registers. So a machine shows one card for its screen, the native one when there is one.

The files of `sys/drm/` that every architecture compiles are listed in [`sources.mk`](sources.mk), and those that reach PC-only code, bochs, fbdrm, the console's client and the PC's probes, in [`pc.mk`](pc.mk), which `sys/arch/x86/sources.mk` includes; `sys/dmabuf/sources.mk` and `sys/virtio/sources.mk`, empty until the virtio transport comes, are included by `rach2/sources.mk`. Each stub's opening comment names the work that replaces it.

## Limits

* No driver binds a device yet, so `/dev/dri` is absent and every ioctl of the core's table, once the dispatcher exists, answers `EOPNOTSUPP`.
* The descriptor operations `mmap` and `llseek` ([`../fs/file.h`](../fs/file.h)) are declared and not yet called by the system calls.

## Tests

* `tests/host/drm_abi_hosttest.c` checks the size and the offset of every member of every structure of `uapi.h`, and every ioctl number, against Linux 6.12's on x86-64, natively, and on i386, by the layout rules of that ABI from each member's type.
* `tests/host/devfs_hosttest.c` registers `dri/card0` in the devfs registry and finds it by path, number and listing, and `tests/host/synthfs_hosttest.c` classifies `/dev/dri` and its nodes.
