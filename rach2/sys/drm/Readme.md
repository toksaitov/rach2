# DRM

`sys/drm/` is the kernel's Direct Rendering Manager, Linux's interface to display and rendering devices: the card and render nodes under `/dev/dri`, their ioctls, buffer objects, mode setting and the firmware framebuffer's handoff to a native driver. `sys/dmabuf/` beside it shares buffers and fences between devices and processes: dma-bufs, fences, sync files, `/dev/udmabuf` and sw_sync. Programs reach both through libdrm, Mesa and the compositors as they do on Linux 6.12, whose numbers and layouts [`uapi.h`](uapi.h) copies. [`drm.h`](drm.h) is the contract every file of both directories keeps. The core is in place: [`drm.c`](drm.c) the devices, their nodes, open files, events and the ioctl dispatcher with Linux's table, [`drm_auth.c`](drm_auth.c) the master role and magic, and [`drm_gem.c`](drm_gem.c) buffer handles, flink names, mmap offsets, the shmem helper and dumb buffers, [`drm_prime.c`](drm_prime.c) PRIME buffer sharing, [`drm_sysfs.c`](drm_sysfs.c) each device's entries in `/sys` and its uevents, and [`drm_debugfs.c`](drm_debugfs.c) its files in debugfs. Mode setting is Linux's KMS: [`drm_mode.c`](drm_mode.c) the mode objects, properties, blobs, framebuffers and the probe of a connector's modes, [`drm_atomic.c`](drm_atomic.c) the atomic check and commit, with its fences and its worker, [`drm_vblank.c`](drm_vblank.c) the vblank counts, waits and events and the CRC entries, [`drm_legacy.c`](drm_legacy.c) the calls before atomic made commits, and [`drm_edid.c`](drm_edid.c) a monitor's EDID and its modes. Four drivers set modes: [`fbdrm.c`](fbdrm.c) drives the framebuffer the loader left as Linux's simpledrm, [`bochs.c`](bochs.c) QEMU's standard VGA as Linux's bochs-drm, [`virtio_gpu.c`](virtio_gpu.c) the virtio GPU display and virgl 3D, as Linux's virtio_gpu, and [`vkms.c`](vkms.c) is Linux's vkms, a display in software for IGT's tests, with planes composed in memory, vblanks from the tick and a CRC of each frame. `drm.4` is the interface a program sees, `drm-kms.7` mode setting, and `bochs.4`, `fbdrm.4`, `virtio-gpu.4` and `vkms.4` each driver. The other files are still stubs: their ioctl handlers answer `EOPNOTSUPP`, as Linux does for a driver without the feature, and their probes find no device.

## Use

`drm_start()` runs once from `kernel.c`, after the network has started, and logs `drm: start`. It starts `/dev/udmabuf`, then probes the display devices in the order the handoff needs: the PC's native drivers (bochs and [i915](i915/Readme.md), through `arch_drm_probe(ARCH_DRM_NATIVE)`), virtio-gpu, then the driver of the firmware's framebuffer (fbdrm, through `arch_drm_probe(ARCH_DRM_FIRMWARE)`), then vkms, the display in software. A probe that finds no device answers `ENODEV` and is not logged; any other error is logged as `drm: NAME: error N`. In QEMU with its standard VGA, bochs binds the device and logs `bochs: registered`, and fbdrm then finds the framebuffer taken. With `-device virtio-gpu-pci` or `-device virtio-vga`, virtio-gpu binds it and logs `virtio_gpu: pci: virtio-vga detected at ...` and each scanout's size; under virtio-vga fbdrm then finds the framebuffer taken. Booted by UEFI, or by BIOS with a `gfxpayload` mode, on a machine whose display no native driver drives, as the Lenovo's while i915 sets no modes, fbdrm binds the loader's framebuffer and logs `fbdrm: registered`. A BIOS boot in text mode on such a machine has no card. With `rach2.vkms` on the command line, vkms binds after them and logs `vkms: registered` with its planes and card.

## Interfaces

[`uapi.h`](uapi.h) is the user ABI, Linux 6.12's `drm.h`, `drm_mode.h`, `drm_fourcc.h` (the formats in use), `dma-buf.h`, `sync_file.h`, the sw_sync ioctls and `udmabuf.h`, in C89: every structure the 6.12 ioctl table names, with Linux's names, members and sizes on i386 and x86-64, and every `DRM_IOCTL_*`, `DMA_BUF_*`, `SYNC_IOC_*`, `SW_SYNC_IOC_*` and `UDMABUF_*` number. `UAPI_IO`, `UAPI_IOR`, `UAPI_IOW` and `UAPI_IOWR` encode a number as Linux's `_IOC` does (direction in bits 30 and 31, size in 16 to 29, type in 8 to 15, number in 0 to 7), and `UAPI_IOC_DIR`, `UAPI_IOC_SIZE`, `UAPI_IOC_TYPE` and `UAPI_IOC_NR` take one apart. Linux's `long` is `intptr_t` and `unsigned long` `uintptr_t`, so a member keeps its width on each ABI; a pointer member carries `__user`. The DRI1 calls that 6.12 no longer has are left out, and the one anonymous union, in `hdr_output_metadata`, is flattened, since C89 has none.

[`drm.h`](drm.h) holds the rules every file keeps, in its opening comment, and the types and functions the files share:

* Drivers. `struct drm_ident` is what a driver says of itself: DRM_IOCTL_VERSION's name, description, date and version, its `DRIVER_*` features and its bus, `DRM_BUS_PCI` or `DRM_BUS_PLATFORM`. `DRM_IDENT_I915`, `DRM_IDENT_VIRTIO_GPU`, `DRM_IDENT_BOCHS`, `DRM_IDENT_SIMPLEDRM` and `DRM_IDENT_VKMS` are the identities Linux 6.12's i915, virtio_gpu, bochs-drm, simpledrm and vkms report; fbdrm reports itself as simpledrm. `struct drm_driver` holds the identity, the hooks `open`, `postclose`, `dumb_create`, `gem_prime_import` and `unplug`, and the driver's own ioctls as `struct drm_ioctl_desc` rows, each with its full number, its `DRM_AUTH`, `DRM_MASTER`, `DRM_ROOT_ONLY` and `DRM_RENDER_ALLOW` flags and its `drm_ioctl_t` handler, which takes the kernel's copy of the argument.
* Devices. `struct drm_device` is a bound device: its driver, features (`drm_core_check_feature`), card index, references, `unique` name, PCI function, master file, open files, buffer objects, mode configuration and commit lock. `drm_dev_init`, `drm_dev_register`, `drm_dev_unplug`, `drm_dev_get`, `drm_dev_put` and `drm_dev_at` make, publish, lose, hold and find one. `drm_dev_register` takes the lowest free index, and its `unique` name, unless the driver set one, is Linux's `pci:0000:BB:DD.F` for a PCI device and the driver's name otherwise. `drm_dev_unplug` removes the nodes and the `/sys` entries, zaps every mapping of the device's buffers, wakes the files and calls the driver's `unplug` hook; the last `drm_dev_put` frees its index and calls the driver's `release` hook, which frees the record. Card N is `/dev/dri/cardN`, major `DRM_MAJOR` (226) and minor N, and its render node `/dev/dri/renderD(128 + N)`, minor `DRM_MINOR_RENDER_BASE` + N, up to `DRM_MAX_DEVICES` (64); they are nodes of the devfs registry ([`../fs/devfs.h`](../fs/devfs.h)), whose `/dev/dri` directory is present only while it has one.
* Open files. `struct drm_file` is one open of a node (`DRM_MINOR_PRIMARY` or `DRM_MINOR_RENDER`), shared by every descriptor of its open file description, with its handles (`struct drm_handle`), its place in the master records and its queue of `struct drm_pending_event`. The node's `file_ops` read, poll, ioctl, mmap and close it; `drm_ioctl` copies the argument by the direction the number and the table's row share, checks the row's flags in Linux's order and calls the handler. `drm_event_reserve` takes room in the file's 4096 bytes of events, `drm_send_event` signals and drops its fence, queues a reserved event and wakes the readers, and `drm_event_cancel_free` drops its fence and gives the room back; an event reserved for a file that closes is freed when it is sent.
* Master. `drm_is_current_master` says whether a file is its device's master. `drm_master_open` makes the first opener of a card with no master master, `drm_master_release` drops the role of a file that closes, and each change calls every `struct drm_master_notifier` registered with `drm_master_notifier_register`.
* Buffer objects. `struct drm_gem_object` is a buffer of whole pages, with its flink name, its mmap offset, its `struct dma_resv` of implicit fences and its driver's `struct drm_gem_object_funcs`: `free`, `mmap` into a `vma_t`, `pin`, `unpin`, the borrowed physical `page` for PRIME, which the console's flush of its shadow also copies through, and `vmap`, the kernel's address of a buffer in video memory, which the console draws through; bochs gives it, and virtio-gpu, whose buffers are scattered pages, does not. `drm_gem_object_init`, `drm_gem_object_get` and `drm_gem_object_put` make, hold and drop one, releasing its own reservation's fences before its storage; `drm_gem_handle_create`, `drm_gem_object_lookup` and `drm_gem_handle_delete` a file's handles to it, the lowest free from 1; `drm_gem_release` drops a closing file's handles. `drm_gem_create_mmap_offset` places the object first-fit in its device's mmap space, from `DRM_FILE_PAGE_OFFSET_START`, and `drm_gem_mmap` finds the object a mapping's offset names and calls its `mmap` hook, which passes the object to `vm_device_map` ([`../mm/vm.h`](../mm/vm.h)). `struct drm_gem_shmem_object` is the shmem helper's object, whose pages a `struct shmem` holds, mapped cached or, with `map_wc`, write-combining; `drm_gem_shmem_create` makes one, `drm_gem_shmem_page` gives a page, and `drm_gem_shmem_dumb_create` is the `dumb_create` hook of a driver that keeps its dumb buffers in memory.
* PRIME. `PRIME_HANDLE_TO_FD` exports a handle as a dma-buf; `PRIME_FD_TO_HANDLE` imports one. A handle caches its dma-buf with a reference until the handle closes, so a repeated import into the same file returns the same handle. A self-import on the same device returns the exporting object, and an imported object reexports the original dma-buf. A dma-buf holds its exporting object, whose `dma_buf` pointer is weak; an imported object holds the dma-buf and uses its reservation. The default import is a shmem object whose `drm_gem_shmem_page` reads the exporter's physical pages, with its cache type. A driver's `gem_prime_import` may replace that helper; it returns one object reference, borrows the dma-buf, and the core attaches its buffer reference and reservation. An export needs an object's `page` hook for import by another device; udmabuf provides its pinned pages. Device loss zaps the importing devices' mappings too.
* Fences. `struct dma_fence` is a point on a timeline that signals once, with an error or none; `struct dma_fence_ops` its driver's names and hooks; `struct dma_fence_cb` a callback. `dma_fence_context_alloc`, `dma_fence_init`, `dma_fence_get`, `dma_fence_put`, `dma_fence_set_error`, `dma_fence_signal`, `dma_fence_is_signaled`, `dma_fence_add_callback`, `dma_fence_remove_callback` and `dma_fence_wait_until` are their life. The [shared-buffer Readme](../dmabuf/Readme.md) gives the buffer, reservation, sync-file and software timeline interfaces.
* Display state. `struct drm_mode_object` (with up to `DRM_OBJECT_MAX_PROPERTY` properties), `drm_property`, `drm_property_blob`, `drm_framebuffer`, `drm_plane` (`DRM_PLANE_TYPE_*`), `drm_crtc` with its `drm_vblank` and `drm_crtc_crc_entry` ring, `drm_encoder`, `drm_connector` (`DRM_CONNECTOR_*` status, `DRM_FORCE_*`, its EDID and override) and `drm_display_mode` are the mode objects; `drm_plane_state`, `drm_crtc_state`, `drm_connector_state` and `drm_atomic_state` (up to `DRM_MAX_CRTCS`, `DRM_MAX_PLANES` and `DRM_MAX_CONNECTORS`) a commit's states; `drm_plane_funcs`, `drm_crtc_funcs` (with the CRC hooks `set_crc_source`, `verify_crc_source` and `get_crc_sources`) and `drm_connector_funcs` the drivers' hooks; and `drm_mode_config` a device's limits, objects and `atomic_check` and `atomic_commit`.
* Making a display. A driver calls `drm_mode_config_init`, which makes Linux's standard properties in Linux's order, then `drm_universal_plane_init` for each plane (its formats, modifiers and IN_FORMATS blob), `drm_crtc_init_with_planes`, `drm_encoder_init` and `drm_connector_init`, and `drm_connector_attach_encoder`; `drm_mode_crtc_set_gamma_size`, `drm_crtc_enable_color_mgmt`, `drm_plane_enable_fb_damage_clips`, `drm_plane_create_rotation_property`, `drm_plane_create_zpos_property`, `drm_plane_create_alpha_property` and `drm_connector_attach_edid_property` add what it has. Each object takes the lowest free ID, as Linux's idr gives them, so the IDs a program sees are Linux's for the same driver. `drm_mode_config_cleanup` frees it all. The `drm_property_create*` calls, `drm_object_attach_property` and `drm_object_property_set_value` and `_get_value` make and use properties; `drm_property_create_blob`, `drm_property_lookup_blob`, `drm_property_blob_get` and `_put` blobs; `drm_framebuffer_lookup`, `_get` and `_put` framebuffers.
* Commits. `drm_modeset_lock` takes the device's commit lock. `drm_atomic_state_alloc` makes a state, `drm_atomic_get_crtc_state`, `_plane_state` and `_connector_state` add an object's copy of its current state, and the `drm_atomic_set_*` calls change it as the properties do; `drm_atomic_check_only`, `drm_atomic_commit` and `drm_atomic_nonblocking_commit` check it and make it the display's, through the driver's `atomic_check` and `atomic_commit` or the core's `drm_atomic_helper_check` and `drm_atomic_helper_commit`, which run the plane and CRTC hooks in Linux's order. `drm_atomic_commit_work` runs the deferred commits whose in-fences have signalled, as the `kdrm` thread does. `drm_atomic_helper_check_plane_state` clips a plane to its CRTC for a driver's check, and `drm_atomic_helper_set_config` is SETCRTC's commit. `drm_helper_probe_single_connector_modes` probes a connector as Linux's helper does: its state, its EDID and modes through the driver's `get_modes`, which calls `drm_connector_update_edid`, the DMT modes up to 1024x768 for a monitor without one (`drm_add_modes_noedid`), each mode checked against the connector's and each CRTC's `mode_valid`, and the list sorted.
* The handoff. `drm_aperture_acquire_firmware` records the range of the firmware's framebuffer a firmware driver drives and the hook that removes it, in a table of `DRM_APERTURE_MAX` (4), and `drm_aperture_release_firmware` forgets a device's ranges when its bind fails after it acquired them. `drm_aperture_remove_conflicting` removes each firmware device whose range overlaps a native driver's, and from then on `drm_aperture_acquire_firmware` answers `EBUSY`, as Linux's sysfb_disable leaves no firmware device to bind once a native driver took the screen.
* Vblanks. A CRTC has vblanks when its driver gives `enable_vblank` and `disable_vblank`, which start and stop its interrupt; `drm_dev_has_vblank` says whether any CRTC of a device has. `drm_crtc_init_with_planes` calls `drm_crtc_vblank_init`, which leaves the vblanks off and held off. `drm_crtc_vblank_get` and `drm_crtc_vblank_put` hold the interrupt on, `drm_crtc_vblank_count_and_time` reads the 64-bit count and the `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` time of the last vblank, `drm_crtc_arm_vblank_event` queues an event for the next vblank and `drm_crtc_send_vblank_event` sends one now. `drm_crtc_vblank_off` and `drm_crtc_vblank_on` are the commit's around a CRTC's disable and enable; off sends every queued event and holds the vblanks off, on adds one to the count. `drm_crtc_wait_flip` waits for a CRTC's `flip_pending` (`DRM_FLIP_QUEUED` while the commit worker has yet to program the flip, `DRM_FLIP_ARMED` until its vblank) to fall below a stage. `drm_vblank_unplug` turns every CRTC off, sends what is queued with `-ENODEV` on its fences, and `drm_crtc_vblank_cleanup` frees a CRTC's queue and CRC ring.
* Debug hooks. `drm_connector_force_write` takes a write of a connector's `force` file, one of `on`, `digital`, `off` or `unspecified` with one newline at most, as Linux's `sysfs_streq` takes it, and the next probe reports that state, calling the driver's `force` hook; `drm_edid_override_set` and `drm_edid_override_reset` set and clear the EDID that stands in for the sink's at each probe. `drm_crtc_crc_set_source` takes a write of `crc/control`, checked by the driver's `verify_crc_source`; `drm_crtc_crc_open` starts the source through `set_crc_source` and makes the ring of `DRM_CRC_ENTRIES_NR` (128) entries, `drm_crtc_crc_read` gives an entry as Linux's line, the frame or ten `X`s and each value as `0x%08x`, and `drm_crtc_crc_release` stops it.
* debugfs. `drm_start` calls `drm_debugfs_init`, which makes `dri`; `drm_dev_register` calls `drm_debugfs_register`, which makes the device's directory, named by its PCI function or its platform device as Linux names it, with Linux 6.12's files: `name`, `clients`, `gem_names`, and with mode setting `framebuffer`, `state` (atomic drivers), `crtc-N/crc/control` and `crc/data`, `encoder-N/bridges` and each connector's `force` and `edid_override`; and the links `dri/N` and, with a render node, `dri/(128 + N)`. The directory holds the device for each node below it ([`../fs/debugfs.Readme.md`](../fs/debugfs.Readme.md)), so a file open across an unplug keeps the device to its last close. `drm_dev_unplug` calls `drm_debugfs_unregister`, which takes the links and the directory out first. A failure to make them is logged and leaves the device without them. `drm_debugfs_path` gives a driver the path of a file of its own in the directory, as virtio-gpu's `virtio_gpu_reset`, since a path through the `dri/N` link makes no file. [debugfs(5)](../../../man/man5/debugfs.5) gives each file's text.
* Helpers. `drm_crtc_handle_vblank`, `drm_crtc_add_crc_entry`, `drm_connector_update_edid` and `drm_kms_hotplug_event` are what a driver calls from its interrupts and probes; `drm_client_dev_register` and `drm_client_dev_unregister` tell the console of a device that comes and goes, and `drm_kms_hotplug_event` calls `drm_client_dev_hotplug`; `drm_framebuffer_dirty` flushes a rectangle of a framebuffer as DIRTYFB does, for the console's shadow; `drm_aperture_is_firmware` says whether a device drives the firmware's framebuffer. The console, `drm_client.c`, listens to the master role through a `struct drm_master_notifier`, and is described with the [display](../display/Readme.md).
* `/sys`. `drm_dev_register` calls `drm_sysfs_register`, which makes the device's entries through the registry of [`../fs/sysfs.h`](../fs/sysfs.h), as Linux's drm_sysfs.c does: below the PCI function's directory, or `/sys/devices/platform/UNIQUE` for a platform device, a `drm` directory with `cardN` and, with DRIVER_RENDER, `renderD(128 + N)`, each with `dev`, `uevent`, a `subsystem` link to `/sys/class/drm` and a `device` link to the device; below the card, a `cardN-NAME` directory for each connector with `status`, `enabled`, `dpms`, `modes`, `edid`, `connector_id` and `uevent`; and links in `/sys/class/drm` and `/sys/dev/char/226:M`. It sends `add` for each, the card first; `drm_sysfs_connector_add` and `drm_sysfs_connector_remove` add and remove a connector later, `drm_dev_unplug` calls `drm_sysfs_unregister`, which sends `remove` for each before it removes them, and `drm_kms_hotplug_event` and `drm_sysfs_connector_hotplug_event` send the card's `change` with `HOTPLUG=1`. A write of `status` sets the connector's force (`DRM_FORCE_*`), as Linux's `status_store` does, under the commit lock: when the force changed, or the write is `detect`, it probes the connector at once, and when the force changed it sends a hotplug, so a `GETCONNECTOR` after the write sees the new state. A device whose `pci_ident` is set has its PCI function show that identity in place of its own while it is registered, as a driver standing in for another chip needs.
* Probes. `bochs_probe`, `fbdrm_probe`, `i915_probe`, `virtio_gpu_probe` and `vkms_probe` bind their devices and return how many, or `-errno`. bochs, i915 and fbdrm reach PC-only code, so `drm_start` calls them through `arch_drm_probe` ([`../arch/arch.h`](../arch/arch.h)), which `drm_pc.c` defines on the PC. `vkms_tick` is vkms's vblank timer, which the timer interrupt calls each tick.
* Sync objects. `struct drm_syncobj` holds a fence: none, a binary fence, or the newest point of a timeline, a `struct dma_fence_chain` ([dmabuf.h](../dmabuf/dmabuf.h)). `drm_syncobj_create`, `drm_syncobj_get`, `drm_syncobj_put` and `drm_syncobj_find` make, hold, drop and look one up by a file's handle; `drm_syncobj_replace_fence` and `drm_syncobj_add_point` give it a fence or a point and wake its waiters and eventfds; `drm_syncobj_find_fence` gives a driver the fence of a point, waiting up to five seconds for its submission when asked; `drm_syncobj_release` drops a closing file's handles. The twelve SYNCOBJ ioctls are Linux 6.12's, and each answers `EOPNOTSUPP` on a device without `DRIVER_SYNCOBJ`, or without `DRIVER_SYNCOBJ_TIMELINE` for TRANSFER, TIMELINE_WAIT, EVENTFD, TIMELINE_SIGNAL and QUERY, the features GET_CAP reports. [drm-syncobj(7)](../../../man/man7/drm-syncobj.7) is the user contract.
* Ioctl handlers. Every handler the core's table names is declared, grouped by the file that defines it: `drm.c` the identity, capabilities, `drm_noop` and `drm_invalid_op`; `drm_auth.c` magic and the master role; `drm_gem.c` handles, flink and dumb buffers; `drm_prime.c` PRIME; `drm_mode.c` the mode objects, properties, blobs, framebuffers and leases; `drm_legacy.c` the calls before atomic; `drm_atomic.c` the atomic commit; `drm_vblank.c` vblank waits and CRTC sequences; and `drm_syncobj.c` sync objects.

## Sources and license

`uapi.h` is transcribed from Linux 6.12's `include/uapi/drm/drm.h`, `drm_mode.h` and `drm_fourcc.h`, which carry the MIT license's permission notice, and `include/uapi/linux/dma-buf.h` and `udmabuf.h` (GPL-2.0 WITH Linux-syscall-note) and `sync_file.h` (GPL-1.0+ WITH Linux-syscall-note), whose note leaves a program that uses the interface free of the GPL. The two sw_sync structures and their numbers are those of `drivers/dma-buf/sw_sync.c`, which has no uapi header; IGT and Android's libsync copy them in the same way. The identities in `drm.h` are copied from the drivers named beside them. `drm_edid.c` copies the mode tables of Linux 6.12's `drivers/gpu/drm/drm_edid.c` (the DMT, established, CEA and HDMI modes) and follows its reading of an EDID and `drm_modes.c`'s CVT and GTF formulas; both files carry the MIT license's permission notice, which `drm_edid.c` repeats with their copyright lines. The rest is Rach2's own, written to Linux's behaviour.

## Design

The opening comment of `drm.h` is the design every file keeps: locking under the big kernel lock with a device's commit lock for commits; who holds a reference to a device, an open file, a buffer object, a framebuffer and a fence, and what close, `dup`, `fork`, `exec` and exit drop; what an unplugged device answers (`ENODEV`, `POLLERR | POLLHUP`, `SIGBUS` on a revoked mapping, fences signalled with `-ENODEV`); and discovery by identity, never by minor number, since card numbers follow the order of registration.

A sync object follows Linux's drm_syncobj.c call for call. A binary object holds the producer's fence itself, so a sync file exported from it reports the producer's error. A timeline is a chain of points, each holding its predecessor and the merged leaves of the fence it was given, never another chain; a point above the newest continues its context, and one at or below it takes the newest's number in a context of its own, which is how Linux numbers points given out of order. A wait puts a callback on each fence and, for a point not yet submitted, a record on the object's list, which every change of the object's fence checks; the waiter sleeps on a queue of its own until a callback or a submission counts a change, or its deadline. An eventfd's record leaves the list when its point is submitted and frees itself when it signals; it never holds the object, whose release frees the records still waiting. Adding a point drops the signalled points just behind it, so a timeline signalled in order keeps one point.

Mode setting keeps Linux's split between state and commit. Every CRTC, plane and connector has a current state; a commit, whether `ATOMIC` or one of the calls before it, copies the states it changes into a `struct drm_atomic_state`, sets them, checks them whole and swaps them in under the commit lock, so a refused commit changes nothing. Each CRTC the commit leaves on or turns on or off gets an event, the caller's or one of the core's, and its flip is pending until that event is sent at the vblank that shows the state, which also signals its out-fence; on a device without vblanks the event is sent as the commit ends, as Linux's helpers do. A blocking commit waits for its in-fences before the swap and for its flips after it. A nonblocking commit whose in-fences have not signalled swaps its state in at once, moves it to a work item, and returns; each fence's callback counts the item down, and the `kdrm` kernel thread, started at the first such commit, programs the hardware once the count reaches zero. A commit that would replace a state the worker has yet to program waits for it, or with `NONBLOCK` is `EBUSY`, as Linux's stall checks have it. The vblank interrupt goes off at the first vblank 5 s after its last holder, which the driver's own vblank checks, so no timer is needed. A connector's modes come from its EDID as Linux 6.12 reads it, table for table, and the host tests compare the lists with those Linux made of the same EDIDs.

The handoff follows Linux's aperture helpers. fbdrm drives the framebuffer the loader left and records its range. A native driver, before it touches its hardware, removes every firmware device in the ranges its device decodes; the console leaves the removed device and moves to the native one when that registers. The native drivers probe first, so on a machine with one fbdrm finds the table closed and never registers. So a machine shows one card for its screen, the native one when there is one.

fbdrm has one mode, the loader's, and copies each framebuffer's damage onto the screen, converting from XRGB8888 to the screen's format, as simpledrm does; its framebuffers are shmem buffers. bochs keeps its buffers as Linux's VRAM helper does: a dumb buffer goes in video memory when there is room, placed from the top so that the firmware's screen at the bottom is used last, and otherwise in system memory; a commit pins each buffer it shows, moving it into video memory first and moving out unpinned buffers, the least recently shown first, to make room. A mapping follows its buffer: a move zaps it, and the next touch faults to where the buffer is now. A mode set enables the DISPI with `VBE_DISPI_NOCLEARMEM`, since without it QEMU zeroes the mode's size of video memory from offset 0, where another buffer may lie; Linux's `bochs_hw_setmode` leaves the bit clear, a fault [the index of upstream faults](../../../dev-log/upstream.md) records.

virtio-gpu sends each command in DMA pages with room for the answer, the memory entries of `ATTACH_BACKING` going on into extra pages, and every control command carries a fence whose id the device's timeline gives in queue order, so a buffer's reservation and `VIRTGPU_WAIT` see the device's progress. A command that finds the queue full waits on a list the answers drain, in order. Commands are answered in the queue's interrupt or by a reap, and the reaper, in process context, frees those nobody waits for; a waiter polls the queue while it sleeps on the fence, so a device without working interrupts is still served, and gives up after five seconds. Its buffers are shmem objects whose pages are all given at creation and handed to the host as the backing of a 2D or virgl 3D resource; a buffer's last reference sends `RESOURCE_UNREF` and frees the pages when the host answers, since until then it may read them. A commit copies the damaged part of a dumb buffer to the host, sets the scanout when the buffer, its source or the mode changed, and waits for the flush, so a commit's event follows the picture. A failed UNREF, or failure to allocate it, unplugs and resets the device before backing is freed. The configuration interrupt only marks a display change; the thread rereads the EDIDs and displays and sends the hotplug, since both need commands answered.

The virgl path is in `virtio_gpu_3d.c`, `virtio_gpu_submit.c` and `virtio_gpu_fence.c`. Probe negotiates `VIRTIO_GPU_F_VIRGL` and reads each capability set's id, maximum version and size; GET_CAPS reads the requested version, copying at most the host's size, with scatter lists for responses that span pages. Each open file gets its own context, created lazily and destroyed at close. Resources share the 2D path's shmem backing and lifetime; an ordinary virgl transfer carries the box, offset and level, and refuses nonzero stride or layer stride as Linux does for a resource without blobs. A context attaches each buffer once before its first use and detaches it before the resource's last UNREF.

Blobs and context initialization are in `virtio_gpu_blob.c`, as Linux 6.12 has them. Probe asks for `VIRTIO_GPU_F_RESOURCE_BLOB`, `VIRTIO_GPU_F_CONTEXT_INIT` and `VIRTIO_GPU_F_RESOURCE_UUID`, and finds the host-visible region, shared memory region 1, which QEMU's `hostmem` puts in a 64-bit BAR, 4 GiB at 14 TiB; the region is never mapped whole. RESOURCE_CREATE_BLOB checks the blob as Linux's `verify_blob` does, sends the commands that make a blob of host memory as a SUBMIT_3D in the file's context, then CREATE_BLOB, waited for, with the entries of the blob's pages when it has guest memory. A mappable blob of host memory alone takes the first piece of the region it fits in, and MAP_BLOB, waited for, has the host map it there; the answer's cache type makes a program's mapping of the whole blob write-combining, uncached or cached, and a kernel mapping, for PRIME's `vmap`, is a window of the MMIO region onto that piece alone. A shareable blob asks the device for its UUID when the device gives them. A blob's last reference sends UNMAP_BLOB before its UNREF, and the piece goes back when the host has answered; a MAP_BLOB the host refuses gives the piece back at once. RESOURCE_INFO calls a blob with any host memory HOST3D. A transfer refuses a blob of guest memory alone, and takes a stride only for a blob with host memory. A blob framebuffer is shown with SET_SCANOUT_BLOB, without the copy a dumb buffer takes.

CONTEXT_INIT takes at most four parameters: an offered capability set up to 63, at most 64 rings, a poll mask within them, and a name. It checks them all before any takes effect, refuses a parameter given twice, and refuses once the file's context exists, with EEXIST; the context's CTX_CREATE then carries the capability set and the name, or `rach2`. Each ring is a fence context of its own. A submission with `VIRTGPU_EXECBUF_RING_IDX` takes its ring's fence context and sends the ring's index to the device, an answer retires only the earlier fences of its own ring, and a ring in the poll mask reserves a bare `VIRTGPU_EVENT_FENCE_SIGNALED` event at submission, which the reaper sends the file at the answer.

EXECBUFFER copies the command and holds every buffer in its handle list until the host answers. In-fence sync files and input syncobjs wait only for fence leaves of another fence context: the device keeps the order of one context's commands, the control queue's or one ring's, and of nothing across them. The submit path never waits for its own earlier fences. Its out-fence joins each buffer's reservation and may be exported as a sync file or an output syncobj, including a timeline point. All fallible allocations precede publication; an abandoned prepared fence is signalled with its error. A later fence response retires earlier points on that timeline, including when responses arrive out of order. WAIT with NOWAIT returns EBUSY for outstanding work; a blocking WAIT allows 15 seconds. The worker frees completed commands in process context as well as serving display changes.

Root can write `1` to `/sys/kernel/debug/dri/N/virtio_gpu_reset` to unplug that GPU for a device-loss test. The core revokes every mapping and removes the nodes, the transport stops DMA, every pending fence signals with ENODEV, every context is invalidated, and every resource's backing is freed. Open descriptors keep their records until close; subsequent ioctls return ENODEV and old mappings fault. Reusing the GPU requires another boot.

vkms has no hardware. Each tick, `vkms_tick` counts the vblanks of the mode set that have passed since the last, at most eight, and each handles a vblank as an interrupt would. Each commit's flush copies the planes it shows into a scene; while a CRC source runs, each vblank asks the `kvkms` kernel thread, started at the first source, to compose the newest scene in 16 bits a channel as Linux's vkms does and add its CRC for the frames since the last it composed.

The files of `sys/drm/` that every architecture compiles are listed in [`sources.mk`](sources.mk), and those that reach PC-only code, bochs, fbdrm, the console's client and the PC's probes, in [`pc.mk`](pc.mk), which `sys/arch/x86/sources.mk` includes, as it does the Intel driver's [`i915/sources.mk`](i915/sources.mk); `sys/dmabuf/sources.mk` and `sys/virtio/sources.mk`, the [virtio transport](../virtio/Readme.md)'s, are included by `rach2/sources.mk`. Each stub's opening comment names the work that replaces it.

## Limits

* On real hardware fbdrm is the only driver that sets a mode; i915 binds the Lenovo's chip under `rach2.i915` without a display.
* virtio-gpu has virgl 3D, blobs and context initialization when offered. On i686 a host-visible region past 4 GiB, where QEMU puts it, is refused with a record, since a page table entry there holds no address past 4 GiB; blobs of guest memory still work, and a mappable blob of host memory alone returns EINVAL. A display change sends its hotplug uevent from the `virtio_gpu` thread, which the first open of the device's nodes makes, since a kernel thread made before init would take pid 1; a change before that open is served by the open.
* No driver draws the console on a DRM card: under virtio-vga the screen shows the firmware's console until virtio-gpu's first command and then the empty scanout, until a program sets a mode or 76-11 brings the console's client.
* The lease calls answer `EOPNOTSUPP`.
* A sync object's wait ends with `EINTR` on a signal, where Linux restarts it with `ERESTARTSYS`; libdrm's `drmIoctl` repeats the call either way.
* A page flip to a target vblank is `EINVAL`, Linux's answer for a driver without `page_flip_target`.
* vkms has no writeback connector, which Linux's has by default.
* The EDID's quirks, DisplayID blocks, HDMI stereo modes, tiles and the non-desktop flag are not read.
* debugfs has no `internal_clients`, and no connector's `vrr_range`, `output_bpc` or the HDMI and DisplayPort files, which no driver here has or IGT's CRC and forcing tests open; `bridges` is empty, since no driver has bridges.

## Tests

* `tests/host/drm_abi_hosttest.c` checks the size and the offset of every member of every structure of `uapi.h`, and every ioctl number, against Linux 6.12's on x86-64, natively, and on i386, by the layout rules of that ABI from each member's type.
* `tests/host/drm_core_hosttest.c`, and `drm_core64_hosttest.c` for x86-64, run the core whole against two fake drivers, one with a card and a render node and one with a card alone: registration, identity, capabilities, the dispatcher's copies and flags, the master role and magic, dumb buffers created, mapped, named and freed, events, unplug, and `kcmp` ([`../proc/kcmp.c`](../proc/kcmp.c)).
* `tests/host/drm_prime_hosttest.c` and `drm_prime64_hosttest.c` include GEM, PRIME, dma-buf, fences and udmabuf whole against modeled descriptor tables, physical pages and device regions: accepted flags and refusals, self-import, import caching, cross-device and udmabuf pages, shared reservations, reexport, allocation failures and every object and frame released after the last mapping.
* `tests/host/drmcon_hosttest.c` and `drmcon64_hosttest.c` run the console's client, `drm_client.c`, over the real master role and mode setting with the console, `/fb` and the keyboard as records; [the display](../display/Readme.md) lists their rows. `tests/drmprobe.c hold SECONDS`, in a boot, takes the master role, shows a pattern and exits without dropping it, for the console's return to be seen.
* `tests/host/drm_kms_hosttest.c` and `drm_kms64_hosttest.c` include the mode-setting files whole against a fake driver of two CRTCs and two connectors: the objects and properties each client cap shows, blobs, framebuffers with modifiers and IN_FORMATS, atomic commits with each flag and Linux's refusals, the calls before atomic as commits, and a file's release.
* `tests/host/drm_vblank_hosttest.c` and `drm_vblank64_hosttest.c` drive the vblank and fence code against a fake driver whose CRTCs count vblanks from a timer: counts and the off-delay, WAIT_VBLANK, the CRTC sequence ioctls, the order of events, flips on sw_sync in-fences that signal late with their out-fences, connector forcing, the EDID override and the CRC ring.
* `tests/host/drm_edid_hosttest.c` and `drm_edid64_hosttest.c` probe a connector with QEMU's EDID, as virtio-gpu and std-vga give it, and the Lenovo panel's, and compare the modes, the EDID blob and the size with Linux 6.12's; and corrupt EDIDs, mended or dropped as Linux does.
* `tests/host/syncobj_hosttest.c` and `syncobj64_hosttest.c` include fences, sync files, sw_sync and the syncobj file whole against modeled descriptors, eventfds, handle tables and a scheduler whose sleeps run a producer's step: binary waits for any, all and submission, refusals, files shared between opens, out-of-order timeline points, waits at points, transfers, queries, eventfds, a failed producer's error read through every consumer's sync file, the calls refused by drivers without the features, allocation failures, and a chain of 200,000 points freed without recursion.
* `tests/fenceprobe.c`, in the `dmabuf` suite, runs slow, concurrent and failing producers against consumers that read a fence's state and then the buffer, over udmabuf, and over a card's dumb buffers and syncobjs where a driver offers them.
* `tests/host/bochs_hosttest.c` and `bochs64_hosttest.c` bind the std-vga driver to a register model of BAR 2 and video memory: its EDID and DMT modes, the DISPI writes of a mode set and a flip, the byte order, the blank, buffers moved between video and system memory and the refusals, and the PCI probe.
* `tests/host/vkms_hosttest.c` and `vkms64_hosttest.c` include vkms whole with the mode-setting files: its objects, modes and parameters, the frames it composes from every format, rotation, overlap and gamma table against the test's own, the vblank period and catch-up on a model clock, and the CRC of each frame.
* `tests/drmprobe.c` checks a real card's nodes, master role, magic, dumb buffers, PRIME self-import, repeated import and mapping lifetime, and `kcmp`, and sets each mode of the first connected connector and reads it back, flipping once on the first with PAGE_FLIP and its event, then, on a driver with CRCs, as vkms, reads two lines of the first CRTC's `crc/data` through debugfs, in a boot, and on the Lenovo as a native case.
* `tests/host/vgpu3d_hosttest.c` and `vgpu3d64_hosttest.c` extend the same device model for capability responses across pages, contexts, 3D transfers with byte readback, asynchronous submissions, foreign and same-queue dependencies, sync files, syncobj timelines, out-of-order responses and reset with pending commands. `tests/vgpuprobe.c`, the `vgpu` family on the virgl display profile, checks the real device and resets it at the end; its `machine.vgpu.blob` case, on the Venus profile on x86-64, makes a context of each offered capability set with a polled ring and reads its fence event, and writes a mappable blob of host memory through its mapping and reads the bytes back through a host copy and a transfer.
* `tests/host/vgpublob_hosttest.c` and `vgpublob64_hosttest.c` extend the 3D model with blobs, the host-visible region, UUIDs and SET_SCANOUT_BLOB, and check placement, mapping and its cache type, the kernel window, the order of UNMAP_BLOB and UNREF, a full region and a refused map, guest blobs and their refusals, CONTEXT_INIT with capability sets 1, 2, 4 and 6 and its refusals, rings with their fence contexts, events and dependencies, and PRIME export and self-import of a blob.
* `tests/host/virtio_gpu_hosttest.c` and `virtio_gpu64_hosttest.c` bind virtio-gpu to a model of QEMU's device and check its commands, fences, modes, scanout, damage, queueing, errors, cursor, display changes, ioctls and unplug; a boot with `-device virtio-gpu-pci` or `-device virtio-vga` runs `drmprobe`, which sets every EDID mode and reads it back, and `hotplugprobe`, which hears the hotplug uevent and the new preferred mode when a VNC client's `SetDesktopSize` resizes the display.

* `tests/host/devfs_hosttest.c` registers `dri/card0` in the devfs registry and finds it by path, number and listing, and `tests/host/synthfs_hosttest.c` classifies `/dev/dri` and its nodes.
