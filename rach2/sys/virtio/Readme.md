# `virtio/`: the virtio transport and its queues

A virtio device is driven through a status byte, feature bits, a configuration space and split virtqueues, the same on every bus (virtio 1.2). `virtqueue.c` is the part every transport shares: a device's reset, its feature negotiation and bring-up, and its queues. `virtio_bus.c` shows each device on the virtio bus in `/sys`, as Linux's drivers/virtio/virtio.c does. `virtio_pci.c` is the first transport, virtio 1.x over PCI: it finds a function by its device type, reads its vendor capabilities and maps the windows they name, and gives it interrupts by MSI-X or by its INTx line. `shmem.c` is the second: virtio-gpu over one region of memory shared with a Linux VM whose backend runs the device, the test bed's transport ([bed/shm](../../../bed/shm/Readme.md)), which `ivshmemd` attaches through `/dev/vgpu-shm` (vgpu-shm(4)). A driver, virtio-gpu ([drm](../drm/Readme.md)), virtio-blk ([block](../block/Readme.md)) or the virtio network driver ([network](../network/Readme.md#the-drivers)), reaches its device only through `virtio.h`, and a transport only through the table of operations there. `virtio_console.c` is a driver that lives here: the virtio console, whose port carries the kernel's console when the boot line names it ([hvc(4)](../../../man/man4/hvc.4)).

## Interfaces

`kernel.c` calls `virtio_pci_probe()` once at boot, before the network's probe and the display drivers', unless `virtio_pci_find` has already run it, as virtio-blk's probe does among the disks', and it takes every virtio function of the PCI bus in the bus's order, as Linux's virtio_pci driver takes each whether a driver of its type exists or not. A function it takes is shown in `/sys` as Linux shows it: bound to `virtio-pci`, with a `driver` link and `DRIVER=virtio-pci` in its uevent; its capabilities read and mapped, with memory decoding on, which makes the kernel the function's PCI owner; its device reset and acknowledged, as Linux's register_virtio_device does before it publishes a device, so that no `DRIVER_OK` or queue a previous owner left stands, and bus mastering, which firmware or an earlier owner may have left on, turned off before that reset and on only once it has finished, so that a device still working through what an earlier binding gave it cannot reach memory freed since; its virtio device below it (`virtio_bus_add`), named `virtioN` for the lowest N no device on the bus holds, as Linux's virtio_index_ida gives it once the transport's probe has succeeded, and given back when the device is taken off the bus; logged as `virtio: found: virtio0: 00:02.0 gpu: ...`; and with the function's `bind` uevent sent. A function whose binding cannot be shown, that lacks a capability virtio 1.x requires, whose window cannot be mapped, whose device does not reset or whose virtio device cannot be added is refused with a record and left as it was: unbound, its windows unmapped, its command word put back less bus mastering, as Linux's pci_disable_device leaves it, and the PCI claim the probe took given back, with no record and no number. The transport keeps an allocated record of each function it took until the function is unbound.

`/sys/bus/pci/drivers/virtio-pci`, made at the first probe as Linux registers the driver, has `unbind` and `bind`, which take a function's name, `0000:BB:DD.F`. `unbind` lets go of a function the transport has, as Linux's virtio_pci_remove: its child driver removed first through the device's `removed`, as virtio-gpu unplugs its card, then the device reset, its interrupts released, its virtio device taken off the bus, its windows and MSI-X table unmapped, its command word put back less bus mastering, the kernel's PCI claim given back and the function shown unbound with its `unbind` uevent, so that vfio-pci may have it. A driver that still holds the device keeps the record, which reaches no register, until its `virtio_put`. `bind` takes a function no driver has as the boot did, heeding its `driver_override` (`(null)` or `virtio-pci`), and gives its device to the driver registered for its type (`virtio_driver_register`), as virtio-gpu registers at its probe; a driver's refusal leaves the function bound to virtio-pci, as on Linux. `/sys/bus/pci/drivers_probe` offers virtio-pci a function vfio-pci does not match ([vfio](../vfio/Readme.md)). A driver finds its device with `virtio_pci_find(id, nth, &dev)`, by its type (`VIRTIO_ID_GPU` and the others of virtio 1.2, 5) and its place among the functions of that type the transport took, counting from zero. `virtio_pci_function(dev)` then gives its PCI function, for a driver that needs the class or the BARs, as virtio-gpu does to tell virtio-vga from virtio-gpu-pci, or NULL on another transport. `virtio_offered(dev)` gives the feature bits the device offers, for a driver that sizes what it asks for by them before `virtio_init`, as virtio-blk reads `VIRTIO_BLK_F_MQ` to know whether `num_queues` is valid. It then brings the device up in the order virtio 1.2, 3.1.1 gives:

1. `virtio_init(dev, wanted, nqueues)` resets the device, waiting at most 100 ms for its status to clear, lets it reach memory again through the transport's `dma_enable`, sets ACKNOWLEDGE and DRIVER, and takes the device's own bits of `wanted` that the device offers, with `VIRTIO_F_VERSION_1`, which it must offer, and the bits the core handles: `VIRTIO_F_INDIRECT_DESC`, `VIRTIO_F_EVENT_IDX`, `VIRTIO_F_ACCESS_PLATFORM` and `VIRTIO_F_ORDER_PLATFORM`. It sets FEATURES_OK, checks that the device kept it, and prepares interrupts for `nqueues` queues; `dev->polled` is then 1 when the transport gave the device no interrupt at all, so that its driver keeps polling. A legacy device, one that refuses the features or one whose reset does not end is refused and marked FAILED. A transport with no device to offer, the shared region's when its VM has gone or its offer was refused, marks FAILED itself while giving the features, and `virtio_init` then returns `-ENODEV`.
2. `virtio_queue_setup(dev, index, done, priv, &vq)` for each queue.
3. `virtio_ready(dev)` lets the interrupts through and sets DRIVER_OK.

A buffer is a list of segments, `struct virtio_sg`, each an address the device reads and a length: first those the device reads, then those it writes. `virtqueue_add(vq, sg, out, in, token)` publishes one at once, and `virtqueue_kick(vq)` tells the device of those added since its last call, unless it has asked not to be told: with `VIRTIO_F_EVENT_IDX`, by an `avail_event` that none of them passes, which QEMU leaves at the first buffer it has not taken, so that a burst of buffers the device has not yet read costs one notify, as Linux's `virtqueue_kick_prepare_split` decides; without it, by the used ring's `VIRTQ_USED_F_NO_NOTIFY`. Under KVM a notify of QEMU's virtio-gpu is an exit that waits for QEMU's main loop. When the device returns the buffer, `done(vq, token, len)` is called with the bytes it wrote, from the interrupt handler or from `virtqueue_reap(vq)` in a driver that polls, with the queue's lock released, so `done` may add to its own queue. A reap takes only the buffers returned before it began; with `VIRTIO_F_EVENT_IDX` it first points `used_event` at the end of those, as Linux's `virtqueue_get_buf_ctx_split` does after each buffer it takes, so the device interrupts once for the first buffer returned after them; those returned meanwhile raise their own interrupt and go to the next reap, so a driver that refills its queue from `done` does not keep one reap, and the big lock, for as long as the device answers. `-ENOSPC` from `virtqueue_add` means the queue is full until a completion.

A queue's `batch`, when a driver sets it, is called once at the end of a reap that took buffers, with the lock released, so that a driver whose `done` gives each buffer back to the device tells the device of them all with one `virtqueue_kick`, as Linux's NAPI poll ends with one. `virtqueue_irq(vq, 0)` asks the device not to interrupt for the buffers it returns on the queue, as Linux's `virtqueue_disable_cb`: by the avail ring's `VIRTQ_AVAIL_F_NO_INTERRUPT`, or with `VIRTIO_F_EVENT_IDX` by a `used_event` one behind the driver's place, which the device passes only after 65536 buffers, and a reap of a queue so silenced leaves `used_event` where it is. `virtqueue_irq(vq, 1)` asks for the interrupts again and returns whether buffers already wait, as `virtqueue_enable_cb`, so that a driver reaps what came between. The virtio network driver silences its transmit and control queues so, and takes the transmit buffers the device has returned when it next sends.

Memory the device reads or writes comes from `virtio_dma_alloc(dev, bytes, &addr)`, at most a page, page-aligned and zeroed, with the address the device uses for it; a larger buffer, such as a resource's backing, is a list of pages, which `virtio_backing_fill` fills where the transport places them and `virtio_backing_release` gives back, and `virtio_dma_addr` gives the device's address of each. On virtio-pci, whose every address is 64 bits wide, the page is a frame from any memory on x86-64 and of the DMA pool on i686 (`alloc_dma64_frame`, [mm](../mm/Readme.md)), a backing is the caller's own pages, and the address is the physical address. On the shared region every page comes from the region's heap and the address is its offset in the region; a backing's pages are heap pages, which the resource's object takes as its own, so that a program's mapping of the resource and the device reach the same memory and the device none outside the region. `virtio_config_read` reads the device-specific configuration as one snapshot, again while its generation changes, at most eight times; `virtio_shm_region` gives a shared memory region the device exposes (virtio 1.2, 2.10), such as virtio-gpu's host-visible memory. `dev->config_changed`, when the driver sets it, is called from the handler on a configuration change.

A driver shows the device bound to it with `virtio_driver_bind(dev, name)` before it registers what it makes over the device, and puts that below `virtio_bus_dir(dev)`, as virtio-gpu puts its DRM card; `virtio_driver_bound(dev)` sends the device's `bind` uevent once the driver is up, and `virtio_driver_unbind(dev)` takes the binding away, with `unbind` when `bind` went out, at a failed bind or the driver's unplug. On the bus the device is `/sys/devices/pci0000:00/0000:BB:DD.F/virtioN`, with Linux 6.12's `device`, `vendor` (the function's subsystem vendor), `status`, `modalias` (`virtio:dNNNNNNNNvNNNNNNNN`), `features` (a `0` or `1` for each of the 64 bits taken) and `uevent` (`DRIVER` while bound, and `MODALIAS`), a `subsystem` link to `/sys/bus/virtio`, the link `/sys/bus/virtio/devices/virtioN`, and while bound a `driver` link to `/sys/bus/virtio/drivers/NAME`, which links back ([sysfs(5)](../../../man/man5/sysfs.5)). The shared region's transport shows no device, and these calls do nothing on its devices.

`virtio_teardown(dev)` resets the device, stops its interrupts and frees its queues; buffers still queued are dropped without their `done`. When the reset does not end, the transport first stops the device from reaching memory through `dma_enable`, which on virtio-pci clears the function's bus mastering, so that neither the queues nor a buffer the driver frees afterwards, as virtio-gpu frees its commands' pages, can be written by a device that never stopped; Linux never reaches that free, since its reset waits for the status without bound. A transport whose reset can time out has `dma_enable`; on one without it, the shared region's, whose reset always finishes, the queues' memory would be kept. The device can then be brought up again from `virtio_init`. `virtio_halt(dev)` stops a device without freeing anything: it resets it, or turns its DMA off when the reset does not end, and returns whether the reset ended, for a driver that must fail what it has in flight before it lets the queues go, as virtio-blk does when a command goes unanswered or the device is removed; the driver's `virtio_teardown` follows once nothing waits on the queues.

The transport's table, `struct virtio_ops`, has the status byte, the feature bits, the configuration and its generation, a queue's size, enabling and notification, the interrupts' setup, start and release, the device's memory and its shared memory regions. `virtio.h` states each operation's contract.

The core logs under `virtio`: `found:` with the function, its type, the BAR and offset of each capability, the notification multiplier and the queue count, and each shared memory region; `features:` with the bits offered and taken; `interrupts:` with the plan; `queue:` with each queue's size, vector and whether it has indirect tables; `refused:` with the reason a device was not taken.

### The console

`kernel.c` calls `virtio_console_start()` once, after `virtio_pci_probe()`. It reads the boot line's `console=` words, registers `virtio_console_bind` as the probe for `VIRTIO_ID_CONSOLE`, so that a function bound later through `/sys` is taken too, and binds the first console the transport took. The bind takes `VIRTIO_CONSOLE_F_SIZE`, `VIRTIO_CONSOLE_F_MULTIPORT` and `VIRTIO_CONSOLE_F_EMERG_WRITE` where the device offers them, shows the device bound to `virtio_console`, and sets up port 0's queues, 0 and 1, and with `F_MULTIPORT` the control queues, 2 and 3, and port 1's, 4 and 5, as Linux's `virtcons_probe` does for every port. With `F_MULTIPORT` it sends `DEVICE_READY` and the device announces its ports: each `PORT_ADD` is answered with `PORT_READY`, a port the device then marks with `CONSOLE_PORT` is a console port and is answered with `PORT_OPEN`, and `PORT_NAME`, `PORT_OPEN`, `RESIZE` (rows, then columns, as Linux 6.12 reads it) and `PORT_REMOVE` are recorded, as Linux's `handle_control_message` acts on them. Without it port 0 is the one port, a console, with its size in the configuration.

The port the boot line names becomes the console's line (`serial_line_attach`, [tty](../tty/Readme.md#the-consoles-line)) as soon as the device has announced it and, for `hvc0`, marked it: `hvc0` is the first console port and `vport0pN` port N whatever its kind, a name Linux gives a generic port's device and Rach2 also takes after `console=`, since QEMU's `virtserialport` holds its sender back while its reader falls behind and its `virtconsole` discards what the reader's socket cannot take (measured with a reader that slept 8 s while 151,672 bytes were written: 8,192 arrived through a `virtconsole`, all through a `virtserialport` and through COM1). The boot waits for the port at most 2 s, reaping the control queue with interrupts off, and a port announced later takes the console from the interrupt. A generic port that becomes the line is told `PORT_OPEN`, without which QEMU gives it no input. While the boot binds the device on a machine with no UART the line is given the log's ring (`klog_replay`); with a UART the records so far went there. `console=ttyS0` beside the port keeps the UART a console too, and named after the port leaves the port output alone. While the port has the console's input, `virtio_console_node` shows its node in `/dev` (`hvc0`, 229:0, or `vport0pN`, 245:N), whose open gives the console terminal.

The line's bytes gather in one of four pages; a full page, and `serial_flush`, give it to the device, and the flush then polls the transmit queue until the device has returned every page, as Linux's `put_chars` waits in `__send_to_port`, so that a reset just after the last byte loses none. The wait is bounded as COM1's transmit is: 30 s while the device takes what it is given, then 2 ms a write until it returns a page again, counted in cycles; what finds no free page is dropped and counted. The transmit queues ask for no interrupt (`virtqueue_irq`). The transmit side has a lock of its own, taken with interrupts off, since a processor without the big lock writes its report through it; a record the transmit itself raises stays in the log's ring; and a panic that finds the lock held, by a processor it stopped, writes each byte to `emerg_wr` where the device has it (virtio 1.2, 5.3.5). Received pages, eight to a port, go into the console's receive ring in the order the device returned them; a page the ring cannot take whole waits, the device runs out of pages and stops reading the host's end, and the ring's `rx_room` resumes it. The ports' receive state is under the console's lock, which the ring already has, so the two never wait on each other.

A port's removal, the device's unbinding through virtio-pci (`dev->removed`) and a failed bring-up give the console back to the UART, reset the device and free every page; the queues' and buffers' memory is freed only after the reset. `virtio_console_counters` gives the bytes and buffers each way, the drops, the waits that reached their bound, the emergency writes and the control messages.

The driver logs under `virtio_console`: `bound:` with the ports and features, `port:` with a port's name or a port it leaves, `removed:`, `left:` for a second console; and under `console`: `enabled:`, `disabled:`, `waiting:`, `refused:` and `none:`, which [hvc(4)](../../../man/man4/hvc.4) lists.

## Design

| File | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `virtio.h` | the status and feature bits, the split ring's layout, `struct virtqueue`, `struct virtio_ops`, `struct virtio_dev` and the calls of the core and of virtio-pci |
| `virtqueue.c` | reset, negotiation, bring-up and teardown through the table; the split virtqueue |
| `virtio_bus.c` | the virtio bus in `/sys`: each device's directory, attributes, uevents and its driver's binding |
| `virtio_pci.c` | virtio 1.x over PCI: discovery, the capabilities and their windows, the common configuration, notification and interrupts |
| `vgpushm.h` | the shared region's layout and the checks both of its sides run, shared with the VM's backend |
| `virtio_console.c`, `virtio_console.h` | the virtio console: its ports, the control queues, and the port that carries the kernel's console |
| `shmem.c` | the transport over the shared region, its thread, its heap and its doorbells, and the control node `/dev/vgpu-shm` |

A queue is at most `VIRTQ_MAX_SIZE`, 128 entries, the device's size cut to the largest power of two no larger than it, so that its descriptors and both rings, 3342 bytes, lie in one page in that order, each at the alignment virtio 1.2, 2.7 asks. Free descriptors are a list through the core's own array `next`, which also links each chain, since the table is written for the device and never read back: a device of the shared region may rewrite it. A chain goes into the queue's descriptors when enough are free; when they are not and `VIRTIO_F_INDIRECT_DESC` was taken, into one of the queue's indirect tables, at the cost of one descriptor. A driver sizes the tables before `virtio_init` with `indirect_len`, a power of two from 16 to `VIRTQ_INDIRECT_MAX`, 256, and `indirect_tables`, at most 16; by default, as virtio-gpu has it, there are 16 tables of 16 descriptors in one page. The tables lie in as many pages of the queue's own as they fill, so virtio-blk's 8 tables of 256 descriptors, 4 KiB each, take 8 pages a queue. A chain may be as long as the queue or, with indirect tables, a table, whichever is longer, as Linux's `virtqueue_add_split` puts a chain longer than the ring in a table; a longer one is `-E2BIG`. Segments of the same way that follow each other in the device's addresses go in one descriptor, of at most the queue's `max_seg` bytes when its driver sets one after `virtio_queue_setup`, as virtio-blk sets a device's `size_max`, and of any length otherwise. The driver's ring index is published after a fence that orders the chain and its ring entry before it, and the device's flags, or its `avail_event`, are read after a fence that orders the index before them. A returned head that is not in use, or a device index more than the queue's length ahead, is logged and not acted on.

virtio-pci walks the function's capability list, at most 48 links, for the vendor capabilities, and takes the first common, notification, ISR and device capability whose BAR is a programmed memory BAR the kernel can address; one in an I/O BAR is passed over for a later one. It maps up to 64 KiB of each window. The common, notification and ISR capabilities are required, and the device configuration is optional. A shared memory capability is recorded, not mapped, since virtio-gpu's is 4 GiB high above the kernel's device window: the first of each id whose BAR is a memory BAR and whose offset and length lie within the BAR's size without wrapping, as Linux's `vp_get_shm_region` takes it; any other is logged as `refused:` with its offset, length and BAR. Both device ids are taken: 0x1040 plus the type for a device of virtio 1.x alone, and 0x1000 to 0x103F for a transitional one, whose type is its subsystem id.

The shared region's transport has one thread for its one attach, and an interrupt thread beside it. It binds virtio-gpu once the device offers a running epoch and its offer passes `vgs_check_offer`, and it drops the driver, which fails every fence, and binds again in a new epoch when the device leaves the epoch, fails or resets, as the transport's rules ask. Each pass reaps both queues and reads the device's display events. With doorbells, the device's eventfds wake the thread through a hook on each, from the KVM ioeventfd that took the VM's write; from `virtio_ready` to `virtio_teardown` the queue's hook also wakes the interrupt thread, `vgpu-shm-irq`, which reaps the queues, as virtio-pci's interrupt does and Linux's transports call `vring_interrupt`, so that an answer reaches its waiter while the thread itself is binding the driver; the hook reaps nothing itself, since it runs under the eventfd's lock and a reap's completions signal eventfds, the device's and a program's, which Linux forbids inside another's signal, as vhost queues its work from the wakeup; an attach that gives one eventfd both as a doorbell the device rings and one it is rung by is refused with EINVAL; and the driver's kick signals the eventfd that KVM's irqfd turns into the VM's MSI-X; the thread then looks at the region every 100 ms, and a look that finds work no doorbell announced counts in `polls`. Without them it looks every tick. The heap is a bitmap of pages taken first fit from a rover, which moves past each take. A command takes a page, and a backing as many as it needs: one run that follows itself where the heap has one, so that its memory entries are one, else the free runs from the rover in turn, since the pages that commands in flight hold, spread by the rover, can leave no run as long as a large buffer in a heap mostly free. A backing leaves a sixteenth of the heap free, at most 512 pages, for the commands (`heap_keep`), so a program that makes buffers until the region is full has its own refused with ENOMEM while the commands that send, detach and free buffers still find pages; without it piglit's streaming-texture-leak filled the region, a CTX_DETACH_RESOURCE and an UNREF found no page, and the driver unplugged the device under every program. `refused` counts what a full heap could not give. A detach, in order or for the VM's death, unbinds the driver, stores GONE in a new epoch and gives the region's pages back. Heap pages still held then, as a buffer whose dma-buf exists keeps its backing through the driver's unplug, are a backing's, which keeps its frames by the object's own references, or a command's, which the driver holds by its address alone; the detached record keeps its heap's map and table of frames, on the list `vshm_kept`, until the last comes back through `virtio_backing_release` or `virtio_dma_free`, or the record is freed. A region attached meanwhile over the same memory leaves those frames out of its heap (`vshm_gone_reserve`) and takes each back as it comes back, so that no two objects share a frame; only the attached region's heap keeps such marks, since a detach clears them from its own (`vshm_unreserve`). One spinlock, `vshm_lock`, is over that list, each kept record's tables and the region whose heap leaves their frames out (`vshm_marked`): a command page's give-back, the attach's marks and the detach's move of a record from its heap to the list each run whole under it, since pages come back from system calls and from the kernel threads that free a buffer, and the tables a record gives up are freed after it is let go, as KVM keeps its irqfds under `irqfds.lock` and frees each outside it. It is taken before a record's heap lock, `vshm_heap`, with interrupts off, in row 11 of the kernel's lock order ([processes](../proc/Readme.md)). The interrupt thread's reap runs under a record's `vshm_irq`, with interrupts off, and its completions take the device's lock, signal eventfds and wake waiters, so `vshm_irq` comes first in row 11, before the virtqueues', the eventfds' and the devices' locks. A backing's give-back walks only the leaves of the buffer's table that exist, and takes the lock for one leaf of 1,024 pages at a time, reading at each take again whether the record is attached or kept, so that a buffer of many leaves keeps interrupts off for a leaf's walk at most; between leaves it lets the lock go every time, where Linux's `cond_resched_lock` lets it go only when a switch is due or another processor waits for it, since a hold here keeps interrupts off and lets the lock go only to let them in. The transport's two threads, the driver's worker and every other kernel thread that may run the driver, DRM's `kdrm` and `kdrmcon` and the `ki915` and `kvkms` that free an imported buffer, are not switched by the tick, as no kernel thread is (`no_tick_switch`, [processes](../proc/Readme.md)), so nothing a system call or another thread changes moves under one of their steps; the transport's wait for the device's answer takes a service point after each look that fails, since a doorbell rung meanwhile ends its next sleep at once.

A blob of the VM's own memory is mapped from the VM's pages when both sides accept `VGS_TF_PAGES`, which the transport takes only on x86-64 with doorbells, since it finds the VM by the KVM ioeventfd on the queues' doorbell. RESOURCE_MAP_BLOB's offset is then the first page asked about, and the device answers with a `struct vgs_pages` and runs of VM-physical pages from that page on, as many as the answer holds: the rest of the command's own page, 124 runs, when the pages left to list are no more, else response pages sized to one run a page left, up to 60 KiB and 3836 runs, or the command's own page when the transport's heap has not got those pages or the chain of 16 descriptors cannot carry them; the driver asks again from the page after the last run until it has the blob, and `vgs_check_pages` and `vgs_check_run` check each answer. `virtio_map_pages` hands the runs to KVM ([kvm](../kvm/Readme.md)), which holds the frames behind them; `virtio_map_frame` gives each page's frame to the driver's mapping of the blob, and `virtio_map_free` lets them go. When KVM takes the frames back, the VM's memory having changed or the VM gone, the transport calls the driver's revoke, which refuses every later fault of the blob and queues the removal of every program's mapping to the DRM commit worker, since the revoke runs inside a change notice, which must not sleep; once the mappings go, the next touch raises SIGBUS. KVM keeps its references to the frames until the driver frees the map, at the blob's free or the device's reset, so a fault that read a frame before the revoke and maps it after never maps a frame reused since. `VGS_IOC_INFO` counts the maps since the attach, the pages of the maps not yet taken back (`map_pages`), those maps (`map_live`) and the maps taken back (`revoked`). A map taken back leaves `map_pages` at once but holds its frames until the driver frees it, so `map_pages` may be 0 while frames are still held; `/proc/stat`'s `kvm_gpa_map_pages` counts the pages of every map not yet freed, taken back or not ([kvm](../kvm/Readme.md)).

Interrupts follow Linux's virtio_pci: an MSI-X entry for the configuration and one for each queue when the table and the free vectors allow; else one entry for the configuration and one the queues share; else the INTx line, whose handler reads each device's ISR status, which says whether it raised the line and why; else none, and the driver polls with `virtqueue_reap`. Every entry, the configuration and each queue, is programmed through PCI's interrupt remapper. While the entries are programmed MSI-X is held, enabled with the function masked, and `virtio_ready` lifts the mask. Teardown and a failed setup disable MSI-X and release its remapping entries before freeing interrupt vectors. A handler is given only the saved registers, so each of the 32 interrupt slots that the devices share has a handler of its own that names its slot. A driver that sets `irq_unlocked` before `virtio_init`, as virtio-gpu does, has its MSI-X handlers registered `ISR_UNLOCKED`, to run without the big lock; a slot is read and counted busy under the slots' spinlock, and a release clears the slot and waits until no handler of it still runs, as Linux's `synchronize_irq` (kernel/irq/manage.c:134-151). The INTx line's handler keeps the big lock. The setup leaves `dev->polled` set when the device has no interrupt at all, so that a driver knows to reap its queues from a tick. `dev->config_atomic`, which virtio-pci sets, says that the configuration is read by register accesses that take no lock and sleep nowhere, so that a configuration change's handler may call `virtio_config_read`, as the virtio network driver reads its link.

## Limits

* Split virtqueues only: `VIRTIO_F_RING_PACKED` and `VIRTIO_F_NOTIFICATION_DATA` are not taken, nor is a queue reset of its own.
* A queue has at most 128 entries, and a buffer in an indirect table at most `indirect_len` segments, 16 unless its driver asks for up to 256.
* A device has at most `VIRTIO_MAX_QUEUES`, 8, queues, and the devices 32 interrupt slots among them: a device of 8 queues takes 9.
* One virtio console is driven, and its ports 0 and 1; a second console is left bound to virtio-pci alone. A port carries the console or nothing: there is no terminal of its own for `/dev/hvc0`, as Linux has, and no byte device for a generic port, so the node exists only while the port has the console's input. A port announced after the boot's wait, or bound later, is not given the log's ring.
* The device-specific fields are read and written as they are, which is right on the little-endian processors Rach2 runs on.
* A blob mapped from the VM's pages has no kernel mapping and is not given to a PRIME importer, since the taking back of its frames reaches only the programs that mapped it.

## Tests

`tests/host/virtqueue_hosttest.c` runs `virtqueue.c` against a model device through the table, at device addresses that are not host addresses ([host tests](../../../tests/host/Readme.md)), and `tests/host/virtio_pci_shm_hosttest.c` runs `virtio_pci.c`'s interrupt setup and capability walk, the binding, virtio device and `bind` uevent of each function it takes, a non-GPU function taken with its device, reset and acknowledged, a refused function, a failed bus registration and a reset that does not finish of a function found mastering, each leaving no number taken, no window mapped, the command word as it was less bus mastering and no PCI claim, and no reset at a probe or a bind that does not finish run with bus mastering on, and the driver's `unbind` and `bind` with their refusals, a driver's refusal and a held record, whose child frees its buffers after a reset that does not finish only once bus mastering is off. `tests/host/virtio_blk_hosttest.c` runs the core under the block driver, with indirect tables of 256 descriptors, a queue for each processor and a device that is halted or removed ([block](../block/Readme.md)). `tests/host/vgpushm_hosttest.c` runs `shmem.c` and the core against a model device in a region of made-up frames, and `tests/fuzz/vgpushm_fuzz.c` gives the same pair whatever a device may write into the region ([fuzz](../../../tests/fuzz/Readme.md)); `tests/cases/machine.bed-shm.json` draws through the test bed's VM over the region under Rach2's KVM ([kvm](../../../tests/kvm/Readme.md)). Its MSI-X rows require a remapped message for every entry, teardown before vector release, shared-vector fallback and cleanup after a remapper allocation failure. Its capability rows use two modeled functions: QEMU's host-visible region at 14 TiB with a second capability of its id, and regions in an I/O BAR, past their BAR, wrapping and empty, each refused. A probe that brought virtio-gpu up and sent it `GET_DISPLAY_INFO` was answered by interrupt on both architectures with `-device virtio-gpu-pci` (an MSI-X entry for each queue), `virtio-gpu-pci,vectors=2` (the shared entry), `virtio-gpu-pci,vectors=0` (the INTx line) and `virtio-vga`. `tests/host/virtio_console_hosttest.c` runs `virtio_console.c` with the serial console's line and the core against a model of QEMU's virtio-serial device, with and without `VIRTIO_F_EVENT_IDX`: the handshake, the `console=` words, output to a late device and to a host that stops reading, input held back by a full ring, removal and return, the panic's emergency write and each allocation failing. Etude's guest cases boot with the console on `vport0p1` ([etude](../../../tools/etude/Readme.md#the-qemu-launcher)), so each of them carries its frames and its probe's files over the driver. `tests/run hvc` runs `hvcmusl` on such a machine, which checks the bound device in `/sys`, `/dev/vport0p1` as the console terminal and the log's records of the port, and on a boot with `console=ttyS0`, where none of it is there.
